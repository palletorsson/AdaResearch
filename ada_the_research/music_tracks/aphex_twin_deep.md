## 2. SIGNATURE SOUNDS

### The "Aphex Acid" — TB-303 Techniques

Richard D. James's acid sound differs from standard acid house in several key ways:

**Characteristics:**
- **Longer, more complex patterns** — not just 16-step loops but evolving phrases
- **Extreme resonance manipulation** — pushing the filter to self-oscillation
- **Pitch slides (glide)** — used more musically, creating melodic phrases
- **Layering multiple 303 lines** — creating counterpoint instead of single-line patterns
- **Recording to cassette** — adds tape saturation and warmth (especially SAW 85-92)
- **Deliberate imperfection** — allowing "happy accidents" in programming

**Key Acid Tracks to Study:**
- "Phonatacid" (Analord 2) — 9+ minute acid odyssey
- "Lisbon Acid" (Analord 7)
- "Halibut Acid" (Analord 4)
- "Grumpy Acid" (Analord 1)
- "MC-4 Acid" (Analord 1)

### Drill 'n' Bass Drum Programming

James is a pioneer of drill 'n' bass — a 1995-2001 movement he helped define.

**Defining Characteristics:**
- **Extreme tempo manipulation** — breakbeats pushed to 160-200+ BPM
- **Micro-editing** — snare rolls, hi-hat fills at inhuman speeds (32nd notes and beyond)
- **Irregular rhythms** — deliberately avoiding 4/4 grid conformity
- **Jungle influence** — chopped Amen breaks, Think breaks, stretched and compressed
- **Hand-programmed** — "I hit the keyboard in a way I'd like the rhythm to sound, then spend 4 hours moving notes where I want them"
- **Unstable time signatures** — shifting polyrhythms within tracks

**Technique Breakdown:**
1. **Sample source breaks** (Amen, Think, Funky Drummer)
2. **Slice into individual hits**
3. **Re-sequence at double/triple speed**
4. **Add rolls, flams, and fills in 1/64th or 1/128th divisions**
5. **Layer with synthesized drums (808/909) for low-end punch**
6. **Heavily compress the result**

**Key Drill 'n' Bass Tracks:**
- "Hangable Auto Bulb" EPs (1995) — origin point
- "4" (Richard D. James Album)
- "Vordhosbn" (Drukqs)
- "54 Cymru Beats" (Drukqs)
- "Come to Daddy" title track

### Selected Ambient Works Character

The SAW 85-92 sound is defined by:

**Sonic Characteristics:**
- **Lo-fi warmth** — recorded to cassette (some damaged by cat!)
- **Simple but hypnotic patterns** — minimalist approach
- **Oceanic bass lines** — deep, rolling sub-bass
- **"Fuzzy" melodies** — degraded by tape, sample rate reduction
- **Shoegaze-influenced pads** — compared to My Bloody Valentine, Seefeel
- **Ethereal vocal samples** — processed beyond recognition
- **Synaesthetic composition** — James has synaesthesia, composed from "lucid dreams"

**Typical Elements:**
- 4/4 beats with shuffle
- Deep sub-bass (40-80 Hz dominant)
- Pads with long attack/release
- Minimal hi-hats, soft percussion
- Reverb-drenched everything
- Bitcrushed textures (early 12-bit/8-bit samplers)

**Key Ambient Tracks:**
- "Xtal" — definitive SAW sound
- "Ageispolis" — cinematic sweep, Satie-esque melody
- "Ptolemy" — driving yet dreamy
- "Tha" — murky, underwater quality
- "Heliosphan"

### Vocal Manipulation (Windowlicker/Come to Daddy Era)

**Techniques Used:**
- **Pitch shifting** — extreme upward/downward manipulation
- **Formant shifting** — separate pitch from character
- **Granular synthesis** — breaking voice into tiny grains
- **Vocoding** — synth-controlled vocal filtering
- **Time stretching** — without pitch correction for alien quality
- **Family samples** — Syro features his wife, children, and parents

**Windowlicker Specifics:**
- Main vocal is James himself, pitch-shifted and processed
- Multiple layers of processed voices
- Vocoder treatments
- Rhythmic gating/stuttering

## 3. SYNTHESIS TECHNIQUES

### Distinctive Bass Sound

**What Makes It Unique:**
1. **Sub-bass focus** — 40-80 Hz fundamental
2. **Saturation/Warmth** — analogue circuits or tape
3. **Simple waveforms** — square and saw, sometimes sine
4. **Slow filter sweeps** — gradual evolution
5. **Detuning** — multiple oscillators slightly out of tune
6. **Low-pass filtering** — removing harshness while retaining weight

**Bass Patch Starting Points:**
```
Oscillator 1: Sawtooth, -1 octave
Oscillator 2: Square, -1 octave, detune +5 cents
Filter: Low-pass, 24dB/oct
Cutoff: 200-400 Hz
Resonance: 20-40%
Env Amount: +30%
Attack: 0-5ms
Decay: 200-400ms
Sustain: 60%
Release: 100-200ms
```

### Filtering Approach

**Resonance Settings:**
- Generally **moderate resonance (30-50%)** for warmth
- **High resonance (70-100%)** for acid lines
- Self-oscillating filter for "screaming" acid sounds

**Envelope Characteristics:**
- **Fast attack** (1-10ms) for punchy sounds
- **Medium decay** (100-500ms) for movement
- **Variable sustain** based on sound type
- **Long release** (200-1000ms) for ambient elements

**Filter Cutoff Sweet Spots:**
| Sound Type | Cutoff Range |
|------------|--------------|
| Sub-bass | 80-200 Hz |
| Acid bass | 400-2000 Hz (sweeping) |
| Ambient pads | 800-3000 Hz |
| Leads | 1000-5000 Hz |

### Polyrhythmic Layering

**Approach:**
- Layer patterns at different divisions (8ths, triplets, 16ths)
- Use odd time groupings (5, 7, 9 notes)
- Pan different rhythm elements
- Filter automation on individual layers
- Subtle tempo differences between layers

**Common Combinations:**
- 4/4 kick pattern + 7/8 hi-hat pattern
- 16th bass pattern + triplet percussion
- Straight beat + shuffled/swung overlay

### Detuning & Chorus Settings

**Typical Settings:**
```
Oscillator Detune: ±5-15 cents (subtle)
Chorus Rate: 0.5-2 Hz (slow)
Chorus Depth: 15-35%
Unison Voices: 2-4
Unison Detune: ±10-25 cents
```

**Creating "That" Sound:**
1. Start with sawtooth oscillator
2. Add second oscillator, same waveform, detune +7 cents
3. Apply chorus (slow rate, moderate depth)
4. Low-pass filter with slight resonance
5. Add subtle reverb

### Typical Scales & Keys

**Preferred Modes:**
- **Minor keys predominate** — A minor, D minor, C minor common
- **Dorian mode** — lifted minor with major 6th
- **Lydian mode** — bright major with raised 4th
- **Mixolydian** — major with flattened 7th

**Harmonic Approach:**
- Simple chord progressions (often 2-4 chords)
- Modal interchange
- Suspended chords
- Minor 7ths and 9ths
- Quartal voicings (stacked 4ths)

**Melodic Characteristics:**
- Pentatonic basis with chromatic additions
- Stepwise motion with occasional leaps
- Repetition with slight variation
- Octave jumps for emphasis

|---------|-----------------|

### Attack/Decay Characteristics

**Drums:**
```
Kick: Attack 0ms, Decay 100-300ms
Snare: Attack 0ms, Decay 80-200ms
Hi-hats: Attack 0ms, Decay 50-150ms
```

**Bass:**
```
Attack: 0-10ms (punchy) or 20-50ms (softer)
Decay: 200-500ms
Sustain: 50-80%
Release: 100-300ms
```

**Pads:**
```
Attack: 100-500ms (slow bloom)
Decay: 500-2000ms
Sustain: 60-80%
Release: 500-2000ms
```

**Leads:**
```
Attack: 5-30ms
Decay: 100-300ms
Sustain: 70-90%
Release: 100-400ms
```

## 5. PRODUCTION PHILOSOPHY

### Key Quotes from James

> "I haven't done something in so much detail before... it's quite similar to guitar solos, only with programming you have to use your brain."

> "Sometimes I just hit the keyboard in a way I'd like the rhythm of the tracks to sound. Then I'll spend four hours moving all the notes where I want them to go."

> "The way I've wired [equipment] together becomes the track in itself."

> "What if you could reconstruct source audio from a selection of other mp3's/audio on your computer?" — on Samplebrain

### Workflow Insights

1. **Hardware-centric until mid-90s**, then hybrid computer/hardware
2. **Recording to cassette** — embraces degradation
3. **Modifies equipment** — since age 12
4. **Multiple studios** — 6 different setups for Syro alone
5. **Extensive sound design** — builds patch = track
6. **Long gestation** — some tracks "6-7 years old" at release
7. **Family involvement** — samples family voices

## 7. PRACTICAL TIPS FOR RECREATION

### Quick Checklist: "Aphex Sound"

- [ ] Analogue or analogue-modeled synths
- [ ] Moderate detuning (+5-15 cents)
- [ ] Low-pass filtering with medium resonance
- [ ] Slow chorus on pads
- [ ] Deep sub-bass (check on monitors, not just headphones)
- [ ] Complex, hand-programmed drums (avoid quantize)
- [ ] Tape saturation or subtle distortion
- [ ] Long reverb tails on ambient elements
- [ ] Minimal hi-hats for ambient, complex hats for drill 'n' bass
- [ ] Family/personal samples for human element

### DAW Template Suggestions

**Ambient Track:**
- Pad synth (slow attack, long release)
- Sub-bass (filtered, detuned oscillators)
- Drum machine (lo-fi, compressed)
- Send effects: Long reverb, tape delay
- Master: Tape saturation, gentle compression

**Drill 'n' Bass Track:**
- Breakbeat sampler (sliced, stretched)
- 808/909 drums for punch
- Bass synth (fast attack, short decay)
- Lead synth (fast, articulate)
- Heavy compression on drums
- Sidechain on bass from kick
