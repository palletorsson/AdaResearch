## How we work together

1. **Read one hall in its existing form.** Begin with what holds the attention and what the reader learns to do. Keep the preceding and following hall in view.
2. **You mark a reaction in ordinary language.** “Keep the counter passage.” “I lose the thread at this paragraph.” “More of the pole-dance association.” “This artifact needs an encounter.” You do not have to supply replacement prose or a full editorial specification.
3. **I return a small proposal beside the original.** Name the passage, why I suggest a change, the replacement, and any implication for the artifact. A factual repair, a change of voice, and a proposed new interaction should be distinguishable.
4. **You choose what enters the book.** “Keep,” “use this version,” or “try again” is enough. We apply that choice and preserve the preceding text. Reading approval and a Git commit are separate actions; this review makes no Git commit.
5. **Move forward with questions still open.** A hall can be ready for the next pass without being finished. A later encounter can give us a reason to return.

Approval should have a scope: a paragraph, an encounter, a hall. Liking one passage should not authorise a stylistic overhaul of its neighbours. A new draft does not become better merely by being newer, shorter, or more uniform.

## Does the rest hold?

Yes, as a working sequence. The progression is legible:

**Close a boundary → enclose a volume → change the assembled form → inspect the cost of its appearance → choose when an approximation is enough → decide what the tools are for.**

The six chapters have substantial existing encounters. My recommendation is to keep their current texts as the starting point and review a few local pressure points. The recommendations below are my reading, not Palle's approval of each chapter and not a fresh headset verification.

## Triangle: a boundary does not automatically become a face

**Keep the two main encounters.** Closing the three edges while the centre remains empty is an excellent next step from Grid. Showing and hiding the fill makes the new operation available to see. The L-shaped outline then gives the filling algorithm a specific difficulty: changing the starting corner can change what its triangle fan fills, despite keeping the intended boundary.

The sentence “The machine's convenient rule need not become a rule requiring every visitor to want a convenient shape” earns its critical force through that experiment. The visitor has a shape they want; understanding the algorithm gives them a way to insist on it.

**Review one transition.** The six-corner coordinate recipe asks a great deal of a beginner all at once. We could keep its precision and offer it as a second, slower encounter after the first successful triangle. That is a proposal about pacing, not a reason to remove the L-shaped comparison. The subsequent quads, folds and side-visibility passages can remain further invitations.

First proposed review target: the transition into “Make a six-corner L in one horizontal plane.” No replacement has been applied.

## Polyhedra: a form can carry or interrupt a body

**Keep the passage from the open corner to the wedge and barrier.** It develops both the geometry of enclosure and the uses of a surface. The slope gives forward movement a way upward. The barrier can redirect a route or protect a place. The signs ask something that their own geometry cannot enforce.

This already carries the methodological argument: arrangement and implementation let us investigate what a form permits. The geometry is useful before we ask what another use could be.

**Review the concentration of new concepts.** Angular defect, Euler characteristic and convex-hull collision arrive before the passage about slopes. All are relevant, but they compete for attention. Read that middle section aloud before deciding whether one account should become a return visit. We should not automatically remove its precision or declare the bodily encounter more important than every mathematical one.

First proposed review target: the move from the pyramid's changing apex into angular defect and Euler counting. The wedge/barrier passage is worth preserving closely.

## Animated Cube: your hand reveals the seam

**This is one of the strongest chapters to keep.** The net folds; the builder reveals its layers; a corner moves while its connection list stays. “The seam was already there. Your hand made its consequence visible” is the voice working through the implementation.

REPLAY reconstructs the presentation of the changed form; RESTORE CUBE resets the corner positions. That difference gives the reader two concrete meanings of returning. The invitation to make a bent shelter or an awkward opening also gives deformation a purpose beyond failing to remain a cube.

**Review only the ending's rhythm.** The concluding instruction to distinguish four operations sounds more like an assessment than the encounter that precedes it. That may be useful for a teacher. We can decide together whether it also belongs in the reader's exit from the room, or whether the question about the next sphere is enough there. No change is necessary before making that judgement.

## Ignorance: what did smoothness cost, and did you want it?

**Keep the invoice and the choice before revealing it.** Choosing a sphere for a particular view, then inspecting the actual mesh count, makes cost and desire meet in a specific object. The difference between triangle entries and triangles with area gives the instrument something surprising to disclose.

The later argument also matters: the five regular convex solids form a finite family because the question restricts it. A faceted capsule or irregular rock can offer something a smooth ideal would remove. That is a productive beginning for the later discussion of noise and blob culture.

**One small clarity proposal:** the paragraph beginning “Count six vertices, twelve edges and eight faces” should name the octahedron being counted. The artifact is present, but the reader has just been looking at several solids. Suggested opening, not applied: “Find the octahedron. Count six vertices, twelve edges and eight faces.”

## Portals: enough from here, endless in principle

**Keep the reversal and the finite limit demonstration.** Choosing a ring that seems round enough, moving closer, and reversing the ladder gives the order itself something to say. Refinement is a direction we have chosen to stage. A visitor can find a reason to return to the facets.

Achilles then brings back Point One's running time through an instrument with its own clock and stopping point. The text carefully separates the ten displayed stages, the easing between them, and the mathematical limit. Those distinctions protect the experiment from making a false claim.

**Review the handover between its two main encounters.** Portal subdivisions, planar-circle perimeter, and Achilles are three related constructions. A short invitation could make their shared question more palpable: when do you decide you have arrived? We should test whether that link is actually missing when reading, rather than assume a new bridge paragraph is required.

## Melencolia: the tools cannot choose the purpose

**Keep it as the sequence's gathering place.** The pyramid detector recognises a connection pattern; the court gives that form an arrangement; the Dürer scene gathers earlier instruments into another kind of attention. The magic square supplies an exact answer without deciding what an exact arrangement is for.

“An exact arrangement can be wanted for itself” is important to preserve. It makes room for ornament, contemplation and desire after so much counting. The closing possibility that building was not yet the question lets the chapter hesitate rather than simply demand another exercise.

**Separate the metadata repair from the literary reading.** The book names cube_scene, prism_block, bigframe, code_display and diamondtoruscollection, but their current roles are secondary. eleven_dots is primary without a book passage. All are present in the map. The two registers need a decision: align the five discussed artifacts with the book and decide what work eleven_dots should do. This review records the mismatch and leaves the current roles unchanged.

Literary review target: whether the final three-judgement exercise helps the reader, or interrupts the unresolved attention the Dürer passage has opened.

## What this review does not settle

Some “further comparison” passages in Triangle, Animated Cube and Ignorance discuss placed secondary artifacts without individual book anchors. The encounter exists in prose, but it will not appear as its own primary entry. We should distinguish **a sustained book encounter** from **a brief reference to something nearby**, then apply that distinction to the actual passages. A rigid word-count threshold would not resolve it.

The new approach is to preserve what holds, locate a specific uncertainty, and let a small proposal be read against its original. Our first useful decision can be as small as naming the octahedron. Our next substantial reading can be the Triangle chapter, with its existing experiments intact.
