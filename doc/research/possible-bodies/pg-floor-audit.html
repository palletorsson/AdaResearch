Three records can disagree.

The source map_data.json describes the room. The portable em_plan.json contains a saved interpretation. A map-authored hall normally rereads its source when Godot builds it, then applies museum staging and saved rulings. A pushed user:// map can override the packaged source. The plan alone is therefore insufficient evidence of the floor in a running headset.

Before we write the next hall

  1. Name who supplies support: museum slab, authored platform, an artifact's collider, or an intentional void.
  2. Stage the generated space: define its footprint, a clear museum margin and an entrance/exit. Keep the circulation route usable independently of random outcomes unless the encounter explicitly tests that dependence.
  3. Check the actual body: measure artifact placement and scale, then test collision at entrances, controls, void edges and the onward route.
  4. Record exceptions in the map: use explicit platforms and the correct wall threshold; use museum.floor_cells for specific museum covers when the legacy grid must retain a zero. Do not globally redefine zero.
  5. Write from that result: distinguish a generated image, generated geometry and a space that actually carries a body.
