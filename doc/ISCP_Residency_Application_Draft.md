# Application Draft: ISCP Residency New York (Konstnärsnämnden)

**Project Title:** Ada Research: Algorithmic Margins and Queer Ecologies

## Project Summary
*Ada Research* is a Virtual Reality artistic research project that explores the intersection of computational geometry, queer theory, and entropy. By guiding players through a landscape of "glitched" algorithms, the project visualizes how code shapes subjectivity and creates "invisible fences" (Morozov). The project is an open-source pedagogical tool—an updated, virtual version of Paul Klee’s *Pedagogical Sketchbook* in drag—designed to help artists and researchers understand the hidden politics of digital tools.

## Purpose of the Residency
I am applying for the ISCP residency to transition *Ada Research* from a singular developmental phase into a participatory, networked dialogue within the world's leading center for creative coding and critical technology: New York City.

My specific goals for this residency are threefold:

### 1. Dialogue with the Creative Coding Community
New York is the epicenter of the open-source creative coding movement. During the residency, I intend to connect with key figures who have shaped the pedagogical tools my project builds upon. Specifically, I want to engage with **Daniel Shiffman** (The Coding Train/NYU ITP), whose work on the "Nature of Code" democratized algorithmic thinking, and **Casey Reas** (co-founder of Processing), whose conceptual work on *software structures* aligns with the project's mission. I also aim to connect with **Lauren McCarthy** (creator of p5.js), whose work on social performance and "artist interventions" offers a critical model for how *Ada Research* can operate not just as software, but as a social provocation.

### 2. Pedagogical Exchange with the School for Poetic Computation (SFPC)
I aim to engage in pedagogical exchange with the **School for Poetic Computation (SFPC)**. Their "more poetry, less demo" ethos and focus on "Decoding Tendencies in Live Coding" and "Narrative Constellations" (Winter 2026 curriculum) aligns perfectly with *Ada Research's* mission. I plan to attend public events and explore potential collaborations or guest critiques, investigating how their radical pedagogy can inform the tutorial structure of my VR world. SFPC's model of "interdisciplinary study in art, code, hardware, and critical theory" serves as a real-world blueprint for the virtual "resistor hackerspace" I am building.

### 3. Fieldwork at NYC Resistor: The Haptic Noise Controller
I plan to conduct practical fieldwork and collaborative feedback sessions at **NYC Resistor**, the hacker collective in Brooklyn. This aligns directly with my research into "Hacker Ethics" and "Commoning" (Federici/Stallman). 

Beyond software, I aim to prototype a **"Haptic Noise Controller"** at NYC Resistor. This physical interface will translate the VR world's "entropy" and "glitch" algorithms into tactile feedback, grounding the virtual queer theory in a physical body. The hacker collective's expertise in hardware hacking is unique and essential for this phase. I want to present the *Ada Research* open-source framework to this community to test its viability as a "resistor hackerspace"—a digital tool for resistance and repair. Their feedback will be crucial in refining the project's architecture to ensure it remains transparent, repairable, and accessible, countering the "black box" nature of commercial AI tools.

### 4. International Dissemination and Critique: Post-Digital Materiality
The ISCP’s visiting critic program is vital for the next stage of *Ada Research*. Having developed the core prototype, I now need to contextualize the work within the international art market and critical discourse, specifically targeting the discourse around **"Post-Internet Art" and "Digital Materiality"** prevalent in New York.

I aim to present the work to New York-based curators and theorists, such as **Christiane Paul** (Adjunct Curator of Digital Art at the Whitney Museum) and organizations like **Rhizome**, who have been instrumental in defining the field. The residency would provide the necessary platform to demonstrate how *Ada Research* bridges the gap between interactive installation and academic research, preparing the work for future large-scale exhibitions (building on discussions with partners like Acute Art). I want to frame the "glitch" not as an error, but as a "Queer Art of Failure" (Halberstam), a concept that resonates deeply with the New York critical scene.

### 5. Queer Ecology and The City
New York City itself is a landscape of "Queer Ecology"—a complex, non-binary system of organic and synthetic life. I plan to research the history of queer performance art in the city (from the Factory to the Piers) as a form of "algorithmic distortion." How did these communities "hack" the city's grid? This historical research will directly inform the "City as Circuit" level in *Ada Research*.

## Work Plan
*   **Months 1-2:** Studio setup and integration of the "Resistor" open-source module. Initial outreach to NYC Resistor, SFPC, and NYU ITP communities. Begin archival research into NYC's queer performance history.
*   **Month 3:** **VR Performance "Show and Tell":** I will organize a participatory "Show and Tell" event (a format popularized by NYC Resistor and ITP) to demonstrate the *Ada Research* VR environment as a live performance tool. This will not be a static demo, but an interactive session where the audience can "live code" the environment in real-time, testing the "Haptic Noise Controller" prototype.
*   **Month 4:** **Open Workshops in VR Development:** I will host a series of open workshops for the local community (in collaboration with NYC Resistor or ISCP), teaching the "Generative Play" method and the *Ada Research* codebase. Participants will learn to build their own "glitched" VR artifacts, effectively contributing back to the open-source repository.
*   **Ongoing:** Regular studio visits with ISCP curators to refine the exhibition format of the VR experience, focusing on the "Post-Digital" narrative. Open Studio events where the VR experience is presented alongside the physical Haptic Controller as a unified sculptural installation.

## Conclusion
A residency at ISCP would place *Ada Research* in the exact context it needs to evolve: between the rigorous critical theory of the art world and the radical openness of the hacker community. It is an opportunity to let the project be "thrown" (Heidegger) into a new, complex system, allowing the friction of New York's digital scene—from SFPC's poetic coding to NYC Resistor's hardware hacking—to generate the necessary entropy for its next evolutionary leap.
