## Where Ada is ahead (most of the chapter)

NOC's oscillation chapter is a p5.js sketchbook: a baton that spins, a circle that slides, a row of circles that undulates, a bob on a spring, a pendulum. Ada's twelve halls have all of it in metres and seconds, with a body in the room, and go past it:

- **The pendulum is NOC's exact rule, and then some.** `-(gravity / pendulum_length) * sin(_angle)`, velocity, damping, angle — the same four lines as NOC's Example 3.11 — but multiplied by `delta`, with a hand whose release is a dot product onto the tangent ("how much of the hand gets through?"), several beginnings that need not coincide, and a prediction curve integrated by the same update at the same step. NOC assumes one frame is one unit of time and says so in a parenthesis.
- **Time becomes a place.** Pendulum's record turns age into depth at 0.6 m/s and lets you walk beside a duration; FINE, COARSE and STROBE are three answers to what a record can afford. NOC's period is "120 frames".
- **Two clocks on sliders.** NOC's Example 3.9 needs a `startAngle` beside the `deltaAngle` or the wave glitches; Sine_Space has both as AMP, FREQ and PHASE on a panel, three layers on one running phase drifting at different speeds, and a passage you can walk through because the walls have no collision.
- **Additive synthesis as a bench.** NOC's Exercise 3.12 ("add multiple waves together") is Synthesis_Lab's five sliders, the mark, the plate that sums the visible rows, and the threshold at which the label gives up on "sawtooth".
- **Polar to solid.** NOC's spiral exercise (change one line) is Bernini's helix read twice (through height, through time) and Sky_Stairs' breathing radius, with the question NOC never asks: what a sampled curve needs before a body can use it.
- **What NOC has no chapter for:** a coupled lattice (3D_Wave), beats (TrigWalkingPath), phase modulation with HOLD and COMPARE (Effect_Sound), a struck bar's exponential decay with two mappings for one hand (AirMusic), chance inside a prepared vocabulary (Cage), Lucier's room.

## What to learn (eleven items, each already performed by a hall or standing in it unnamed)

1. **Sixty ticks a second is the desk.** Pendulum: "The recorder works on a physics clock ticking sixty times per second… 16.7 to 33.3 milliseconds." XR Tools sets `Engine.physics_ticks_per_second` from the headset's refresh rate at XR start (`start_xr.gd:326`, multiplier 1), so the museum's primary venue ticks 72 or 90 times a second; the desk and every headless probe tick 60. The mechanism holds — a 25 ms request between ticks alternates between two neighbouring gaps — but the numbers are the desk's (at 90 Hz: 22.2 and 33.3 ms; at 72: 13.9 and 27.8). NOC is frank that its time unit is the frame; Ada's chapter should be frank about which frame.
2. **The damping line is a trick, and it runs faster in the headset.** NOC: "rather than model the resistance forces… reduce the angular velocity by an arbitrary amount… 0.99 for each frame." `control_pendulum.gd`'s shipped `free` regime is that trick exactly — `damping` 0.995 per physics tick, kept "bit identical… including its frame-rate dependence" by the author of the other four regimes, which use `exp(-2ζω₀·dt)`. Per second the velocity keeps 0.740 at 60 Hz and 0.637 at 90 Hz: the same release dies sooner in the headset. The prediction curve integrates at the machine's own `dt`, so it agrees with the live swing on each machine and differs between them. Name the trick in the Intro, or make `free` rate-independent (a shipped-default change; Palle's call).
3. **The wagon-wheel effect.** NOC names it in passing (a spinning object "appears to be rotating more slowly or even in the opposite direction because of the large changes between each frame"). STROBE in Pendulum is that fault chosen on purpose — "it can also make repetition look like stillness" — and a footnote can say so, with the film camera as the familiar case.
4. **Wavelength.** NOC: when the wave is drawn across space "the term period shifts its meaning… the spatial period of a wave is wavelength." Pendulum turns a 2.84-second swing (2 m line) into 1.7 m of record; Sine_Space says "4.5 cycles over seven metres" (1.56 m each). Neither says the word.
5. **Hooke, and what the small-angle approximation does.** 3D_Wave's "restoring influence towards an equilibrium… a neighbour displaced differently contributes a force" is Hooke's 1660 "as the extension, so the force"; the Intro's "small-angle approximation in which length… sets the period" never says the approximation replaces sin θ by θ — which is what makes the pendulum a Hooke oscillator, the rule every sphere of the lattice runs. `spring_demo` (F = −kx, a regime axis: ringing · critical · overdamped) stands in the Synthesis Lab unmentioned.
6. **One ratio loses the half.** Unit_Circle: "Knowing the height therefore does not recover the whole angular position." NOC's `atan` gives −37° for both (−4, 3) and (4, −3); `atan2` takes two numbers to keep the directions apart. The hall has the second rod already; say that it restores what the first loses.
7. **The radian.** Never named in twelve halls; PHASE is "half a turn", the code says `TAU`. NOC defines it as arc over radius; on the unit circle the angle is the distance the point has travelled along the rim (6.28 m a turn). `UnitCircle.gd` stretches each turn to 14 m (`wave_length` 28 for two cycles), so the graph's axis is the rim unrolled, not to scale.
8. **Frequency is one over the period.** Period in Intro and Pendulum, hertz in Effect_Sound and AirMusic, never joined. 440 Hz is one return every 2.3 ms; the scope's `SCOPE_PERIODS = 2.0` is 4.5 ms of the note.
9. **Lissajous.** NOC's Oscillator class (a vector of two angles with two angular velocities) is a Lissajous figure without the name. `lissajous_curves` stands in TrigWalkingPath and Synthesis_Lab, unmentioned, with ratios 1:1 (a diagonal; a circle at a quarter-turn phase — the Unit_Circle point drawn by its own projections), 2:1, 3:2, 5:4 and 1:φ, "the one value that does NOT close". The beat display adds two oscillations on one row; the figure puts them at right angles. Same pair, two combinations.
10. **The hall's other pendulums.** Pendulum says the bob "has only swung in one plane"; across the hall `foucault_pendulum` keeps its plane while the program turns the paper under it at 0.05 rad/s × sin 45° — one turn in three minutes, about 700 times faster than the Earth would at that latitude (Paris, 1851: just under 32 hours). `WavePaintings` is NOC's Exercise 3.15 built: three double pendulums, the middle bob grabbable, painting unrepeatable traces. Both unmentioned; both are recorders in a chapter about what records keep.
11. **A clock is not a state.** NOC contrasts a bob scripted by `sin()` (Exercise 3.7) with one that answers forces (Hooke's section): "that kind of one-line solution won't do if what you really want is a bob… that responds to other forces." The Intro's coda has both — `y_oscillation_cube` is `A·sin(2πft)` from time alone, `oscillation_controlled_cube` listens to the pendulum's `oscillation_updated` signal — and describes them at rest. A push tells them apart.

## Not to learn

- Frames as the unit of time, and `angleAcceleration = acceleration.x / 10` ("yes, this is arbitrary").
- The Asteroids and cannon exercises; `heading()`.
- The Ecosystem Project (oscillation driving a creature's legs and wings): that belongs to the biome and the nature system, not to the book.
