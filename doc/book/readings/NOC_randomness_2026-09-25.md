# What the randomness chapters can learn from *Nature of Code* ch. 0

Read 25 September 2026 against the fourteen `final.md` of the `randomness` sequence (17,516 words — the book's largest: Definition 999 · Entropy 1,785 · Remove 1,734 · 10 PRINT 1,339 · Cubes 1,381 · Rotate 808 · Walk 1,707 · Gaussian 1,123 · Mushrooms 1,497 · Space Geometry 625 · Examples 1,205 · Pheromone 632 · Space 611 · Game 2,070). Source: https://natureofcode.com/random/ (Daniel Shiffman, 2nd ed.; about 9,000 words). No agents; the artifacts were read by hand where a claim needed them.

## Where Ada is ahead (most of the ground)

NOC's chapter is a toolbox: the random walk, uniform vs weighted vs normal vs custom distributions, then noise. Ada's sequence already contains all of it and a great deal NOC has no room for:

- **The seed as an operation, not a number.** Random_Definition's +1 DRAW — one discarded draw and the same stream dresses sixty-four cubes differently — is a better account of "pseudorandom" than any sentence in NOC.
- **Position vs step.** NOC's Exercise 0.7 (map noise to step size instead of position) is Ada's rails: the blue block takes a place, the pink block takes a step from where it is. "A walk needs somewhere to start."
- **What a record keeps.** The terrarium's trail that the step rule never reads; the glass that folds an overshoot back and draws only the kept positions; the die's glass that keeps frequencies and loses order; the shelf that lets its oldest pair go. NOC has one screenshot with a trail.
- **The set before the choice** (Random_Remove), **the gauge that forgets** (Random_Entropy), **draw / choose / reveal** (Random_Game's door trials), the coin's SPIN / DROP / TILT gauges, the sampler's guard on `u1` and its clipping count. None of this exists in NOC.
- **Honesty about the mixer**: Random_Space says its base "is made from several sine expressions". NOC has to confess that p5's `noise()` is value noise and not Perlin; Ada's PERLIN side really is FastNoiseLite's gradient Perlin (`perlin_noise_bridge.gd:48`).

## What to learn (ten items, each grounded in a hall)

1. **Name the 1955 book.** NOC opens on it: *A Million Random Digits with 100,000 Normal Deviates* (RAND, 1955; the digits made in 1947 by an electronic roulette wheel), the last of the printed tables, ended by machines that could make numbers faster than a reader could look them up. Random_Definition's page "takes its look from a book of random digits printed in 1955" and already says the true thing — a table anyone could open at the same line — without the name (task .013). One footnote, with Coveyou's line for the epigraph it deserves.
2. **The coin ribbon is a random walk waiting to happen.** NOC's first image: a coin on a balance beam, heads a step forward, tails a step back. Ada keeps the last thirty tosses *in order* on the coin console (Random_Cubes) and stands the one-dimensional walker two halls later (the pink block on its rail). Neither chapter points at the other. One sentence at the ribbon ("read it as steps — where would the walker be?") and one at the rail ("the coin you tossed two rooms ago") make the sequence's own halls do NOC's opening.
3. **Oversampling has a picture in the hall, and LONG STEP is its remedy — say Lévy.** NOC: random walkers revisit positions (oversampling), so a forager takes a rare long step — the Lévy flight. Ada's 128 field raises a cell for every revisit ("a high place records repeated visits") and the terrarium's LONG STEP is a power-law step (`WalkMode.LEVY_FLIGHT`, `LEVY_OFFSET`, `LEVY_EXPONENT`, `LEVY_STEP_CAP`) that the chapter describes without naming. Name it, say what it is for, and footnote Paul Lévy and Mandelbrot's coinage.
4. **Say what a standard deviation means to a reader.** NOC's rule of thumb — 68 % within one deviation, 95 % within two, 99.7 % within three — is absent from the sequence; the sampler says "the standard deviation is 0.15" and nothing else. One sentence at the sampler ("two draws in three land within 0.15 of the middle, nineteen in twenty within 0.30"), and one at the splatter, whose "about two draws in five" is the *two-dimensional* version of the same rule (`safe_zone_radius` = `stddev`; a disc, not an interval: 1 − e^−½ ≈ 0.39). The contrast is a small gift: the same σ, two in three on a line and two in five on a plane.
5. **Name the two methods the halls already run.** The mushrooms' rejected candidates — "a number crossed a threshold" — are the accept–reject method (propose, test, keep or throw away), which NOC names and calls "a type of Monte Carlo method (named for the Monte Carlo Casino)". The dartboard hall never says why it is called Monte Carlo. Footnotes: Metropolis and Ulam 1949, the casino, and the sampler's beads as the alternative (a formula instead of a rejection).
6. **Same average, different feel.** NOC: a new walker every 100 frames, or a 1 % chance each frame — the same rate, and "the latter incorporates chance and feels more dynamic". Random_Game already stands both (the spawner's exact half-second clock; the podium's drawn 0.3–1.3 s delays) and says "the word cannot tell you where to look". One sentence with the equivalence — a clock every half-second, or one every tenth with a one-in-five chance: the same cubes an hour, a different room to stand in.
7. **The hidden frequency knob in the mushroom bed.** NOC's increment lesson (sample noise 0.01 apart, not 1, or the clouds vanish). The clearings come from `noise.get_noise_2d(pos_x * 2, pos_z * 2)` on a field with `frequency = 0.5` (`mushrooms.gd:650–662`): features on the order of a metre. That factor is the FREQUENCY slider the reader meets two rooms on, fixed here by hand. One sentence.
8. **Perlin, dated.** The PERLIN side of Random_Space_Geometry is gradient noise after Ken Perlin's 1985 paper (the Tron work of 1983; the Academy Award of 1997; simplex in 2001); the mixer two rooms on is sines. A footnote — and the honest note NOC makes about its own library, in Ada's favour.
9. **With and without replacement, with the arithmetic.** NOC's Exercise 0.2 (two aces, reshuffled or not) is the hoppers in Random_Remove. One sentence: the chance of drawing the number you just drew is one in eight on the left and none on the right; after seven pulls the right-hand case has one answer and the left still has eight.
10. **A σ control on the sampler** (NOC Exercise 0.4: "create a slider to adjust the standard deviation"). `distribution_sampler` has `gaussian_std` as a variable and no control for it, so the sentence in item 4 cannot be tested in the hall. A WIDE / NARROW button (0.08 / 0.15 / 0.25) is a small code change; the histogram then shows what σ is.

## Not to learn

- The Walker class as an OOP lesson: the book is not teaching JavaScript.
- "random() is random enough for the examples in this book": Ada's whole first hall is about why that sentence hides the seed.
- The closing warning against answering every question with Perlin noise: the sequence's stance already ("randomness varies what the program has made variable").
