# What the cellular automata chapters can learn from *Nature of Code* ch. 7

Read 25 September 2026 against the eight `final.md` of the `cellularautomata` sequence (6,384 words: Introduction 1,364 · ElementaryRules 783 · GameOfLife 601 · BeyondBinary 706 · ExpandingSpace 758 · SoftRules 892 · AgentsCircuits 546 · EdgeOfChaos 734). Source: https://natureofcode.com/cellular-automata/ (Daniel Shiffman, 2nd ed.). No agents; the artifacts and maps were read by hand. The sequence was skeptic-verified in the 24 Sept spine reading and carries 25 open tasks already; this note adds eight and does not repeat those.

## Where Ada is ahead (keep — and there is more of it here than in fractals)

- **The schedule is a fourth element.** NOC has grid, state, neighbourhood, and treats in-place overwriting as "one major blunder" to be fixed with a second array. Ada's Introduction closes on three choices — what a cell can hold, which neighbours it consults, *and when its answer becomes available to others* — and ExpandingSpace turns that third choice into an instrument (SYNC / SCAN / REVERSE: "the loop has a direction too"). Nothing in NOC lets a reader watch the blunder change a result.
- **Edges are named, not skipped.** NOC lists three edge policies and takes the lazy one. Ada says which each hall uses: the board is a ring ("after cell 31 comes cell 0"), the 3D volume's outer cells stay empty and do not wrap, the dish wraps, the reaction–diffusion field wraps by remainder.
- **The 1D history is walked.** NOC warns that a stacked 1D CA "is not a 2D CA". Ada's bridge makes depth do the work of time and lets the body find out ("a solid-looking arrangement can hide that difference").
- **The body as a condition** (the decaying bridge), **the lamp as a one-way boundary** (LINK), **two runs and their difference** (EdgeOfChaos), **integer vs fractional storage**, and the honest clocks ("held at generation 48; the museum clock continues"). None of these exist in NOC.
- **The textile.** NOC opens on kente cloth as decoration. Ada's rug says outright that "these few operations do not account for that tradition, its materials or its makers".

## What to learn (eight items, each already reachable in a hall)

1. **Wolfram's four classes are the missing map of the 256 rules — and the last hall's name is class 4.** NOC: rule 222 (uniform), rule 190 (repeating), rule 30 (random), rule 110 (complex), then "class 4 as the bridge between CA and nature". Ada's explorer takes any rule 0–255 on its RULE slider and has buttons 30 / 90 / 110 / 184 (`ca_rule_explorer.gd:35, 251–258`; `FAMOUS_RULES` names 30 "Chaotic", 90 "Sierpiński", 110 "Turing Complete", 184 "Traffic", 250 "Solid"), yet the chapters visit only 90 and 30. EdgeOfChaos ends "Let the name remain a question" without saying what the name refers to: Wolfram's classes (1984) and Langton's *Computation at the edge of chaos* (1990). A Search beat — dial 222, 190, 30, 110, say what each does before the classes are named — gives the question its referent and the hall its edge (where does class 3 stop and 4 begin? nobody can say from the picture).
2. **Nobody is named.** Eight chapters, one attribution ("Conway's Game of Life"). NOC gives Ulam and von Neumann (Los Alamos, 1940s, 29-state self-replicators), Wolfram (the numbering; *A New Kind of Science*, 2002), Conway via Gardner's 1970 *Scientific American* column. Ada's halls add Gray–Scott (1984) and Lenia (Chan, 2019; task .012), Langton's ant (1986) for `langton_swarm`, Kermack–McKendrick's SIR (1927) for `disease_spread_ca`, and Cook's 2004 proof for the explorer's "Turing Complete" label — which is what the AgentsCircuits footnote should cite: the resemblance is not the proof, and the proof exists elsewhere.
3. **Colour the change, not the state** (NOC Ex. 7.3: births blue, deaths red). The dish draws alive and dead only (`game_of_life_petri.gd:39–40`). The chapter's best sentence — "Following the figure, we can miss the cells that cease to participate" — is exactly what the two extra colours cure; ElementaryRules already counts CHANGED. A small code change and one sentence.
4. **Name the three kinds of pattern once, after they have been watched.** NOC: still lifes, oscillators, spaceships. The dish has all three — the block in MEET, BLINKER, GLIDER — and the chapter's "Returning, travelling and staying can belong to different parts of the same small event" is the observation; the names can follow it.
5. **The variations are standing in the halls and are called "other works".** NOC's Variations section — grid shape, probabilistic, continuous, historical, moving cells, nesting — is a frame Ada could use for what it already places: `hexagon_ca_vr` (six neighbours, birth on two — "the shape of the neighborhood reshapes the world") in BeyondBinary, `langton_swarm` (turmites: cells that move) in Introduction, `disease_spread_ca` (probabilistic SIR) in EdgeOfChaos, `rule110_computer`, `mold_network`, `ca_growth_network`. Make the Introduction's three choices four (add the schedule) and let each hall say which one it changes: states and history (ElementaryRules), history as depth (GameOfLife), the 2D neighbourhood and its shape (BeyondBinary), continuous state, schedule and the body (ExpandingSpace), two fields (SoftRules), procedure vs resemblance (AgentsCircuits), storage precision (EdgeOfChaos). Then "other works remain" becomes "the hexagon changes the second choice".
6. **Conway's design goals are an edge statement.** Gardner's three conditions — no pattern provably grows without limit, some apparently do, simple patterns grow and change and then end by fading, settling or oscillating — describe a rule tuned to sit on the edge of its own behaviour. BeyondBinary's MEET (a glider into a block; the field is empty by generation 6, task .002) is the hall's own instance of "fading away completely". One paragraph, and the hall named Edge of Chaos has a second referent.
7. **Blur is a cellular automaton.** NOC: image processing works with CA-like rules; a pixel's new colour is the average of its neighbours. EdgeOfChaos's fractional pull *is* that average. One sentence connects the hall to every texture in the noise and colour sequences.
8. **The hall names promise other rooms.** "ElementaryRules" holds a three-state 3D automaton, "GameOfLife" holds the Rule 30 history bridge, "BeyondBinary" holds binary Life. NOC's names are literal. The visitor reads the pearl names ("ca gameoflife") on the wall; the contents were dealt after the names. A rename or a re-deal is a sequence decision, not prose.

## Not to learn

- "Cells are like a spreadsheet" and the domino metaphor: Ada's cells are things the body meets.
- Skipping the edges to keep the code short.
- Random initial rows as the default (NOC Ex. 7.2): Ada's single seed and SEED pair are the better teaching start; the `rule110_computer` already seeds at random.
