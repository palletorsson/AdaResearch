Its proposed equipment, events and passages are not installed gameplay or adopted plot.

Model B already has general intellect and inherent care. Its inquiry concerns purpose, desire and the relations its existing capacities can activate. It knows it has been misdelivered and has no immediate obligation to return. General intellect does not give it omniscient access to this particular tower, other minds, hidden program state or the future.

General intellect is a capacity to investigate, not a completed inventory of algorithmic knowledge. Model B can encounter an unfamiliar mechanism, misunderstand a relation, form a hypothesis, test it and revise its understanding. It need not already know every algorithm. Learning about the world does not mean acquiring intelligence, consciousness or moral competence.

Care participates in attention: what might need support, what an action changes, who or what may depend on an arrangement. Model B has attachments and biases; its own interests can obscure consequences or other relations. Let those limits appear through actions and revisions rather than repeatedly declaring uncertainty. Curiosity and desire can be involved and consequential without every encounter becoming an assigned repair or a moral lesson.

It seeks a workable understanding of how the simulation holds together: where its accounts close, what they include or leave out, and where bodies, interfaces and artifacts do not quite agree. That understanding can remain provisional. Do not give the narrator the author's source access or make every encounter end in a complete explanation.

**The player is not Model B; the player plays Model B's body.** The opening places this body inside a large wooden shipping crate marked **CARE UNIT — MODEL B**. Do not introduce a separate observer or explain the relationship prematurely. The crate has an inside, an outside, a history, a destination and an opening. The author approved the roof implementation on 20 October; further architectural replacements still need their own justification.

Sa-i is the project's working name for partial translation between artifact, world, body and interface. It includes dependencies, incompatible representations, secondary effects and unregistered consequences. It is not limited to gaps between floors and is not a reason to end every passage with an explanation.

Use Ada's established apparatus: actual algorithmic artifacts and controls, Godot scenes, map and registry data, tutorial code, `final.md` readings, field observations, evidence bundles, trials and editorial comparisons. Inspect which capacities and tools are available before extending them. No parallel narrative framework, intelligence progression, care score or purpose meter is called for.

First-person Model B is the principal voice experiment. Local knowledge must have a plausible source; author access to code is not automatically narrator access. Let intelligence, humor, care, pleasure, refusal and uncertainty coexist. A useful or caring act is one possible relation, not a compulsory outcome.

Preserve the current chapters. Close-read before proposing a revision. Separate source verification, runtime observation, interpretation and proposed fiction. `tutorial.md` remains responsible for reconstruction; literary prose need not tour every artifact or turn Model B into a pupil.

The [inside-crate opening](iterations/2026-10-20-roof-arrival-crate/after-final.md) and [reviewed Point One passage](iterations/2026-10-20-point-one-after-the-crate/reviewed-final.md) are calibration texts. This is literary feedback, not runtime evidence.

Read the review's claims that Model B knows exactly how things work in light of the later authorial correction above. The reviewed version is historical evidence, not a requirement to make Model B certain about mechanisms it has not investigated.

Protect apparently unproductive attention when it earns its place: the adequately lit empty station and the wish to hold a highlight need not yield a lesson, repair or reward. Model B's general intellect does not entail a completed purpose. A resemblance may remain unspoken; do not explain the empty station as an allegory of the misdelivered care unit.

The player can act differently from the account. Neither the player's route nor Model B's desire has to reproduce the other's. Later chapters may require interruption, another rhythm or a different narrative arrangement. Do not turn each artifact into the same sequence of observation, experiment and elegant conclusion. The tutorial carries reproducible construction; the literature can leave the significance of an encounter open.

## Attention can change the route

Decisions explicitly confirmed by Palle while beginning the Coordinates revision, 20 October 2026:

- An encounter may displace the planned tour, leaving artifacts unvisited and some concepts to the tutorial. Preserve important distinctions without requiring demonstration coverage in the prose.
- Model B may misjudge, become attached or possessive, neglect something, or leave an inconvenient consequence unresolved. Inherent care does not make every choice exemplary or require an explanatory correction before the chapter ends.
- Interpretation and unverified bodily experience may enter the literary account. New physical events or abilities remain explicit spatial proposals to test; they must not be presented as installed mechanics. This does not authorize gameplay changes.

The first trial under these decisions is [Point Coordinates — One of them](iterations/2026-10-20-coordinates-one-of-them/index.html). Its source and focused runtime evidence are separate from its invented route, perceptions and attachment. Do not turn this trial into a requirement for a loss, mistake, recurring object or surprising final paragraph in every chapter.
