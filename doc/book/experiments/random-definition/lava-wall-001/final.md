# Random enough for what?

A wall of lava lamps offers a moving surface to look at, but randomness is not simply a style of movement. What matters is how a result is produced, what can be observed, what can be repeated, and what someone needs to predict.

Our virtual lamps can return to the same moment. Their seed, model, tick and sensor rule are available. Change the rule that admits an observation and the resulting numbers change, even when the lamps do not. The sensor participates in making the result. So do the choices that turn an image into a hash, a seed, and finally a row of heights.

Cloudflare's physical installation supplies camera observations to a system that mixes several sources of randomness. Its cryptographic generators serve a different purpose from the small, exposed generator in this room. The resemblance of the lamps does not make the two apparatuses equivalent. [Cloudflare's technical account](https://blog.cloudflare.com/lavarand-in-production-the-nitty-gritty-technical-details/)

For simulation, a suitable pseudorandom generator gives us variation we can reproduce and investigate. For security, unpredictability must withstand an adversary's information and resources; a properly seeded cryptographic generator can meet that purpose. Physical noise can contribute uncertainty, but its usefulness depends on measurement and processing. Neither attractive disorder nor a statistical test alone settles the question.

Whether nature is fundamentally deterministic is outside what this apparatus can establish. We can still ask practical, answerable questions: who can predict this outcome, from which observations, and with what consequence if they are wrong? “Random enough” needs a purpose and a description of what is known.

Keeping a result makes an observation available for return. It does not produce a fresh unpredictable event. In this room, the saved image is an invitation to reconstruct the conditions that made it legible—and to build a different instrument when that reconstruction exposes a better question.

This passage may initiate an experiment just as the tutorial may. If visitors read the changed hash as proof of increased randomness, revise the comparison and the text. If revealing the seed changes their predictions, preserve that observation and investigate what information mattered. These are proposals for inquiry; no visitor finding has yet been recorded for this version.
