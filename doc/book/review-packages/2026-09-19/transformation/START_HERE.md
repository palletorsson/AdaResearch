# Review principles: strengthen the encounter before cutting the text

Author's direction, 2026-09-19. Applies when interpreting the current first-pass reviews and commissioning future reviews of Ada Research.

## Scope of the review

The current reviews are a first-pass audit of the relationship between manuscript, currently promoted artifacts, source implementation and museum sequence. They are not a final editorial verdict on the text.

The recent curation pass deliberately reduced maps to a primary-artifact scaffold, with other works classified as secondary or decorative. This made the spine clearer but left an intentionally partial situation. Manuscripts can still refer to artifacts, comparisons or returns that exist in the project but are no longer prominent in the current room or review bundle. Check the actual roles and placements in each map; the scaffold is not a permanent one-artifact quota.

A perceived gap between text and artifact does not automatically justify cutting or weakening the text. First identify which layer is missing.

## Revision hierarchy

> Restore or strengthen the encounter → reconsider placement and reveal timing → revise the relation between text and artifact → only then consider cutting the text.

1. If the text is strong and an existing secondary or previously placed artifact already performs the missing work, restore, promote or reposition that artifact.
2. If the text is strong and the required encounter is conceptually important but no current artifact performs it adequately, make this an artifact-development task rather than an editorial cut.
3. If the artifact is strong but the manuscript no longer notices what it makes possible, revise the text so that the encounter can carry more of the argument.
4. Reduce or remove a passage only when its claim is no longer important to the sequence, or when neither the existing artifact ecology nor the intended future project supports it. Record the reason.

The primary-artifact filter is a curatorial instrument, not a definition of what each chapter is allowed to contain. Secondary artifacts can become primary again when they provide an essential comparison, counterexample, return, bodily consequence or missing piece of evidence.

## Match intervention to maturity

Author's clarification, 2026-09-19: sequences and halls are at different stages. Do not apply equal revision pressure, an equal number of changes, or a standard rewrite to all of them. Review length and the number of recommendations are not measures of how much work a sequence needs.

Assess the current material locally before choosing the scope of intervention. Identify what already works, which findings remain uncertain, and which verified gap materially affects the learning path or critical argument. Text, artifact capability, spatial staging and runtime verification can have different levels of maturity within the same hall.

- **Preserve:** when the encounter and writing already carry the argument, leave them intact. A review may warrant no change.
- **Targeted improvement:** when a specific mismatch interrupts an otherwise strong hall, repair that connection, control, placement or passage without reopening the whole sequence.
- **Development:** when essential comparisons or capabilities remain absent, concentrate artifact recovery, development and substantial writing work there.

These are working judgements about the present task, not permanent ratings of a sequence. Prioritise consequential gaps, dependencies and recoverable material over cosmetic uniformity. Stop a pass when its chosen gap is resolved; do not keep revising a mature hall merely to match the effort spent elsewhere.

Local access gives a broader evidence base than the frozen review bundle: current source and configuration, secondary artifacts, earlier placements, history, available captures and executable experiments. Use that access to confirm, qualify or reject recommendations and record the reason. Access alone is not verification: state what was inspected or tested, and keep headset experience and learner understanding distinct from source inspection. The author's artistic judgement remains part of the assessment.

## Returns can do new work

Recurrence is not redundancy merely because a concept or artifact family appears elsewhere. Compare what changes in scale, body, receiver, context, temporal structure or question. Identify the new work of the return before proposing consolidation or removal.

For a finding such as “the prose explains more than the artifact shows,” ask first:

> Is the prose doing unnecessary explanatory work, or has the curation pass temporarily removed the artifact that was meant to carry that work?

The purpose is to find the strongest final relation between writing, artifact, source and embodied encounter. The current reduced map is one stage in that process.

## How to record a finding

Keep received reviews and their dated source packages intact. Apply this guidance in a separate assessment or `DECISIONS.md`; it does not retrospectively change what the reviewer saw. An absent source in a bounded review bundle means “not inspected,” not “does not exist.”

For each actionable finding, record:

- **Passage and purpose:** map, source path, short quotation, and the technical, poetic or critical work worth preserving.
- **Evidence and limits:** package date/version; distinguish TEXT, SOURCE, CAPTURE, HYPOTHESIS and RUNTIME TEST. State what was not inspected.
- **Missing layer:** manuscript, artifact capability, placement/reveal timing, implementation, sequence relation, or incomplete review evidence. Several layers can be involved.
- **Recovery candidates:** relevant current secondary/decorative artifacts, previous placements and source/history references. If no adequate candidate is found, state the search scope and the proposed development task.
- **Decision and next action:** restore/promote, develop/repair, reposition/reveal, revise text, cut with reasons, or investigate further. Explain why this action addresses the gap.
- **Intervention scope:** what already works, why this hall needs preservation, a targeted improvement or further development, and what would make this pass complete. Do not expand the scope merely to satisfy every review suggestion.
- **Verification:** the learner action and observable consequence to check; distinguish source inspection from a runtime or headset test still required.

For recurrence findings, also state what changes between the encounters and what the reader brings from the earlier one. For a proposed cut, explain why recovery, development or changed staging would not serve an important claim.

Future work must remain visibly proposed. Preserving a strong passage does not establish that its described control or bodily consequence works today. Record any current mismatch and the task needed to support it; keep factual corrections distinct from decisions to remove an argument.

## Place in the spine

primitives → **transformation** → array_tutorial

Editorial intention (a question to test against the material): Translate, rotate and scale the familiar body; discover what remains the same and how a changed relation opens or closes a passage.

Arriving from: From an unseen point to lines, traces, grids and faces: what must be encoded before a body can appear, move or be recognised?

Preparing for: Give copies addresses, extend a row into a plane and a flyable volume, then encounter monochrome repetition across floors, walls and time.

Declared objectives (source claims, not proof of completion):
- Distinguish collection from the translation, rotation and scale of an object.
- Translate along x, y, z and combined directions without changing orientation.
- Distinguish a destination from constraints on the journey to it.
- Compare rotation axis, pivot and angle through crossings, arrays and repeated profiles.
- Use uniform scale while holding the player and a reference doorway constant.
- Distinguish reversible transformation from removal of geometry.
- Compare body-driven openings with timed obstacles.
- Name invariants precisely: translation and rotation preserve distances; uniform scale preserves angles and proportions, not lengths; non-uniform scale can change shape.

## Inspecting artifacts and space

Use `SOURCE_GUIDE.md` for the complete file-reading protocol. `space/<Hall>.md` connects A-numbered placements to registry entries and scenes; its SVG shows the authored plan. `space.json` preserves the grid, settings, utilities and instance configurations. `artifact-index.json` contains registry candidates, including their descriptions and dated measurements.
