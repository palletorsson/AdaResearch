Concept: Haraway's situated knowledge as a design constraint on computation — objectivity as the careful accounting of which perspective a system occupies rather than as the absence of perspective. Three viewing platforms render the same dataset differently, and the differences are the argument.
Sequence role: Fourth map in the Post-Foundations Crisis sequence. Follows SpeculativeComputation_Paraconsistent_Engineering, which turned contradiction into load-bearing infrastructure; leads to SpeculativeComputation_Collective_Knowledge, which turns standpoint into a shared condition rather than a solitary one.
Technical angle: Three viewing platforms — ground-level, aerial, and a slanted local-community vantage — render a shared dataset differently even though the underlying coordinates, heights, and distances are constant. An annotation bench lets the learner mark what each view shows and what each view hides, and the annotations combine into a running account that reads as partial and located rather than universal. A second station trains a small classifier from a chosen standpoint and makes the standpoint visible in the output labels.
Critical angle: The map replaces the view from nowhere with an explicit account of where the view is coming from. Situated knowledge, locatable knowledge, accountable knowledge — Haraway's vocabulary is treated as a set of design obligations rather than as a political slogan, and the map asks what it takes to build systems that satisfy those obligations.
Key artifacts: the three viewing platforms carry the standpoint mechanism; the annotation bench carries the accounting practice; the classifier station makes the chosen standpoint legible in the model's outputs.
