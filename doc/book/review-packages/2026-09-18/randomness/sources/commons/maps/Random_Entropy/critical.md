# The neighbours the number leaves behind

Choose two tiles that look good together. Perhaps they clash. Press SORT and watch them leave each other. The number above the desk does not move. It has performed its calculation correctly, and something you chose has disappeared from the arrangement it measures.

The enlarged first-forty strip keeps the earlier neighbours available beside the sorted row. This second record matters. The visitor can compare two uses of the same sample without having to hold its whole history in memory. Sorting helps us find every occurrence of a colour. The original order preserves who came before and after whom. Neither use is represented in the entropy reading.

The exclusion happens at a definite line: `counts[s] += 1`. The accumulator knows the symbol and increments its tally. It does not retain that symbol's position or ask which symbol preceded it. A later loop compresses those ten counts to one scalar. Two losses occur: positions disappear into counts; then the count vector disappears into a number from which it cannot generally be reconstructed. The code gives each omission a place we can inspect.

This does not make the measurement useless. It gives it a contract. The sample shares determine the average information value reported here. Changing those shares changes the reading. Moving the symbols while keeping their counts does not. If our question concerns neighbours, rhythm, a message or a route through the row, we need another observation. Asking the number to answer that question would enlarge its authority without enlarging what it knows.

There is queer potential in this precise mismatch. An arrangement can become more useful to one body and less useful to another while the gauge declares equality. We need not appoint disorder as liberation and sorted blocks as its opposite. A block of one colour may make a task accessible. An unexpected neighbour may make a relation desirable. The number cannot decide which of these possibilities matters to the visitor who encounters it.

The common substrate is still here. Original and sorted are permutations of the same instances. No kind of tile has to become a different material before another arrangement becomes possible. Yet the instrument offers only these two layouts. It does not let the visitor regroup by a chosen pair, turn a colour into a sound, or build a passage from the tiles. Those are possible next experiments, not capabilities claimed for this desk. To investigate bodies in space, we would have to implement and test how those arrangements receive a body.

The display adds its own decisions. CONTRAST makes four counts zero, but four small bars remain. A minimum height keeps their marks visible. Meanwhile the largest count in each sample fills the same ten centimetres of the panel. The scale shifts under comparison. These are practical accommodations to a small board, with consequences for what can be inferred by looking. The count ledger lets the visitor notice them instead of treating a visible mark as proof of an occurrence.

DISCLOSE changes how much of the measurement appears on the upper panel. At oracle it shows the number without the bars. The desk's records remain. Authority here depends partly on which surface we approach and how long we stay. A visitor can accept the glowing number, or step closer and follow the counts. The room makes that difference available as an action.

For the later QFEP inquiry, this is groundwork: name the observable, its distribution and the operation that reduces it before asking an entropy term to describe a world. This marginal histogram entropy is not a measured amount of freedom, terrain difficulty or ontological possibility. The relation to those questions has to be constructed through further experiments. A simulation can help us think about theory by making the limits of that construction available to experience.

We leave with one pair still remembered. The next hall will remove cells at random. Before the draw, a rule decides which cells may disappear. Once again the algorithm begins after a choice has already been made.
