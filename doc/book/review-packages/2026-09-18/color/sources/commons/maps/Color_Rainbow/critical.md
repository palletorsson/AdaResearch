# The party does not wait for the right answer

A platform game often makes a burst of colour acknowledge something you have earned. Here the welcome patch celebrates arrival, and a button lets the visitor repeat it. The programmed event is still constrained: ninety-six pieces of confetti and a bounded lifetime. Its social invitation has changed without requiring an unbounded simulation.

The arch offers another small displacement. It remains recognisable while the control changes its division into colours. Six, seven, forty-eight, two: each configuration comes from an authored list or rule. The button exposes a choice that the finished rainbow image could otherwise make look inevitable.

This does not put every possible rainbow within reach. Four choices are available. The mode called continuous has forty-eight strips. The code's saturated hue sweep does not include the whites, dark colours or pale pinks already present in the banners. Those differences are not impossible for RGB; they are outside this particular generating rule. The next modification would have somewhere precise to begin.

The mushrooms borrow the bodily vocabulary of platform games and wear pearl collars. They do not become enemies. The room can use familiar forms to invite proximity and pleasure without making difference a threat. This is a staging decision, not a claim that friendliness follows automatically from their geometry.

There is also a material limit. An opaque rainbow can look substantial while offering no collision surface. The coloured steps can carry the body because collision was built for them. Appearance alone does not establish the bodily possibility. The level route keeps that distinction available without demanding a jump.

For Ada, the experiment is methodological: hold a recognisable structure, change one rule, and see what another arrangement permits us to notice or want. The celebration can accompany that inquiry before there is a correct answer. The pillar field continues with another question: where does a rule have a colour that the room has not yet given a body?
