# Florensky_Paraconsistent — Summary

The seventh map in the foundationscrisis sequence. A 13x12 grid, max height 3, structured as two overlapping diamond rooms sharing a central zone. The left diamond is warm, ordered, lower — representing the F-term (order). The right diamond is cold, entropic, higher — representing the entropy term. At the center, both diamonds are present: the player stands in both rooms simultaneously. The height peaks at 3 in the overlap. The architecture performs paraconsistent logic spatially: contradiction as coexistence, not collapse.

Three artifacts. `florensky_sphere` at the center of the overlap oscillates between assertion (blue) and negation (red), resting naturally in superposition (purple, both). Interaction collapses it to one state; release returns it to both. The sphere's natural condition is contradiction held without resolution. `schrodinger_box` in the left wing demonstrates quantum superposition: open the lid and the contents resolve to alive or dead; close it and superposition returns. The auto-reset argues that definite states are temporary and superposition is the norm. `superposition_display` in the right wing visualizes continuous superposition — two basis states oscillating in antiphase, never collapsing.

Pavel Florensky — mathematician, priest, executed by Stalin — proposed that truth contains its own negation. Paraconsistent logic formalizes this: contradictions are contained, not catastrophic. The explosion principle (from contradiction, anything follows) is rejected. A system can hold A and not-A without becoming trivial. This is the third response to the foundations crisis: where Russell restricted expressiveness and Brouwer restricted logic, Florensky refuses to restrict anything and instead refuses to let contradiction propagate.
