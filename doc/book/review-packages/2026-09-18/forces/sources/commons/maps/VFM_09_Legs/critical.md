# Permission to stand, permission to move

The table's footprint makes a permission spatial: under these initial conditions, which mass locations can upward contact forces sustain? That question belongs to an arrangement of bodies, surfaces and forces. Counting limbs alone cannot answer it. Finite feet, supported area, load position and possible motion all matter.

The four-leg walker belongs to another arrangement. A controller advances its mesh while inverse kinematics solves toward chosen targets. The image of support is convincing without body support being simulated. This is a specific opening for artistic research: put the two implementations beside one another so their difference becomes inspectable.

SHIFT preserves the plate's shape while changing its custom mass centre. It also preserves an assigned inertia tensor. This body has properties granted independently in code; the apparent material does not determine all of them. The X-ray marker and explicit rule let that independence become an experiment rather than a hidden shortcut.

The static outline does not certify a way of walking. Moving, catching, leaning, gripping and using another contact are possible extensions, each with their own conditions. The retained one-to-eight row can generate such questions without presenting existing anatomical categories as mathematical necessities.

Next, the Arena can ask which relations an agent has to the player: direct movement, collision, force, sensing, response. Naming those implemented relations gives us places to change them.
