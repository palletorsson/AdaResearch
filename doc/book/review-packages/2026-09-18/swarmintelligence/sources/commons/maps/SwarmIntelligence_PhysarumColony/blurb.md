Slime mold builds networks. It spreads, explores, finds food, then optimizes — reinforcing efficient paths, pruning dead ends. No brain, no neurons, yet it solves mazes, recreates rail networks, computes. Intelligence without centralization. The blob that thinks by growing.

The `PhysarumColony` simulates the organism agent-by-agent: each virtual filament deposits a chemical trail, senses trail concentration ahead, turns toward higher concentration, and the network self-organizes. Trail diffuses; low-flow paths decay; high-flow paths reinforce. The emergent topology approximates Steiner trees — minimum-cost networks connecting multiple points. Physarum polycephalum collapses the distinction between computation and material process. When slime mold solves a maze, there is no algorithm running on a substrate. The substrate IS the algorithm. Physarum's network optimization preceded human network theory by hundreds of millions of years.
