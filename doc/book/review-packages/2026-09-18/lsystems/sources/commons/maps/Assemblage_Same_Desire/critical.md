# The path the procedure did not seek

The textile makes a difference between producing a mark and proposing a route. Its threshold chooses one of two outcomes without inspecting neighbouring cells. Yet the geometry of adjacent marks can invite following, grouping or enclosure. A reader does not invent those constraints from nothing; the generator does not need to anticipate everything they make possible.

Holding the random record lets us press that relation. Changing the alphabet preserves each decision but changes the relations we perceive. Changing the threshold preserves the samples but alters decisions. Changing the seed changes the sampled record. These are separate opportunities to intervene in what might otherwise appear as one opaque act of generation.

The floor application adds another distinction. A visible line may organize attention without obstructing a capsule. The current marks decorate a continuous supporting surface. Were we to turn them into walls, accessibility would depend on dimensions, collision shapes, connections and the body attempting the passage. Pattern, graph and traversable world require different descriptions, even when they begin with the same array.

This is useful to the book's question about possible bodies. The shared substrate allows an outcome to become a line, a block or a surface mark. It does not make every change equivalent, and it does not automatically supply a new way to move. The desired body can exceed what this particular interpretation has been written to do. That limit gives us a concrete place to change the program.

The rest of Assemblage should retain its differences. A weaving draft coordinates related structures; the two-mark generator makes local selections. The pattern galleries name makers and traditions; their shader interpretations do not exhaust those practices or settle questions of attribution and return. The origin gallery situates the project in a particular history rather than treating every surface as ownerless raw material.

Likewise, a self-referential plaque and an intentionally tilted scale are invitations to inquiry. Their theatrical form is not a proof of Gödel's theorem, and chance in a textile is not a demonstration of undecidability. We can compare limits without collapsing distinct kinds of limit into a single metaphor. The title The Same Desire can remain a methodological invitation to move between practices, with enough specificity to discover where the comparison stops working.

The generator does not select the most beautiful route, the most useful cloth or the most desirable body. The next hall introduces a score that does select. We should carry the possibility that a result matters in ways the score has not been written to recognize.
