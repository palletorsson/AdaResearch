# What changed, and what remained possible?

The sideways tree invites a desire to find another form. The experiment lets that desire encounter its operation: the direction components exchange places. Each new position has an exact correspondence with the earlier one. Recognition may falter before the relation does.

That is valuable without making the reflection a proof of new topology. Distances, angles and the abstract branch connections survive. A fixed floor or an inhabiting body could make the reflected arrangement have different consequences, but this drawing has no branch colliders. The next step would be to implement and test that relation. The book's question about possible bodies becomes more specific when we can distinguish a changed appearance from a changed condition of support or access.

The operation also presses a linguistic expectation. F is familiar as forward, while the blue reader takes it across. Yet the program is still following its assignment. A change of interpretation need not be a failure to follow rules. The reader's commitment is in executable code, and the visitor has a limited, visible way to revise it. This does not attribute belief or consciousness to the turtle.

The older essay asked how technical practices mediate between signs and forms, and whether one formalism exhausts the possible. Those remain useful questions. Three mathematical curve families are not evidence of three cosmotechnics by themselves, and the geometry of a finite drawing cannot establish a claim about cultural difference. A fuller discussion of Simondon or Hui needs its own reading and argument. The manuscript is preserved in the archive; this pass develops the implemented comparison.

The finite curve gallery supplies another limit. A fourth-generation Hilbert panel visits 256 sampled grid vertices. More detailed stages may pack the display densely, while its texture remains a bounded raster. The parameter interval, the resulting image, the stored points and the occupied pixels are different objects to describe. Calling the construction space-filling must not turn a finite set of addresses into a claim that it has visited every point a body could encounter.

The new desk permits a few transformations of meaning and preserves a reference. It does not yet offer visitor-authored rules or arbitrary interpreters. Those are opportunities for a later pass. A useful extension would let F place a wall, leave a sound or register a visit, while retaining the same sentence and reporting the added consequences. Increased variety alone would not tell us which extension provides a useful difference for this body.

The first pass therefore keeps this question open through a dependable comparison. We can make an unfamiliar reading, locate what it preserves and undo it. Architecture will add a further demand: a drawn branch must meet the needs of a person trying to move, pause or return.
