# Point One - Critical Reflection

## That Which Has No Part

Euclid's definition: "A point is that which has no part."

This is not just a mathematical statement - it is a declaration of **atomicity**, of fundamental indivisibility. The point cannot be broken down further. It is the **minimal unit**.

But what does it mean to have "no part"?

The point:
- Cannot be split
- Cannot be shared
- Cannot be distributed
- Cannot contain multiplicity

The point is **singular, discrete, and absolute**. It is the first assertion of the **one** against the continuous.

## Discretization as Violence

The transition from Point_Zero (infrastructure) to Point_One (individual unit) is the moment where **continuous space becomes discrete**.

Before the point, there was only the field - the origin as stable reference, but no marked positions. With Point_One, space becomes **countable**:
- This point (1)
- Not that point (0)
- One, not zero
- Present, not absent

This is the introduction of the **binary** into geometry. The point makes space **digital** - either there is a point here, or there is not.

What this excludes:
- **Gradual transitions** (no "almost a point")
- **Partial presence** (no "half a point")
- **Ambiguity** (no "maybe a point")
- **Continuity** (gaps appear between discrete points)

## The Body Creates Position

In Point_One, you **grab** the point. Your hand moves through space, and the point follows.

This is crucial: **The body instantiates the point**.

The point's position is not given by mathematics - it is produced by your hand's movement through VR space. The controller's tracking system writes new (x, y, z) coordinates 90 times per second.

The point becomes a **record of embodied action**. Where you placed it, that's where it is.

But here's the tension: The point has no memory. It doesn't remember where it **was**, only where it **is**. The duration of your movement - the gesture, the arc of your arm, the hesitation - all of this is erased.

Only the final position remains.

## What the Point Cannot Hold

The point is defined by what it **excludes**:

- **Duration** - It exists only in the present moment
- **Trajectory** - It has no memory of how it arrived at its position
- **Thickness** - It has no volume, no materiality
- **Texture** - It has no surface quality
- **Temperature** - It is pure abstraction, untouchable
- **Multiplicity** - It is singular, never plural

The point is the most **compressed** representation of position. Everything about embodied location except the coordinates themselves is discarded.

## Euclid and Colonial Geometry

When Euclid wrote "that which has no part," he was establishing the **axioms** that would govern Western geometry for millennia.

But axioms are not neutral. They are **choices** about what counts as foundational truth.

The Euclidean point privileges:
- Discreteness over continuity
- Singularity over multiplicity
- Abstraction over materiality
- Measurement over experience

These are the same logics that enable:
- Property boundaries (this land, not that land)
- Colonial mapping (dividing continuous territories into discrete parcels)
- Surveillance (tracking bodies as point locations)
- Logistics (reducing movement to origin/destination pairs)

The point is not innocent. It is a **tool of organization** that makes space calculable, divisible, and ownable.

## Queer Points

What would a queer point look like?

Perhaps:
- A point that **remembers** its trajectory
- A point that exists in **multiple positions simultaneously**
- A point that **drifts** over time
- A point that has **thickness**, occupying uncertain space
- A point that can be **partially present**

The point could refuse singularity. It could be **becoming** rather than **being**.

## The Isolated Cube

The map's architecture includes an isolated cube at (4,2) - spatially separated from the continuous platform.

This cube **is** the point made architectural. It demonstrates singularity through physical isolation. One cube, apart from the field.

The point is always already **separated** - defined by its distinction from what it is not.

## Conclusion: Individuation

Point_One teaches **individuation** - how the continuous becomes discrete, how the field produces units, how bodies create marked positions.

But we must ask: **Who benefits from discretization?**

Systems of power require:
- Countable subjects (population management)
- Trackable positions (surveillance)
- Discrete properties (ownership)
- Singular identities (governable bodies)

The point is not just a geometric primitive. It is the **atom of administrative logic** - the minimal unit that can be counted, located, and controlled.

When you grab the point and place it in space, you are performing the act of **making discrete**. You are converting continuous VR space into a marked, nameable, retrievable position.

This is both necessary (we need to build with discrete elements) and troubling (discretization enables capture).

The question is not whether to use points, but whether we can remain **aware** that the point is a **political technology** disguised as mathematical truth.
