# From enclosure to passage

Follows Point_Triangle_Context, where closing a loop, emitting faces and displaying them became separable operations. Here faces meet at a corner, enclose a region, then support or interrupt a body's movement. Point_Animatedcube follows by exposing the construction of a familiar closed mesh.

The first passage asks: **what must be constructed before a boundary encloses space, and what must be added before it directs a body?**

Primary encounters, in book order:

1. `grab_trihedron`: inspect three faces and the missing base; distinguish its visible opening from its convex collision hull.
2. `grab_tetrahedron`: compare with a regular four-face enclosure. This is a comparison between held objects, not an installed assembly puzzle.
3. `prism_block`: compare the solid, quartered and shell versions, whose collision surface is retained. Existing `wp` utilities carry the question into ascent.
4. `concrete_barrier`: follow the striped enclosure, inspect its extruded profile and distinguish material from collision.
5. `street_sign`: compare the STOP instruction and four arrow configurations with the physical routes.

The five former additional primaries remain placed and become return visits: `pyramid_edit`, `cube_scene`, `diamonds`, `rock_spawner`, `rock_scanner`. They keep separate questions about editable corners, counting, repetition, packing and sections. The tutorial carries these encounters; the technical reference retains the deeper mathematics. No artifact, utility or floor cell is removed or moved.

Critical opening: a visually open form may retain a closed collision model; a sign can influence movement without imposing collision. A useful barrier need not be oppressive and an open route need not be beneficial. Ask whose action a proposed change would allow. The map does not currently let visitors rearrange the fixed barriers or signs.

The current inventory has 34 placements and four utility wedges. The whole-room walk and headset reach remain review tasks. Existing component checks cover barrier stamping and blocking, a museum wedge ascent with a supplied capsule, and the sign's lack of collision. They do not establish that a tracked hand is stopped by the trihedron or that all floor joins are traversable. See the dated iteration record for current evidence.
