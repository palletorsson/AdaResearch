## What this book is trying to do

Ada Research is a VR museum and a book made together. Its reader is a curious beginner who wants to learn how code makes a world and how that world permits, positions and confines a body. The recurring research question is **what bodies and forms of existence become possible with the capabilities we have learned, and what remains excluded?**

A hall should offer an encounter with one or a few relevant primary artifacts. The reader asks, tries, notices, then gets enough implementation to understand what happened. That understanding opens another experiment or a critical question. Existing secondary work should be folded in when it contributes; one primary was a starting scaffold, never a permanent quota.

The voice should be explicitly poetic and technically specific. It oscillates between discovery, anomaly and explanation. Code can carry the rhythm of the prose. Do not flatten the author's queer associations into neutral textbook language, or replace the encounter with a theoretical slogan. A straight line, a crooked walk and its sampled trace can sustain a question without being made equivalent.

We are still completing and aligning a first pass across the spine. Several early chapters have had repeated editorial and runtime revisions; later chapters may have only a scaffold. Review the actual passage, and distinguish incomplete development from a failure of the overall method.

## One line per chapter

These are editorial questions for review, not claims that every current hall already fulfils them.

1. **primitives** — From an unseen point to lines, traces, grids and faces: what must be encoded before a body can appear, move or be recognised?
2. **transformation** — Translate, rotate and scale the familiar body; discover what remains the same and how a changed relation opens or closes a passage.
3. **array_tutorial** — Give copies addresses, extend a row into a plane and a flyable volume, then encounter monochrome repetition across floors, walls and time.
4. **color** — Add colour to forms already learned, then investigate its scientific conditions and associative force through light, surfaces, neighbours and perception.
5. **tiling** — Return to the familiar floor with independent colour control, then author patterns that dress architecture and bodies, leading towards shaders and their limits.
6. **change** — Separate a changing value from accumulated history; ask how timing, repetition and a mismatch of phases make different events possible.
7. **forces** — Move from vectors to interactions that carry bodies; distinguish a prescribed motion from motion produced by forces and constraints.
8. **formfinding** — Follow a body's descent or relaxation and discover how an objective, constraints and stopping rule decide what counts as an arrived-at form.
9. **wavefunctions** — Let periodic rules organise movement, sound and terrain; feel how phase and superposition can alter the space a body encounters.
10. **randomness** — Ask how a draw, a distribution and a sampling rule produce different kinds of uncertainty, opportunity and difficult ground.
11. **noise** — Discover correlated variation's power to make organic-looking forms, then question the easy answer of adding noise and the possibilities it can obscure.
12. **cellularautomata** — Let neighbours and local updates grow histories and spaces; test what kinds of global form can emerge from a limited rule.
13. **fractals** — Repeat a construction across scales and experience the holes, recognitions and computational costs made by finite recursion.
14. **lsystems** — Turn rewriting rules into paths, branches and architecture; ask what the grammar can generate and what its alphabet cannot yet express.
15. **proceduralgeneration** — Compose the learned capabilities into generated environments, making their choices about growth, connectivity and support inspectable.
16. **softbodies** — Let deformation and constraints participate in making form; investigate which movements a material model admits, resists or cannot retain.
17. **isosurfaces** — Find a boundary within a sampled field and examine what the chosen level, resolution and reconstruction make visible or lose.
18. **boolean_surfaces** — Combine and subtract solids so that cuts, cavities and intersections become a vocabulary of possible passage.
19. **swarmintelligence** — Explore how many locally acting bodies organise one another and their surroundings without assuming that collective order needs a leader.
20. **machinelearning** — Return to landscapes as spaces of learning: examine how representation, evidence and an objective shape the possibilities an optimiser can find.
21. **graphtheory** — Make relations explicit as nodes and edges, then test how the chosen network enables routes while leaving other relations unrepresented.
22. **foundationscrisis** — Put particular formal systems under pressure and distinguish what each can establish from the limits, contradictions or alternatives encountered.
23. **qfeplaboratory** — Investigate the project's relations between order, entropy and change through concrete models, treating QFEP as a research framework rather than a settled universal law.
24. **postfoundationscrisis** — Return from those limits to making: what systems, bodies and shared worlds can we build when their exclusions remain open to revision?

## How to review

- Follow the pedagogical dependency: what can a beginner already do, what is added here, and what does the next hall need?
- Locate each criticism in a map, source path and short quotation; distinguish missing explanation, misleading claim, repetition, weak transition and an unsupported critical leap.
- Read the primary-artifact passages as encounters. Ask whether the prose names an available action and an observable consequence before interpreting it.
- Preserve useful ambiguity in poetry while checking technical assertions precisely. Geometry, topology, information entropy, unpredictability and political freedom are related research questions, not interchangeable quantities.
- Treat theory and citations as situated connections. Flag historical or scientific claims needing a source; do not invent authority for the author's interpretation.
- You may inspect implementation in the online repository. Mark findings as **text evidence**, **source-code evidence**, **interpretation**, or **runtime question**. Source inspection cannot establish comfortable reach, visibility, traversability, frame rate or the felt experience of VR.
- Avoid inventing controls, outcomes, crashes, dangers or bodily sensations. A proposed artifact improvement belongs in a separate suggestion, not inside a replacement passage describing the current room.
- Keep developed passages where they work. Prefer a small, located revision over giving every hall the same question/exercise/critique template.

## Return format

First give one line per chapter: **what carries it / its main gap / the next useful revision**. Then select at most five high-value revisions, each with the original passage, a proposed replacement and a short reason. Separate **edit now**, **inspect implementation**, and **test in VR**. Finish with questions for the author only where a decision cannot be inferred from the material.

Do not judge novelty or the success of the whole research project solely from unfinished prose. Do assess whether the claims, encounters and transitions earn the reader's attention.
