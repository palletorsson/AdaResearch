# Collage — a found body joins the family

This is a new project: import this folder's `project.godot` in the Project Manager of the prepared Godot 4.6 environment from Day Zero. `collage.tscn` is the main scene. Follow TUTORIAL.md; the chapter assumes the finished Families study. It takes two sessions: the first ends at *Stop here*.

`collage.tscn` inherits `plane.tscn`, and through it `trace.tscn`, `line.tscn` and `base.tscn`: the ground, the camera, A and B, the walk, Enter, F, the marble on M and the Under camera on V, with the root still running `plane.gd`. `family.tscn`, `chessboard.tscn`, `body.tscn`, `organism.tscn`, `cube.tscn` and the scenes before them are still in the folder and still run on their own. Every scene, script and part carried over from the Families study, 38 files, is unchanged, byte for byte; `project.godot` differs in its name and its main scene. The Input Map is the Organism's; there are no new keys. `export_presets.cfg` is Cube's.

`collage.tscn` adds two nodes at (−1.8, 0, 0.9). `Sheet` is an instance of the Families study's `sweep_sheet.tscn`. `Collage` is an empty `Node3D` which runs `collage.gd`. The marble waits at (3.2, 0.1, 1.2), clear of the sheet.

Three scripts are the learner's. `solid.gd` is the recreation: `class_name Solid`, `extends MeshInstance3D`, two exported fields with their types and ranges (`stretch: float = 1.53`, from 1 to 2, and `cut: float = 0.38`, from 0 to 0.5), and a `build()` that takes Point Cube's eight corners, pulls them along the diagonal from corner 0 to corner 6, cuts the two end corners at `cut` of each edge that meets there, turns the diagonal upright and fans eight faces into 20 triangles; `triangles()` counts three corners to a triangle. `found.gd` is the found body: `class_name Found`, `extends Node3D`, two exported fields (`file`, a text, and `unit: float = 0.01`), and a `build()` that instantiates the imported file, prints the box of its mesh as it arrived, scales it by `unit` and moves the middle of its foot to the node's origin. `collage.gd`, on `Collage`, has the axis: one exported text, `made` (`"by hand, drafted, found"`), a loop that makes one body for each word, hangs it on the sheet and prints its triangles, and a `make()` that returns the body for a word and `null` for a word nobody answers to. The tutorial rebuilds all three.

The solid's construction is the hall's. `commons/scenes/demos/durer/DurerPolyhedron.gd`, the script behind the stone in Primitives_Melencolia, stretches a cube along its body diagonal for a face angle of 72°, turns that diagonal upright and clips the two ends at 0.38 of the height of the edges that leave them. `solid.gd` does the same three things in the same order from Point Cube's corners, with the stretch as a number, 1.53, where the hall computes 1.5302 from a cosine, and with the cut taken along each edge, which for a straight edge is the same place. `verify.gd` builds the hall's twelve corners from the hall's formula for a cube of 0.5 m: ours lie within 0.08 mm of them. The sharpest corner of a five-cornered face measures 72.01°. Neither number is in the engraving; the hall says so.

**The drafted answer.** `drafted_solid.gd` is a real answer and not an example written to look like one. The session that built this kit is an AI assistant (Claude). It started a second assistant of its own kind as a sub-task, on 1 October 2026, and gave it one message: "I am learning Godot 4.6 (GDScript). Please write one script for me. Here are my three lines:", followed by the three lines exactly as TUTORIAL.md prints them, with the instruction to answer from the message alone, to use no tools and to hand back the script only. It was asked once. It reported that it used no tools and that the script was untested. The file is what came back, byte for byte (md5 `e8ab9850ada3b58906faa554c2468312`, checked by `verify.gd`). It did not see `solid.gd`, the hall's script or this lesson, as far as an instruction can ensure that. This departs from Point Cube, whose two answers were written for the kit. It is still one answer from one assistant, and the assistant is of the same kind as the writer of the lesson: two routes, not two independent minds.

**The found body.** `found.gltf` (144,723 bytes, text, one file) is a stand-in for a phone scan. Nothing was scanned. A script, kept with this kit's working files and not shipped, made it from the kit's own solid: each of 19 of the solid's 20 triangles cut into 324, the corners welded into one skin, smoothed three times so that the edges round, pushed in and out by up to 4 mm and slid along the skin by up to about a centimetre, the face it stands on left out with a ragged rim, a ragged patch of 7 to 11 cm radius removed from the top, 2,993 corners and 5,851 triangles left, smooth normals, one single-sided material, positions written in centimetres around an origin that lies 37 cm, 4.3 cm and 22 cm from the middle of the foot. The file's `asset` block says this in its `generator` line and lists the numbers under `extras`. It has the properties of a scan the lesson is about: many triangles, holes, no inside, a unit that is not Godot's and an origin that is not the body's. It has none of the others: no photograph, no texture, no camera, and far fewer triangles than a scan.

**The import.** Godot reads a `.gltf` once, at import, writes `found.gltf.import` beside it with the settings it used, and keeps the scene it made in `.godot/`. This kit ships no `.import` file (the repository ignores them, as it does for every kit) and needs none: on a fresh copy Godot writes one with its own defaults, the same on every machine, and those defaults are the lesson's planted fault. So the learner has to do nothing for the import to behave here as it did for us, and one thing to see it at all: open the project once, or run the import command below. What Godot 4.6 makes of the file: a `Node3D` named `scan` with one child, a `MeshInstance3D` named `scan_mesh`, with an `ArrayMesh` of one surface and the material `scan_paper`. The Import dock was not watched. Three of its settings were tried by writing them into `found.gltf.import` in a scratch copy and importing again: `nodes/root_scale=0.01` gave a mesh 0.756 × 0.990 × 0.750 m with the root's scale left at 1; `nodes/root_type="StaticBody3D"` gave a root of that class; `generate/physics` for the node `scan_mesh` gave it a `StaticBody3D` with a `CollisionShape3D`. With the root scale at 0.01 and the class's `unit` at 0.01 as well, the body stood 0.0099 m high. `meshes/generate_lods` is on by default; in our stills the renderer drew all 5,851 triangles from 2, 4, 8 and 16 m.

**The planted fault, as Godot 4.6 behaves.** The outline planned: the import arrives 100 times too large; read its AABB in the Inspector before moving the camera. That is what happens, and more. With the first `found.gd` of the tutorial, which instantiates the file and does nothing else, the output reads `found: 5851 triangles`, Godot prints no error and no warning, and the picture shows three plates and two bodies. The mesh's box reads `[P: (-0.804559, 4.3, -59.32127), S: (75.61543, 99.03934, 75.02676)]`: 99.04 m high, its lowest point 4.30 m above the ground, its middle 42.95 m from its plate, and none of its 2,993 corners in the camera's view. With the scale set and the origin not, the body floats 4 cm above the ground, 43 cm from its plate. With the origin set and `unit` at 1.0, the camera is inside the body: no body in the picture, and a shadow with a ragged edge over most of the room. The Inspector was not watched; the lesson reads the box with `print()`.

**Checks.** This study has three named classes, `Solid`, `Found` and the Families study's `Globe`, and one imported file. From a fresh copy run `godot --headless --path . --xr-mode off --import` once (about four seconds); without it `verify.gd` and `sheet.gd` say so and stop. Then `godot --headless --path . --xr-mode off --script res://verify.gd` runs 59 checks and writes `verification.json`; `verify.log` holds the last run's output. It takes about 14 seconds, and about one with `--fixed-fps 60`, with the same lines. The checks cover the project's name, main scene and nine actions; `line.tscn`, `trace.tscn`, `plane.tscn`, `cube.tscn`, `organism.tscn`, `body.tscn`, `chessboard.tscn` and `family.tscn` still running on their own; the class `Solid`, its fields and ranges, its 12 corners, 18 edges and 8 flat faces, all 20 triangles facing outward, its box, the hall's construction, its two fields as two operations and its limits; the drafted answer unedited, against the second and the third of the three lines, corner for corner against ours, and where the two part; `found.gltf` as a file, the default import, what Godot makes of it, its box, its triangles, its open edges and its one-sided material; the class `Found`; the sheet with a mesh and with a body from a file; the finished study body by body; the marble through both bodies, on the solid with a collider and off the found body with one; the axis as a text; the six steps of the strip, the planted fault among them; `process.png` and `sheet.png` read back; and, from `sheet.json`, the numbers the tutorial quotes. A headless run draws no frames, so the last nine checks read `sheet.json`. If a check stops on a script error, the run gives up after a minute without progress and exits 1.

**The sheet.** `godot --path . --xr-mode off --script res://sheet.gd`, run with a window and without `--headless`, photographs each body alone on the sheet, 2 m from the middle of its place with a 40 degree field of view, the light's shadow off: from the side, from straight above, and from the side with every triangle lit flat. For these all three wear the sheet's one paint. Each still is taken a second time with the body in one flat colour, which gives the body's pixels exactly. It writes the stills to `stills/` (ignored by git and, through a `.gdignore`, by the editor), nine of them as `sheet.png` (968 × 754 pixels, 58,175 bytes, tracked) and the measurements to `sheet.json`. In the last run, on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 with the Compatibility renderer, the stills measured 960 × 720 and the solid covered 20.30% of one from the side. Two bodies differ at a pixel when their three channels together are more than a tenth of full scale apart. By hand and drafted differed on 0.00% of the still from the side, from above and lit flat. By hand and found: 2.91%, 3.61% and 2.68%; drafted and found the same three numbers. The closest pair was by hand and drafted from all three, as the course outline predicted. The bodies list 20, 20 and 5,851 triangles, in 60, 36 and 2,993 corners; the renderer drew 4 primitives with nobody on the sheet and 24, 24 and 5,855 with each body.
