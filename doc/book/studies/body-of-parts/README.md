# A Body of Parts — the first assemblage

Open this project's `project.godot` in the prepared Godot 4.6 environment from Day Zero. `body.tscn` is the main scene. Follow TUTORIAL.md; the chapter assumes the finished Organism study. It takes two sessions: the first ends at *Stop here*, which names the script as it should stand at that point.

`body.tscn` inherits `plane.tscn`, and through it `trace.tscn`, `line.tscn` and `base.tscn`: the floor, the camera, A and B, the walk, Enter, F, the marble on M and the Under camera on V, with the root still running `plane.gd`. It does not inherit `organism.tscn`. This is the first study whose new scene is not the last one grown longer: the cube and the limb come in as parts, instanced by code, and inheriting them as well would put two cubes and two limbs on the floor. `cube.tscn` and `organism.tscn` are still in the folder and still run on their own. The Input Map is the Organism's; there are no new keys. `export_presets.cfg` is Cube's.

`body.tscn` adds one node, `Body`, an empty `Node3D` on the floor at (0, 0, 0.9), which runs `body.gd`. Everything below it is added by that script: four pivots and the 21 nodes of four part scenes.

The parts. `parts/` holds four scenes. `cube_part.tscn` is the `Cube` branch of `cube.tscn` (the mesh node running `cube.gd`, unchanged, with its `Body`). `limb_part.tscn` is the `Hip` branch of `organism.tscn`, its root renamed `Limb`, with the thirteen nodes below it; its three bars are instances of `cube_part.tscn`, each with the bar's position and its scale of (0.16, 0.16, 0.6). `point_part.tscn` is `A` from `line.tscn`, the sphere of 7.5 cm. `plane_part.tscn` is a mesh node whose mesh is `parts/walk.tres`, with a `Body` and the supplied `plane_part.gd`. In the same step `cube.tscn`, `organism.tscn` and `line.tscn` changed: `Cube`, `Hip` and `A` are now instances of the part scenes, at the places and under the names they had, which is what the editor's *Save Branch as Scene* leaves behind. The part scenes and the three changed scenes were written as files, not saved from the editor; the editor steps in TUTORIAL.md are marked *(editor)* and were not watched.

The saved walk. `parts/walk.tres` is one walk kept as a resource, so that the study runs without walking: B driven D, S, A, W, 0.9 m each (the last leg stops 0.1 m short), through `plane.gd`'s own recorder, closed and filled, and the mesh saved with `ResourceSaver.save()`, as Plane's *In your room* saves `my_plane.tres`. It reads `closed, 3.51 m around, 28 triangles`. Twelve of the 28 triangles have no area: the first and the last leg run straight through the fan's first corner. That is Plane's fan, kept as it is; the sixteen others cover 0.80 square metres. The mesh keeps the walk's own numbers: it begins at (0.75, 1.12, 0), and its middle is (1.2, 1.12, 0.45). The repository ignores `*.tres`; this folder's `.gitignore` takes `parts/walk.tres` out of that rule, because the kit does not load without it.

`body.gd`, on `Body`, is the complete learner script: `add_part()`, which makes an empty pivot under a given parent, places it, and instances one part scene under it; four parts; one exported axis, `parenting`, with four values (flat, a chain, a chain with offset pivots, two chains from one hip) that give the same four parts different parents; and one angle that grows by `speed * delta` and turns every pivot, up to 30 degrees. The tutorial rebuilds it from one `instantiate()`.

Supplied parts. `parts/plane_part.gd` has one job: in `_ready()` it makes the plane's collision shape from its mesh, solid from both sides, with the three lines Plane's room used. `sheet.gd` renders the argument and the process strip. `lattice.gd`, `organism.gd`, `organism_frames.gd`, `second.gd` and `second.json` are the Organism's and belong to `organism.tscn`; this chapter does not use them.

The planted fault is the tutorial's first try at a chain: `point.add_child(plane)` on a pivot that is already the root's child. Godot answers `Can't add child 'PlanePivot' to 'PointPivot', already has a parent 'Body'.`, once for each of the three calls, and leaves the tree as it was: the root still has four children, the body looks the same, and nothing follows the point. The repair is to name the parent when the part is added; `reparent()` is the other way.

The guest. `guests/body-of-parts/` is the one body this chapter hands to the learner's room, the project `../my-room/`: the scene `body-of-parts.tscn`, whose root runs `body_of_parts.gd`, and beside it everything it needs, copied in: `point_part.tscn`, `plane_part.tscn` with `plane_part.gd` and `walk.tres`, `cube_part.tscn` with `cube_part.gd` (the kit's `cube.gd` without its drafts, which are files outside the folder), and `limb_part.tscn`. `body_of_parts.gd` is the lesson's script of *One function, four parts* with the turn of the argument: `add_part()`, four parts, every pivot the root's child, one angle up to 30 degrees. The pivots stand closer together than in the study, so that the body fits on a place, 1.6 m wide and deep: its box runs from (−0.45, 0, −0.70) to (0.675, 0.50, 0.75) at rest and from (−0.615, 0, −0.792) to (0.702, 0.50, 0.648) with every pivot turned. A supplied `paint()` gives each part one colour of the course look, written out as hex because a guest names nothing outside its folder: the cube BODY `#c6ff3d`, the plane SECOND `#22d9ff`, the limb THIRD `#ff8b1f`, the point BONE `#f4efe6`. The study itself keeps the colours it was built and photographed in. The guest declares no `class_name`. `check_guest.gd`, in the kit's root, is the base's `supplied/check_guest.gd`, copied unchanged; `verify.gd` preloads it. The `.gitignore` takes `guests/**/*.tres` out of the repository's `*.tres` rule, for the guest's copy of the walk.

The room test, run once on 2026-10-01 in a scratch copy of `my-room` without `.godot/`, the guest's folder copied in and its line added to `GUESTS`: `godot --headless --path . --xr-mode off --script res://guest_trial.gd -- body-of-parts` passed 8 of 8, exit code 0. The room printed `My room: place 2 is res://guests/body-of-parts/body-of-parts.tscn` and ran 180 frames with no error, and the walker, sent at the guest from the aisle, ended "arrived", 0.05 m from the middle of the place, its feet at 0.29 m: it stepped up onto the body. With the room task done as *In your room* describes (a ring written by hand, the cube commanding the limb and the ring) the same trial passed 8 of 8 and the walk ended "blocked", 0.18 m from the middle, its feet at 0.02 m. A second ring copied in as `gift_part.tscn` and added under the root ran with no error. One still of the guest in the room was made with a window and looked at. Six faults were planted in the guest, each in a copy, and run against `verify.gd`: a path outside the folder, a `class_name`, a part that is not there, a pivot moved off the place and a changed colour each failed it; the cube lifted 0.1 m did not, because the point still touches the ground.

Checks. `godot --headless --path . --xr-mode off --script res://verify.gd` runs 88 checks and writes `verification.json`; `verify.log` holds the last run's output. They cover the project's name and main scene and the nine actions; each part scene loading and instancing, what each brings, and who owns whom; the saved walk against a walk made again through `plane.gd`; `line.tscn`, `trace.tscn`, `plane.tscn`, `cube.tscn` and `organism.tscn` still running on their own, the limb still taking 33 cells; the flat body's tree, its 25 nodes and its places at rest; the root moved (all 25 nodes follow, no `position` changes); one joint turned (only its nine nodes move); a plane turned on its own origin against one turned on a pivot; the bodies that come with the parts; the root scaled evenly (90 degrees kept at 2 and 4) and along one direction (123.0 degrees); the planted fault and `reparent()`; for each value of `parenting` the tree, the pivots' places, seven places on the body with every pivot at 30 degrees against turns worked out by hand, and one second of `_process` at four frame rates; the four values agreeing at rest and under a turned root; the body carried by B and scaled by four; the seven steps of the strip, and `process.png` read back tile by tile; and the guest: its scene loads, it runs two frames with no error, it names nothing outside its folder and declares no `class_name`, it is four parts on four pivots under one root, each part in one colour of the course look, and it stands on the ground and fits on a place, at rest and with every pivot turned. The log contains one `ERROR` on purpose, announced by the line before it. A headless run draws no frames, so the last eight checks read `sheet.json`. Before today's guest checks the count was 82.

The sheet. `godot --path . --xr-mode off --script res://sheet.gd`, run with a window and without `--headless`, renders the four values of `parenting` in three poses (at rest, with the root turned 30 degrees, with every pivot turned 30 degrees) from the study's camera and from a camera of its own that looks straight down. It writes the stills to `stills/` (ignored by git and, through a `.gdignore`, by the editor) and their measurements to `sheet.json`. In the last run, on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 with the Compatibility renderer: at rest all six pairs differed on 0.00% of the frame from both cameras, and with the root turned they did again; with every pivot turned the closest pair was flat and two chains (0.55% from the top, 1.47% from the study's camera) and the farthest the chain with offset pivots and two chains (4.77% and 5.32%). The course outline had expected the difference to appear when the root turns, and the chain and the chain with offset pivots to be the farthest pair; they are the second closest (2.97% and 2.58%). The sheet also renders the root scaled (2, 2, 2) and (1, 1, 2).

The process strip. The same run makes `process.png` (2264 × 266 pixels, 25,847 bytes, tracked): seven tiles of 320 × 240 from one fixed camera, one after each operation, named under each tile: point, + plane, + cube, + limb, pivots set, a chain, every pivot turned. The first four instance each part under the root as the tutorial first does, so the plane hangs where it was walked until *pivots set* brings it down. *A chain* differs from *pivots set* on 0.00% of the tile, while the root goes from four children to one. The outline's steps were point, plane, cube, limb, save each as a scene, instance under one root, set the pivots: saving as a scene changes no picture and comes before the first tile, instancing under one root is what the first four tiles do, and the strip adds the two operations the argument turns on.
