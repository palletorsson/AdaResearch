# Organism — bone, tendon, muscle

Open this project's `project.godot` in the prepared Godot 4.6 environment from Day Zero. `organism.tscn` is the main scene. Follow TUTORIAL.md; the chapter assumes the finished Point Cube study. It takes two sessions: the first ends at *Stop here*, with the whole script as it should stand at that point.

`organism.tscn` inherits `cube.tscn`, which inherits `plane.tscn`, `trace.tscn`, `line.tscn` and `base.tscn`. Everything from Cube is there unchanged and still runs: the root carries `plane.gd`, the repaired cube stands at (1.05, 1.37, 0.6), the walk, Enter, F, the marble on M and the Under camera on V work as they did, and `cube.tscn`, `plane.tscn`, `trace.tscn` and `line.tscn` still run on their own. The Input Map is Cube's; there are no new keys. `export_presets.cfg` is Cube's too.

`organism.tscn` adds one node, `Organism`, with two children. `Hip`, a pale point at (−0.9, 1.12, 0.8), carries the limb as a chain of nested nodes: `Hip > Upper > Lower > Foot > Toe`. `Upper`, `Lower` and `Foot` are the joints, each drawn as an upright pin, each 0.3 m along its parent's z. Each joint has a `Bar`: a mesh node that runs last chapter's `cube.gd`, unchanged, with its `Body`, and whose own transform, a scale of (0.16, 0.16, 0.6) and a position of (0, 0, 0.15), makes the 0.5 m cube a bar of 0.08 × 0.08 × 0.3 m that begins at its pin. `Toe` is a pink point at the end of the foot's bar, 13 cm lower. `Lattice` is the other child: 8 × 11 cells of 0.1 m, 0.12 m apart, the first at (−0.6, 0.99, 0.24), with a label that counts them.

Why the bars are stretched by their nodes and not by their corners. The cube's corner list stays what it was, eight positions a quarter of a metre from the node's origin, and the node's transform does the rest. That keeps `cube.gd` untouched, makes the difference between a position in a list and a position in the room the first thing the learner prints, and sets up the chapter's rule: a stretch on a leaf is harmless, a stretch on a joint shears whatever hangs below it.

`organism.gd`, on `Organism`, is the complete learner script: one `angle` that grows by `speed * delta`, three joints turned by it about their upright axes, one line that tells the lattice where the toe is in the room, and one exported axis, `pivot`, with four values: at the end, at one third, at the centre, outside. The axis moves origins only: every bar slides back on its pin and the first pin moves forward by as much, so the bars rest in the same place at all four values. The tutorial rebuilds the script from an empty `_ready()`.

Supplied parts. `lattice.gd` has one contract, `carve(at) -> int`: `at` is a position in the room; every cell within `bite` (0.16 m, the hall's figure) of it, measured along the floor, is removed with its collider, and the call returns how many cells that removed. Removed cells stay removed; `lost` lists them and `fill()` builds the lattice again. Its nested loops and its cells made in code belong to the chessboard chapter and are not read here. `organism_frames.gd` is the limb as it stood before delta, `angle += 0.01` a frame, kept for one measurement. `second.gd` makes that measurement. `sheet.gd` renders the argument.

The planted fault is the tutorial's first lattice line, `$Lattice.carve($Hip/Upper/Lower/Foot/Toe.position)`. Godot reports nothing. The toe's `position` is (0, −0.13, 0.3) whatever the joints do, and the lattice loses the six cells that stand over that spot in the room, columns 4 to 6 of rows 0 and 1, in the first frame; the count reads `82 of 88 cells` for the whole turn. With `global_position` one turn takes 33 cells along the toe's path, `55 of 88 cells`, and none of the six.

Checks. `godot --headless --path . --xr-mode off --script res://verify.gd` runs 83 checks and writes `verification.json`; `verify.log` holds the last run's output. They cover the nine actions; the four earlier scenes still running on their own; the inherited nodes, the cube and the marble on it; who is whose parent in the chain, and the thirteen nodes below `Hip`; each bar being the cube's own corners and triples under a scaled node; the foot's and the toe's `global_position` against a sum worked out by hand at six sets of joint angles while their `position` never changes; `basis.z`, `global_basis.z`, `to_global()` and `to_local()`; the colliders following the bars; a child that cannot move its parent; the bars turned in place of the joints; one second stepped at 30, 60, 90 and 144 frames with and without delta; the four values of `pivot` (the same rest, the toe where the lesson says, the gap at the knee); the lattice's contract; the cells lost to the toe's `global_position` against the path worked out by hand, and the six other cells lost to its `position`; the marble through the cut and on the cells that are left; the cut at six frame rates; the shear under a stretched joint; and the hip carried by B through the lattice. A headless run draws no frames and has one frame rate, so the last ten checks read `second.json` and `sheet.json`.

The second. `godot --headless --path . --xr-mode off --fixed-fps 30 --script res://second.gd` runs the limb for one second of its own time at the rate given, with and without delta, and keeps the result in `second.json`. Measured at 30, 60, 90 and 144: with `angle += 0.01` the angle after one second was 0.30, 0.60, 0.90 and 1.44 rad; with `angle += speed * delta` it was 0.6000 rad in all four, the toe at (−0.159, 0.99, 1.088). The script waits 60 frames first, because the first frames of a run are uneven even at a fixed rate.

The sheet. `godot --path . --xr-mode off --script res://sheet.gd`, run with a window and without `--headless`, renders the four values of `pivot` at rest, with every joint at 30 degrees, and after one whole turn, from the study's camera and from a camera of its own that looks straight down. It writes the stills to `stills/` (ignored by git and, through a `.gdignore`, by the editor) and their measurements to `sheet.json`. In the last run, on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 with the Compatibility renderer, the closest pair at 30 degrees was one third and the centre (1.27% of the frame from the top, 0.52% from the study's camera); at rest no pair differed on more than 0.18%. The sheet also renders three things outside the axis: the lattice told the toe's `position`, the bars turned in place of the joints, and a joint stretched to (1, 1, 2).

Every step of TUTORIAL.md was also run as its own script in Godot 4.6 (last on 2026-10-01, tab-indented as the page shows them), with the behaviour and messages the text reports; so were the variations under *Try*, the carried hip under *Become it* with the A key held for 115 frames, and a room assembled as *In your room* describes. The cut at 3, 5, 10, 30, 60 and 144 frames a second and the uneven first frames were measured in runs with `--fixed-fps`. The marble was dropped over the cut six times while the limb turned. The editor's warning about a non-uniform scale was found as text in the 4.6 binary and not watched; parts marked *(editor)* describe the editor's windows.
