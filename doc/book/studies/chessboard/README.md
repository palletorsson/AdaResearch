# The Chessboard — purely repetitive and therefore structural

Open this project's `project.godot` in the prepared Godot 4.6 environment from Day Zero. `chessboard.tscn` is the main scene. Follow TUTORIAL.md; the chapter assumes the finished Body of Parts study. It takes two sessions: the first ends at *Stop here*, which names the script as it should stand at that point.

`chessboard.tscn` inherits `plane.tscn`, and through it `trace.tscn`, `line.tscn` and `base.tscn`: the ground, the camera, A and B, the walk, Enter, F, the marble on M and the Under camera on V, with the root still running `plane.gd`. It does not inherit `body.tscn`: the body of parts would stand inside the floor. `body.tscn`, `organism.tscn`, `cube.tscn` and the scenes before them are still in the folder and still run on their own; every scene, script and part carried over from the Body of Parts study is unchanged, byte for byte; `project.godot` differs in its name and its main scene. The Input Map is the Organism's; there are no new keys. `export_presets.cfg` is Cube's.

`chessboard.tscn` adds one node, `Chessboard`, an empty `Node3D` on the ground at (−1.75, 0, −2), which runs `chessboard.gd`. Everything below it is added by that script: 64 instances of `parts/cube_part.tscn`, 192 nodes. The scene also sets the position of one inherited node: the marble waits at (2.6, 0.1, 1.2) and not at (2.0, 0.1, 1.2), where it would lie inside a cube of the floor.

The part. `parts/cube_part.tscn` is the Body of Parts' cube part: the mesh node running `cube.gd`, half a metre on every edge, with its `Body` and a trimesh shape of twelve triangles. Its orange material is saved in the scene file, so every instance wears the same one until a script gives it another. With B where it starts, A stands over cell (2, 4) and B over cell (5, 4).

`chessboard.gd`, on `Chessboard`, is the complete learner script: a loop inside a loop that instances the cube part 64 times and places each by its indices times `spacing`; a `StandardMaterial3D` made in code for every cube; `colour_of(x, z)`, which answers a colour for two indices; one exported axis, `rhythm`, with four values (every cell alike, the (x + z) % 2 chessboard, x % 3 stripes, a hue from x + z) that choose the rule; one `if` that disables the collider of cell (5, 5); and `count_materials()`. The tutorial rebuilds it from one `instantiate()`.

Supplied parts. `sheet.gd` renders the argument and the process strip. `marble.gd` is Plane's probe: `drop(at)`. `lattice.gd`, `organism.gd`, `organism_frames.gd`, `second.gd`, `second.json`, `body.gd` and `parts/plane_part.gd` belong to the earlier scenes; this chapter opens `lattice.gd` to read its two loops and does not run it.

The planted fault is the tutorial's first hue line: `Color.from_hsv((x + z) / (2 * n), 0.8, 0.6)`. `x`, `z` and `n` are whole numbers, the division gives 0 for every cell, and the whole floor is red, `(0.6, 0.12, 0.12)`, with 64 materials and no error. Started with the debugger attached (`-d`), Godot prints `WARNING: Integer division. Decimal part will be discarded.` and the script's line. The repair is `float(x + z)`. The warning's mark in the script editor is described and was not watched.

Checks. `godot --headless --path . --xr-mode off --script res://verify.gd` runs 57 checks and writes `verification.json`; `verify.log` holds the last run's output. It takes about 36 seconds, because the gap is measured with 27 real drops and a headless run keeps real time; with `--fixed-fps 60` added it takes about a second and gives the same numbers. The checks cover the project's name, main scene and nine actions; `line.tscn`, `trace.tscn`, `plane.tscn`, `cube.tscn`, `organism.tscn` and `body.tscn` still running on their own; the cube part and what two instances share; the floor's 64 cubes, each index against its position; the finished chessboard, its ghost, a ray and the marble over a solid cell and over the ghost, with M pressed; the lesson's first steps built by hand (one cube, a row, the floor), and one material shared against one each, with one colour changed; the four rules cell by cell; the planted fault; which rules change when x and z are swapped or the floor is turned; the luminance of the fifteen hue bands; the gap; the seven steps of the strip; `process.png` and `sheet.png` read back tile by tile. A headless run draws no frames, so the last ten checks read `sheet.json`. Four scripts broken on purpose in a copy (the planted fault, x and z swapped in the position, the ghost's `if` switched off, one material made before the loops) each failed the run, on 6, 1, 5 and 10 checks.

The sheet. `godot --path . --xr-mode off --script res://sheet.gd`, run with a window and without `--headless`, renders the four values of `rhythm` from the study's camera, 5.9 m from the middle of the floor, and from a camera of its own that looks straight down. It writes the stills to `stills/` (ignored by git and, through a `.gdignore`, by the editor), the four from above side by side to `sheet.png` (1292 × 266 pixels, 23,403 bytes, tracked), and the measurements to `sheet.json`. In the last run, on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 with the Compatibility renderer, the floor covered 29.65% of the frame from above in all four. Cell by cell the closest pair was every cell alike and the stripes (11.12% of the frame from above, 14.84% from the top: 24 of 64 cells); the chessboard and the stripes differed on 14.26% and 19.79%, about half the floor, as every cell alike and the chessboard did (15.04% and 20.05%); the hue ramp differed from each of the others on the whole floor (29.22% and 39.06%). By the stills' average colour the order turned round: the chessboard and the stripes were the closest pair (17.77 apart from above, in 255ths summed over the three channels; 17.67 from the top) and every cell alike and the chessboard the farthest (71.43). The course outline had expected the chessboard and the stripes to look most alike from 6 m; that holds for the averages and not for the cells. It had also called the hue ramp the only floor with a direction: with x and z swapped 30 cells of the stripes change, and with the floor turned half round 48 of the stripes and 56 of the hue ramp.

The process strip. The same run makes `process.png` (2264 × 266 pixels, 33,858 bytes, tracked): seven tiles of 320 × 240 from one fixed camera, one after each operation, named under each tile: one cube, a row, the floor, two colours, hue by index, a ghost cell, spacing opened. The first three are built by `sheet.gd` by hand, with the part's own material, as the tutorial first builds them; the last four are `chessboard.gd` with one thing changed at each step. *A ghost cell* differs from *hue by index* on 0.00% of the tile, while the colliders go from 64 to 63. The strip's camera also counted draw calls: 5 without the floor, 10, 45 and 325 with one cube, a row and the floor, and 325 with one material as with 64. `course.json` lists no process for this chapter; these seven are the steps the lesson takes.

The gap. With w = 0.5 m and d = 0.2 m, g = d at a spacing of 0.70. Every number in the tutorial's section comes from the floor as the lesson builds it, the marble dropped over the middle between cubes 3 and 4 of row 4, at 60 physics steps a second. Dropped from 2 m: on top up to s = 0.59, between the cubes from 0.60 to 0.678 (0.31 to 0.44 m up), through from 0.679; in steps of 1 mm from 0.660 to 0.705, widening and narrowing, the same switch. Let go at 0.75 m: on top up to 0.699, through from 0.700, both ways. Measured in a copy of the project, not by `verify.gd`: at 90 steps a second the dropped marble stuck at 0.68 only and passed from 0.69, and at 120 it stuck nowhere among the spacings tried (0.60 to 0.68 in steps of 0.02 on top, 0.70 through); with `BoxShape3D` for every cube it went through from 0.60 on, dropped from 2 m, and from 0.70 let go at 0.75 m; the marble's `continuous_cd` switched off changed no outcome; with `physics/3d/solver/contact_max_allowed_penetration` at 0.002 in place of 0.01 the dropped marble passed from 0.690, stuck from 0.665 to 0.685, and passed once at 0.660; at 0.03 it passed from 0.680. Nothing was rolled: the marble's contract is `drop(at)`.
