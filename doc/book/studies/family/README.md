# Families — one body in a single class

This is a new project: import this folder's `project.godot` in the Project Manager of the prepared Godot 4.6 environment from Day Zero. `family.tscn` is the main scene. Follow TUTORIAL.md; the chapter assumes the finished Chessboard study. It takes two sessions: the first ends at *Stop here*, which names both scripts as they should stand at that point.

`family.tscn` inherits `plane.tscn`, and through it `trace.tscn`, `line.tscn` and `base.tscn`: the ground, the camera, A and B, the walk, Enter, F, the marble on M and the Under camera on V, with the root still running `plane.gd`. It does not inherit `chessboard.tscn`: the floor of cubes would stand under the sheet. `chessboard.tscn`, `body.tscn`, `organism.tscn`, `cube.tscn` and the scenes before them are still in the folder and still run on their own; every scene, script and part carried over from the Chessboard study is unchanged, byte for byte; `project.godot` differs in its name and its main scene. The Input Map is the Organism's; there are no new keys. `export_presets.cfg` is Cube's.

`family.tscn` adds two nodes at (−1.8, 0, 0.9). `Sheet` is an instance of the supplied `sweep_sheet.tscn`: a dark sheet of paper on the ground and one function. `Family` is an empty `Node3D` which runs `family.gd`. The scene also sets the position of one inherited node: the marble waits at (3.2, 0.1, 1.2), clear of the sheet.

Two scripts are the learner's. `globe.gd` is the class: `class_name Globe`, `extends MeshInstance3D`, two exported fields with their types (`segments: int = 16`, from 4 to 64, and `rings: int = 8`, from 1 to 32), a `build()` that keeps the two inside those limits and makes a `SphereMesh` from them, called from `_ready()`, and `triangles()`, which counts the triangles the mesh lists. `family.gd`, on `Family`, is the sweep: two exported text fields, `axis` (`"segments"`) and `values` (`"4, 8, 16, 64"`), and a loop that makes one member for each value, sets the named field with `set()`, sets the rings to half the segments, adds the member, hangs it on the sheet under what it reads back with `get()`, and prints what was asked beside what was built. The tutorial rebuilds both from one `SphereMesh.new()`.

Supplied parts. `sweep_sheet.tscn` and `sweep_sheet.gd` are the sweep sheet. One contract: `hang(member, title)` stands a member next in the row, on the ground, 1.2 m from the one before, writes the title on a plate at its feet, and gives a member with no material the sheet's one paint; `hung` is the list of what it holds. It does not build, set or count a member. `sheet.gd` photographs the members and makes the process strip. `marble.gd` is Plane's probe: `drop(at)`. `chessboard.gd`, `lattice.gd`, `organism.gd`, `organism_frames.gd`, `second.gd`, `second.json`, `body.gd` and `parts/` belong to the earlier scenes.

The planted fault, and what the outline had planned. The course outline planned this fault: the sweep sets a typed `int` from a text field as the string `"16"`, `set()` refuses it in silence, and four identical spheres come back. In Godot 4.6.stable that does not happen. `set("segments", "4")` on a field typed `int` makes the field 4, and nothing is printed. Measured on such a field: `"16"` gives 16, `" 64"` gives 64, `"sixteen"` and an empty text give 0, `"3.9"` gives 3, `"4 8 16 64"` gives 481664, 3.5 gives 3, `true` gives 1, 640 is taken although the field's range ends at 64; a `Vector3`, a list, a dictionary, a `Color` and `null` leave it unchanged. The other direction is refused: a field typed `String` keeps its value when handed 16, 2.5 or `true`, and a field typed `bool` keeps its value when handed the text `"true"` or `"false"`. A name the class does not have changes nothing, and `get()` of it is `null`. Godot printed nothing in any of these cases, with the debugger attached or without. The lesson is built on that: the planted fault is the tutorial's first `axis` line, `"radial_segments"`, which is `SphereMesh`'s word and not the class's. Four members of 16 segments and 288 triangles stand under plates that read 4, 8, 16, 64, and the output is `4: 288 triangles` four times. The repair is the name, `"segments"`, and then a read-back: the plate shows `member.get(axis)` and the line prints asked beside built. By `=` the same two mistakes are loud: `member.segments = "4"` gives `Parse Error: Cannot assign a value of type "String" as "int".`, and `member.radial_segments = 4` gives `Invalid assignment of property or key 'radial_segments' with value of type 'int' on a base object of type 'MeshInstance3D (Globe)'.` The integer-division warning of the chessboard returns twice and is named: `WARNING: Integer division. Decimal part will be discarded.`, read from runs started with `-d`. Its mark in the script editor, the Debugger panel, the Inspector's two text boxes and the range sliders are described and were not watched.

Checks. This study has a class with a name, and Godot lists named classes when it imports a project. From a fresh copy run `godot --headless --path . --xr-mode off --import` once; without it `verify.gd` and `sheet.gd` say so and stop. Then `godot --headless --path . --xr-mode off --script res://verify.gd` runs 56 checks and writes `verification.json`; `verify.log` holds the last run's output. It takes about 6 seconds, and about one with `--fixed-fps 60`, with the same lines. The checks cover the project's name, main scene and nine actions; `line.tscn`, `trace.tscn`, `plane.tscn`, `cube.tscn`, `organism.tscn`, `body.tscn` and `chessboard.tscn` still running on their own; the class, its name, its base, its two exported typed fields and their ranges; that a member has no mesh until it enters the tree; the triangle counts of the four members, listed and with area; fields set after `add_child()`; the limits in `build()` against `@export_range` and against `SphereMesh`'s own floor; every row of the `set()` table above; the sheet's contract; the finished study, member by member; the marble through a member and on top of one that was given a body; a member made again from its two numbers; the planted fault, a sweep of `rings` under the half rule, and four spellings of the text field; the six steps of the strip; `process.png` and `sheet.png` read back; and, from `sheet.json`, the numbers the tutorial quotes. A headless run draws no frames, so the last twelve checks read `sheet.json`. If a check stops on a script error, the run gives up after a minute without progress and exits 1.

Negative tests. In a copy of this folder, eleven faults were planted one at a time, and each failed the run with exit code 1: the planted fault (8 checks failed); the same with the plates showing what was asked (9); no read-back with the right name (2); the fields set after `add_child()` (6); the half rule left out (7); the two `clampi` lines left out (2); the fields not exported and without a written type (1); `triangles()` dividing by 2 (5); no `_ready()`, so `build()` is never called (14, and the run gave up); the class without its name (the run stopped at the import message); the values split at semicolons (3, and the run gave up). The copy with nothing planted passed 56 of 56.

The sheet. `godot --path . --xr-mode off --script res://sheet.gd`, run with a window and without `--headless`, photographs each member alone on the sheet, 2 m from its middle with a 40 degree field of view, from four standpoints: from the side at the height of its middle; from straight above; from the side with the member turned half a segment; and from the side with every triangle lit flat. The light's shadow is off. Each still is taken a second time with the member in one flat colour, which gives the body's pixels exactly. It writes the stills to `stills/` (ignored by git and, through a `.gdignore`, by the editor), twelve of them as `sheet.png` (1292 × 754 pixels, 123,989 bytes, tracked) and the measurements to `sheet.json`. In the last run, on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 with the Compatibility renderer, the stills measured 960 × 720 and the body covered 29.58% of one. Two members differ at a pixel when their three channels together are more than a tenth of full scale apart. From above: 11.84, 14.60, 15.87, 2.86, 4.12 and 0.99%. Turned: 10.57, 14.08, 15.25, 3.44, 4.84 and 0.76%. Lit flat: 17.97, 22.04, 21.77, 13.10, 13.97 and 4.65%. The process strip.
