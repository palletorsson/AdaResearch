# Point Cube — when things go wrong: the drafter is a machine

Open this project's `project.godot` in the prepared Godot 4.6 environment from Day Zero. `cube.tscn` is the main scene. Follow TUTORIAL.md; the chapter assumes the finished Point Plane study. It takes two sessions: the first ends at *Stop here*, with the whole script as it should stand at that point.

`cube.tscn` inherits `plane.tscn`, which inherits `trace.tscn`, `line.tscn` and `base.tscn`. Everything from Plane is there unchanged and still runs: the root carries `plane.gd`, so the walk, Enter, F, the marble on M and the Under camera on V work as they did, and `plane.tscn`, `trace.tscn` and `line.tscn` still run on their own. `cube.tscn` adds `Cube`, an empty mesh node with an orange material at (1.05, 1.37, 0.6), and its child `Body`, a static body with an empty collision shape. The Input Map is Plane's; there are no new keys.

`cube.gd`, on `Cube`, is the complete learner script: eight corners in one list, twelve triangles as triples of corner numbers, a loop that hands them to `SurfaceTool` and reports a triple that asks for a corner that is not there, flat normals, a collider made from the mesh, and one exported axis, `draft`, with four values: my one face, the one-line draft, the three-line draft, repaired. The tutorial rebuilds it from the corners. The cube is 0.5 m on every edge and stands at the height of the walk, from 1.12 m to 1.62 m, inside the square a walk of D, S, A, W makes.

The two drafts. The chapter teaches directing and checking drafted code, and needs no assistant to run. `drafted_cube.gd` is "what came back" for the tutorial's three-line instruction (what should change, what must not change, one observation that would show the result is wrong). `drafted_vague.gd` is what came back for one line, `make a cube`. **Both were written for this kit as examples of typical faults. No assistant produced them.** The first keeps our corners and our front face and has three planted faults: triple 3 is `[0, 2, 8]` and asks for a ninth corner, which stops `build()` with Godot's `SCRIPT ERROR: Invalid access of index '8' on a base object of type: 'Array[Vector3]'.`; triples 8 and 9, the top, run counter-clockwise, so the top is absent from outside and a marble falls through it; triple 11 is `[1, 5, 5]`, a triangle without area, so half the bottom is missing. The second answer is a cube of 1 m with all twelve triangles counter-clockwise: its corners and triples are the ones the museum's own builder stores in `commons/primitives/animatedcubebuilder/animatedcubebuilder.gd`. Neither file is edited in the lesson; `cube.gd` reads their lists at `draft` 1 and 2. A learner may put the same instructions to a real assistant and check the answer with the same instruments.

Checks. `godot --headless --path . --xr-mode off --script res://verify.gd` runs 68 checks and writes `verification.json`; `verify.log` holds the last run's output. They cover the nine actions; the three earlier scenes still running on their own; the inherited nodes; 8 corners, 12 triples and 36 indices; every triangle of the repaired cube facing outward, `dot(normal, centroid - middle) > 0`, two to each of the six directions, each face two triples sharing a diagonal; flat normals; the one-sided collider; a marble resting on the repaired top at 1.71 m, falling past the cube beside it, and rolling off when dropped 3.5 cm from the top's diagonal; the cube standing on a walked plane; both drafts as files (what the first kept, and its four differing triples; the second's doubled corners); the first answer run as it came back building nothing; the four values of `draft` building what they claim (2 outward triangles; 12 inward; 8 outward, 2 inward on the top and 1 without area; 12 outward) and the marble ending on the floor, inside the cube at 0.97 m, on the floor, and on the top; and the marble being fooled when `backface_collision` is switched on. A headless run draws no frames, so the last eight checks read `sheet.json`. The log contains one `SCRIPT ERROR` and one `ERROR` on purpose, each announced by the line before it.

The sheet. `godot --path . --xr-mode off --script res://sheet.gd`, run with a window and without `--headless`, renders the four values of `draft` from three standpoints: the study's camera, Under, and a third camera behind the cube that the script brings, because the other two never face the back and the right. It writes the stills to `stills/` (ignored by git and, through a `.gdignore`, by the editor) and their measurements to `sheet.json`. In the last run, on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 with the Compatibility renderer, the closest pair from above was my one face and the three-line draft (0.36% of the frame); from below and from behind it was the three-line draft and the repair (0.95% and 2.03%).

The build. `export_presets.cfg` holds one preset, `Windows Desktop`. `godot --headless --path . --export-debug "Windows Desktop" <folder>/point-cube.exe`, with `<folder>` an existing folder outside the project, was run on 2026-10-01 with the 4.6 export templates installed: it took three seconds and wrote `point-cube.exe` (100,557,824 bytes), `point-cube.pck` (84,700 bytes) and `point-cube.console.exe` (50,176 bytes). The exported build was started on the same machine, with a window for 240 frames and headless for 300, and printed no message. Never export into the project folder. The build was not copied to another computer, and no Android or headset build was made.

Every step of TUTORIAL.md was also run as its own script in Godot 4.6 (last on 2026-10-01, tab-indented as the page shows them), with the behaviour and messages the text reports; so were a room assembled as *In your room* describes and its build. The debugger's variables were read with Godot's command-line debugger (`-d`). The build's behaviour in *What the cube owes* (a save to `res://`, `assert()` and `push_error()` in a debug and a release build, the `.exe` without its `.pck`) and the two messages for Godot 3 spellings were measured in scratch projects outside the study. Parts marked *(editor)* describe the editor's windows and were not watched. A beginner learner trial remains distinct from these execution checks.

Fixed-camera desktop study. It contains no XR player and has not been tried in a headset. The museum's builders, their corner handles, the folding net and the shelf of boxes are encounters in the hall, not parts of this download. The room (`room.tscn`, `room.gd`, `my_plane.tres`) is the learner's own and is not shipped.
