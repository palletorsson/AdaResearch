# Point Plane — when the line closes

Open this project's `project.godot` in the prepared Godot 4.6 environment from Day Zero. `plane.tscn` is the main scene. Follow TUTORIAL.md; the chapter assumes the finished Point Trace study.

`plane.tscn` inherits `trace.tscn`, which inherits `line.tscn`, which inherits `base.tscn`. A, B, the segment, the readout, the mover on B, the strip and the Record label come from Trace unchanged; `trace.tscn`, `trace.gd`, `line.tscn`, `line.gd`, `segment.gd`, `mover.gd` and `trace_strip.gd` are copies of the Trace kit, and `trace.tscn` and `line.tscn` still run on their own. `plane.tscn` puts `plane.gd` on the root and adds `Face` (an empty mesh node with a pale material), `FaceBody` (a static body with an empty collision shape), `Marble` and `Under` (a second camera, below the plane, looking up).

`plane.gd` is the complete learner script: Trace's recorder, a condition that closes it, an outline joined last to first, a triangle fan through `SurfaceTool`, generated normals, a collider made from the mesh, and one exported axis, `side`, with four values: clockwise, clockwise with flipped normals, counter-clockwise, counter-clockwise double-sided. The tutorial rebuilds it from the Trace script. Keys: WASD or the arrows move B, Space pauses the recorder, Enter closes the walk, F builds the face, M drops the marble from one metre above B, and holding V looks from below.

Supplied parts. `marble.gd` is a probe body with one contract, `drop(at)`: it starts at `at` with no speed and falls, and its height is shown in the Record label. It is the desktop stand-in for a visitor's body: it meets collision shapes and never meshes. `Under` is the stand-in for looking under the plane. `trace_strip.gd` is the Trace kit's display, opened in this chapter. The Input Map adds `close` (Enter), `fill` (F), `drop` (M) and `look` (V) to Trace's five actions.

Checks. `godot --headless --path . --xr-mode off --script res://verify.gd` runs 66 checks and writes `verification.json`; `verify.log` holds the last run's output. They cover the nine actions and their keys; `line.tscn` and `trace.tscn` still running on their own; Enter refused until three positions are kept; the outline drawn with its last position equal to its first; n − 2 triangles for n kept positions, every one sharing the first; normals present and agreeing with Godot's clockwise rule triangle by triangle; the trimesh collider; a marble that falls through the closed outline before F and rests at 1.12 m plus its radius after it, and falls past the plane when dropped outside the walk; the four values of `side` (winding, normals and cull mode each as named, the marble held at every one, rays from above and below both stopped); the collider's default, which has a side; smooth and flat normals being identical on a plane; a walk longer than the capacity losing its start; and a fan from the wrong corner of an L holding a marble in the notch. A headless run draws no frames, so the last four checks read `sheet.json`.

The sheet. `godot --path . --xr-mode off --script res://sheet.gd`, run with a window and without `--headless`, renders the four values of `side` from above and from below on one fixed walk. It writes the stills to `stills/` (ignored by git and, through a `.gdignore`, by the editor) and their measurements to `sheet.json`: how much of the frame each face covers, how bright it is, how much shadow it casts, and which two values look most alike from above. TUTORIAL.md quotes those numbers. In the last run, on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 with the Compatibility renderer, the closest pair from above was clockwise and counter-clockwise double-sided, differing on 0.82% of the frame.

Every step of TUTORIAL.md was also run as its own script in Godot 4.6 (last on 2026-10-01, tab-indented as the page shows them), with the behaviour and messages the text reports; so were `room.gd` and the line that saves the mesh. The line-strip message was measured on a `SurfaceTool` outside the study. Parts marked *(editor)* describe the editor's windows and were not watched. A beginner learner trial remains distinct from these execution checks.

Fixed-camera desktop study. It contains no XR player and no visitor's body, and has not been tried in a headset: "stand on your walk" is checked here by a dropped marble, at the plane's height of 1.12 m. The museum's triangle puzzle, drawing point and live twin are encounters in the hall, not parts of this download. The room (`room.tscn`, `room.gd`, `my_plane.tres`) is the learner's own and is not shipped.
