# Seven places for a passage

The fragments hang at your waist. Steel passes through them and continues upward as a thin suspension. Below, their shadows almost complete another floor.

Seven pieces are held higher. Beneath them, a passage runs through the field. The other fragments stay close on either side. You can see the far wall before you have found a way to reach it.

Change the arrangement. There are still forty-nine pieces, still seven overhead. Their horizontal addresses have not moved. But the openings are now separate pockets. The body that crossed a moment ago meets a fragment where the passage used to continue.

Nothing has been added. Something has been closed.

Mona Hatoum's *Remains to be Seen* holds concrete and steel in a suspended grid.[^hatoum] Its regularity carries the suggestion of a building that is no longer whole. Our fragments have no building to remember. We generated their corners. An address survives each rearrangement, but an address is not a past.

Here the rule decides which places are raised. Another decision has already been made: how much room your body occupies. Keep the passage and widen the visitor. The same opening refuses the crossing. The word *open* needed a body before it could mean anything definite.

Around the outside, the room remains clear. You can take the longer way and look back through the space you could not enter. Light has been making that journey all along.

[^hatoum]: The anchor is Mona Hatoum, *Remains to be Seen* (2019). [White Cube's exhibition account](https://www.whitecube.com/gallery-exhibitions/remains-to-be-seen) describes concrete-like floor fragments and steel rebar aligned in a suspended cube. This is a new 49-fragment rule study; its two heights, rearrangement and walking capsule are ours.
