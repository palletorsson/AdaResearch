## What is missing, and what repeats

- **The chapter about arrays never shows the loop that makes the many.** It asks what we can do with many copies, and every code block uses `x`, `y` or `z` without the `for` that produces them. A loop does appear earlier in the book, over triangle vertices in Point_Triangle_Context. The first loop over an array's addresses only arrives in Color_Context_Placed. The Array hall's own source has the loop, so the revision can quote true code.
- **Zero-based indexing is never taught, but Tutorial_3D relies on it.** The console "returns to zero after three."
- **Nesting is never explained.** `values[y][z][x]` is the reader's first sight of an array in code, and it is already an array of arrays.
- **Wrap-around runs through the whole chapter without a name.** The 3D console returns to zero, the tiles turn with `%4`, the checker uses `%2`, and sixteen steps fold onto twelve columns. Four separate incidents could become one concept with a single sentence.
- **"The structure does not decide the meaning" is said three times** in Array, Tutorial_Tiling_Floor and Tutorial_Disco. The first is earned by the ambiguous zero. The later two need a new turn: what would the reader have to add, or who does decide?
- **Tutorial_Disco reads like an operating manual.** "Track" is used before it is defined. "The bridge" is never introduced, and a museum visitor will picture something to walk on. The independence of the two clocks is presented as a caveat when it could be a discovery.
- **Two small naming traps.** Array's grid is `[x, z]`, but the tiling code calls the same axes `cx, cy`. And "the first index selects a row" will stall a beginner unless the table's orientation is visible.
- **One claim is slightly off.** Tutorial_Tiling_Walls says the control "holds it open" while someone is on the landing. In the code, the wall refuses to turn in either direction while anyone stands in its sweep, and stops mid-turn if someone steps in. Saying that the wall will not turn through a body is both more accurate and more interesting.

## Revisions, in order

1. **Array: show the loop, and let it teach zero.** From the hall's own `row_3_x.gd`:
   ```gdscript
   array_data = [[1, 1, 1, 1]]
   for i in range(4):
       var cube_instance = pickup_cube_scene.instantiate()
       cube_instance.position = Vector3(i * 1.0, 0, 0)
   ```
   > One instruction, repeated with a changing number, makes the row. `range(4)` counts 0, 1, 2, 3: the first address is zero, so the fourth cube lives at index three. Notice the double brackets. Even this row is stored as a grid with one row in it, which is where the next hall begins.
2. **Tutorial_Tiling_Floor: name the remainder** right after the `%4` line:
   > You have met this before. The console's index went round to zero after three; here the turn goes round after four. When a count must return rather than run out, `%` sends it back to the start. Listen for it again on the disco floor.
3. **Tutorial_Disco: define before instructing.** Say what a track, a step and the bridge are before the exercises use them. Turn "stop the sequencer" into the two-clocks discovery rather than a warning.
4. **Tutorial_Disco, last line.** Make the handoff concrete. For example: next, the value in a slot becomes a colour, and what you read in it will depend on its neighbours.
5. **Tutorial_Tiling_Walls.** Fix the hold sentence, and clarify "The thin paper followed us along the way." It is the one blurry sentence in the chapter's best paragraph.
