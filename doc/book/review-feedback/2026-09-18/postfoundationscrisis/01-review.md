# Post-Foundations Crisis — 01 Review

Source-aware editorial and reader-experience review of the **Post-Foundations Crisis** sequence.

Basis: `postfoundationscrisis-book.md`, `postfoundationscrisis-review.md`, and the supplied ZIP snapshot on branch `palm-scanner-door-entry`, captured 2026-09-18. No repository code was executed.

This sequence is structurally cleaner than most of the later spine. The declared eight-hall order and museum-authored order agree.

The review therefore concentrates on a different question:

> **Does the sequence genuinely return from critique to making, or does it sometimes turn a critical slogan into another authored answer before the visitor has tested it?**

The final manuscripts are already unusually disciplined about this. They repeatedly catch their own artifacts doing persuasive work:

- warm/pink versus grey groups;
- red authority versus green distribution;
- a confidence percentage with no evidence;
- “contributors” that are not people;
- “no root” inside a process with clear age/spatial advantages;
- molecules/DNA named by a catalogue rather than discovered chemically;
- a ledger that appears accountable while recording no visitor and forgetting old evidence.

That self-critique is the sequence’s strength.

This review uses two linked lenses:

1. **Structural / technical:** what the current source actually computes, stores, changes and forgets.
2. **Reader experience:** where the visitor can discover an exclusion, persuasion, provenance loss or constraint before the prose supplies the critical interpretation.

The strongest current arc is:

> **compressed distinction → participation geometry → retained conflict → situated claim → collective accretion → local connection → catalogue-constrained assembly → accountable return**

A second formulation is:

> **After the foundations crisis, the project does not need a new universal foundation. It needs practices that keep decisions, positions, conflicts, sources, constraints and exclusions available for revision.**

That is a very strong final-sequence thesis.

# 2. Sequence structure

Post-Foundations Crisis has one of the clearest routes in the whole spine.

Unlike many previous sequences, no hall-order repair is necessary. The declared and museum-authored orders already agree:

1. Algorithmic Bias Visualization
2. Applied Ethics
3. Paraconsistent Engineering
4. Situated Computation
5. Collective Knowledge
6. Rhizome Network
7. Lab Equipment Simulation
8. Post-Crisis Synthesis

The progression is also conceptually coherent.

## 1. Bias — representation allocates distinguishability

The first question after QFEP is appropriately small:

> if a model compresses some distinctions, what becomes hard to tell apart?

The chapter refuses to treat the authored pink/grey arrangement as empirical evidence about actual embeddings.

That is exactly the correct first post-crisis move:

> **name the representation before judging its world.**

## 2. Applied Ethics — spatial design distributes invitations

The next room changes scale:

> representation of categories → arrangement of participants.

A room plan can make one orientation, position or address relation easier.

The chapter correctly refuses to infer actual equal participation from equal angular spacing.

Thus:

> **affordance/invitation ≠ observed social outcome.**

## 3. Paraconsistent Engineering — disagreement can be retained as data

The sequence now asks what to do when two accounts conflict.

The key is not “accept both as true.”

It is:

> preserve both sources and the unresolved relation long enough to investigate.

That gives the sequence an operational use for the preceding foundations crisis.

## 4. Situated Computation — a judgement should carry its conditions

Now provenance becomes part of the claim.

The current installation is intentionally artificial:

- same subject;
- four authored verdicts;
- four authored percentages;
- explicit map-assigned positions.

That is enough to demonstrate:

> confidence and position can be displayed without being earned from evidence.

The chapter then asks for the missing next step:

> expose criteria and observations.

## 5. Collective Knowledge — aggregation can erase provenance

This is the correct follow-on.

Situated Computation kept views separate.

Collective Knowledge sends contributions into one coherent form.

The problem becomes:

> what survives the merge?

The source currently preserves almost nothing about the contribution after it lands.

That limitation should become more visible.

## 6. Rhizome — decentralisation is not equality

The central form is replaced with a growing graph.

No node is assigned the variable “root.”

But local proximity and temporal availability still distribute connection unevenly.

That is an important correction to a simplistic rhizome metaphor.

## 7. Lab Equipment — available vocabulary constrains what can be assembled

The network becomes a recognisable body.

The catalogue and target specification now matter.

The chapter’s strongest question is:

> what body is difficult to describe with this vocabulary?

That is the right late-sequence return to the book’s recurring question about bodies and exclusions.

But the current source needs to make the catalogue genuinely constraining for the question to work fully.

## 8. Synthesis — keep one consequence attached to a decision

The final room should not provide a master principle.

It should provide a habit:

> one decision → one affected case → one proposed repair → one observable consequence → return.

The current manuscript achieves this intellectually.

One small interaction could make it embodied.

# 3. Reader-experience lens

## Algorithmic Bias Visualization — Less room for a distinction

### Curiosity

The manuscript begins well:

> pick two warm labels and two grey labels; move PERSPECTIVE; compare their separation.

This creates a concrete perceptual question before introducing embedding theory.

### Discovery

The source makes the spatial interpolation clear.

For every term:

```gdscript
default_position.lerp(compressed_position, blend)
```

The same index remains associated with the same word.

That supports the manuscript’s strongest claim:

> presence in a representation is not the same as useful distinguishability inside it.

### But PERSPECTIVE is not only spatial

The artifact also changes visual salience as `blend` increases.

Source:

```gdscript
_marginalized_mat.emission_energy_multiplier = 0.4 + blend * 0.8
_default_mat.albedo_color.a = 1.0 - blend * 0.4
```

The warm group glows more.

The grey group is instructed to fade.

Whether the alpha change is fully visible depends on material transparency/runtime configuration, but the emission change is definitely another display variable.

So the comparison is not:

> same visual treatment / changed spatial compression.

It is:

> changed spatial compression + changed salience treatment.

The manuscript currently attributes the difficulty primarily to “space available to tell them apart.”

That is directionally right, but the artifact should isolate it.

### “Perspective” is also a misleading control name

No camera is moved.

No player transform is moved.

The entire 1–2 m artifact changes the coordinates of its spheres/tags.

The final manuscript already catches this:

> “the script calls this perspective, but it changes the represented geometry.”

Good.

The source identity does not.

It says:

> “places you INSIDE the compressed region.”

It does not.

The visitor stands outside a small model whose points move.

### Reader timing problem

The header says:

At low blend:

> **DEFAULT PERSPECTIVE — everything looks fair**

At high blend:

> **COMPRESSED PERSPECTIVE — feel the crowding**

That tells the visitor the intended moral before they complete the comparison.

The manuscript itself is more careful.

### Stronger artifact sequence

Initial header:

> **COMPRESSION 0.00**

or:

> **ARRANGEMENT A**

As slider moves:

> **COMPRESSION 0.50**

At the end, a small optional interpretation panel can say:

> **PROJECT READING: some distinctions have been crowded together.**

Keep materials fixed during the primary comparison.

Then a second control could later expose:

> **SALIENCE**

if you want to investigate how brightness/colour compounds spatial compression.

### Category caution

The manuscript’s paragraph about:

- queer;
- disabled;
- refugee;
- normal;
- professional;
- objective

is essential.

The artifact’s two-colour grouping is itself another authored reduction.

Do not cut that criticism.

A future grouping comparison would be valuable, but the first necessary fix is to make the existing spatial experiment clean.

### Return

QFEP’s scalar compression becomes a representational compression with named terms.

## Collective Knowledge — More contributions than contributors

### Curiosity

The opening count comparison is excellent:

> contributions can rise without source count rising.

The source makes this literally true.

### The source’s count is even stranger than the label suggests

There are twelve peer positions.

`_seen_peers` records whether a peer has contributed **during the current version**.

Every new facet increments:

> `_edits`.

The first contribution from each peer in that version increments:

> `_contributors`.

When the artifact reaches 60 facets:

- placed facets reset;
- `_seen_peers` resets;
- `_contributors` does **not** reset.

Therefore `CONTRIBUTORS` can become:

> 13, 24, 36, 100…

even though only twelve peer nodes exist.

What the number actually measures is closer to:

> **cumulative peer-version participation events**.

The manuscript catches this:

> “A number labelled CONTRIBUTORS can therefore outgrow the twelve source positions.”

Excellent.

### The source’s “peer production” identity overclaims agency

Header says:

> many self-selected peers.

Actual source:

```gdscript
idx = rng.randi() % peers.size()
```

The program chooses a source at random.

No peer selects itself.

No contributor chooses:

- content;
- placement;
- acceptance;
- revision.

When a packet lands:

> the central algorithm chooses the next location on one predefined helix.

Thus the current model is more exactly:

> **distributed origins / centralized acceptance and assembly.**

That is not a flaw if named.

It is an excellent object for the sequence.

### Provenance disappears after landing

The packet remembers `peer`.

Once a facet is placed:

- the transform depends only on placement index;
- colour interpolates by placement progress;
- no source/peer identity is retained in the facet representation.

So the common object literally loses the visual provenance that the previous Situated hall was teaching us to keep.

This is perhaps the strongest cross-hall finding in the sequence.

### High-value artifact repair

Do two things.

#### Correct the readout

Show:

```text
EDITS TOTAL
ACTIVE SOURCES THIS VERSION: N / 12
SOURCE-VERSION PARTICIPATIONS: M
```

or a simpler equivalent.

#### Preserve source on the facet

Give each peer:

- stable colour;
- small source code;
- or trace line.

A landed facet keeps that source mark.

Then add one later operation:

> **DISPUTE / REVISE**

which changes or removes an existing piece rather than always appending.

This directly enacts the manuscript’s concluding distinction:

> knowledge maintenance ≠ uninterrupted growth.

### Timing

The text already asks the right questions.

The artifact should now carry more of the answer.

## Rhizome Network — Growing without a named root

### Curiosity

The chapter starts correctly from local action:

> a new node appears; which earlier nodes will it choose?

This makes “rhizome” a procedure rather than a picture.

### Source truth

A candidate position is drawn in a fixed annulus around the device origin:

```gdscript
rad = randf_range(spread * .4, spread)
```

Then it selects up to three nearest existing nodes **before** inserting itself.

This ensures:

- no self-link;
- multiple existing-neighbour links.

Good.

### The source identity overstates “growth”

The comments say:

> new shoot at the rim of the current cloud.

But the radius is relative to a **fixed origin**, not the current cloud extent.

The cloud does not progressively push its frontier outward.

All nodes are sampled within essentially the same bounded radial region.

So the more accurate description is:

> random bounded spatial accretion with nearest-neighbour links.

### “No root” versus “no centre”

The source gives no vertex a stored `root=true`.

That supports:

> no designated root.

It does **not** support:

> no centre forms  
> no hierarchy ever forms.

The initial two nodes are present longest.

Every future node can choose them if spatially near.

Earlier nodes therefore have more opportunities to accumulate degree.

The manuscript correctly says:

> earlier/well-positioned nodes can become busy.

Keep that.

### Existing birth-order colour helps

Nodes interpolate from cool white to teal by index.

So an observant visitor can already see early/late age.

A high-value small extension would be:

> **DEGREE**

that temporarily sizes or labels nodes by current number of edges.

Then “no designated root” can be compared directly with emergent hubs.

### Another useful control already exists as an export

`links_per_node` defaults to 3.

A future:

> LINKS 1 / 2 / 3

would be an excellent experiment.

With one link, the structure becomes much more tree-like/disconnected from one of the two initial components.

With several links, cycles/cross-connections become possible.

That would let the visitor see:

> rhizomaticity is partly authored by the number of relations each new node is permitted to make.

### Reader timing

Good.

The manuscript does not romanticise the source identity.

No major rewrite needed.

## Lab Equipment Simulation — The inventory before the body

### Curiosity

The chapter begins with exactly the right question:

> keep one part in view across different assemblies.

This should let the visitor discover whether:

- the part moves to another role;
- or the part itself changes.

That distinction is more important than the names “Molecule” and “DNAHelix.”

### Major source finding: the inventory pieces are retyped to match the target

At startup, 40 `MolecularPart` nodes receive random catalogue mesh types.

During assembly:

```gdscript
var part: MolecularPart = parts[i]
part.set_part_info(node_id, node_part_type)
_apply_mesh_from_catalog(part, node_part_type)
part.set_target(target_pos)
```

`_apply_mesh_from_catalog()` replaces:

- mesh;
- size;
- material colour;
- mass

according to the target node’s requested part type.

Thus the program is **not** selecting the appropriate existing catalogue part from the floating inventory.

It takes `parts[i]` and turns it into the required target type.

During float mode:

```gdscript
part.clear_target()
```

does not restore its original type.

So the next floating inventory retains the types assigned by the previous assembly until another assembly retypes them.

### Consequence for the manuscript

The current text says:

> “The catalogue defines available kinds of part… An assembly chooses instances…”

The catalogue does define kinds.

But the current assembler does not enforce a fixed inventory of those kinds.

The assembly specification can effectively request the kind it needs and the program mutates the selected part to match it.

That weakens the chapter’s final question:

> “Which bodies has the catalogue already imagined?”

because the inventory is less constraining than it appears.

The *catalogue vocabulary* still constrains which shapes can be requested.

The *floating inventory instances* do not constrain the target.

This distinction should be explicit unless the source is repaired.

### Preferred artifact fix

Make part type stable per physical instance.

Assembly should:

1. read target node type;
2. find an available floating part of matching type;
3. assign it to the target;
4. leave unused/missing targets visible if inventory lacks the required kind.

Then the visitor can discover:

> **the target asks for a part the inventory does not have.**

That would transform the hall.

A body would genuinely become difficult or impossible to assemble under a restricted catalogue.

Exactly the chapter’s question.

### Source package gap

`MolecularDesigner.gd` reads:

> `assemblies.json`

through `FileAccess`.

That JSON is not included in the review snapshot.

Therefore the source package cannot verify:

- actual catalogue entries;
- actual assembly names;
- target nodes;
- bonds;
- scales.

The `.tscn` label claims:

> **11 Assemblies**

but visually lists twelve names:

- VRBody
- Chair
- Table
- Lamp
- Shelf
- DNAHelix
- Insect
- Spider
- Octopus
- AlienTree
- CrystalTree
- Molecule.

That label is internally inconsistent even before opening the missing JSON.

Future bundles must include `assemblies.json`.

### Movement clock

The manuscript already catches one numerical issue:

> `dock_damp` is applied once per frame.

That is source-supported.

There is a second frame-rate issue.

Idle floating computes a sine-derived offset and then calls:

```gdscript
translate(offset)
```

**without multiplying by delta**.

That integrates the sampled offset every rendered frame instead of placing the part at a bounded oscillatory offset.

Its drift and speed therefore depend strongly on frame rate.

If the float phase is artistically important, this should be repaired.

### Chemical language

The final manuscript is correct:

> bonds are declared connections, not chemical bonds established by simulation.

Protect that distinction.

> **practices for keeping the consequences of design revisable.**

The halls progressively ask the maker to keep more context attached.

Algorithmic Bias asks:

> which distinctions did the representation compress?

Applied Ethics asks:

> which participation relations did the arrangement make easy?

Paraconsistent Engineering asks:

> what provenance survives disagreement and resolution?

Situated Computation asks:

> where did this confident claim come from?

Collective Knowledge asks:

> which sources and revisions survive aggregation?

Rhizome asks:

> does removing a named root actually distribute influence?

Molecular Designer asks:

> which bodies were already easy to express in the available vocabulary?

The Ledger asks:

> which consequence will you refuse to let disappear before returning to change the work?

That is a coherent final argument.

The sequence becomes weaker only when an artifact supplies the ethical answer before the visitor has performed the inspection:

- “everything looks fair” / “feel the crowding” before spatial comparison;
- red authority versus green distribution before the room has been tested;
- “paraconsistent” before the branches even overlap;
- “contributors” after provenance has already been collapsed;
- “no root” as if it meant no centre;
- “inventory” when the parts simply change type to satisfy the target;
- “accountability” in a machine that automatically forgets every old example.

Those are not reasons to remove the artifacts.

They are the final sequence’s material.

The highest-value work now is therefore:

1. **make Bias From Inside a clean compression experiment before its ethical interpretation changes salience and labels the answer;**
2. **expose Applied Ethics’ already-implemented PLAN axis so the red/green pair becomes one authored argument among several rather than the default verdict;**
3. **expose Paraconsistent Engineering’s MERGE axis so keeping, resolving and separating disagreement become comparable actions;**
4. **preserve contributor provenance and correct the counting semantics in Collective Knowledge, then add one revision/dispute operation;**
5. **make MolecularDesigner’s inventory genuinely constrain assembly—or rename the ontology if parts are intentionally mutable—and include the missing assemblies JSON in future review bundles;**
6. **as a next small final-pass improvement, give the Accountability Ledger one PIN slot so the visitor can keep one consequence visible while the stream continues.**

The reader-experience principle I would carry out of the museum is:

> **Let the visitor discover what the design has already decided before the critical text tells them why that decision matters.**

And the structural principle for the final sequence is:

> **Accountability is not the absence of reduction. It is keeping the reduction, its source, its beneficiaries, its exclusions and the possibility of revision close enough that someone can return and change the work.**
