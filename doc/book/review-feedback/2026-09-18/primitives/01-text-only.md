## 2. Five high-value editorial revisions

### 1. Point One — reach the point sooner

**TEXT — current:**

> “Through the entrance window, frames recede within frames… There is a temptation to follow them back to a first frame…”
> …
> “Alice's rabbit is already ahead… We carry a question forward; others fold behind us.” 

**Proposal:**

> Through the entrance window, frames recede within frames. The room offers more beginnings than we can follow. The code we leave unread keeps working while our attention moves elsewhere. To begin with the point, we have to choose somewhere to count from.

Then go directly to:

> “Find the origin marker.”

**Reason:** this preserves the crucial epistemic move—beginning requires selective attention—without teaching it four times before the first spatial point. It also lets later rooms *enact* containment rather than repeatedly explaining it.

---

### 2. Point Lines — make the Euclid claim match its source

**TEXT — current:**

> “two distinct points determine a straight line. Here we draw only the part between them: a segment.” 

The attached footnote cites Euclid, Book I, Postulate 1, specifically drawing a straight line between two points. 

**Proposal:**

> Keep hold of this small beginning: **between two distinct points we can draw a straight segment.** Move an endpoint. The segment changes length and direction while the connection remains.

**Reason:** the current mathematical statement is standard, but the cited historical postulate supports construction more directly than uniqueness. Either add a source for the uniqueness formulation or make the prose match the source already given. For a beginner, the proposed wording also matches the object actually in hand.

---

### 3. Triangle — make the algorithmic variable easier to isolate

**TEXT — current:**

> “Beside your first triangle… make a six-corner L… For a precise version…” followed by six coordinates, then two complete tracing orders and point reuse instructions. 

**Proposal for the main route:**

> Make a six-corner L in one horizontal plane, like a square with one corner removed. Close it once and inspect the fill.
>
> Press **NEW OUTLINE**. Trace the same boundary again, but begin at the next corner. Keep the shape fixed; change only where the sequence starts. **Should the inside change?**

Keep the coordinate table as an optional **measured version** beside the instrument or in the technical section.

**Reason:** the research variable is *starting index while boundary stays fixed*. The current instruction makes manual construction accuracy compete with that insight. This revision makes the experiment conceptually cleaner without changing its mathematics.

### 5. Melencolia — let the sequence actually end

**TEXT — current:**

> “The objects stayed still while your viewpoint changed. In transformation, you can watch from one place as objects move, turn and scale…” 

**Proposal:**

> Look back toward the five pyramids. They have become another picture from here. The objects stayed still while your viewpoint changed. Choose one relation—the repeated corners, the tall centre, the distance between the platforms—and leave it unresolved.
>
> **We leave with enough to continue. There is still something here we have not finished looking at.**

Let Transformation introduce translation, rotation and scale itself.

**Reason:** Melencolia is doing more than bridging chapters. It completes Primitives' argument that technical recognition does not exhaust a form. The current trailer slightly reduces that achievement to preparation for the next lesson.

**CAPTURE — verification status:** every text hash differs from its recorded review; every map hash except Animatedcube also differs, and every hall says learner verification `False`. Therefore statuses such as `implemented_component_verified` cannot be treated as verification of this exact captured text/map combination. 

**TEXT/SOURCE CLAIM — declared objective:** the package still lists:

> “Triangle rasterization: the GPU's atomic unit.” 

The manuscript itself has become much more precise:

> “The word `TRIANGLES` asks the renderer to treat each three vertices as a face.” 

I would update the objective rather than restore the stronger slogan. “GPU atomic unit” needs qualification and a technical source if retained.

**TEXT — naming:** `Primitives_Polythedra` is the internal hall identifier throughout the package; the mathematical English term is **polyhedra**. Keep the identifier if changing it risks paths, but use *Polyhedra* in user-facing titles.

**HYPOTHESIS — Animatedcube:** the present wording proves only that `v6` affects one of the two triangles on the square being discussed.  Before editing, the current snapshot should be checked for every face/triangle referencing vertex 6. I would not carry an older branch result into this package without that source check.

The attached manuscript otherwise handles its significant historical references unusually carefully: the Academy inscription is explicitly treated as later tradition, Shannon is explicitly described as a technical neighbour rather than a theorem applicable to this recorder, and Jordan–Brouwer is carefully separated from collision and habitability.

The larger conclusion is that **Primitives no longer needs conceptual expansion**. Its strongest revision strategy is subtraction and verification. The sequence already develops a coherent method from the invisible point to Melencolia: progressively more can be built and recognised, while each new capability reveals the additional rule that made it possible. The next pass should make that progression easier to *experience before it is explained*.
