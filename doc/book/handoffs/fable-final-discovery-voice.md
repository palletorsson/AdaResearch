# final.md: discovery and critical tutorial

Editorial direction from Palle, carried into a six-room voice pass by Astra on 11 September 2026. This supplements the existing authorial direction in `doc/book/DISCOVERY_AND_REMAINDERS.md` and `doc/book/VOICE_STUDY.md`. It is a voice trial for Palle to read, not a declaration that the book's voice is settled.

The reader is learning how to make a world while discovering what each way of making permits, selects and leaves outside it. The narrator is a knowledgeable companion who gives the reader time to notice. Code provides a precise way into an encounter. Its clarity should make another experiment possible, rather than settle the meaning of the room.

## Agreed clarification, 21 September 2026

Palle identifies a drift towards technical instruction language and asks to resume map-by-map work. The voice must be poetic and critical while remaining concrete and intelligible. Ontology and epistemology belong to what the encounter makes present, what a body can do there, and how a claim becomes knowable. They are inquiries to develop in the material, not vocabulary to add after a tutorial.

The existing instructions were sufficient to name this direction. Applying them now requires a small, visible editorial loop:

1. Read the present chapter beside its earlier versions, critical material and tutorial. Inspect the artifact ecology and the preceding/following encounter. Name what is already worth keeping.
2. Find an investigative lead in a particular action, discrepancy, pleasure or desire. State the computational capability it could teach and the bodily consequence or way of knowing at stake. The investigation may begin in the writing or in an artifact; this is no prescribed order of paragraphs.
3. When the encounter is inadequate, recover, promote, reposition or develop an immersive artifact that lets the question be explored. A further interface is appropriate only when it serves that encounter. When the artistic direction is still unclear, ask Palle a specific question about the material, showing what has already been found. Do not manufacture generic criticism to finish a hall.
4. Draft a short continuous passage first. Let the reader notice and want to know something; give the necessary principle and code enough clarity to become usable; allow the changed understanding to open another possibility. Preserve sentence rhythm, pleasure, strangeness and ambiguity of meaning without obscuring the mechanism.
5. Present that passage beside its baseline and the proposed or implemented encounter. Use the author's reading response to develop this map before extending a style across others. An approved local change does not establish a universal voice recipe.

Keep source accuracy, but distinguish its literary use from its verification. Relevant code belongs in `final.md`; exhaustive controls, registry inconsistencies, implementation inventories and test status usually belong in `technical.md` or the review record. A limitation belongs in the book when the reader can investigate what it does. An unfinished implementation is not automatically the chapter's philosophical conclusion. Planned encounters retain the visible status required by [the encounter-status agreement](../ENCOUNTER_STATUS.md).

The point and line chapters are reference passages for this work, including the running clock, the unanswered code, the rabbit, the undrawn point and the difference between following a stripe and being compelled by a boundary. Their value is the particular relation they develop, not a surface style to copy. A mature chapter may need no change.

Recover earlier writing selectively. Compare its actual purpose and claims before restoring it; keep useful factual corrections, source links and artifact improvements. Do not revert the museum or replace every current manuscript with an older snapshot. The [first comparison](../iterations/2026-09-21-voice-reset.md) finds useful material to recover in Rhizome while retaining the stronger latest assembly and clock passages.

## Agreed clarification, 14 September 2026

Palle identifies the reader as someone who wants to understand the nature of code and space as a micropolitical order: the implementation helps establish what a body can do, where it can move and what confines it. Grounding this inquiry in a tutorial makes the work concrete. Retain the earlier discovery direction while strengthening the reader's ability to follow a program as it develops.

The working principle is: **stay with the operation long enough for both its usefulness and its assumptions to become perceptible.** A reader should leave with a capability they can use and a question they can return to. The question may unfold across several halls; each passage does not need a declared political conclusion.

### A program the reader can hold onto

Use a recognisable object, procedure or state across successive explanations. Name what is carried forward, which operation is newly introduced and what that addition enables. Returning to a familiar construction can teach something even when its visible result initially stays the same. A short code excerpt needs enough surrounding account for the reader to understand its inputs, state and consequence. Keep its connection to the active source and distinguish an excerpt from a complete program.

Physical instructions support the encounter. They should lead into an understanding of the operation: HOLD copies a sentence and its interpreted geometry; a stack saves state so a later branch can resume from a particular place. A list of buttons alone does not supply that understanding. Exhaustive setup, configuration and implementation inventories belong in technical.md; the conceptual operation belongs in final.md as well.

Give the reader room to predict and discover where that is productive. Direct definitions, diagrams and explanations can also provide a necessary foothold. The question/action/observation/code rhythm is flexible: do not withhold a term merely to manufacture mystery, or force every room into identical paragraphs. The voice remains a knowledgeable companion, with room for pleasure, strangeness and a changed purpose.

### The body is part of the inquiry

Establish enough of the encounter for someone away from the headset to imagine what happens. Learning through approaching, reaching, following, being stopped or finding support is part of the subject. Inspect the actual rule before claiming a bodily consequence. Visible geometry, collision, movement permissions and the visitor's physical conditions contribute different things.

Treat “code is law” as a proposition to investigate through an enforcement mechanism. Which condition constrains movement? Which body does it apply to? Who can change it? A simulation's order is assembled through authored code, engine behavior, hardware, interfaces and human decisions. Access to revising that order is uneven. A changed image alone does not prove changed access.

Let a specific consequence open the critical question: a collision boundary can confine, support or shelter; a sampling threshold can omit a tremor or make an address easier to hold. The reader needs the operation to investigate those different uses. Queer inquiry and the desire to make other worlds can guide which variations we pursue. Complexity, disorder and novelty do not settle their value in advance.

The tutorial also selects what receives attention. We need a path because time is finite; record important deferred questions so later capabilities can reopen them. Do not remove relevant works merely because the first encounter concentrates on one comparison.

### What we learn from The Nature of Code

Shiffman's [Vectors chapter](https://natureofcode.com/vectors/) returns to a bouncing ball while replacing separate position and speed variables with vectors. The initially familiar result gives the reader continuity while preparing a more composable program. The [Introduction](https://natureofcode.com/introduction/) also proposes an Ecosystem Project that develops across chapters, and describes a mixture of code excerpts, complete examples and open exercises.

The [Forces chapter](https://natureofcode.com/forces/) explicitly discusses choices and omissions in modelling, including applying wind without simulating the air or a reaction upon it. Ada can develop such acknowledged construction choices into sustained bodily inquiry. We should not imply that this reference book presents simulation as an unmediated copy of nature. This comparison is an editorial reading, not a claim of identical aims or prerequisites.

## Write the encounter

Begin with a particular body, relation or discrepancy: a bob crossing the same place in another direction; a mark that continues after the bob turns; a fold that changes a route. Establish enough of the scene for a reader without the headset to imagine it.

Give an action before its expected consequence when the reader can discover that consequence. Let a tentative explanation last long enough to be tested. Follow with the smallest actual code operation that makes the observation understandable. Identify excerpts and omissions; preserve the names and units needed to reproduce the comparison. Put exhaustive parameter inventories and derivations in technical.md.

Return to the encounter with a changed purpose. A coarse record can be useful for duration; a strobe can reveal repetition while concealing motion; an extra random draw can become a palette-making choice. The limitation is part of a working solution. Do not assume the finest, smoothest or most faithful representation is always the desirable one.

Critical inquiry should arise inside these choices. Which part of a gesture is admitted? Which direction counts as width? What does a shared seed hold equal, and what does it leave unrelated? What can the current controls change? These questions can open onto bodies, access, taste and agency without declaring that a small program proves or refutes a whole theory.

Keep desire available. A listener may want to return to a timbre before knowing its name; a visitor may follow an apparent passage even when its walls have no collision. The unfamiliar can invite a relation. Avoid making every anomaly a defect or every irregularity a symbol of freedom.

Let sentence rhythm vary with the encounter. The book needs continuity, not an identical paragraph recipe in every room. Do not finish every observation with a second sentence explaining its moral. A room may leave the reader a new use, an unresolved comparison or a question worth carrying ahead.

## Keep the account honest

The [encounter-status agreement](../ENCOUNTER_STATUS.md), accepted 17 September 2026, applies across the book: visitor instructions presented as current must refer to encounters placed and traversable in the build being documented. Keep implementation evidence, museum availability and accepted future text distinct. Mark approved passages awaiting placement visibly in the working draft; use their actions and observations as the design brief. Carry those markers into reading copies until the encounter has the required evidence.

An invitation to try is not a claim that a person has already tried. Keep runtime and listening status in field notes and the review. Do not invent a control, promise an exact drag result, or prescribe a subjective sound quality as the only correct answer. Describe enough of the implemented consequence for a reader on the page to follow it.

No pristine original is promised behind the implementation. Even a baseline has an envelope, a finish, a clock and a chosen starting condition. Explain the decisions that matter here; allow the reader to recognise that others remain for later.

Preserve primary artifact anchors, and align the book hero and line with the artifact the chapter actually teaches. A technically accurate source citation cannot establish that a passage has the right voice: read it aloud and ask where the reader gets to think.
