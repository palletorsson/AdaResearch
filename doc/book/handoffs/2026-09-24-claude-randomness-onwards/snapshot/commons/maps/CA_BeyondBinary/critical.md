# A body with no travelling address

The eye follows five cells as something that travels. The array stores no such something. It stores occupied and empty addresses, then another present. “Glider” is a useful recognition across those changes. It can help us predict an encounter without becoming an additional variable hidden inside each cell.

This is one place for the research question “what bodies are possible?” to land. A recognisable body can persist while the sites participating in it change. That does not establish that every body works this way. The museum gives us a particular construction whose conditions we can vary and whose code we can inspect.

The fixed witness complicates our attention. Following A loses the outline; following the outline loses some of the individual changes that sustain it. The lamp translates this selective attention into furniture. It receives only one address. It cannot tell whether occupancy belongs to a glider, a stationary block or something we have not named. The familiar figure is richer than that single signal, yet the lamp can still make the room respond.

LINK exposes the added relation. The lamp is not automatically part of Life's neighbourhood. Its glow supplies no feedback. We decided where to sample, what to light and when to count an onset. The gold line is an explanation of a connection implemented in code, not evidence that the automaton naturally wants a lamp. Such choices matter when we transfer a simulation into architecture, a garment or another agent.

MEET changes the glider's conditions without changing its rule. This prepared block is neither an enemy nor a moral lesson about collision. It is another configuration of the same cells. In the tested encounter, both recognisable arrangements disappear. Other beginnings require their own observations; we should not make this ending the meaning of encounter itself.

The queer invitation is to investigate which relations allow a body to continue, alter its role or cease to be recognisable. It does not require an organic silhouette. A square grid and two states can already unsettle a simple account of identity; their strict limits also leave much outside. Wanting another body can motivate a new neighbourhood, a state with memory, another coupling. None is automatically freer merely because it is more elaborate.

The floor preserves another decision. Its empty cells remain walkable. The earlier bridge treated occupancy as support above a basin. A value has no single architectural destiny: colour, collision and response are implemented separately. We can enjoy a beautiful patterned room and still ask which of its relations are actually doing something.

Finally, generation 48 is an observation budget. It does not establish extinction, equilibrium or an entropy threshold. The current experiment is held while the larger museum continues. What we leave unobserved remains a question for another return.
