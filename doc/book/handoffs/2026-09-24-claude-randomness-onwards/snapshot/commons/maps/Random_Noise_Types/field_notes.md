# Random_Noise_Types — field notes

## 2026-09-12 — N1: can randomly proposed points still keep their distance (Fable, the first hall of Astra's noise arc, after Random_Game; Astra's card: "build matched-volume uniform and minimum-distance clouds from explicit seeds; show requested, accepted and rejected/attempt counts; do not secretly reduce spacing to achieve the requested population; let a player bring two accepted points closer together and distinguish generation constraints from ongoing enforcement, with an easy local restore; keep the labels precise — minimum-distance sampling approximates a blue-noise-style distribution, it is not white versus pink audio noise; separate those secondary galleries or correct their context")

**The ruling.** `randompoints` had the algorithm and none of the means. Three modes (uniform, gaussian, dart-thrown minimum distance) drew off the global stream after `randomize()`, so no cloud could be seen twice; `apply_grid_config` was `pass`, so no map token could reach any of it; and the dart thrower dropped the points it could not place in silence — the packing limit was real and invisible, which is the one thing about this algorithm a room must not hide. So: one named five-digit seed through a single `_rf()` (null generator falls through to the global stream, so an unseeded placement draws exactly what it drew before), a written record of what the rule cost, and an opt-in `stand:compare` bench that puts the two rules side by side in matched volumes.

**One seed, two rules, one volume.** The bench holds two square slabs of the same size, drawn as twelve-edge frames so "matched" is visible rather than asserted, and the probe measures both frames rather than trusting the number. Left: every candidate kept. Right: the same volume under the minimum-distance rule. Each accepted point wears its excluded neighbourhood as a shell of HALF the rule's distance, which is the exact geometry — if no two centres are closer than d, shells of radius d/2 can touch and never overlap, so overlapping shells on the left are not a fault but what having no rule looks like. The refusals are not tidied away: they stay where they fell, as grey ghosts between the points, and there are more of them than there are points (39 refusals for 24 places, in 63 draws, at the shipped seed).

**A slab, not a cube.** The first build sampled a 0.9 m cube and photographed as fog: forty translucent shells deep, with every point printing its own world coordinates over itself (the point scene carries a `Label3D` for that). The volume is a 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.12 m slab standing upright now, so each cloud reads as a plate of circles and the touching-versus-overlapping contrast is legible from a standing eye; the coordinate labels are hidden and the readout carries the numbers. The shipped default stays a cube — this is the bench's own framing.

**The rule's distance is derived from the density, not typed in.** `#dist:0.13` is unusable: the grid reads `#key:number` as a rotation unless the key is in `CONFIG_PARAM_NAMES`, and `dist` is not there (`radius` is, and is accepted as the override). So the bench sets the distance to eight tenths of the mean spacing the requested population implies in the volume it was given. Fixing it against the density rather than in metres keeps the packing limit in view at any count, which is the whole reason the right-hand plate is interesting.

**The packing limit, on purpose.** The probe asks for four times the population and checks two things: that the shortfall appears (45 of 96 placed, 51 unplaceable, 220 refused) and that the closest pair is still not under the rule — the system does not quietly relax the distance to report a full count. Astra asked for the shortfall to be reported; it is on the plate, in words: `51 could not be placed at all: the volume ran out of room`.

**Birth, not enforcement.** The points are the project's pickable `grab_sphere` spheres, so a hand can carry one. Carried into a neighbour's shell, both shells turn red, the readout counts `moved 1 · under the distance 1 — the rule was applied at birth, not since`, and nothing pushes anything back. That is the answer to Astra's third item, and the honest one: the distance was a condition of admission and never a force. RESTORE puts every point back where it was generated, and it is a button rather than a law. In the live lane the desktop pointer's own grab ray finds a point at 1.1 m, the right button carries it, and the carry alone brings the gap to 0.07 m against a 0.13 m rule — no stand-in was needed.

**The bench faces the door, and the first live run stood behind it.** The token's `180` turns the bench's front — readout, captions, panel — toward the north door, so a visitor meets its face on the way in. The first live run stood the rig on the far side and aimed at its back: every press found nothing (`raycast_colliding: false`), and the captions in the first captures read mirrored for the same reason. The visitor's spot, the rig and the standing view are all on the front side now. The captions also moved from lying flat on the bench to standing up under their plates, which reads better either way.

**The galleries are separated, not corrected in place.** `WhiteNoiseGallery` and `NoiseColors3D` stay in their own row in the room's west half; the bench moved to the open east half at (10,3), four to six metres away, and the probe measures that distance. White and pink there name slopes of audio noise spectra, which is a statement about frequency; the plates are about a spatial relation between neighbours. The words travel between the two subjects and the mathematics does not, and the final says so in its closing rather than pretending the row is a continuation.

**Exactness decisions.** Twenty-four points, not forty: the slab reads at twenty-four and crowds at forty. The ghosts are 11 mm spheres, grey and unshaded — big enough to read as refusals, neutral enough not to editorialise. RULE cycles the right plate between the minimum-distance rule and the shipped gaussian, which crowds the middle and keeps no distance at all; the caption changes with it, so no plate ever claims a rule it is not under.

**Rejected.** A single volume with the two rules alternating in time (press to switch): it makes the comparison a memory test and there is no moment when both are in view. Rejected too: enforcing the distance after birth (a repulsion that pushes points apart), which would have made a prettier toy and destroyed the room's only real distinction.

**Pending.** A headset walk; a person carrying a point with their own hand rather than the desktop pointer's; Astra's review. The galleries' own internal claims (what each noise colour means) are untouched — they are somebody else's exhibit and only their relation to this bench is addressed here.
