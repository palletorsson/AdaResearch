# From encounter to revision

Recorded 15 October 2026 from the Random Definition work with Palle. This is the working method to resume, test and improve, not a claim that a particular chapter or room is finished.

Read this alongside [the two writing roles](WRITING_ROLES_AND_DECISIONS.md) and [the recursive book architecture](../RECURSIVE_ARCHITECTURE.md). Use the [cycle record](ENCOUNTER_CYCLE_TEMPLATE.md) for one question at a time. Keep records with the relevant experiment or dated iteration, rather than creating a parallel research archive.

## The principle

Let a concrete encounter make a question necessary. Give the reader enough situated description and inline explanation to investigate it. Change something small enough to compare, retain the evidence, and let the result revise the apparatus, writing or question.

A subject attracts possible artifacts and associations; it does not yet supply an order. A candidate gem is a consequential tension we can investigate: a relation that could make something happen differently in a simulation. “Randomness” is a subject. “The same events yield different distinctions when the clock changes” is a candidate. Its interest still has to be encountered. An unsuccessful or uninteresting comparison belongs in the record too.

## One cycle

1. **Resume from the actual state.** Read the hall's current tutorial, final, individual plans, saved wall and most recent evidence. Identify the implemented artifact and version. Distinguish a reader's words, a technical result and the writer's interpretation. Preserve the previous edition before substantial changes.
2. **Locate the encounter.** Describe what is visible or audible, what the visitor can do, and what persists or disappears. Check these claims against the implementation and, when available, an actual encounter. Source inspection does not prove VR legibility. Explain a new term briefly at its first useful appearance; do not explain it only through other unfamiliar terms.
3. **Name the tension and the next question.** State what is surprising, resistant or worth retaining. Specify what would become different if the rule, measurement or assignment changed. Keep QFEP, edges and leaks as possible connections supported by a particular consequence, not required labels or automatic evidence of significance.
4. **Propose a route.** Select from the research pool. For each transition, identify the attention carried from one encounter and the reason for the next. Try removing or swapping a stop: what discovery becomes unavailable? These are editorial hypotheses until a reader responds. Subject connections can remain many-to-many; walking order is a separate route. Random Definition currently uses one shared room/final order. Other halls need an explicit decision, not an inherited assumption.
5. **Make one comparison.** Establish a baseline with parameters, source, seed where applicable and relevant state. Change one condition while holding the others fixed. Record the predicted difference and what would count against it. When studying a recording procedure, replay the same retained event through two procedures; repeating a gesture produces a new event. A stored random outcome can be replayed without reproducing its physical acquisition.
6. **Read and encounter.** Offer the complete relevant text as well as the object. Before explaining the intended conclusion, ask what the participant notices, what the marks mean, what changed or stayed, and what they want to investigate next. Record their words and which draft/build they encountered. Keep author feedback, independent-reader feedback and hypothetical readings distinct. Do not turn an unexpected response into confirmation of the planned thesis.
7. **Preserve, reconstruct, revise.** Keep configuration and relevant state, replay the result, and ask for independent reconstruction from the tutorial where the experiment calls for it. Use the observation to propose or implement a further change. Review both texts: revise, retain with a reason, or leave an open question. Either text may initiate this cycle. Update the plan and affected wall notes; if shared order changes, change both room and final. End with a next comparison, a justified stopping point, or a clearly named dependency.

## What to judge separately

### A writer can be differently situated

For the [independent literary handoff](INDEPENDENT_LITERARY_HANDOFF.md), share verified encounter conditions without prescribing a literary argument. Three selected examples calibrate attention rather than impose a template. The writer produces one complete chapter; record a literary reading first, then a separate technical reading, and return both for revision. Facts remain constraints from the outset. Keep the dossier, interpretations and reader reports distinguishable; differences can initiate changes to the apparatus. A different model or chat is not by itself evidence of independent judgement. Palle's selection of cellular automata for the third example does not yet approve a passage.

### A writing orientation must have a consequence

The artifact-to-text relation is a **writing orientation**: a proposed way of attending to a supported action, material fact or limit. Author provenance can suggest the operation; it cannot establish that the operation worked. Keep implementation evidence, intended literary effect and observed reading effect as separate fields.

The [composition feedback and follow-up](../iterations/2026-10-15-composition-consequences/README.md) add two editorial tests. The **artifact test** asks whether a passage makes a particular observation, investigation or formal experience available that its reference did not. The **map test** asks whether that passage changes the significance of an identified encounter elsewhere in the sequence. The latter needs the complete chapter, not just isolated snippets. This literary artifact test is distinct from a technical instrument test.

Name the expected difference and a possible failure before reading; then record the response against its edition, reading order and setting. Empty observations remain empty. A preference reported in supplied criticism is useful editorial evidence, but is not automatically an independent participant trial. Repeating a sentence, preserving a graph edge or passing a code test does not pass either literary test.

Discard a candidate that supports neither test. Evidence on one test can justify a limited revision; evidence on both makes a stronger candidate without automatically adopting it. Retain the reference when a gain does not justify its cost. A deliberate recurrence can be valuable, and a locally good passage can still duplicate work already done elsewhere. Change order only in a separate comparison when the relation needs it; keep room and final aligned where that is the hall's rule.

The next supplied reading adds five concrete fields to each currently proposed writing orientation: **what changes in the reading; what becomes newly perceptible; which other artifact is affected; what the strategy risks; what can remain unspoken**. Keep the actual response beside these intentions. These fields are more useful here than a generic literary score.

Assign an operation where its effect is needed in this particular sequence, not permanently to an artifact or author. A relationship may belong in the map's metadata without becoming an explanatory transition. Let MOVE / KEEP disclose movement becoming a number when that action occurs; announcing it at the preceding machine can remove the discovery. The working principle from the supplied feedback is: “The map composes relationships. The artifact realizes them. The prose attends to what happens.” This is an editorial direction to test, not a claim that the current map has already achieved it.

Respect the reading's scope. A response to a passage may report a meaningful relationship to another artifact without completing the whole-chapter map test. Record both. Likewise printed recurrence can represent duration without establishing what waiting through the sound in VR feels like. Preserve the edition being discussed when the apparatus changes; later source checks must not silently replace its evidence.

### Evidence levels

| Check | Evidence sought | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Artifact | Instrument works; technical claims match its actual procedure. | That the encounter matters to someone. |
| Experiment cycle | Baseline, controlled change, comparison, preservation, replay, reconstruction and a further proposal are possible and recorded. | That all participants reached the same interpretation. |
| Cross-hall integration | Data, configuration or knowledge travels where the design specifies it. | That every hall must share data or repeat a structure. |
| Runtime and VR | Loading, stability, performance, reach, visibility and interaction checked separately. | Literary rhythm, curiosity or understanding. |
| Reader and research | An observable difference, a meaningful question or a reason to reconstruct the apparatus; ability to explain the needed concept. | A universal verdict or an ontology proved by one simulation. |

When comprehension falters, investigate the prose, display and underlying claim. More explanation is one possible revision. A better view, slower event, changed order or narrower claim may be more useful. Ask whether additional words reduce ambiguity without making the middle a catalogue. Follow the scope-review rules in the writing brief; do not remove a prerequisite merely to meet a word target.

## Length follows the encounter

**Palle's clarification, 16 October 2026:** the entropy chapters may be a little longer because they contain many artifacts. The usual 600–1,500 prose words is a guide, not a ceiling. Allow more space when it helps establish what is actually visible, explain a new concept briefly, follow a consequence, or make the need for the next encounter intelligible. Do not compress a necessary distinction merely to meet the earlier range.

The number and depth of productive encounters determine length; artifact count alone does not assign a paragraph quota. Give the strongest discoveries more time and keep repetitions brief. Judge density locally: does this passage let us see, understand or question something we could not before? A longer chapter can be easier to read if it gives those moments room. Keep room and final order aligned, and record where a transition still feels unearned. Word counts inform that reading check rather than deciding it.

## Random Definition: the worked case and its limits

The first pilot remains the 48-value seed bench. The present visitor route starts at returning colour windows and ends at the slot machine. Those are different kinds of order.

**Subsequent selection, 15 October:** Palle chose a six-encounter trial with a new moving-sphere opening and the RAND book retained as a main encounter. The selected sequence is moving spheres → RAND → lamps → Geiger → MOVE / KEEP → Out / Back. The previous thirteen-stop route above is its predecessor. All previous artifacts remain as main or optional encounters. The [six-encounter record](../iterations/2026-10-15-six-encounters/README.md) and current plans supersede the old route and word-count exception. Select the gems first; normally allow 600–1,500 prose words according to their depth, without filling a quota or requiring one gem per object.

Palle's full reading identified “keeping something while something else disappears” as a stronger continuity than a catalogue of random-number sources. The held RAND digit, timing record, sensor capture and gesture-derived seed became related acts of preservation. The carried sphere made a different limit bodily: direction and distance can vary while return stays compulsory.

The revisions corrected what a saved number page can establish, separated appearance/statistical properties/predictability, and described the actual movement encoding. Palle then requested a slower fall from the top and more situated prose. The live page now admits a row every 2.5 seconds; the final identifies visible marks and explains new concepts inline.

## Handoff

Leave links to the current pair and plans, the preceding version, the cycle record and evidence. State what changed, what was checked, what remains conjectural and the exact next action. Link the process from the book's re-entry page and writing brief. A blog may narrate the discovery, but the cycle record retains the limits and decisions needed to resume it.
