Palle: 'start with the seven kits'. Each kit is a small Godot 4.6 project under doc/book/studies/<kit>/ that a learner opens, a TUTORIAL.md that builds the chapter's body step by step, and a verify.gd that checks the chapter's claim headless. A kit STARTS FROM the previous kit's finished state (TUTORIAL_LINEAGE.md l.11: 'Each starting scene must visibly inherit the relevant parts of the preceding result'), so the seven are built in this order, each from the one before:

## Conventions (match the existing kits exactly: read doc/book/studies/point-lines/ and doc/book/studies/point-trace/ first)
- Files: project.godot (config/name = the chapter title's short form; main scene = the kit's scene; keep the renderer and input settings of the previous kit), base.tscn (inherited), the inherited scenes and scripts, the chapter's new scene + script(s), README.md (like point-trace's: what the kit is, how to open it, what is supplied vs built, the lineage line), TUTORIAL.md (the lesson), verify.gd (a headless SceneTree script: `godot --headless --path <kit> --xr-mode off --script res://verify.gd`), verification.json (written by verify.gd), .gitignore (copy point-trace's: .godot/, *.log ...). LF line endings. No .uid/.import files shipped (gitignored anyway).
- TUTORIAL.md voice: the existing kits' voice - plain, exact, short sentences, 'we', code first then the explanation, nothing claimed that was not run. Structure: a first paragraph that says what the previous chapter gave us and what we do now; sections that build the script in runnable steps (each step a complete code block the learner can paste, then 'Run it. ...' what you see, then why); the ARGUMENT section (the exported axis, 3-5 values, 'write your prediction before you render', the comparison, the claim); the DEBT paragraph after the making; the PLACE paragraph (stand on it / carry it / walk into it; desktop and headset); 'In your room' (the project step); what carries forward to the next chapter; a short 'Borrowed' list with the contract of each supplied part. One planted bug: a step that deliberately contains an error, with the exact Godot message (measured, from a real run) and the fix.
- The chapter's data (body, argument, debt, place, code_new, project_step, halls) is below. Build THAT body; keep the axis as an @export; the argument must be visible in a still.
- Godot: C:/Users/palle/Desktop/Godot_v4.6-stable_win64.exe (non-console: add --log-file <path> to read prints; --xr-mode off; wrap long runs in python tools/godot_watchdog.py --expect=<file> -- <args>). Headless draws no frames but ticks _process/_physics_process and physics. If the class cache is stale after adding scripts: `godot --headless --path <kit> --import` once. Run verify.gd until every check passes; then run the main scene headless for ~3 s (--quit-after 180) and confirm no SCRIPT ERROR. Record the checks in verification.json and verify.log.
- Do NOT edit: any earlier kit, commons/maps/**, doc/book/course/opening.json, curriculum.json, the encyclopedia. Create only the new kit folder (and a scratch folder under the session scratchpad for experiments). Do not stage or commit.
- Code ladder: the learner has met, in order: statements/output/errors; variables/properties/lifecycle; conditions/booleans/calling; vectors/expressions/xr; functions/parameters/return; arrays/loops/meshes; (ch07) surface_tool/normals/collision; (ch08) ai_assist/debugging/export_build; (ch13) transforms/basis/delta; (ch42) instancing/composition; (ch09) nested_loops/materials; (ch14) classes/export/types; (ch18) import_assets. A kit may use only ideas met at or before its chapter; if it needs more, supply it as a named borrowed part with a one-line contract.
- Rulings that bind: distance belongs to Point Lines; list before append; condition before threshold; grids are not prerequisites; no unexplained helper (STUDENT_TOOLBOX.md l.11); Melencolia has no compulsory new coding mechanism beyond import (ch18).


## ch07: Plane: when the line closes  ->  kit `point-plane` (from `point-trace`)

- verb: close | code_new: ['surface_tool', 'normals', 'collision'] | code_returns: ['arrays', 'loops', 'meshes', 'vectors']
- halls: Point_Triangle_Context (carrier), Primitives_Polythedra (field trip)  (read their tutorial.md and final.md for the museum reading; the kit is desktop, the hall is the encounter)
- BODY: Close the walk: the recorded points become a triangle fan through SurfaceTool, normals are generated, and a collider made from the mesh lets you stand on the plane you walked.
- ARGUMENT: axis winding and normals; values ['clockwise, flat normals', 'clockwise, smooth normals', 'counter-clockwise (reversed)', 'reversed, double-sided']; predict: 'clockwise flat' and 'reversed double-sided' will look most alike from above: both are visible and flat-lit. 'reversed' alone vanishes from the front.; claim: A surface has a side: what you see of a plane is a promise its winding and normals make (Godot winds front faces clockwise).
- DEBT: Culling (Gaboury, Image Objects, 'Culling Vision'): hidden-surface algorithms decide what exists for the eye; a one-sided plane is invisible from behind while its collider still stops you. The fan also fails on concave walks: the approximation owes the shape its dents.
- PLACE: Stand on your walk: the plane made from your own trace becomes floor; walk to its edge and look under it.
- GODOT: ['SurfaceTool PRIMITIVE_TRIANGLES, add_vertex(), generate_normals(), commit()', 'ArrayMesh', 'create_trimesh_shape() on a StaticBody3D', 'cull_mode', 'planted bug: vertices added counter-clockwise (the OpenGL habit): the plane is invisible from above and visible from below']
- IN YOUR ROOM: Your room so far: one plane you walked, three points and one line. Commit it.

## ch08: When things go wrong: the drafter is a machine  ->  kit `point-cube` (from `point-plane`)

- ARGUMENT: axis how much the prompt fixes; values ["vague ('make a cube')", "counted ('8 corners, 12 triangles')", "measured ('0.5 m, resting on the floor')", "located ('at the end of my walk, in Godot's clockwise winding')"]; predict: vague and counted will look most alike: both come back as a default 1 m cube at the origin, and both are likely inside out, because the model's idea of a cube comes from engines that wind counter-clockwise. - DEBT: Algorithmic enclosure and the default body: the assistant learned from other people's code and repeats its norms (a cube at the origin, a capsule player, OpenGL winding). ## ch13: The organism: bone, tendon, muscle  ->  kit `organism` (from `point-cube`)

- verb: articulate | code_new: ['transforms', 'basis', 'delta'] | code_returns: ['vectors', 'lifecycle', 'loops']
- halls: Trans_Rotation (carrier), Trans_Body (carrier), Trans_Translation (field trip), Trans_Pre (field trip)  (read their tutorial.md and final.md for the museum reading; the kit is desktop, the hall is the encounter)
- BODY: A limb of three segments as a parent-child chain with pivots, each joint turning by angle += speed * delta in _process: the course's first body that moves by itself. Beside it, Trans_Body's lattice loses cells where the limb passes.
- ARGUMENT: axis where the pivot sits on each segment (same joint angles); values ['at the end', 'at one third', 'at the centre', 'outside the segment']; predict: 'at the end' and 'at one third' will look most alike after 30 degrees: both bend like a finger. - DEBT: Klee maps active, medial and passive onto brain, tendon and bone, and onto male and female organs ('Male organs (anthers), active'). - PLACE: Become it: attach the chain to your hand (headset) or the carried point and walk it through Trans_Body's lattice, carving a passage with your own movement ('chip from chip').
- GODOT: ['the Node3D hierarchy, transform and basis', 'rotate_object_local()', 'pivots as parent nodes', '_process(delta)', 'to_global() and to_local()', 'planted bug: rotation applied to the mesh child instead of the pivot, so each segment spins on its own centre and the chain comes apart']
- IN YOUR ROOM: Give one body in your room a jointed part that moves.

## ch42: A body of parts: the first assemblage  ->  kit `body-of-parts` (from `organism`)

- verb: assemble | code_new: ['instancing', 'composition'] | code_returns: ['transforms', 'basis', 'properties', 'lifecycle', 'delta']
- halls: Trans_Introduction (carrier), Trans_Scale (carrier), Trans_Body (field trip), Trans_Pre (encounter)  (read their tutorial.md and final.md for the museum reading; the kit is desktop, the hall is the encounter)
- BODY: The parts you have made so far, saved as scenes and instanced into one body: the point (ch2), the cube (ch8), your plane (ch7) and the three-segment limb (ch13), each a PackedScene, each added as a child of one root Node3D with its own pivot. Move the root and every part follows; turn the limb and only the limb's children follow; scale the root and the whole body scales, cube and plane together. The scene tree is the first assemblage: a body is parts in relation, and the tree says which relation.
- ARGUMENT: axis how the same four parts are parented; values ['flat: four children of the root', 'a chain: each part the child of the one before', 'a chain with offset pivots', 'two chains from one hip']; predict: At rest, flat and chain look identical: parenting changes nothing you can see in a still. Write that down, then render the sheet one second after the root turns 30 degrees: flat keeps every part where it was relative to the root; the chain swings the last part furthest; offset pivots make the same chain bend at different places. The closest pair is flat and chain at rest; the farthest is chain and offset pivots in motion.; claim: An assemblage's structure is invisible at rest and decisive in motion: the tree, not the parts, says what the body does.
- DEBT: A hierarchy is a command chain. The root moves, the leaves obey, and nothing in the picture shows who commanded whom. Klee drew it as brain, muscle, bone, active to passive; Godot draws it as a tree whose root you chose. The debt is that the choice of root is a choice of who gets to be the body's centre, and the default (the first node you made) is not a neutral one.
- PLACE: Carry the root: pick the whole body up (XR hand or desktop carry) and the parts come with it, pivots and all. Then walk into Trans_Scale with it: the room scales by four, the body scales with the room, and the door you could not use before is usable; the body's relations survive the scale, which is what a hierarchy is for.
- GODOT: ['PackedScene and instantiate()', 'add_child, the owner and the scene tree', 'local versus global transform in a chain', 'scale propagates to children; shear if non-uniform', 'Node3D pivots by nesting an empty parent']
- IN YOUR ROOM: Your room's first assemblage: at least three parts of different origin (one made in code, one drawn by hand in the editor, one carried in from a classmate's scene) under one root. Decide the root and write one line in the README saying why that part commands.

## THE PROCESS STRIP (added 2026-10-01; binds kits 4-7: body-of-parts, chessboard, family, collage)

Palle supplied a slide of a body written as a chain of operations (primitive -> displace -> scale -> boolean -> material), one picture per step with the operation named under it. Every kit from here on ships that strip:

- Extend the sheet script (the sheet.gd pattern from point-plane and point-cube; windowed, under the watchdog; headless draws no frames) so that it also renders ONE STILL AFTER EACH OPERATION of the chapter body, in order, from one fixed camera, and composes them left to right into a single image, process.png, in the kit folder (tracked; keep it under about 300 KB: 5-7 tiles of 320 x 240), with the name of the operation under each tile.
- TUTORIAL.md gets a short section, The process, before In your room: the strip as one line (operation -> operation -> ...), the image, and one sentence saying which step changed the body most. The hand-in for In your room includes the learner strip.
- verify.gd checks that each step of the strip changes what it claims (the count of parts, vertices, triangles or children after each operation), so the strip is a tested sequence and not an illustration.
- course.json lists the steps as process for chapters that have one (ch42 and ch18 do). If the measured chain differs, ship the measured one and report the difference.
- The project has its own grammar of operations, MeshGrammar (commons/mesh_grammar/: a seed, rules, generations, and a snapshot of every generation). Kits 4-7 do not use it; the long-track kits for extrude (ch45) and displace (ch46) will. Do not copy it into a kit unless the chapter says so.

## THE DECISION AND THE FIELD NOTE (added 2026-10-01; binds kits 6-7: family, collage)

Palle's QFEP method handoff adds one step after the comparison: decide whether to follow the difference or to settle, and keep the reason. The template and its explanation are doc/book/course/opening/FIELD_NOTE.md; a worked note is doc/book/course/field_notes/2026-10-01-pyramid-family.md. Read both.

- The ARGUMENT section of TUTORIAL.md ends with a short 'Decide' paragraph: after the sheet, name one difference the learner could follow (say what the next intervention would be) and say that settling is equally right; ask for the decision and its reason in the field note. Do not decide for the learner.
- Ship the kit's own filled note as FIELD_NOTE.md in the kit folder: the question, the baseline, the intervention, the prediction as the outline stated it, what you measured, another cause you considered, your decision and reason, and the command that replays it. Keep what was observed, what you conclude and what you guess on separate lines. If the prediction was false, the note says so.
- Do not say entropy rose, or call a boundary a phase transition, unless a distribution and an estimator are stated.
