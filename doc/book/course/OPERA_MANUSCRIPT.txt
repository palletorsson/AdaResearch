ACT I
A PLACE, AND WHAT CAN STAND IN IT


PRIMITIVES

0. Point Zero
Light between the boards.
The count did not wait.
Three bodies still in the packing.
Named already. Here.

        What wakes the next one?

1. Point One
Here, a place with nothing in it.
Three points. One has a place. One appears. One stops a body.
Even the absence has a lamp.

2. Point Coordinates
A point in the hand goes where the hand goes.
Set it down. One point, two addresses.

3. Point Lines
Point to point. Let go. The link stays.
Two points in the dark, one in each hand. Joined: a line of light. The wall measures it. Reset, and both go home.

A blue plank on sawhorses says do not cross. The way round is longer.
A red line. Who gave it force?
Walk this line. Six metres, gate to gate. The floor measures every step aside.

Move one end. Let go. A thing a line can be falls on the table: a plank, a ruler, a warning.
Three rolls of tape. Fire, move, fire: a strip between two points. Strip beside strip, the line has a surface. Any wall will take it.

4. Point Trace
Through the glass the line leaves a gap.
Hold still.

A little wider.
A little wider.

Let go. The line goes on downhill.

5. Point Triangle Context
Three edges close round a gap. Again a place with nothing in it.
Draw across it. A face.
Two faces share one edge.

6. Primitives Polythedra
A fourth point. The faces close on an inside.
On a wedge, forward is a little higher.
On a pyramid the same slope turns a body away.

7. Point Animatedcube
One corner, two folds.
Six faces laid flat. Fold. A cube again.
Boxes in columns, all alike. One can be carried to the table.

8. Primitives Ignorance
Five ways for the corners to meet.
More around. Fewer up the side.
How smooth is enough from here?

9. Primitives Columns
One outline. More sides, another column.
Hollow, the column holds something.
Open at both ends, it lets a body through.

10. Primitives Profiles
A letter becomes a space.
The outline leads a ball.
A profile, turned. Pores, feet, bristles. One body, five rules.

11. Primitives Portals
This body has no way through.
Closer, and round enough is rounder.
A smaller gap is still a gap.

12. Primitives Melencolia
Four corners, and the gap between them is a place.
Did height make this the centre?
The tools are still here.

        Here first. Then a point, a line, a face, an inside.


TRANSFORMATION

13. Trans Pre
Still. Within reach.
Here, then there.
Turned. A face comes back.

14. Trans Introduction
From here to there the deck carries.
Same step. A handspan short.

15. Trans Translation
The same face travels and does not turn.
Two ways reach one place, each on its own permission.
Move the pivot, and the way bends somewhere else.

16. Trans Rotation
The wheel turns. A floor becomes a slope.
The shape stays. The foothold changes.
One place holds the turn.

17. Trans Carousel
One turn for all. Farther out, farther to go.
Neighbours, together, in opposite directions.

18. Trans Scale
The room grew, and the body is the measure.
A deck can grow across, or unfold across.
Grown together or grown alone. Which frame took the change?

19. Trans Body
Come near. An opening. Gone again behind.
Between the blocks there is room while a body is close.
One cut stays.

20. Trans Pit
An opening again. This one keeps its own time.
For a moment, enough room.
The block travels on. Learn the pause.

21. Trans Playground
A pause, a passage. One movement leads to the next.
Press. A worker on six legs carries a deck. Press again, and it waits, still loaded.

        (Here first. Then a point, a line, a face, an inside.)
        Moved, turned, grown, and still the same. The far side may have changed.


LOGIC

22. Logic Event
One movement asks the next.
Almost is still a distance.

One change.
Just enough to wake it.

        (Here first. Then a point, a line, a face, an inside.)
        Moved, turned, grown, and almost the same.
        Almost is a distance. One change can be enough.


ARRAYS

23. Array
An empty place again. This one has a number.
Moved, and still named.
A square goes missing. The address stays.
Behind this place, another place.

24. Tutorial 3D
Inside the address now. Count from within.
Same places. Another way to leave them open.
Three stones to a rod, and the rods are thin. Nothing sags. Is it weight?

25. Tutorial Tiling Floor
One tile. Another instruction, another field.
Two rhythms underfoot. The floor holds the same.

26. Tutorial Tiling Walls
Step back. The rule stays.
A different tile is not an opening.
Six addresses, another arrangement.

27. Tutorial Disco
The next moment has an address.
The clock comes to a cell and nothing sounds.
Two clocks meet, then drift apart.

        (Here first. Then a point, a line, a face, an inside.)
        (Moved, turned, grown, and almost the same.)
        (Almost is a distance. One change can be enough.)

        A silent cell is still a place.
