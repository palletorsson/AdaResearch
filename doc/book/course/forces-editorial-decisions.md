# Forces: keep what already works

8 October 2026. The author asked that good writing be allowed to steer the work.

Keep a passage when it makes the encounter perceptible, makes an accurate distinction, or gives the next lesson a useful inheritance. Repair a control name or example locally when that preserves the passage. A new artifact inventory is not a reason to replace a good argument. Archive displaced versions and name the reason for replacement.

Three decisions govern this revision:

- **Launch keeps its analytical flight.** Its distinction between a prediction and an impact is useful. The tutorial reconstructs one flight before returning to complementary angles.
- **Choreography keeps the phase rods as primary.** The earlier proposal to promote the conveyor would lose the strong comparison between prescribed repetition and a spring. The conveyor remains the physical experiment beside it; “Everything worked, and this happened” remains in the reflection.
- **The familiar point carries the physical lessons.** Motion, field, resistance, spring, attraction and impulse extend its behaviour. The cube returns when shape and contact matter. The player becomes the final receiver. This is continuity of construction and learned operations, not automatic transport of one physical instance between halls.

Motion’s final.md is now aligned with its current weighted spheres; the earlier version is archived. The other ten final.md files remain preserved. Several still describe earlier exhibit placements or interfaces. They are not all current operational instructions. Preserve their arguments while checking those references in a later reflective pass; do not silently transfer an old experiment’s measurements, solver or controls to a replacement artifact.

The revised tutorial.md files supply complete small reconstructions, their declared common files and independent variations. Production artifacts can use additional grips, damping, recovery and controls. Each tutorial names the relevant difference rather than claiming to reproduce the whole exhibit.

The author's accompanying “Fractal Thinking” note offers an editorial check across scales: **operation → artifact → hall → sequence → return to the operation**. Use this as a method, without claiming that every hierarchy is a mathematical fractal. A hierarchy alone also does not establish a recursive algorithm. Do not require each lesson to explain the research method.

Before promoting an observation into the larger story, ask:

- What happened, under which conditions, and can we observe it again?
- Does it change what the student can predict, make or understand?
- Does it change the room or the next lesson, or belong as an optional experiment here?
- Which claim is supported by the code and observed behaviour, and which is our interpretation?

For example, the spring's restoring rule belongs in the small reconstruction. Motion passing through the coupled row belongs in the room. Comparing that motion with prescribed phase belongs in the sequence. Any claim about control or unintended consequences in the reflection must return to those particular events. Preserve a surprise without automatically turning it into a new required subject.
