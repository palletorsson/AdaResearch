## One paragraph per hall

**Evolution.** Encounter the live drifting population and distinguish it from the authored static arrangements. Carry the Graph Theory PointBody as a run/pause control for a small population table. Track ancestry and energy before calling a change improvement. Keep explicit fitness-ranking creatures and flowers as comparisons. Carry the environment's consequential rule into Gradient Landscape, where the objective becomes an explicit surface.

**Gradient Landscape.** Carry Evolution's PointBody into parameter space: move it to choose the slope and intercept of a line fitted to five visible observations. Separate the observation diagram from the loss surface. Reconstruct mean squared error, Huber loss, a central-difference gradient and one descent step. Switch losses at the same parameters; reset before comparing descents. Change one fixed outlier and inspect the altered residuals. Test a large step that raises loss and a proposed step outside the display window that is refused. Keep optimizer races as supporting encounters. Carry the distinction between observations, parameters, measure and procedure into Classification.

**Classification.** Keep the observations while the familiar PointBody takes the role of a selected cluster centre. Reconstruct nearest-centre assignment and mean updates as two separate actions; predict a fixed point's membership before each reassignment. Compare K 2, 3 and 4 on fixed blobs, rings and a regular grid. Make tie and empty-group policies explicit, and distinguish current squared-distance SSE from the old exhibit's pre-update total distance. Moving the point clears obsolete assignments, and the unheld rigid body follows its centre on physics ticks. Group indices have no supplied meanings; preserve that distinction when Neural Networks and Perception introduce labels.

**Neural Networks.** Carry Classification's PointBody into a different role: it now selects an input while weights remain fixed. Reconstruct stable binary cross-entropy and the full chain rule, then check every derivative numerically and fit the reversed labels. Separate check positions were used while selecting a repeatable start; they are development cases, not an untouched evaluation set. Inspect the original exhibit's answer-bearing extra inputs before accepting its training story. Keep activations, parameters and supplied names distinct as Perception expands the complete model to six hand coordinates and three labels.

**Perception.** Continue the point as a pickable sphere in its museum bay. **Sequence Memory.** Make two movements whose mean pose could be the same but whose order differs. Retain timed samples for both hands, fit one whole-phrase prototype, then compare two taught phrases. Test reversal, one stationary hand and changed timing; allow unknown and ambiguous outcomes.
