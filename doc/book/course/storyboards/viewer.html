      The agreement

      The museum is already here.

      We walk through its existing rooms. The tutorial selects operations the student can understand, change and reuse. The reflective text follows its own critical and poetic questions. Other artifacts belong to the field without needing an exercise or a paragraph.

      The paragraph comes first. Encounter → what the learner brings → what they make or change → what they learn → what travels onward. Correct that choice before we write the full tutorial.

      Clear edges between the parts

      A position is not its visible sphere. A surface is not automatically a collider. A supplied hold is not an explanation of an XR player. Small teaching constructions keep direct references, with no hidden scene lookups. We name what the surrounding museum supplies.

      Some halls teach a new construction, some return to a tool with a new purpose, and some let making and reflection settle without introducing syntax. We do not teach every artifact or force every hall into the same amount of code.
