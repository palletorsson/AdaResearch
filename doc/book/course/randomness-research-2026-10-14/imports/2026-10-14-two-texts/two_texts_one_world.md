I think the distinction between `tutorial.md` and `final.md` is central to the architecture. In my previous description, I made the tutorial too much of a supporting document. It should be one of the two main ways through which the book produces knowledge.

They are not two descriptions of the same artifact. They are two different operations on the same world.

- Tutorial: Gives the reader the capacity to construct, understand, test and change the apparatus.
- Final: Gives the reader an encounter with the apparatus, develops its artistic and conceptual consequences, and imagines possibilities beyond its present implementation.

Both can generate new experiments. Both can be changed by the results.

## 1. Two texts, one experimental world

|                            | `tutorial.md`                               | `final.md`                                                    |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primary mode               | Construction and experimentation            | Encounter and interpretation                                  |
| Starting point             | What can we build and understand?           | What happens when we encounter this?                          |
| Knowledge                  | Code, principles, dependencies, methods     | History, aesthetics, philosophy, speculation                  |
| Reader's role              | Programmer, builder, experimenter           | Visitor, observer, participant, critic                        |
| Relationship to simulation | Makes mechanisms reproducible               | Proposes behaviours and possibilities to investigate          |
| Relationship to discovery  | Changes the implementation                  | Changes its meaning, purpose or future direction              |
| Success                    | The learner can reconstruct and investigate | The encounter opens a consequential way of thinking or acting |

The tutorial can become increasingly experimental without sacrificing its precision. And the final can become increasingly speculative without pretending that everything it imagines has been implemented.

Neither should be subordinate to the other.

## 3. A concrete example: Random Definition

Consider the existing seed bench.

TUTORIAL — THE KNOWN OPERATION

The student learns how a pseudorandom generator works, how to save a seed, how successive draws become RGB values, and why discarding one draw changes the picture.

They reconstruct a small 4×4 grid and verify that the same recorded conditions reproduce the arrangement.

FINAL — THE ENCOUNTER

The visitor discovers a colour relationship worth preserving. The writing asks why an apparently random result can become desirable, what the apparatus preserves, and who decided that colour was the only variable.

Then it imagines a grid that can leave its fixed positions and perhaps become wearable.

EXPERIMENT — THE UNKNOWN

The final's proposition becomes a simulation: what happens if the same recorded values determine spatial connections instead of colours?

AI can first text-simulate several interpretations, then help construct a Godot prototype.

OBSERVATION — THE CONSEQUENCE

Perhaps the connected grid behaves unexpectedly. Perhaps it becomes unstable, or a particular arrangement produces something we want to develop.

The observation is preserved with its source, parameters, implementation and unresolved questions.

REVISION — THE RETURN

The final may now describe an actual encounter instead of an imagined one.

The tutorial may acquire a new chapter about graph connections, constraints or deformable structures. The original version remains recoverable.

This is how the book can generate the next part of its own curriculum.

## 4. From explaining knowledge to exploring the unknown

I would also develop the distinction between tutorial and final across the course.

| Stage           | Tutorial develops                                           | Final develops                                                           |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Foundation      | Known mechanisms, runnable examples, precise reconstruction | Situated encounters, questions and conceptual distinctions               |
| Experimentation | Controlled variation, comparison, saved states, testing     | Unexpected consequences, ambiguity, alternative interpretations          |
| Exploration     | Construction of new experiments from acquired capacities    | Speculation that can challenge the current implementation                |
| Open research   | New methods, revised rules, uncertain results               | Discoveries, contradictions, learning debt and new artistic propositions |

This is not a rigid four-part progression. It is a change in emphasis.

In the early halls, the tutorial knows more than the student. In later halls, the tutorial can teach the student how to construct an experiment whose outcome neither the author nor the student knows.

That is a very different conception of a textbook.

And the final changes too. It need not always conclude with an authoritative interpretation. It may end with a question that becomes the starting point for another simulation.

## 5. How the book should be assembled

I would retain the existing hall structure:

Random Definition

`final.md`

Literary encounter and speculative research

`tutorial.md`

Reconstructable programming and experimental capacity

`map_data.json` + Godot scenes

Current implementation and spatial encounter

Linked experiment records

Propositions, text simulations, tests, observations and learning debt

These four parts form the working unit, but not every hall needs a new experiment, and not every experiment needs to become part of the published edition.

A published book could offer the final as the primary reading experience, with the tutorial accessible alongside it. The experimental records become a deeper research layer, permitting the reader to follow a proposition into its implementations and historical revisions.

The word final is therefore slightly misleading. It is final for a particular edition, not the final interpretation of the work.

## 6. The most important editorial principle

I would resist making the tutorial explain everything in the final, or requiring the final to justify every technical decision made in the tutorial.

Their difference is productive.

The tutorial might build a particle emitter using a Gaussian distribution, while the final notices that the resulting cloud resembles a particular kind of body or atmosphere. That resemblance might generate a new investigation involving boundaries, persistence or desire.

The tutorial could then change because of the final.

Conversely, an unexpected numerical error encountered during reconstruction might lead to a final text about the instability of the apparently smooth world.

The two texts need not agree completely at every moment, provided the apparatus distinguishes known behaviour from conjecture and preserves the reasons for their differences.

That disagreement can itself be productive material.

## 7. The larger idea: two kinds of learning

I think Ada is developing two complementary forms of learning:

Learning how something works. This is the tutorial's essential responsibility. It gives the reader operational knowledge: code, mathematics, procedures and the capacity to reconstruct a result.

Learning what something might become. This is where the final takes the lead, opening relationships, desires, histories and possibilities that may not have been anticipated by the existing implementation.

But these forms of learning continually exchange roles.

A technical reconstruction can reveal new artistic possibilities. A literary speculation can require new technical knowledge. An AI-generated proposal can introduce learning debt, which becomes a reason for both investigation and writing.

And this leads to a formulation I think could organize the whole book:

The tutorial teaches us how to construct a world. The final investigates what it means to inhabit that world and what other worlds it might permit. The experiment allows both texts to discover what neither knew before.

That third element—the experiment—is what transforms the book from a pairing of technical education and critical writing into a recursive artistic research apparatus.
