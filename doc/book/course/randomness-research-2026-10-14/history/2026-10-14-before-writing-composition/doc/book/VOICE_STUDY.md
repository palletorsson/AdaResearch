## What to learn from the novella

The folded boarding pass on page 9 gives one otherwise interchangeable day a difference. On page 25, making the scheduled bed would displace its occupant; the nurse leaves it unmade. The reader encounters an act and its circumstance before being asked to recognise a theory of freedom. Procedure supplies enough repetition for a small change to become legible.

Material details carry that difference. The warmth of thermal paper has a source. A fold has a width. A pencil mark can be too faint for a scanner. A form's missing field has consequences for what someone can report. This makes the critical question available through a particular object and action.

The prose also lets another narrator encounter an earlier trace. The reader's memory does some of the work of connecting them. Repetition accumulates a changed relation, rather than merely repeating an assertion. The later gathering of the five voices makes agency relational: an act can become available to someone else.

There are limits to the transfer. Repeated clipped sentences, negations and declarations of protocol could become a mannerism across 190 rooms. Sometimes the novella explains the meaning of an image after the image has already made it available. Ada should vary its rhythm and leave room for an inference to stand. Its tutorial also owes the reader a causal account that fiction can leave mysterious. The mechanism should become clear enough to reproduce or change, even when its significance remains open.

## Earlier voice proposal (not accepted)

A companion who knows the instrument, stays close to the reader's hand, and is willing to reconsider what the instrument is for. Warmth comes through attention. Humour can come from a precise discrepancy: the sign has issued an instruction without acquiring any way to notice whether it was followed.

The sentences should be clear enough to act on and specific enough to resist a ready-made moral. A rounded number, an occupied bed and a missing form field do different work. Naming their mechanism lets an interpretation have consequences.

Use **you** for a possible action, prediction or comparison. Do not prescribe the reader's feelings or declare that an experience has transformed them. Use **we** when sharing an explicit decision or operation, with space for disagreement. Let the author take responsibility for a chosen frame, threshold or comparison instead of attributing every choice to the computer.

Offer a small uncertainty and an action that can make a difference to it. Give the observation enough space to be noticed. Then introduce the code or concept when it helps account for that observation. Withhold the verdict long enough for the reader to participate; supply the operating knowledge when it is needed.

Let an exact detail earn its place. Five millimetres matters if a hand can explore the threshold. A capacity of 4096 matters if the reader is examining what spends a place in the record. Numbers should not merely decorate a paragraph with the appearance of authority.

Allow sentences to change pace. A short instruction can interrupt a longer account of what changes over time. A return to an earlier phrase can acquire another referent. Code can provide a repeated operation, a condition, a changed interval or a bounded memory. One small executable rule can give a passage its rhythm without being called a poem in the passage itself.

Keep strangeness attached to an operation. A point needs visible geometry before a hand can find it. A trace connects positions that were recorded at different moments. A useful approximation can offer a new way to draw. The reader can discover beauty in what it enables as well as investigate what it fails to retain.

Freedom can become something the reader practises: noticing a rule, learning how it acts, giving the operation another purpose, and considering the consequences for another person or task. Following the written variation should leave space to formulate a variation the author did not supply.

Give each room a local cadence. The clock can accumulate; a line can extend and return; a trace can interrupt itself; a comparison can alternate. Reuse an experimental method without repeating the same paragraph pattern or ending every room with the word *remainder*.

## Trial passage: Point One's opening

You arrive late.

The number is already changing. It continues while you look around the room. When you return to it, try to place your arrival somewhere in the count.

Watch the increments. Does an increase of one tell you how much time passed between frames?

The display begins with this reading:

```gdscript
var frames: int = int(Engine.get_process_frames())
```

It counts process frames since the engine started. Your arrival has no separate entry in that number. A count of one more frame also leaves the interval between frames unspecified. To compare those intervals, we would need another reading.

Find the dark point and pick it up. Move it a little, then return it as nearly as you can to where it was. Its appearance may change as you handle it. Follow the position through the change.

A mathematical point has no surface. This one has been given a visible sphere and a body a hand can acquire. We have made a location available to touch, and touching has brought us more than a location.

```gdscript
var p := Vector3(1.0, 0.5, 0.0)
```

Three components, used here as a position. There is nowhere in this value to put the sphere's radius. Nor does it tell us what should happen when you release it.

Those decisions can be made separately. Before following the coordinates to the wall, choose something about the point's visible body you would change while keeping its position. Consider how that change might make it easier or harder for someone to find.

## Trial passage: a turn within Point Trace

Take the pen whose readout says 80 mm. Draw a slow loop, then try a smaller one inside it. Watch the retained-point count. Find a movement your hand can make while the count stays where it is.

Hold still for a breath. The machine has time to do other things. Does the pen give that breath another place in its line?

This pen considers a new reading when movement reaches its threshold, rounds the position to its recording grid, and leaves out a rounded position identical to the last one. The threshold is five millimetres; the grid you chose is eighty. They make different decisions about the same movement.

Take a finer pen and try the small loop again. Compare the places where each record turns. Their segments connect the positions they kept; they cannot tell you every route your hand took between them.

Now use the coarse pen for a different purpose. Try keeping one stored address steady while your hand moves around it. Find the edge where the address changes. What was too coarse for the little loop may be useful for holding a place.

Choose which difference you want the next drawing to retain. A smaller grid could help with one difference. A timestamp could help with another. Write down your choice before changing the recorder.

Later in the encounter, when the reader investigates capacity, the existing rule can enter:

```gdscript
if _trail_points.size() > trail_max_points:
    _trail_points.pop_front()
```

This excerpt removes the earliest position when the list exceeds its capacity; the surrounding implementation also maintains sample times. The current capacity is 4096 accepted points. A proposed smaller-capacity version would make the departure of the beginning easier to watch. The choice of what to retain could then become a drawing decision.

## What would make these drafts better

Read each passage aloud while following its actual encounter. Check whether the instruction arrives before the answer, whether the explanation removes a useful obstacle to understanding, and whether a question permits more than the author's preferred response. Cut an abstract sentence if a nearby operation already lets the reader infer it. Retain explanations necessary to distinguish mechanisms or reproduce the result.

For the online tutorial and compiled book, use the same operative prose and code. The online version can offer the experiment and deeper source access. The printed version must still establish the setup, action, evidence and distinction in its own sentences. Neither should require an unseen effect to carry the argument.
