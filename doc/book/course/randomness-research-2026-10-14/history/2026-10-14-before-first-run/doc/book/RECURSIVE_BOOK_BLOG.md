# The architecture of a book that can simulate itself

Draft · 14 October 2026. The linked companion records exist; the proposed height comparison has not run in Godot.

The Ada Research book brings passages into relation with computational artifacts, programming lessons and records of observation. A reader can follow its literary sequence without installing the museum. They can also reconstruct a selected mechanism or follow an unfinished question into the research behind the chapter.

Two texts participate in making that research. The tutorial gives the reader means to construct, understand and alter an apparatus. The final follows what it is to encounter that apparatus and what else it might permit. A difficulty in reconstruction can open a new literary question. An imagined encounter can require a new lesson. Both texts can initiate experiments, and either can change because of what happens.

The book and museum develop through each other. A passage can describe something that has not yet been built. An AI collaborator can offer several readings of that sentence, expose the assumptions each would require, and trace what might happen if those rules operated. A subsequent Godot experiment can make one interpretation executable. The imagined sequence remains a hypothesis until there is something running to compare it with.

Consider the colour bench. Three successive values become the red, green and blue amounts of a cube. The values carry no instruction to become colour. We can keep the same forty-eight values and ask what heights they might describe. A small experiment can hold the data fixed while changing its assignment. We would then have something precise to inspect: what the code changed, what the visitor noticed, and what the original sentence failed to anticipate.

The sleeve is a larger question. A corner of the grid might be drawn towards a wrist while its neighbours follow. But what joins them? A visible line, a rigid connection and a deformable constraint offer different answers. One may look connected and fall apart. Another may hold together without being wearable. The distance between these readings is material for both programming and writing.

What happens must be allowed to return to the page. An unstable constraint may demand another implementation. An encounter may make a confident sentence inadequate. A result may leave two interpretations unresolved. We preserve the proposition, the chosen assumptions, the run and its limits, and the later decision. Their differences are part of the work's memory.

The return asks something of both texts without requiring them to agree or change together. A tutorial may need repair while the final keeps its question open. A literary reading may change while the mechanism remains the same. Some passages will never become experiments. Their place in the book depends on the work they do for a reader.

This gives the book two relations to time. An edition offers a particular route through the material. Its chapters and images remain identifiable after the software changes. The research continues around it: unfinished experiments, rejected versions and competing accounts can lead elsewhere. A later edition can show what it has learned without pretending the earlier one already knew it.

Authorship also extends across time. People, programs and AI systems can contribute material before the initiating artist understands all its implications. The artist may return as a reader of something they did not personally compose. Learning debt makes that situation available for investigation. It asks what has entered the work, whose contribution is known, what remains uncertain and how another encounter might change that understanding.

The readable book need not display this history after every sentence. A companion record can hold it behind the reading. There, a reader can move from a passage to a proposed experiment, from an observation to its source, or from a revised paragraph to the question that prompted it.

The book can therefore help produce knowledge that was unavailable when its writing began. Its sentences can initiate experiments; their consequences can redirect its future work. For this to happen, proposition, simulation, execution, observation and interpretation must remain distinguishable. A plausible account of software cannot stand in for an encounter with it.

The next step is small: one retained set of values, two explicit interpretations, an actual comparison and a passage willing to change.
