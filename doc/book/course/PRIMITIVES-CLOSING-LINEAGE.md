# Primitives: construction, choice and geometric desire

3 October 2026. Working agreement following Palle's column gallery and Gamwell reading.

The end of Primitives should leave the student able to make a form and locate their own decisions within its construction. It should also leave the reader curious about why anyone wanted that form. Technical competence and cultural interpretation continue together, with different kinds of evidence.

Palle's next clarification locates the central divide earlier: between an intelligible form and a material construction, and the hope invested in closing that gap. A mathematical definition can be exact when its rendered representation is finite. Geometry need not fulfil every desire we attach to it. The movement from Plato toward Melencolia is our interpretive connection, not a claim that Dürer illustrated this one argument. Display and aesthetic judgment enter within that larger tension.

This is an order of encounters in Ada. It is not a historical progression from naive geometry to enlightened doubt, nor a claim that the artists discussed share one intention. The ending retains pleasure, making and the wish to continue.

## One column before a gallery

The column tutorial is now an executable companion inside Ignorance: `commons/maps/Primitives_Ignorance/tutorial-column.md` and the starter's `primitive_column.tscn`. The existing octahedron function lesson remains its prerequisite. The [closing tutorial review](../iterations/2026-10-03-primitives-closing-tutorials/index.html) presents both new studies and their runtime evidence.

1. Describe one outline as radii at successive heights. Introduce the profile explicitly; the learner should be able to point from a data entry to its place on the body.
2. Use a supplied, inspectable ring-and-face builder. Reuse lists, loops and triangles. Geometry is created in code; no `$` references to prepared scene objects.
3. Change one profile entry while height and sampling stay fixed. Observe where the body changes.
4. Change full height while retaining its widths. Then restore height and vary radial resolution separately. This reduced outline joins radii with straight intervals; adding entries also redistributes their heights, so it is not an independent smoothness control.
5. Retain an outline of your own. Melencolia then teaches whole-body placement with three identical pyramids from the museum's court; composing a pair of related columns is an available return.

The student owns the profile, position-generating function, dimension arguments and sampling choices. The prepared world, materials, light, input and mesh-builder internals remain supplied. The room has collision; the lesson column does not. The current production lathe uses curved sections and sine; importing that whole implementation into the first exercise would introduce too much at once. The reduced lesson names this difference and supplies an inspectable circle sampler. Portals reuses that sampler; neither lesson claims to teach its trigonometric derivation.

Keep both gallery arrangements. `varied_forms = false` holds profile and height fixed for a controlled resolution comparison. The default varied composition explores what those choices can become together. This is currently a scene setting, not an in-world switch. The full room is built but has not been inserted into the museum spine. Decide its physical connection before promoting its proposed passage into a map's `final.md`.

## Distinguish the decisions

| Claim | What can support it |
|---|---|
| A figure has a stated mathematical property | A definition and mathematical argument. |
| The program creates particular vertices, faces or behaviour | Source inspection and appropriate runtime evidence. Numerical representation has its own limits. |
| A model meets a requirement | A declared purpose, measurement and tolerance. |
| A proportion or arrangement is compelling | Situated aesthetic judgment, with room for disagreement. |
| An artist intended or influenced something | Specific historical evidence. Similar form alone is insufficient. |
| This room invites a reading of reverence, control or release | A clearly situated interpretation of the encounter. |

These are boundaries for the writing, not headings to repeat in every chapter. A typed function can specify the values it accepts and returns; that alone proves neither geometric regularity nor artistic value. Likewise, a cultural association cannot establish an algorithm's behaviour.
