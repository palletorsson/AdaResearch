ENDLESS MUSEUM — THE POINT GAINS POWER
Thirty ideas / ten comic plates / sixty frames
Concept illustrations for the existing proposal. Images are schematic; these are not museum captures, measured simulations or evidence of implemented behavior.
Each row reads left to right: setup, then reveal. Grey ghosts represent other recorded positions. Diagram scales and panel perspectives are illustrative, not measurements.

01. The arrow you can carry
core / Vectors_Act1_WhatAVectorIs
Translate both endpoints by the same offset. Their displacement stays unchanged. The ghost and solid pairs on the right compare the relation; the two comic panels are not a scale drawing.
Learn: Displacement; Reference frame; Magnitude; Direction normalization (revisited)

02. Aim without changing strength
practice / Vectors_Act1_WhatAVectorIs
Separate direction from magnitude by normalizing a nonzero displacement. Handle coincident endpoints separately.
Learn: Normalization; Direction versus magnitude; Zero-vector policy

03. Two moves deliver one point
core / VFM_02_Operations
Place two displacements head-to-tail. Their sum reaches the same endpoint as the direct displacement; this is a geometric construction.
Learn: Vector addition; Head-to-tail construction; Compatible quantities

04. Same arrival, different journey
practice / VFM_02_Operations
Retain both routes between the same endpoints. Travelled path length can differ while total displacement agrees.
Learn: Displacement versus path length; Ordered samples

05. The target moved
practice / Vectors_Act2_VectorArithmetic
Keep the source point fixed and move the destination. Recalculate the relation from the new target position.
Learn: Vector subtraction; Relative position

06. The rail keeps only part
core / Vectors_Act2_VectorArithmetic
Project onto the rail. Preserve the perpendicular remainder instead of pretending the rail describes the whole displacement.
Learn: Target minus source; Signed dot product; Projection; Perpendicular remainder

07. The receiver is looking elsewhere
practice / Vectors_Act2_VectorArithmetic
Compare the receiver's facing direction with a target direction. This picture tests direction only; distance and obstruction need separate rules.
Learn: Normalized dot product; Angular threshold; Selection rule

08. Where you push will matter
optional extension / Vectors_Act2_VectorArithmetic
Preview torque with the lever arm and force direction. A turning-moment diagram comes before any claim of a simulated hinge.
Learn: Cross product; Lever arm; Torque direction

09. The cup across the room
core / VFM_05_Launch
The dashed prediction assumes uninterrupted flight. Contact with the hoop changes velocity and therefore the remaining journey; translucent samples show successive positions.
Learn: Initial velocity; Constant acceleration; Trajectory assumptions

10. Your body follows the arc
practice / VFM_05_Launch
A launch can reach the platform without bringing horizontal velocity to zero. The receiving surface and recovery route matter.
Learn: Launch state; Ballistic motion; Landing velocity

11. Let go of the command
core / VFM_03_Motion
End the applied push and observe the motion that remains. The room still determines friction, drag and later contacts.
Learn: Force; Mass and acceleration; Retained velocity; Engine integration

12. Same push, different body
practice / VFM_03_Motion
Compare equal force over equal time from rest, using different TOTAL moving masses. If a trolley is used, include it in the mass; drawn arrows are schematic.
Learn: F=ma; Controlled comparison; Mass versus visual size

13. Two points can carry you
practice / VFM_03_Motion
Hold a sphere in each hand. Aim down/back and press to thrust up/forward; release stops thrust, while momentum and gravity continue.
Learn: Vector sum of forces; Sustained thrust; Counter-thrust

14. Same arrows, different journeys
core / Vectors_Act3_FieldsAndMotion
Use the same direction samples as a velocity instruction or as an acceleration instruction. Equal-time positions reveal equal steps or increasing steps.
Learn: Field sampling; Assignment versus accumulation; State update

15. The drain becomes an orbit
practice / Vectors_Act3_FieldsAndMotion
Combine an inward component with a tangential component. Circulation is a candidate outcome; stable orbit is not guaranteed.
Learn: Radial and tangential vectors; Force superposition; Incoming velocity

16. The tray refuses to move
practice / Vectors_Act4a_KineticForces
Compare a resisted push with movement after the resistance is overcome. Overshoot depends on continuing input and the selected friction model.
Learn: Static threshold; Sliding friction; Normal contact

17. The room takes speed away
core / Vectors_Act4a_KineticForces
Launch identical bodies equally and change drag. Resistance opposes velocity; the paths and remaining speeds diverge.
Learn: Velocity-dependent drag; Coefficient; Mass response

18. Slide, roll or tip?
optional extension / Vectors_Act4a_KineticForces
Compare a constrained orientation with free rotation on an incline. Sliding and tipping depend on geometry, friction and the initial state.
Learn: Angular freedom; Friction; Shape and contact

19. Where the pipe lets go
practice / Vectors_Act4a_KineticForces
The channel constrains the point until its exit. Outside, velocity initially follows the outlet tangent; gravity then curves the trajectory.
Learn: Contact constraint; Tangential departure; Gravity after release

20. The point returns
core / Vectors_Act4b_Oscillation
Displace the point from equilibrium and release. With low damping it overshoots; other damping values may return without crossing.
Learn: Restoring force; Rest length; Damping; Equilibrium

21. The second spring answers
practice / Vectors_Act4b_Oscillation
Give two spring-mounted points room to collide. Contact transfers motion and each body's own spring responds.
Learn: Coupled consequences; Contact transfer; Dynamic anchors

22. A pole becomes your bridge
optional extension / Vectors_Act4b_Oscillation
Release a hinged pole of sufficient length onto a receiving support. A precise success makes a bridge; failure is not required.
Learn: Hinge axis; Limits; Torque and support

23. Two cycles try to meet
core / Change_Choreography
Observe a periodic release and a periodic receiver together. Small timing differences accumulate until an earlier alignment is missed.
Learn: Phase; Period; Relative rate

24. The conveyor remembers the delay
practice / Change_Choreography
A paused outlet produces a queue. Reopening at the same average rate as the input preserves the backlog; higher outflow is needed to clear it.
Learn: Input/output rate; Accumulation; State through time

25. The point retrieves more than you asked
core / VFM_07_Gravity
Apply attraction to every eligible body in range. The doorstop can answer alongside the intended point, changing the doorway.
Learn: Pair forces; Receiver eligibility; Equal and opposite response

26. Three bodies, two beginnings
optional extension / VFM_07_Gravity
Compare two three-body trials with a declared small initial difference. Keep trajectories for inspection; the illustration alone does not establish numerical accuracy or chaos.
Learn: Pair accumulation; Initial conditions; Sensitivity versus integration error

27. Take away one support
core / VFM_09_Legs
Remove a support from a loaded arrangement. The remaining contacts and centre of mass determine whether it holds or becomes a ramp.
Learn: Support region; Mass projection; Static balance limits

28. The dog must brace before it fires
optional extension / VFM_09_Legs
Proposed extension: connect permission to fire to the robot's supported stance. A supplied animated gait must be distinguished from physical balance.
Learn: Support versus commanded pose; Stateful action; Optional recoil

29. Make a passage with one shot
core / VFM_08_Arena
Keep the catalyst in hand and emit a separate projectile. Pushing a cube can open one passage and obstruct another; the cube remains in the world.
Learn: Eligibility and occlusion; Interaction states; Impulse received once

30. Deliver it through the room you changed
core / Vectors_Act5_ForceAsPlace
Reuse the pole, displaced cubes and other consequences to deliver the continuing point. Persistence between halls must be implemented explicitly.
Learn: Field membership; Receiver adapters; Composition and transfer
