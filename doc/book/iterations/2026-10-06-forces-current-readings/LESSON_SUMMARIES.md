# Forces — proposed lesson summaries

Snapshot of the execution plan on 6 October 2026. These are proposals, not completed learner programs.

## 1. Vectors_Act1_WhatAVectorIs

Return to two endpoints and calculate their difference in world coordinates. Shift both together, predict the unchanged displacement and inspect what XY and XZ projections omit. Compare short, long and zero vectors; explain normalization and its small-length guard before using it independently. Projections and readouts are supplied. Carry direction and magnitude with a declared frame, keeping the measured vector distinct from the display's scale.

## 2. VFM_02_Operations

Add two displacements, then compare travelling their two legs with taking the direct result. Swap their order and predict the same destination but another corner; cancel with the opposite vector and explain a nonzero journey with zero displacement. Carriage and drawing are supplied. Carry separate endpoint and path-length accounts, with the instantaneous reset explicitly excluded from the reported trip.

## 3. Vectors_Act2_VectorArithmetic

Project a measured gap onto a supplied unit rail using a dot product. Construct the retained component and subtract it to obtain a remainder, then recombine both to check the original. Predict rail reversal and movement perpendicular to the rail. Controls and diagrams are supplied. Carry a signed along-axis measurement, its omitted component and the requirement that the receiving axis be normalized.

## 4. VFM_05_Launch

Return to the familiar sphere as a flight marker, keeping its visual recipe. Drive it with the supplied constant-acceleration position formula and change one initial condition. Predict an apex or landing comparison; distinguish slower playback from different gravity. Angle conversion and duration are supplied. Carry the position-at-time function and its no-drag assumptions. This marker is prescribed motion; the next lesson introduces a physical receiver without redesigning the sphere.

## 5. VFM_03_Motion

Keep the familiar sphere's geometry and appearance, but explicitly replace frozen pickup behaviour with the declared dynamic-body setup. Author a finite force pulse and predict velocity change from force, mass and duration. Compare two masses with drag off before contact; COAST ends the command without erasing motion. The engine supplies integration and collision. Carry the sphere and force schedule, with its changed physical responsibility explained once.

## 6. Vectors_Act3_FieldsAndMotion

Give a small numerical receiver position and velocity state. Compare assigning velocity from a sampled field with adding acceleration to retained velocity, then advance position using the new velocity. Predict two uniform-field steps before trying a sink. The runner, samples and drawing are supplied. Carry this explicit update convention and unit conversions; reversing an influence need not immediately reverse an already moving receiver.

## 7. Vectors_Act4a_KineticForces

Write one linear resistance function, force equals minus coefficient times velocity, and send it through the supplied rigid-body adapter. Predict initial acceleration for two masses at equal speed and coefficient before comparing runs. Keep coefficient units explicit and let the engine integrate. Carry a reusable drag term into Oscillation; walking resistance, mower work and prescribed circular motion remain separate comparisons rather than extra required implementations.

## 8. Vectors_Act4b_Oscillation

Adapt the numerical position/velocity study to one axis. Sum restoring force and the familiar drag term, update velocity from force and mass, then update displacement. Predict the force direction and explain why the body can cross zero displacement with continuing velocity. Rail, handle and timestep runner are supplied. Carry a spring-damper calculation, distinct from the earlier animation that prescribed a sine-shaped position.

## 9. Change_Choreography

Return to timed angles and introduce period and phase through the rods. In the paired-dial study, halve the excess rate difference while retaining the period, and predict the gap after thirty seconds. SAME RATE should stop further drift without closing the existing gap. Displays are supplied. Carry a quantitative distinction between editing a stored angle and editing the rate that continues to change it.

## 10. VFM_07_Gravity

Use a supplied softened attraction law to calculate one pair's force. Apply opposite contributions to two receivers, dividing each by its own mass, and predict their unequal accelerations before integration. The pair loop and stepper are supplied. Compare a responsive source with an explicitly held one. Carry paired contributions and the omitted-response question; many-pair cost is a bounded introduction to computational work, not an optimization course.

## 11. VFM_09_Legs

Predict whether adjacent or diagonal support pads hold the same centred load, then release and compare. State the assumptions about pad positions, contact and load before interpreting a fall. Hull construction and the physics solver are supplied. Carry a testable support hypothesis, distinguishing the commanded walker from a plate whose movement responds to contact; this comparison does not require rebuilding either complete mechanism.

## 12. VFM_08_Arena

Hold positions fixed and insert a screen, then predict which target remains eligible before feeding begins. Trace a small eligibility condition separately from the selected target and completed-meal history. Sight testing, selection machinery and controller are supplied. Carry the distinction between an available action, a recorded event and an attributed intention, ready to use the same discipline when later agents appear purposeful.

## 13. Vectors_Act5_ForceAsPlace

Compose the existing field controls and predict what zero acceleration does to a moving cube. Compare this with an immediate stop and with the visitor's separately implemented receiver. Membership, damping and contact handling are supplied. Carry a receiver-aware account of a place's influence: the same apparent region can change different bodies differently, and leaving an influence does not necessarily erase motion already acquired.
