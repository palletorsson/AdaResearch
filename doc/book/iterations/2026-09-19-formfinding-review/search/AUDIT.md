## Source findings and manuscript corrections

### Annealing is a hybrid cabinet

`algorithms/optimization/simulatedannealing/simulated_annealing_visualization.gd:1415` accepts a downhill proposal directly and otherwise samples `exp(-delta_energy/max(T,0.0001))`. The proposal remains random (`:1475`), including when cold; it never becomes the gradient rule from Descent. The uphill-probability histogram and the all-proposal acceptance percentage describe different populations (`:712`, `:888`).

The default schedule is adaptive (`:1504`): high recent acceptance reduces the cooling factor, and low acceptance raises it, within 0.8–0.999. The slider sets `initial_temperature` (`:940` onward); RESET applies that temperature (`:1333`), while proposal width already depends on the setting during a run. The run stops at temperature <=0.1 or 1,000 steps. Thus the manuscript's 0.002-temperature example is a counterfactual below the default stopping temperature, not a usual observed phase.

`steps_since_improvement` resets on any downhill proposal, not only a new global best. After 80 consecutive non-downhill proposals, the separately enabled hop branch can relocate unconditionally and reheat to at least 30% of initial temperature (`:1468`, `:1535`). HOP invokes that separate mechanism. The corrected prose preserves the willingness to accept worsening without claiming every escape is annealing acceptance.

### Q compares deliberately different receivers

`commons/artifacts/max_q_basin_room/max_q_basin_room.gd:22` already specifies a five-metre open tank, deck 1.05 m, depths 0.95/0.30 m and a 0.28 m cube. These are source-local defaults, not the final installed dimensions. The retained manuscript's seven-metre overhead plate and old dimensions were stale before this pass. The original actual-museum baseline placed it at y=0, not overhead; its 270-degree front was blocked by an outer wall. Do not treat the frozen review's elevation account as a current runtime finding. In the final museum, the 4.9 local-metre tank is displayed at 0.5 scale: side length 2.45 m, rim 0.525 m, depths 0.475/0.15 m, cube 0.14 m. Root's final prose should avoid quoting the source dimensions as installed measurements.

The cube is static mesh geometry (`:140`); the other receiver is a 3×3×3 Verlet lattice with gravity, damping, floor constraints and periodic random displacement (`:150`, `:244`). No shared Q objective is evaluated. The drawn shallow bowl and `_alive_floor_h` even use different radial constructions (`:79`, `:216`); the latter also adds a centre nudge when clamped. These conditions remain unchanged, now stated as part of the interpretation rather than offered as evidence distinguishing living from dead matter. The final manuscript caveat must remain.

### Forge's authored detector is real, with precise limits

The registered `commons/primitives/balance/balance_puzzle.tscn:7` overrides script defaults: **12 pieces, 0.6 m threshold, 1.5 s stability dwell**, not the review bundle's eight/0.4. `balance_puzzle.gd:297` computes maximum piece height; `:323` checks every piece's linear/angular speeds (0.05/0.1). Motion decays accumulated dwell at twice elapsed time; falling below height clears it. It does not recognize contact topology or prove a coherent stack is on the platform. Transformation then invokes an authored walker animation (`:361`, `:448`). The manuscript correctly treats the walker as acknowledgment and protects a useful low arrangement's refusal; keep that argument.

`gradient_descent_optimizer.gd:69` samples an authored scalar objective with a finite-difference gradient (`:76`), not a neural network or physical soft body. The original deterministic start reached `(0.4,-0.4)` and remained there for 2,500 updates because its nonzero raw gradient could never satisfy the only stop. The new explicit BOUNDARY ending repairs that bounded failure; it must not be called convergence.
