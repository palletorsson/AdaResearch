## The cast

17×17 dance floor · step_sequencer · playhead · dark_sphere

## The walk

The array becomes a **dance floor.** Seventeen by seventeen, big enough to get lost in — every tile an address (row, column, state) that lights up when you step on it. Near the entrance the step sequencer is a 2D grid of toggles: each column a beat, each row a sound, a playhead sweeping left to right reading indices and firing samples. You stop reading the grid and start *inhabiting* it — 289 cells holding pattern, position, and light at once, and a rhythm you assemble by switching cells on. This is the chapter's gate: cross the floor, work the sequencer, and you have array literacy in your body.

## The turn (critical)

Scale is the argument. A 4×4 grid is a puzzle you solve; a 17×17 grid is an **environment you're inside** — and getting lost in a data structure is the experiential proof that arrays are spaces, not containers, the claim the whole chapter has been building toward now large enough to swallow you. The step sequencer lands the final move: it adds *time*. Until now the array held where; the playhead makes it hold *when*, turning the grid from spatial to spatiotemporal and quietly handing the baton forward to Wavefunctions, where temporal pattern becomes everything. The disco metaphor is exact rather than cute — a dance floor is precisely a grid where bodies, position, and time intersect, and music is what an array sounds like when you run a playhead across it. The chapter that began with a single carpet tile ends with a room full of people moving through addressable space in time. That is what an array was always for.
