# Collective Knowledge: change a piece without enlarging the pile

20 September 2026. Hall: `SpeculativeComputation_Collective_Knowledge`.

The chapter already separated contributions from contributors and asked whether accumulation amounts to knowledge. Its existing machine erased source identity from the landed pieces and offered no way to reconsider one. This pass makes those comparisons available while retaining the manuscript's caution about an automated model.

## Findings and decisions

Twelve generated source nodes send packets into a centrally composed helix. They are program-selected positions, not people choosing to collaborate. Originally every landed piece took its colour from placement order. Now each record retains a source index, and COLOUR switches between the original order gradient and source colours without moving any geometry. PIECE holds flow, selects a landed piece and draws a line back to its numbered source. Source identity remains recoverable without relying on colour alone.

FLOW pauses packet motion and new launches together. The hall's launch interval is 0.7 seconds. Its full-size table brings the sculpture and six controls into view; passive placements retain a compact automatic exhibit. An interactive form holds at sixty pieces. VERSION clears the form and pending packets, keeps one previous version in memory, and waits for FLOW.

The old cumulative `_contributors` counter remains, now labelled PARTICIPATIONS. It counts a source once per version and can exceed twelve. SOURCES counts distinct nodes in this version; EVER counts distinct nodes across versions. PIECES counts current pieces and ADDITIONS counts arrivals across versions. REVISIONS counts a different operation. Rebuild starts a fresh session; these totals are not persistent saved data.

DISPUTE toggles a red frame around the selected piece. REVISE changes that existing piece's orientation and height, retaining its source, original pose, immediately previous pose and revision number. The intervention is named VISITOR. Neither the piece count nor the addition count increases. Revision leaves the dispute in place: changing something is not an automatic settlement.

The records and previous version are bounded. Outlines use one MultiMesh, and dynamic readouts avoid an accumulating baked-texture cache. Rebuilding frees only owned nodes, preserving the museum's attached children. Interactive material separates source colours clearly; the original white emission washed out that comparison in the first readable capture.
