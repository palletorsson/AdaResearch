## The clock correction changes the experiment

Changing renewal alone would have left another rendering dependency: each call previously chose a new random turn for every creature. More rendered frames meant more turns. The revised artifact therefore advances its entire simulation on a fixed clock and updates the display afterward. It consumes elapsed time; it does not discard time to preserve a preferred outcome.

At twenty simulated seconds, schedules delivering 30, 60, 90 or 144 updates per second now produce identical positions, energies, births, deaths, visited cells and random-generator state. A deliberately uneven schedule gives the same state. The boundary test checks no renewal at tick 119 and exactly one cleared cell at tick 120, including headless execution.

This is a version change, not proof that the earlier extinction time remains true. Evolution's reflection now labels the 25 September curve as archived evidence from the old implementation. Its 35.8-second endpoint was not regenerated or assigned to the corrected model. The former script is retained under this report's before/ directory. The small tutorial model still uses its own four-second per-cell regrowth rule; the two experiments have not been collapsed into one.
