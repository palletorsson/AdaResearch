Use gap 0.1 for the 0.8/0.9 contraction example, then 1/512 for a nearly coincident beginning. The default expanding pair rejoins at step nine. Its history still distinguishes the earlier paths. Pair separation is not monotonic and is not itself an entropy estimate.

The wall independently enumerates words from 1,024 equally likely midpoint starts. Eight-symbol record entropy is 1 bit under halving and 8 under doubling; longer doubling records cap at 10 bits. For the ideal doubling map under uniform continuous starting measure, the half-interval partition reveals successive binary digits. Its one-bit-per-step result does not follow from two leaves or an indefinitely extended finite computer run. See Albert Fisher's [dynamical-systems notes, sections 4.2–4.4](https://www.ime.usp.br/~afisher/ps/notes20230808.pdf) for the map and symbolic representation. The local finite enumeration is checked directly.

## Try one guess

Four normal-height tables hold sixteen half-scale chairs each. Their small size keeps all chances together in view. A has 8 pink/8 black; B has 8 pink/4 black/4 black with arms; C has 12 pink/4 black; D has sixteen pink.

Choose a category, then DRAW. A gold ring marks the result; the chair remains available, so selection is with replacement. CHANGE ONE on A–C converts a black chair to pink. RESET restores the starting inventory and seed 435. D's chairs can be lifted, turned and placed back on its tabletop; no arrangement changes their colour prediction.

A and B have the same best chance, 50%, and the same min-entropy, 1 bit. Their Shannon readings differ: 1 and 1.5 bits over chair type. Merging B's black categories to record colour alone restores Shannon H to 1. C has best chance 75%, Hmin ≈0.415 and Shannon H ≈0.811; D is certain, both readings zero. The [NIST definition](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/min_entropy) supplies the largest-probability formula.

Each table uses its own private seed-435 generator. Readings describe the stipulated uniform-ID category distribution. They are not estimates from a few observed draws, and they do not certify cryptographic unpredictability: knowledge of generator state can reveal the next selection. Matched draw counts across tables select matched IDs with potentially different categories.
