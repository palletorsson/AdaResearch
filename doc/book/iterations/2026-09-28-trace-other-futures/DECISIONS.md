# A tool has other futures

The author's note brings hauntology into the project's account of entropy as difference: things carry earlier purposes while their connections can sustain other uses. Tinkering makes some of these possibilities workable. This is now part of the book at three scales.

- **Trace:** one short passage at the existing transfer of a drawing into Grid, with a footnote. The recorded gesture can acquire another task. The room's experiments, code and ending remain.
- **Preface:** the kitchen appliance entering a laboratory and the game camera entering a scanner give this question a material beginning.
- **Afterword:** a new section develops inheritance, connections, requisite variety and the resources needed to change a use. It asks who can propose a different task and who inherits its consequences.

## The connection and its limits

Hauntology's relation to inheritance and the absent opens the question; the application to technical capacities is Ada's proposal. Ashby's law describes regulation within specified conditions. Tinkering can change those conditions and purposes, requiring another argument. The existing distinction between measured entropy and the project's ethical account of difference remains in place.

The examples have been made specific: Kinect is a game camera, and the microwave is equipment within a laboratory. Neither example establishes that any device can become anything. No laboratory procedure or device modification is proposed here.

## What the room already does

Releasing a drawing dot or stick with at least two retained positions sends a copy onward. Point_Line_Grid receives that record and displays enlarged and rounded versions. It does not automatically make the drawing into a walkable structure. The board image, ball trail, head trace and desk page remain in Trace; the new passage does not change that distinction.
