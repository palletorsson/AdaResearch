# Voice studies across three maps

Three short comparisons test whether the voice developed with Random Definition can help other map texts. My provisional answer is yes, especially where instructions crowd an encounter. Several existing passages already have the qualities we want. A shared “we” is useful here because it can stay beside a particular action; changing the pronoun alone would achieve little.

The trials retain visible operations, introduce short definitions where needed, and give a consequence time to become a question. Each uses a different movement: cloth challenges an apparent cause; the rings separate a value from its appearance; the bridge separates visibility, support and recovery. They do not all borrow the randomness chapter's concern with keeping records.

These are proposed passages, not adopted chapters or reports of observed visitors. The original chapters, tutorials, room order and writing walls remain unchanged. Source checks cover the authored maps, current sequence neighbours, generated museum placements, scenes and active scripts. No new runtime or headset trial was performed.

## What I would carry forward

Noise is the clearest candidate for a lighter final passage. Its current text is technically rich, but the local comparison can carry the definition without an immediate code block. The tutorial remains the place to reconstruct the rule; later final prose can still explore the limits of its display.

Cloth is a modest shift in pacing. Its existing experiment already earns its argument. The original wall encounter adds something the trial leaves aside: architecture can leave the cloth's pins untouched while changing where a person can pass. A complete revision should keep that difference.

The bridge is the least certain improvement. Its original sentence about seeing the same address from a little lower down is already scientifically precise and poetically effective. The trial may make the mechanism easier to understand at the cost of explaining the discovery too early. Keeping the original would be a useful result.

Point Trace and the pendulum chapter also contain strong encounter-led writing. They are reasons to revise selectively. Today's strategy should increase the range of the book, rather than make every hall sound like the sixth randomness trial.

## How to judge the samples

Read each version before opening its editorial note. Then describe what is attended to, what changed in the apparatus, and what seems worth trying next. Confusion, indifference or a desire to stay with the current object are useful responses. Check whether the text earns the next action without prescribing a feeling.

For a later trial, reverse the reading order with another reader and keep their responses separately from these editorial expectations. Before adopting a complete chapter, restore its incoming and outgoing links and compare it with the actual room experience. No numerical score or reader preference is claimed here.
