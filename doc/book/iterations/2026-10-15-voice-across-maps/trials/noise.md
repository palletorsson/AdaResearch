Two rings stand upright on the bench. One has a folded surface. The other keeps its round outline while colour changes across it. We can watch them together without yet knowing what they share.

FREEZE holds their local clock. Pale markers pick out matching places on the two rings; SAMPLE moves the markers together. The plate gives the value found at that address in a numerical field—a rule that supplies a number for each position.

A negative value makes the relief turn inward. On the colour ring, that same sign leads to black. The two appearances have a common beginning, but something different happens to the number on its way to each surface.

There is room for many depths of a hollow. Under this colour rule, every negative value receives zero brightness. A little below zero or far below it, the result is black. The plate can distinguish values that this part of the image lets us confuse.

We press REMAP. The field stays held. So does the relief. On the colour ring, differences within the dark region can become visible.

The new rule fits values from minus one to one into brightness from zero to one. Zero, previously black, is now halfway along that scale. We have changed the route from number to appearance; the numbers themselves have not changed.

We can return to the first rule and let the dark patch close again. Beside it, the hollow keeps its shape. Next, AMPLITUDE lets us change how far the relief moves while the field stays held. The agreement between these two rings is numerical. What each makes available to the eye can still be changed.
