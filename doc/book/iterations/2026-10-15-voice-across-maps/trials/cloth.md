Eight narrow strips hang from the gantry. Their edges overlap; from the front, they almost make a single curtain. Below the beam, the long surfaces settle and sway. Along the top, a straight edge stays still.

We press SUPPORT. The gantry disappears.

For a moment, the missing beam is the most conspicuous thing in the room. The cloth goes on hanging. Its upper edge remains straight across the empty space. Whatever the beam explained to the eye, removing it has not released the strips.

Each strip is held at eight points along its top row. These are pins in the simulation: positions required to stay fixed while the rest of the surface responds to gravity. They were chosen from the cloth's original coordinates. Taking away the visible frame leaves those requirements in place.

PINS changes the arrangement. Only the two upper corners of each strip remain held. Between them, the other six points can descend. A dip opens in what had been a straight edge. The cloth has the same material settings; its shape begins to answer a different set of attachments.

With RELEASE, the corners let go too. Now the upper edge can follow the rest of the strip towards the floor.

The two disappearances have different consequences. Removing the beam left the hanging intact. Removing the pins lets it fall. We had been looking at them together, and the visible structure had offered a persuasive account of what was holding.

REBUILD hangs a fresh rank in the same place. Before changing its stiffness, we have another place to look: the points at which it is permitted to move.
