Concept: Recursion takes root in organic form — the recursive tree as the archetype of branching growth. Stochastic variation introduces randomness into the recursive rule, so that no two trees are identical even though they share the same generative grammar.
Sequence role: Second map in the Fractals sequence (10th spine, lambda_edge phase). Bridges from Fractals_1's geometric recursion to biological pattern by replacing cubes with branches. The inverted_tree_cloud foreshadows the reflection motifs in Fractals_11; the mobius_world introduces topological recursion (a surface that is its own mirror). Still beginner level — the tree is intuitively graspable before formal dimension theory arrives; leads to Fractals_3.
Technical angle: L-system-style branching with angle, length ratio, and depth parameters; stochastic variation by adding random perturbation to branch angle and length at each recursive call. The small_subdivision_cube maintains the cube-recursion thread from Fractals_1, showing that geometric and organic recursion coexist. The cube_desk applies recursive structure to functional design.
Critical angle: Trees grow without blueprints — no central plan, no global coordinate system, just a local rule applied at every branching point. The stochastic tree is the first encounter with the idea that randomness and structure are not opposites: noise within recursion produces naturalism, not chaos. This is growth without memory, form without intention — the lambda_edge principle that rules at the boundary generate the richest structures.
Key artifacts: inverted_tree_cloud shows recursive branching reflected into cloud form; mobius_world introduces topological self-reference; cube_desk and small_subdivision_cube maintain the geometric recursion thread; dark_sphere anchors.
Gap: Needs a parameter-space explorer for the stochastic tree — sliders for branch angle variance, length ratio variance, and depth — so the learner can map the transition from deterministic tree to stochastic forest.
