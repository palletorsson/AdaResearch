# Room to gather — 2 October 2026

A native Godot companion for Colour / Color_Walls, informed by Liam Gillick's screens, platforms and contingent architectural supports. Author approved the twelve-panel comparison after discussing the relation of space, arrays and assemblage. It is not a reconstruction of a particular artwork.

## What was made

- Twelve persistent, individually numbered framed panels, 2.32 × 2.52 m, each with its own transparent material and solid collider.
- Three arrangements: a 4 × 3 canopy at 3.25 m; twelve staggered upright screens; six overhead and six upright around an open centre.
- Three independent colour rules: alternating four colours; groups of three consecutive array indices; one pink.
- A fixed gallery, lights, two benches, ceiling suspension rails, comparison cameras and a desktop walking body.
- Two separate registers: [the short encounter](READING.md) and [the making score](MAKING.md). A portable Godot ZIP uses the production sources.

The nine-state photograph comparison preserves its camera and lighting. The three additional views are at 1.65 m eye height from the entrance. All images are native Godot viewport captures. No museum installation is implied: registry flags are `map_ready: false` and `include_in_map_data: false`. Existing halls, final.md files and sequence order are not edited by this experiment.

## Placement and lineage

Primary proposed home: Color_Walls, where coloured surfaces already distinguish view from passage. Earlier returns: Color_Context_Placed (colour grouping versus placement), Color_Pillar (an arrangement makes room and occupies it), Array (index and repetition), Transformation (position and orientation). This is an application of those capabilities, not a new compulsory lesson or another hall.

The experiment separates three decisions: the reader follows a frame through a changed room; the visitor meets actual collision and gaps; the student changes one colour-index expression, then optionally one position. Neither the chapter nor its learner score must explain the complete gallery renderer.

## Reference and interpretation

- [Liam Gillick and Suzanne Cotter, Somewhere Specific in Mind](https://liamgillick.info/Somewhere-Specific-in-Mind-Liam-Gillick-and-Suzanne-Cotter): the artist's discussion of secondary structures, context, production and occupation.
- [Kerlin Gallery: Liam Gillick](https://www.kerlingallery.com/artists/liam-gillick): modular structures, Plexiglas and aluminium, and the visual language of managed environments.
- [Artist's own index](https://liamgillick.info/): further works and texts, not a geometric plan for this scene.

The four RGB colours are our palette. They are not asserted as measured Plexiglas or RAL matches. Array indexing is our computational reading, not a claim about Gillick's production method. The study supplies no simulation of collective discussion, institutional permission or social equality.

## Evidence

Run `python tools/capture_colour_assembly.py` from the repository root. It copies only these scene sources into a private temporary project, imports them with Godot 4.6, runs the probe and writes figures, logs, hashes, verification and ZIP here. It does not connect to or stop user game sessions.

Native checks cover twelve persistent identities, independently wired arrangement/colour buttons, unchanged colours when placement changes, unchanged transforms when colour changes, collision rays, scripted standing-capsule traversal, the occupied-area arrangement guard, view switching and reset. Image saving is counted separately in the report by its `saved` prefix. The walker reaches the open centre under the canopy and in Gathering; Screens stops the same straight path. These are scripted collision tests, not physical-keyboard, headset or learner acceptance tests.

Rendering uses Compatibility/OpenGL, two-sided alpha compositing and ordinary frame shadows. The ceiling's bright surface depicts daylight; a fixed directional light supplies it. There is no coloured-light transmission, ray-traced glass, simulated panel weight or automated accessibility certification. Layout changes are discrete, so the viewer refuses them while its walking body occupies the panel area.

See [native verification](figures/verification.json) and [source provenance](figures/provenance.json). All 33 behaviour checks and 13 image saves pass. The [publication report](publication-verification.json) checks links, comparison mappings, source hashes, ZIP contents and exact HTTP responses. Run the iteration's `verify.py` to repeat these checks.

Browser automation failed before initialization with the environment's sandbox-helper ACL error. There is no rendered-browser interaction claim. The source page is script-free; native images were visually inspected, and the local server delivers the page, images, Markdown, reports and ZIP byte-for-byte.
