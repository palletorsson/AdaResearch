# Room to gather

Twelve sheets of colour wait above the floor. Pink repeats along one side; blue holds the other. Beneath them, the room has acquired another ceiling, low enough to feel as though it belongs to the people standing here.

Keep the colours. Change the arrangement.

The sheets stand up. What was above you is now in your way. A bench remains visible through yellow, then through blue. Your eyes can reach it before your body can. You have to find a gap.

Each frame carries a small number. Follow one as the room changes again. Six sheets return overhead; six stand around an open centre. Nothing has been added to the collection. There is somewhere else to stand.

Now leave the arrangement alone. Give three consecutive frames the same colour, then the next three another. Across a gap, two sheets begin to read together. A neighbouring sheet joins a different company. Colour has made another set of relations inside the one made by distance.

Make them all pink. The differences of height and angle persist. So do the interruptions to your walk.

Liam Gillick's screens and platforms bring the language of the managed interior into this encounter: the meeting area, the partition, the place prepared for something to happen. These familiar structures can make us comfortable before we have asked what their comfort arranges.

The benches still sit at the sides. The centre is open. We have called this arrangement *Gathering*, but nobody has gathered yet. Who would move a chair here? Who could move a panel?

The array has a place for every sheet. For a moment, leave the place between them without an assignment.
