A line acquires

Keep where the hand went. Change what follows it. A thin line becomes a ribbon, a wash, a broken brushstroke—and a drawing begins to occupy the room.

A new standalone Godot study. Every illustration below is a native render of the working code. The studies have not yet been placed in the museum.

01 / Path → section → surface

What is added to a line?

The recorded positions do not say how wide the stroke should be, which way its face should turn, or whether another stroke can be seen through it. Those are additional decisions. Here we can change them without redrawing the path.

A turning ribbon: its section makes one full turn along the retained path.

The section is carried along the gesture using a transported frame. It does not turn to face the viewer. Even a ribbon with no added twist can bend through space when its path does.

02 / A body depends on the view

The stroke disappears.
Its shadow stays.

This comparison uses one planar path and one ribbon. Only the camera moves. From the front it has a broad face; exactly edge-on it has no visible width. The light still reaches that face from another direction, so the shadow remains.

“Visible”, “has a surface” and “occupies a solid volume” name different properties. Walking around one small construction can make that distinction felt before it is taught.

03 / A gap in the image

Same triangles.
Different permission to see.

These three coloured strokes have separate depths. The loaded stroke, wash and dry brush use identical vertex positions. Switching the surface changes the overlaps and apparent openings.

Wash: colours combine through alpha blending. This is not a simulation of mixed pigment.

In this separated-depth example, reversing the creation order produced an identical image from the same camera. That result does not solve transparency for arbitrary intersecting ribbons; those can still expose sorting problems.

A shader opening is also different from the hole carried out of an O or B in the glyph studies. The glyph construction changes the boundary of the mesh. The dry brush changes which fragments are drawn. This gallery has no collision shapes to confuse with either one.

04 / Stored gesture ≠ rendered detail

How much of a movement
does the mesh keep?

The recorded path is retained. A second step samples it by distance before building the surface. Making that interval larger reduces the mesh; it does not delete the original record. Returning to fine detail can therefore recover this drawing.

A bend that was never recorded cannot be recovered this way. Resampling the retained path and reconstructing a lost movement are different tasks. The study also caps rendered samples for long drawings; it announces that reduction and keeps the recorded positions.

05 / The picture leaves its preferred view

A face held together
by where we stand.

The face uses eight authored strokes at different depths. Eyes, nose and outline are separate pieces. This is a composed test drawing, not a captured hand gesture or a reconstruction of the Mona Lisa.

Turning ribbons, near the front. The separate strokes read together as a face.

The drawing becomes both image and arrangement. Its preferred view is a choice we can inspect by moving. The narrow line, broad face and translucent wash offer different ways to hold that arrangement together.

06 / Make a gesture of your own

The same experiment
can begin with your hand.

In Godot, choose a stand with 1–6, press C, then Shift + drag to draw. Release to lift the pen. Shift + wheel moves the drawing plane in depth. Ordinary dragging orbits around the result. Every stand updates from the same drawing.

Use [ / ] for width, , / . for twist, - / = for opacity and S for sampling. X changes the next colour. Z undoes a stroke or an example switch. F5 / F9 save and load positions and colours locally.
