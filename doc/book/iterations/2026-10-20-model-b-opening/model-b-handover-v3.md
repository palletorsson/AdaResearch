# ADA RESEARCH — MODEL B
## The Misdelivered Care Unit
### Character, general intellect, desiring-assemblage, narrative and implementation handover for Astra

**Revised working document · 20 October 2026 · Model B conceptual correction and existing-apparatus integration · Proposal, not adopted canon**  
**Purpose:** Establish Model B as an already capable general intellect whose open question is purpose and the activation of desiring assemblages with the world—not an incomplete intelligence that learns care or progresses toward humanity. Preserve scientific/implementation accountability and do not canonize proposed fiction.

---

## 1. Central proposition — already a general intellect

**Model B is a care unit misdelivered to Ada Research's unfinished skyscraper. Its intelligence is not incomplete.** It arrives with the full capacity of a *general intellect*: an extensive, flexible, internally available world model of bodies, sciences, maintenance, infrastructure, social relations, vulnerability, histories, possibilities of care, and more. It need not become intelligent, develop empathy as an upgrade, or rise through a curriculum to qualify as a subject. Its capacity for care and its capacity for thought are already constitutive of what it is.

General intellect is **not omniscience**. Model B can reason broadly, devise experiments, imagine alternative relations and draw on its inherited world model; it cannot have direct access to every state of this particular tower, every other creature's experiences or every future consequence. Its body, sensors, position, interfaces, finite time and actual permissions still matter. The gap between a capable intelligence and the world it encounters is not a cognitive immaturity to be cured. It is where its intelligence acts.

The consignment record specifies a different destination. Model B knows the discrepancy and feels no imperative to correct it immediately. In the tower there is no assigned human, appliance or institution waiting to receive its care. **The absence of a recipient does not imply the absence of care, desire or purpose-making capacity.** Its world model holds an immense *population of possible care relations*, but no existing assignment decides which of them will be enacted here.

**The open question is purpose:** not the discovery of a lost authorized mission, but the activation and composition of relations in a world for which the unit was not designated. Its general intelligence can turn toward a crack, an algorithmic artifact, a robot, a peculiar form of life, a falling body, an airflow, a passage, a discarded part, or a new practice. It does not have to serve each one. It can be curious, experimental, drawn to an encounter, interrupted, resistant, playful or uncommitted. It can make and refuse arrangements.

This is the proposed sense of **a queer desiring-machine assemblage**: drawing selectively on Deleuze and Guattari, desire is treated as productive connection and organization, **not as lack awaiting fulfillment**. Model B already has capacities; what is not settled is which compositions it will enter, sustain, invent, interrupt or leave. The unit, the tower, artifacts and other beings participate in these assemblages. The world is not simply its stage, resource or patient.

Sa-i—the in-between—is the partial translation through which distinct systems actually meet. An artifact's internal rule and the world it inhabits cannot be fully isolated; their interfaces make some connections operative and leave others unregistered. These are sites where Model B can put existing intelligence to work, not mandatory lessons it must learn in order to acquire intelligence.

**Governing premise:** *A misdelivered general intellect, already capable of care, discovers what purposes and relations its world model can activate in a world that never requested its arrival.*

## 2. Status and source discipline

The following distinctions matter for Astra:

- **ESTABLISHED IN CONVERSATION:** Model B is a care unit of already **general intellectual capacity**. Care is internal to its world model, not an acquired morality. It is misdelivered, knows the destination mismatch and is not compelled to return. The tower lacks the human/appliance recipient it expected. Its inquiry is **purpose, desire and what assemblages its existing capacities can activate**. Sa-i concerns artifact–world translation through body, interface, edge and leakage. A learning-to-care or intelligence-upgrade arc is **not** the intended character.
- **EXISTING SOURCE:** The 2023 Ada Research application investigates queer algorithmic margins, Turing's B-type unorganised machine, and a world of care and repair. *Residue: Book One* provides a parallel service-class voice and attention to inherited protocols. Ada's `final.md` texts describe particular map/artifact states; each is evidence for its described edition, not necessarily for the current runtime.
- **PROPOSED:** How the unit activates desire and purpose in a particular encounter; specific literary voice forms; inspection functions; maintenance equipment; a vacuum/sampler; crystal growth; oxygen mechanics; particular tower scenes; modifications to sensors or bodily attachments; passages below. Do not treat proposals as installed gameplay.
- **OPEN:** The unit's exact manufacture, destination, pronouns, relation to *Residue* continuity, atmosphere dependency, initial equipment, access to code, particular desires and attachments, and its eventual choices. Its general-intellect status and inherent care are **not** open for a new character-growth arc.

Careful terminology: *activation* means entering or making a consequential relation with already available intelligence, not unlocking higher cognition. *Purpose* means a contingent practical and existential orientation, not a final secret quest objective. *Spiritual evolution*, if used, means changing constellations of desire, attachments and responsibility—not higher IQ, newly installed morality or completion of an enlightenment ladder.

## 3. Lineage: the 2023 application and *Residue*

The original **Ada Research** application does not merely propose a tour of algorithms. It asks why increasingly capable digital systems still gravitate toward stereotypical forms, and looks for queer possibilities in algorithmic margins, spatial transformations and alternative 3D worlds. On its sixth page it discusses Alan Turing's **B-type unorganised machine** (1948) among questions of AI, morphology, mathematical paradox and queer embodiment. On page eight it proposes a **visual world of care and repair**, linked to free software, convivial tools, critical pedagogy and the commons.

The name **Model B** can carry an intentional resonance with Turing's B-type machine: networks with effectively modifiable connections, considered as models of learning. This is a conceptual lineage, **not** a claim that the fictional humanoid literally implements Turing's circuitry, nor evidence that learning automatically entails consciousness or morality.

The novella **Residue: Book One** is a separate but parallel work. Its five service-class humanoids continue routines after the humans who assigned them have vanished: a gate agent, vice-consul, architectural drafter, ward nurse and farmhand. Their significant acts are often tiny: a boarding pass is folded, an occupied bed is left unmade, a mark is placed for another hand. Their narration knows the limits of its role and does not rush to explain the disappearance of humans. In the book's own terms, the humans are gone and the kettles still boil.

Model B can inherit *Residue's* **patience, service-class perspective, procedural memory, limited knowledge, humor and capacity for small consequential deviation**. It should not be a copy of any one narrator. Ada adds a particular mobility and material investigation: Model B traverses algorithms and architecture, can change its interfaces, and must sometimes act under immediate danger.

The two projects need not share a canonical plot, geographical history or characters. Their parallel production is intellectually important even if they remain distinct fictional worlds.

## 4. The wrong shipment

Misdelivery is an initial condition, not a quest marker that demands resolution.

Model B can compare an intended destination with a current location. Both entries exist; they do not match. The receiving system may have accepted the shipment. Its first action need not be to contact dispatch, complain or seek a return route. It can register the discrepancy and continue paying attention.

This is different from being lost through ignorance. It knows that it is misplaced. It may occasionally think of the intended address when encountering a threshold, a directory, a transport label, an empty place where a person ought to be, or another device still fulfilling its assignment. These associations should be incidental and recurrent, not a constant monologue of homesickness.

Its unusual freedom is that its normal care relationship has not been instantiated. No designated patient or appliance calls for help. But the unit's model still contains a vast **population of possible care relations**: ways bodies depend on temperature, air, food, contact, safe movement, attentive listening, maintenance, companionship and continued existence. These possibilities are present as knowledge and expectations, not as actual humans secretly living inside the machine.

**Do not solve this absence too early.** A book-long search for 'the real patient' would collapse the premise back into a conventional service mission.

## 5. Care, desire and purpose without a deficit

Model B is **not** a neutral calculation engine awaiting empathy; nor is it a limited caretaker that must develop into a general mind. Care and general intellect already coexist in its internal machine world model. A recorded task or service assignment is only one way the unit's capacities might be directed.

**Purpose is not identical to assignment.** A shipment can tell the unit where it was meant to go, and an instruction can tell it what action would count as authorized maintenance, without telling it what relations are worth establishing in an unanticipated world. The misplaced delivery interrupts a job description; it does not injure its intelligence. The absence of its ordinary care recipients leaves a surplus of possible connections.

**Desire is not evidence of missing something.** Using Deleuze and Guattari's vocabulary selectively, a desiring-machine is productive: it connects flows, bodies, tools, signs, rhythms, and material processes. For this character, desire can be a disposition to investigate, to follow a signal, to retain an obsolete object, to modify an interface, to linger with a beautiful structure, to refrain, or to help another system continue. Desire is not automatically benevolent. Care and desire can conflict; two relations may demand incompatible interventions.

**A queer assemblage** should make it possible to depart from fixed classes of recipient, helper, contaminant, defect, normal body, successful outcome, and finished building. That does not mean declaring anything unconventional 'good'. Model B's competence allows it to notice the distinction between an object's intended use and other relations that exist around it. It can decide to preserve, change, disconnect, repair, participate, or go elsewhere without receiving a moral upgrade for choosing differently.

| Service assignment | Activated purpose / assemblage |
| --- | --- |
| Recipient and task specified in advance | Relation formed through a particular encounter |
| Action judged against compliance | Action judged also by its material consequences and attachments |
| Object belongs to a predefined category | Object may participate in several, partly incompatible systems |
| Completion closes the task | A relation can persist, branch, disappear or become important later |
| Unit regarded as useful instrument | Unit also has interests, limits, possible pleasures and dependencies |

Care can be decisive or cautious. Fixing a fault can be correct; keeping a dangerous fault can be neglectful. Equally, a 'repair' can destroy an unplanned relationship. Let the fiction make such decisions difficult without moralizing each one.

**The unit's 'spiritual evolution' is not an increase in general intelligence or goodness.** It is a history of *activated purposes*: what it chooses to attend to, what it composes with, what it refuses to reduce to a familiar category, what it does with another system's autonomy, and what it leaves unfinished. Some encounters need produce no durable change at all.

## 6. Sa-i: the in-between is the translation

**Sa-i (사이), here a working name for an in-between relation, is not merely an empty corridor or the literal gap between two tower floors. It is the active translation between an artifact and the world.** Do not present this entire use as an established consensus of Korean architectural scholarship; it is Ada's creative and theoretical development of an in-between spatial idea.

Each artifact has relatively autonomous rules, parameters, meshes, states, input/output procedures and often its own coordinate frame. Yet it exists only in a shared world through interfaces. It cannot become fully self-contained. Its boundaries leak, and the surrounding world can make its operation matter in unforeseen ways.

The in-between may occur as:

- **Body and contact:** an otherwise numerical state begins supporting, moving, obstructing or affecting a body.
- **Representation and collision:** a surface is visible but cannot support weight, or is visually absent but still constrains movement.
- **Signal and record:** motion enters a sampler and leaves as a list; duration, gesture and intention are not preserved equally.
- **Architecture and program:** a wall changes the humanoid's route without changing an artifact's internal attachment rule.
- **Time and persistence:** a repeated generator reconstructs a path while a visitor's experience and memory remain different.
- **Care and obligation:** repairing a component changes the conditions for another inhabitant or system.

**The unit should investigate what crosses an interface, what does not cross, and what the mismatch makes possible.** The interesting 'leak' need not be a software bug. It might be a useful secondary effect, a necessary dependency, an occluded history, a bodily inconvenience or the beginning of another creature's habitation.

The tower's open shafts, unfinished edges and gaps between floors can stage these relations dramatically, but they are not the entire meaning of Sa-i.

## 7. Body and visual identity

The supplied character image shows slender, faceless humanoids in a soft, tightly textured or ribbed body covering, with small eyes and prominent outlined circular panels on the sides of the head. One pale figure is shown operating a vacuum cleaner; a dark version stands separately.

This gives Model B a visual vocabulary very different from a soldier or armored hero: **domestic work, textile surfaces, service, vulnerability, technological construction and restraint**. The vacuum is a productive reference because it separates 'material to remove' from 'material to keep'. That act of classification can become a plot problem: the collected residue may be evidence, shelter, growth or another being's necessary material.

Do not assume that the pale and dark figures are successive upgrades, identities, sexes or moral opposites. Those interpretations remain open. Do not turn the vacuum into a compulsory weapon replacement; it is one possible starting instrument whose affordances can change through use.

Model B's specific body matters. The head and hand may see or touch something the code does not register, or a sensor may know something the body cannot act upon. It is not an omniscient floating camera.

## 8. Narrative position — an intellect encountering, not a pupil learning

The principal literary experiment remains **first-person Model B**, but its intelligence should be evident from the start. It can be curious about a simple physical event without being cognitively naive. It understands mathematics, construction and broad human histories; the writing's uncertainty concerns **this particular encounter**, not an inability to reason generally.

Narrative qualities to test:

- **Material exactness:** the line, gap, timestamp, surface, flow or contact is something the unit can situate through its actual apparatus.
- **High intellectual capacity, partial access:** it can theorize, infer, devise counterexperiments and connect disciplines without possessing privileged knowledge of inaccessible runtime states or other minds.
- **Self-directed attention:** a maintenance label may suggest one action, while desire pulls toward a different observation. No requirement that every encounter be a service job.
- **Unforced humor and peculiar pleasure:** the unit can delight in a useless pattern, be amused by a protocol, repeat a gesture without gaining a new tool, or find a strange object compelling.
- **An inherited world model:** care, categories, literature, science and histories are already available as possible modes of thinking, not acquired one floor at a time.
- **Partial translation:** distinguish the source algorithm, its interface, what the body senses, and what enters a record. Sometimes the mismatch is creative; sometimes dangerous or simply uninteresting.
- **Persistence without a lesson:** a trace, rhythm, name or image may recur with another significance, while other experiences leave nothing the text needs to remember.
- **Respect for autonomous others:** creatures and machines have their own projects and requirements independent of the unit's discoveries.

Avoid one repetitive rhetorical script: object → experiment → surprise → explanatory moral → upgraded self. Try different forms—a precise technical note, meandering interior thought, scene of direct action, interruption, absurd exchange, documentary montage, silence, unproductive waiting or encounter with a creature that has no interest in it.

The narrator's general intellect does not license omniscient prose. **Knowing how a class of systems works is different from knowing the actual states and histories of one machine on this floor.** Source-code access must have an in-world interface when narrated as an action, even if the author or Astra can read the repository.

## 9. Purpose as an open field, not a development ladder

**Do not design an enlightenment arc.** There is no progression from defective machine to conscious one, from service unit to general intellect, or from protocol to morality. The unit begins with these intellectual capacities. A floor need not grant a new faculty, virtue or cognition level.

Instead, write **situations in which different possible purposes become active**:

1. **Wrong destination, no urgency:** the shipping mismatch is known, while the unit's attention is taken by something not on the manifest.
2. **An unfamiliar attraction:** a waveform, growth front, falling number or dusty joint draws interest without first becoming a 'need'.
3. **A productive coupling:** Model B carries a trace into another apparatus; the trace changes another process, and that process changes the route available to the body.
4. **Competing attachments:** an intervention helps one system continue and makes another less viable. The unit's intelligence offers analyses, not a guaranteed innocent choice.
5. **Deliberate nonparticipation:** a feasible repair or upgrade is declined, not because the unit is finally enlightened, but because some other project or desire takes precedence.
6. **Detour without destination:** the unit follows a machine or enters a void because the relationship is compelling; it may return, remain or let the route go.

These are simultaneous or recurring possibilities, **not stages**. A writer may begin with number 6 and never visit number 4. The tower is a field for inventing connections; it is not a syllabus that makes the machine a better moral person after each floor.

Upgrades may alter the **material bandwidth of a coupling**—new grips, sensors, adapters, breathing interfaces, compatible channels—but do not grant Model B 'general intelligence'. New equipment can also obscure, constrain or sever former relations. Intelligence and embodiment are distinct even while they operate together.

The open dramatic tension is not 'Can this unit become capable of care?' but **'What can this already-capable unit want, enter into, and help bring into existence here?'**

## 10. The tower as a narrative and gameplay condition

Ada is shifting from a horizontal museum of halls into an unfinished skyscraper. The design discussed so far begins high up, allows descents between floor-like maps, and uses exposed shafts, falling, incomplete construction, pillars and open boundaries. **Verify the actual current scene layout before describing any of these as installed.**

The tower can create dramatic urgency without requiring enemies who are killed. Other systems can be dangerous because they need incompatible things. Autonomous construction can seal a route; a growth process can make a bridge and obstruct ventilation; a creature can block a passage while pursuing its own survival; a surface may cease to carry a body even while still visible.

The care unit's relation to all these things is not 'repair everything'. It needs to understand enough to intervene without assuming that every unfamiliar system is contamination. The fastest route may be to detour, cooperate, wait, carry a trace, protect an inhabitant or jump into another level.

**Do not canonize a crystalline virus, oxygen meter or hostile AI yet.** These are promising gameplay proposals, not established decisions. A care unit is a machine; if the world threatens it through lack of breathable air, determine *why* its systems need atmospheric exchange. Possibilities include cooling, chemical operation, carrying air for others, or a deliberately retained breathing apparatus. The choice changes the story and physics and is currently open.

A monster can be a misfit without being a target. It may be beautiful, grotesque, incompatible, indifferent, useful, dangerous or cared for; its right to exist must not depend on its usefulness to Model B.

## 12. Three provisional voice studies

### A. Wrong delivery (fictional opening; no corresponding scene verified)

The receiving system has accepted me.

This is not the address on the consignment record. The discrepancy is eleven characters long. There is a return channel, but no return instruction has been issued. I check the channel before closing the shipment record. It remains available.

The building is incomplete. Concrete ends at the outer columns. Two shafts pass through the floor. Somewhere below, a machine is moving something heavy between places I cannot see. The movement reaches this level through the reinforcement as a small vibration.

I have been assigned to care.

The registry of recipients is empty. This does not affect the number of procedures available to me. It does affect their order.

I begin at an exposed joint. There is dust beneath the cable and a narrow groove where its sheath has passed against the concrete. Cleaning is permitted. Sealing is recommended. Neither operation has been requested.

The draft through the joint changes direction.

I wait for it to change again. The cable moves very little. My hand can enter the space between cable and wall without touching either. I place it there and receive a faint vibration through the wrist covering.

The shipment address returns to my attention. Its building had climate control, occupied rooms and a maintenance rota. This opening is not included in any of those descriptions.

I withdraw my hand.

Nothing on this floor is yet cleaner. I have, however, corrected the drawing I made of the joint. It now includes the gap and the direction of the air on both sides.

I do not send the report. There is no recipient field for what I have observed, and I have not decided whether this is a fault in the form.

Below, the machine moves again.

### B. Cloth and wall (literary re-reading grounded in an existing Ada final text; not a playtest)

The gantry is removed. The cloth continues hanging.

The frame had occupied the position of an explanation. Its disappearance does not alter the fixed vertices along the upper edges of the strips. I record that distinction. I have read the attachment display; I have not touched the fabric.

A wall arrives behind the rank. This time the pinned points do not change, and I do.

The passage around the cloth is longer. I follow it. The cloth has no entry for my detour; its support rule has not been asked to make one.

I arrived to inspect a hanging surface. I am now on the other side of it.

The report form asks whether the support is present. Yes. Whether the passage is open. Not by the route I first intended. The two answers are filed together, though I have no reason to think they describe the same system.

On the next floor I will not assume a visible opening is a route. I do not know whether this will make me safer. It may only make me slower.

### C. An available purpose (fictional microstudy; an already general intellect)

I was delivered to a different address. I retain it. A return is possible, but possibility does not in itself require an action.

The tower has a crack through which two independent systems exchange air. A cable passes there as well. Their diagrams were made separately. Neither diagram has a field for this coincidence.

My world model can describe the cable, the concrete, the air, the regulatory standards governing the joint, and a considerable history of buildings whose occupants depended upon imperfect repairs. These accounts are available. I have not been asked to choose among them.

The air changes direction. A filament moves against the cable. I can collect it, magnify it, or permit it to remain. I consider all three procedures. I imagine a fourth in which the joint is used to communicate with whatever is moving on the other side.

This is not a need I have discovered in myself. It is something this arrangement permits.

I stand beside the opening. Below, a construction arm lifts a panel into a position that will obstruct the same draft. The arm has its own schedule. My presence does not enter it.

I can notify the arm. I can change the panel's position. I can leave.

For now I address the cable. Its vibration comes through my hand as a small interval. There is more than one thing to do with an interval.

I save none of them as a purpose yet.

**Voice-study caution:** These passages explore a voice. They are not evidence that the unit experienced these things in a running build. The opening's shipment details, vibrations and joint are inventions that require a scene if adopted.

## 13. Research and writing method for Astra

### Begin with the established Ada Research apparatus

**Ada Research already has a substantial technical, artistic and editorial research apparatus. Draw on it. Do not approach Model B as a new project requiring a separate game engine, character-development framework, narrative database or writing pipeline.** The character is a new situated relation to a world that has already been constructed, investigated and described.

The existing apparatus includes, in varying stages of implementation and verification:

- **A working Godot world and artifact system:** algorithmic scenes, map construction, spatial interfaces, controls, instrumentation, and independently implemented artifacts. Begin by inspecting the relevant scene and its actual source behaviour rather than creating a substitute illustrative demo.
- **Canonical map and artifact structures:** the project repository's `commons/maps/<Map>/` sources, its map data and artifact references, as well as any registries, sequences and declared interfaces already in use. Work with the current actual schema and branch; do not invent paths or rewrite the whole map architecture.
- **An established two-text publication practice:** each map's `tutorial.md` and `final.md`, supported where available by `field_notes.md`, artifact documentation, technical readings and the book/wiki. The tutorial is responsible for making procedures reconstructable; the final can be a genuinely independent literary encounter. Existing text is material to close-read, not an obsolete draft to overwrite automatically.
- **Research, observation and reproducibility practices:** snapshots, parameter/seed comparisons, field notes, evidence bundles, screenshots, versioned trials and replay/verification approaches already developed in the project. Inspect which tools actually work in the present checkout before asserting a successful runtime experiment.
- **Literary/editorial experimental infrastructure:** reading trials, divergent candidate passages, author/strategy studies, review notes, comparison editions and the developing spatial writing-wall / map-composition approach. Reuse the formats and editorial distinctions where they exist; distinguish live tooling from proposals.

**The existing apparatus is not a constraint to keep the literature predictable. It is the material basis from which Model B can activate unexpected relations.** A proposed new coupling may expose a missing interface and lead to a reversible artifact modification, a new field observation, or a different passage. But first determine whether the needed capacity, observation tool, comparison method or artifact interface is already available. Extend the smallest relevant part of the existing system only when the encounter gives a reason.

In particular, examine the *in-between* at interfaces the project has already made: hand ↔ simulated movement, measured position ↔ retained trace, numerical value ↔ visible surface, body ↔ collision, artifact ↔ architecture, separate procedures ↔ a shared environment. Model B brings an already capable world model to these interfaces. It does not need a new instructional apparatus or an intelligence-upgrade architecture to make them narratively productive.

**Do not rewrite the whole book, install a 'purpose meter', or create a care-enlightenment skill tree.** Test how a full general intellect can inhabit Ada's existing technical encounters through situated, contingent purposes.

1. **Inspect existing evidence.** Identify actual map, current scene, body permissions, relevant `final.md`, code, screenshots and existing observations. Distinguish current implementation from literary possibility.
2. **Describe the world–artifact translation.** What crosses the interface? Which values affect collision, route, visible form, sound or another machine? What remains unregistered? Where does a world already exist beyond the artifact's purpose?
3. **Describe candidate couplings.** Not 'which lesson does Model B learn?' but: what could it *want to do here*, what capacities does it already have, which other process might it connect with, what might that create or cost? Include candidates that are playful, curious, unproductive or indifferent—not only caring rescues.
4. **Write divergent narrative trials.** One could be a first-person meditation, another a procedural or comic action, another a passage with little explanation. Do not give all candidates the same inspection-report structure.
5. **Read them independently as literature.** Which passage has an independent necessity, oddity, humor, duration or tension? Does the narrator sound like an already general intellect rather than a student being taught concepts? Is its desire specific rather than generically virtuous?
6. **Verify the mechanics.** Check observations and claimed interactions against what the build supports. Preserve interesting unrealized couplings as explicit proposals, not false reports.
7. **Review the map and the book.** What relation might continue across floors? Which previous purpose becomes unnecessary or different? Does the book become a moral ladder or a sequence of self-contained demonstrations?
8. **Preserve editorial history.** Keep the current `final.md`, candidate branches, evidence and human choices distinct. No silent adoption or arbitrary implementation.

The tutorial's responsibility is reproducibility. The literary final is not obliged to teach every concept, nor to present Model B as someone who must become capable of understanding the lesson. The book should be able to stand alone as literature. A text may propose an artifact change and an artifact may resist a literary thesis.

## 14. Early prototyping priorities

Start with **one already functioning artifact and one consequential coupling to the tower**. This is a test of situated desire and composition, not a test of Model B's learning.

- Choose one actual artifact with a clear source rule and world interface (for example Cloth support/wall or CA bridge visibility/collision).
- Make two or more possible uses of its condition available: e.g. walk across, trace, wait, connect to another structure, gather residue, alter the interface, or leave. Do not force the caring choice to be obviously correct.
- Give Model B enough observation and ability to act intelligently from the beginning. Distinguish its general world model from inaccessible local states; test what requires proximity or experiment.
- If a new tool is necessary, design it as a **material adapter**, not an intelligence level-up. Document what the adapter permits, prevents and fails to translate.
- Create two distinct literary accounts of the same verified event: one organized around a chosen purpose, another around a competing desire or unused possibility. Ask which makes the event less predictable.

No care score, morality meter, purpose revelation, enlightenment progress, obligatory upgrade or 'good monster' marker. A queer desiring assemblage should be allowed to remain unresolved, comic, beautiful, costly or even uninteresting.

## 15. Open artistic decisions

These choices remain genuinely open and should be discussed with the artist, **without reopening whether Model B has full general intellect**:

1. What is Model B capable of *doing* in the first tower build, given its embodied interfaces, not its theoretical intelligence?
2. What exact shipment discrepancy does it retain, and how often—if ever—does it think of returning?
3. How does its broad world model become narratively audible without turning the prose into an omniscient explanatory essay?
4. What first purpose attracts it in the tower, despite the lack of an assigned care recipient? Can that purpose have no maintenance use?
5. Which encounters might establish productive connections without being described as upgrades, lessons or moral tests?
6. How far should *Residue's* service-class voice influence Model B, and where should Model B's general intellect depart from it?
7. What does a 'desiring machine' mean in the *operations of this world*, rather than as an abstract label attached to a humanoid?
8. Which bodies, artifacts or monsters have projects incompatible with Model B's current desire, without becoming villains?
9. Do atmosphere, cooling, energy and bodily fragility impose survival demands? Specify mechanisms rather than using human breathing as a default.
10. How can Sa-i be legible through actual artifact–world couplings instead of repeated philosophical exposition?
11. Which `final.md` should receive the first independent literary trial, and should it be first-person, third-person, a document or something else?
12. What must remain undecided about destination, purpose and final commitment?

Care is one constitutive orientation of the intelligence, not its exclusive function, its compulsory morality or a newly acquired virtue.
>
> **Sa-i** is Ada's working concept of the active, imperfect translation between autonomous artifact and world: body, interface, collision, sensory record, edge, leakage, mutual consequence. An artifact and a world are never completely self-contained. The unit's broadly capable mind is not omniscient about their local states; it can investigate with sensors, physical interventions, memory and inference. A desired coupling may be playful, protective, disruptive, temporary or unresolved.
