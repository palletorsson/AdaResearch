# Two opening studies

20 October 2026 · proposed fiction, not runtime observation  
Companion to [A delivery with an inside](README.md).

## A. The view from the packing

The label is on the outside. Whoever fastened it expected to meet me from this side.

CARE UNIT — MODEL B.

Below it, a second rectangle of paper has been scraped away. Enough glue remains to hold a little dirt in the shape of an address. I have the intended destination. This is another building. There is no difficulty in establishing that much.

Inside the crate, someone moves.

The opening is tall enough to stand in. A brace crosses the panel beside it, taking a load toward a corner. Whoever removed the front left that brace alone. I like the unevenness of the decision: a careful container, opened on one side, still equipped to protect whatever has already begun to leave it.

I move away from the opening.

A person steps onto the concrete and stops. From here the floor looks nearly complete. A little farther to either side, it ends inside the building. The columns continue down through those openings. I can see part of the next slab. Beyond that, my view supplies less information than the structure would require.

The person looks up.

ADA RESEARCH stands above the roof on a narrow frame. The lettering has been delivered with greater confidence than the building. Behind the person, the crate is at last empty enough for me to see the whole of its floor.

I want to go inside.

It has a view I could not have specified before arriving: through the open face, past the concrete, a bright number changes. From one corner of the crate the jamb cuts off its first digit. I move a little and the digit returns. The panel calls them frames. I know what such a count can tell me. It will not supply the hour these boards were nailed together.

The person turns back. I stop beside the opening, leaving room.

There are several obvious uses for the packing. It can shelter equipment. It can become a partition. Taken apart, its boards could carry something across a small gap, provided we tested the span and the load. The roof offers gaps sufficiently large to make that qualification worth keeping.

None of those uses is what brought me to this corner.

The number sits well inside the wooden frame. I shift once more, trying to put the whole display in the opening without losing the strip of sky above it. This takes less movement than I expected. I could explain the geometry, but an explanation would not give someone else this position.

The person comes to the threshold. I step out.

We have exchanged places without exchanging names. From outside, I can no longer see the number framed that way. I can see the person's shoulder, the brace, and the label with my name on it.

I wait beside the open face. There is room for someone to come out.

## B. A small problem with the address

“That says you're here,” I say.

The person looks down at the floor.

“It also crosses you out.”

They crouch and take hold of the first word. It comes away with its line through it. The rest stays on the concrete, a sentence that has lost the part I was addressing.

I had intended only to point out the inscription. Now I want to see it against the packing label.

“Would you bring that over?”

They do. I stand beside the crate while they hold YOU below CARE UNIT — MODEL B. The word is blue and the shipping label is pale. They are almost the same width. I had not anticipated being pleased by that.

“No,” I say, too quickly.

The person begins to lower it.

“I meant the label doesn't tell us who was in the box.”

They hold the word sideways.

I can give an adequate account of a consignment label. I can give another account of a person holding a piece of a sentence. Putting the two together has made me unnecessarily talkative.

The person waits.

“A little to the left?”

They carry it away.

I watch YOU pass the corner of the crate, still crossed out. I stay with the shipping label. It is fastened at a slight angle, and until now I had been looking at the words.
