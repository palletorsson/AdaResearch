# Swarm — first observed learner session

Prepared 11 October 2026. Not yet conducted.

Use the [11 October review edition](../2026-10-11-swarm-tightened-readings/index.html) and the existing museum. Choose a reader who did not build these lessons and who has the stated preparation: the museum's controls, PointBody, arrays, coordinates and steering. If that preparation is missing, record it; do not attribute the gap to this chapter automatically.

## One passage to observe

Start with Boids. Give the reader its tutorial and access to the installed hall. Ask them to find the principal experiment, make a prediction about changing the neighbourhood, and try it. Do not explain the black hand point before they read its instructions.

Then ask them to reconstruct the six-point study from the printed files inside a working copy of the prepared project. Ask which parts carry over from Flow Fields, which are supplied by the museum, and how the study differs from the installed thirty-body table. Record any missing dependency or instruction precisely.

Let the reader choose one further variation and state what it may change before executing it. Record their explanation afterwards, including a result that contradicts the prediction. Successful copying alone does not answer this question.

If time and preparation permit, use the optional Particle Swarm RIDGE study as a separate session. The reader chooses the comparison and records the hypothesis before seeing the earlier ghost-student outcome. Follow the existing study brief rather than adding another task here.

## What to keep

| Moment | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Finding the experiment | What they chose first; where the text directed them |
| Acting in the room | Intended action, observed response, control or reach difficulty |
| Reconstructing | Files used, missing assumptions, exact errors, assistance given |
| Making a variation | Prediction, changed variable, conditions kept fixed, result |
| Explaining the outcome | Their explanation before and after inspecting the evidence |

Note edition, hall, desktop/headset mode and prior experience. Capture the learner's words where useful. Separate observer interpretation from their account. Let them try before helping; when help is needed, record what it supplied.

Afterwards, classify each problem as instruction, prerequisite, control, implementation or interpretation. Fix the smallest demonstrated gap and repeat the affected task. A single session identifies problems to investigate; it does not establish general teaching effectiveness.

An AI rehearsal must state its context and tools. It can expose reconstruction gaps, but it cannot substitute for the bodily experience of finding, reaching and using these controls in VR.
