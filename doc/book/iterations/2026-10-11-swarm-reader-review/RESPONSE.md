# Swarm — hold the edition, observe the learner

11 October 2026 · Response to the second external review

The review supports the current seven-hall progression and recommends no further substantial rewriting before learner observation. We accept that direction. The tutorials, reflections, printed code, museum controls and sequence order remain unchanged in this response.

The strongest finding is that the student can now distinguish the installed encounter from the focused reconstruction. The same PointBody supplies continuity while the source of coordination changes: trace, direction, neighbours, state, two channels, scored memory, population support. The optional RIDGE investigation creates room for a prediction whose outcome is not prescribed.

Keep the Population Support distinction explicit: omitting supplied arrivals from the account does not rerun the ecosystem without those arrivals. An alternative description is not automatically an alternative history.

The reviewer read the evidence; they did not independently execute the tests. The edition records 265 author-operated Godot checks, 70 preserved code/layout/order hashes and 144 checked links and anchors. These support reconstruction and consistency. They do not establish that a new learner can navigate the room, understand the code or devise an experiment. No new learner or headset session took place in this response.

The next step is a small observed session using the [learner-session brief](LEARNER-SESSION.md). Record where assistance becomes necessary before deciding what to change. A fresh AI can separately rehearse code reconstruction; label that evidence distinctly from a person using the headset.

This also gives the research methodology a concrete claim: Ada can preserve the relation between an intention, a readable instruction, executable code, an installed encounter and recorded checks. That makes discrepancies easier to locate. It does not make the checks a complete account of the work.
