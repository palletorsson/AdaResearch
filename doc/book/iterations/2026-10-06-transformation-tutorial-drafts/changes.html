A cube that moves, turns and grows

Learning elements

  • Translation: changing position
  • Rotation: changing orientation
  • Uniform scale: changing size
  • Elapsed time and a repeating value
  • Collection: overlap detection and a signal

In Trans_Pre, one cube rises. The next rises and turns. Another adds a pulse in size. Follow one corner: each example keeps the earlier movement and adds another operation.

We continue with the cube shape from Primitives. Here Godot's BoxMesh supplies it; we change the whole object's transform. The construction of faces has already done its work. This lesson makes one animated pickup that you collect by walking into it.

The complete study

The Endless Museum is already running. Its room, light, camera and player are supplied. Put an empty Node3D named TransformStudy in clear floor space beside the exhibits and attach lesson.gd. Use floor level as its origin, zero rotation and unit scale. Each coordinate below is in this study's local frame. The script creates every part of the pickup.

Read the complete result once, then use the small edits below to build up its behaviour.

extends Node3D

var cube := MeshInstance3D.new()
var start := Vector3(0, 1.3, 0)
var rise := 0.35
var turn_speed := 45.0
var pulse := 0.2
var period := 4.0
var elapsed := 0.0

func _ready() -> void:
    assert(period > 0.0 and pulse >= 0.0 and pulse < 1.0)
    cube.mesh = BoxMesh.new()
    add_child(cube)
    cube.position = start

    var sensor := Area3D.new()
    sensor.collision_layer = 0
    sensor.collision_mask = 1
    sensor.set_collision_mask_value(20, true)
    cube.add_child(sensor)
    var hitbox := CollisionShape3D.new()
    hitbox.shape = BoxShape3D.new()
    sensor.add_child(hitbox)
    sensor.body_entered.connect(_on_body_entered)

func _process(delta: float) -> void:
    elapsed += delta
    var wave := sin(TAU * elapsed / period)
    cube.position = start + Vector3.UP * rise * wave
    cube.rotation_degrees.y = turn_speed * elapsed
    cube.scale = Vector3.ONE * (1.0 + pulse * wave)

func _on_body_entered(body: Node3D) -> void:
    if body.is_in_group("em_walker") or body.is_in_group("player_body"):
        print("Collected")
        queue_free()

BoxMesh.new() describes a one-metre cube centred on its origin. The MeshInstance3D holds its position, orientation and size. The same mesh remains throughout.

Add one movement at a time

First set turn_speed and pulse to 0.0. Keep rise at 0.35. The cube moves 35 centimetres above and below start, keeping its size and facing.

elapsed += delta counts seconds. sin(TAU * elapsed / period) supplies a repeating value between −1 and 1; TAU is one full cycle in radians. For now, use this as a timing tool. With period = 4.0, the value is zero at the start, one after a second, zero after two, minus one after three, and zero after four. Waves will return to how this curve is made.

Restore turn_speed to 45.0. The cube now rises and turns 45 degrees per second around Y. Its mesh has not changed. After eight seconds it completes one turn.

Restore pulse to 0.2. The size factor now ranges from 0.8 to 1.2. Multiplying Vector3.ONE gives all three axes the same factor, so the cube keeps its proportions. Rise and pulse share the wave; the turn continues at its own steady rate. The assertion keeps the period positive and prevents the pulse from collapsing the cube to zero size.

Each update assigns a pose from the starting values and elapsed time. Adding that whole pose repeatedly would make the cube drift.

    cube.mesh = BoxMesh.new()

    cube.position = start

    var sensor := Area3D.new()

    cube.add_child(sensor)
    var hitbox := CollisionShape3D.new()

    sensor.add_child(hitbox)
    sensor.body_entered.connect(_on_body_entered)

func _process(delta: float) -> void:
    elapsed += delta
    var t := pingpong(2.0 * elapsed / period + 0.5, 1.0)
    cube.position = start + Vector3.UP * lerpf(-rise, rise, t)
    cube.rotation_degrees.y = turn_speed * elapsed
    cube.scale = Vector3.ONE * lerpf(1.0 - pulse, 1.0 + pulse, t)

        print("Collected")
        queue_free()

Add one movement at a time

First set turn_speed and pulse to 0.0. Keep rise at 0.35. The cube moves 35 centimetres above and below start, keeping its size and facing.

elapsed += delta counts seconds. pingpong makes t travel between zero and one at a steady rate, reversing at each end. The factor 2.0 / period gives one outward-and-return journey per period. Adding 0.5 starts halfway up, moving upward. With period = 4.0, t is 0.5 at the start, 1 after a second, 0.5 after two, 0 after three, and 0.5 after four.

lerpf(-rise, rise, t) chooses a height offset between the lower and upper limits. At t = 0.5 it gives zero: the cube is back at start. This is the steady rise and fall of the museum example. It changes direction immediately at the ends; a softened turn would be a different timing choice.

Restore turn_speed to 45.0. The cube now rises and turns 45 degrees per second around Y. Its mesh has not changed. After eight seconds it completes one turn.

Restore pulse to 0.2. The second lerpf now chooses a size factor between 0.8 and 1.2. Multiplying Vector3.ONE gives all three axes the same factor, so the cube keeps its proportions. Rise and pulse share the returning fraction; the turn continues at its own steady rate. The assertion keeps the period positive and prevents the pulse from collapsing the cube to zero size. The installed specimen uses a larger pulse, from 0.7 to 1.3; this study keeps the same operation with a smaller range.

Each update assigns a pose from the starting values and elapsed time. Adding that whole pose repeatedly would make the cube drift. elapsed is kept between updates; t is calculated afresh from that clock, and the three transform properties are assigned from those values.

What survives its movement?

In Melencolia, the pyramids stayed still while your viewpoint changed. Here, stay a moment and let an object change in front of you. What survives its movement?

The cubes are black, white and gray. Light catches an edge; a shadow changes as a face turns. Four cubes are spaced down the middle of the hall. Smaller markers stand beside the row. Beyond the cubes, wedges lead up to a raised path. Here we can follow how a game pickup gets its animation.

The first cube holds still. Pause before approaching and watch a corner: nothing shifts. Then walk into it: even without an animation, it can be collected. Keep its shape in mind as you go to the next one.

This cube moves up and down. Follow it through a complete rise and fall. Its position changes while its size and facing stay the same. Moving an object from one position to another is translation. Here that movement follows the vertical Y direction.

The script chooses a height between two endpoints:

global_position.y = lerp(original_y - bob_height, original_y + bob_height, t)

The animation moves t back and forth between zero and one. lerp, short for linear interpolation, uses that number to choose a height between the lower and upper endpoints. At t = 0.5, the cube is halfway between them. Each rise and fall takes it through the same heights, at a steady speed on each leg.

The third cube keeps moving up and down. What has been added?

Follow a corner. The cube turns as well. Changing its orientation — which way it faces — is rotation. This line adds a small turn around the upright Y axis on each update:

rotate_y(rotation_speed * delta)

Point Zero's small counter example received delta without using it. Here delta is the time since the previous update. Multiplying the turning speed by those elapsed seconds gives the turn to add now. A longer interval calls for a larger turn.

The rise and the turn continue together. Follow the same corner once more: it travels around the upright axis as well as up and down.

At the fourth cube, watch the edges move apart and come together. It grows and shrinks while it rises and turns. Changing size is scale.

var size_factor: float = lerp(1.0 - pulse_scale, 1.0 + pulse_scale, t)
_apply_pulse(size_factor)

The same lerp now chooses a size factor between 0.7 and 1.3. _apply_pulse multiplies the cube's starting size by that factor. A factor of one leaves the size unchanged; below one it shrinks, above one it grows. All three dimensions change together, so it keeps its cube shape.

Beside each moving example, a smaller marker isolates the operation just added: one moves up and down, one turns in place, one grows and shrinks. They let us separate movements that the larger cubes combine.

The three moving demonstrations behind you stay in place for another look.

The transport script moves the cube towards target_position, its destination:

global_position = global_position.move_toward(target_position, move_speed * delta)

The destination is two metres farther along the room's z direction. move_speed * delta gives the distance the cube can travel during this update: its speed multiplied by the elapsed time. The cube keeps its size and facing.

To carry you, the script also applies each small displacement to the rider it has detected on top:

var movement_delta = global_position - previous_position
_translate_rider(carried_player, movement_delta)

movement_delta is the difference between the cube's new position and its previous one. _translate_rider adds that difference to your position too.
