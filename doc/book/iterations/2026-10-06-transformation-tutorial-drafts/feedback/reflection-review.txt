## The reflective sequence has a clear conceptual arc

It moves through:

**What survives transformation?**  
→ **When does a transformation make something usable?**  
→ **How can the same destination contain different journeys?**  
→ **How does orientation alter relation and available space?**  
→ **What does scale mean relative to a body?**  
→ **What happens when the environment responds to the body?**  
→ **What happens when transformations become moving boundaries?**

That is considerably more interesting than a reflective sequence simply shadowing “translation / rotation / scale.”

Across the seven texts you repeatedly ask versions of:

I think there is a second equally important question:

> **What becomes possible?**

Those two questions almost define the chapter:

**What survives the transformation?**  
**What becomes possible because of it?**

For example:

- cube translates → geometry survives / another place becomes reachable
- plank rotates → length survives / crossing becomes possible

## Final assessment

I would make **no structural rewrite**.

The reflective sequence is coherent, bodily, technically grounded, and sufficiently different from the tutorial track.

The only editorial change I consider important is in **The Body**: explicitly name the distinction between **responsive state that is recomputed now** and **historical change that remains after the body leaves**.

Everything else is refinement rather than repair.

And I think the sequence now has an unusually strong beginning/end correspondence:

> **Beginning:** what survives when an object moves, turns and changes size?  
> **End:** those same operations can reorganize the world enough to make room for another body.

That is a complete reflective arc.
