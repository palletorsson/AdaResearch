The entrance gives you a choice before you can see where either way leads. A mass remains in the middle. The passage curves around it, carrying the far side out of sight.

A torus has been taken from the block. The removed ring leaves a corridor around a core. You can follow either side and reach the other opening. A small change in the cutter has changed what a route can be: the way through now includes going around.

Where cuts cross

In the next room, two cylindrical cuts cross at the height of the body. Their meeting makes a place where you can change direction. Higher up, a diagonal cut and a vertical shaft take other pieces from the block. The ceiling opens in directions the floor does not follow.

Pink and blue run across the junction. From here, one opening frames another space; another faces the ground outside. The cut has made room, but it has also made a relation between rooms. Leaving through the side changes which one you approach next.

The horizontal cuts admit a walk. The higher cuts admit views and light.

A tilted room, a level walk

The third passage has flat faces. Three box-shaped cutters lean in alternating directions, and their overlaps leave abrupt changes in the wall. The floor stays level. The enclosure inclines around a body that need not incline with it.

Dark reflective surfaces make the edges difficult to follow at first. Pink lines continue across some of them. Select CHALK at the entrance and look again: the construction becomes easier to separate from the shimmer, though the cuts have not changed.

Alternating planar cuts make an angular enclosure above the shared level floor.

The same four finishes are available in each room. We can carry a surface from one construction to another, or hold the construction and change its skin. Neither comparison settles what the space is. Each gives us another way to notice how it receives us.
