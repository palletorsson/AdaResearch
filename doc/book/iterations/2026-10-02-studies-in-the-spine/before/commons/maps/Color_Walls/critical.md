# An invitation to look

The coloured sheets stop short of enclosing a room. A chair is visible through two of them, concealed by an opaque version of the same surfaces, or visible again from another angle. The comparison separates visibility from passage, but the gaps complicate its promise of privacy: opacity is local to a surface, while a view can go around its edge.

The pale backing is a further condition. The earlier dark render concealed the overlaps; the surrounding architecture and material treatment had to change for them to become legible. The exhibit's setting participates in its argument.

A queer reading can begin with the inadequacy of one permission as a description of another. Being visible, being accessible, and being able to act are separable conditions. The simulation gives us a controlled example; it does not make every social threshold reducible to a collider. Ask what other bodies, heights or modes of navigation would change the encounter. The current capsule and standing controls supply one answer, not the possible body in general.

The colour models disclose another selection. Five hundred samples make each model inspectable. The black corner is deliberately brightened to remain visible. The display changes the thing it asks us to read. Neither an accusation of failure nor a claim to an untouched original is necessary: name the accommodation and ask whom it helps.

Finally, RGB and HSV interpolation keep endpoints while producing different interiors. This gives a precise limit to the claim that shared code makes every transition available: the actual algorithm chooses a route, and sixteen samples expose only part of it. Ask what the shorter hue arc excludes, or what would happen if a different path were authored. Those are proposed experiments, not hidden controls in this room.
