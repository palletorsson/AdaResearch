# Rotation — crossings, arrays and inhabited repetition

A 39 by 60 grid combines the former rotation and spectacle halls. Its 2,340 cells replace a combined 5,095, reducing the footprint by 54.1 percent while keeping the 8.4 m crossing blades, 2.04 m array cubes and full-size tunnel.

The first court compares centre-pivot Y, X and Z crossings and retains their grid banks. Short wedge runs return from the raised landings. The second court uses four two-column, six-row arrays at eighteen-degree increments. The last court places five hollow segments on the centreline and five carousel profiles beside them. Only the duplicate cake was removed.

The teaching order is axis and pivot → repeated orientation and order → spatial enclosure → angular speed and radius. A continuous side route supports comparison, observation and access to the next hall without forcing every moving crossing. The historical source map and documentation are preserved in the refresh snapshot.
