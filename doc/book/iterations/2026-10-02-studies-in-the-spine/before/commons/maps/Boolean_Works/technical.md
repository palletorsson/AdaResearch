# Technical register · When the Negative Gets Big

`Boolean_Works` is a 17×56 museum map. It contains ten authored artifact families: `csg_compose_workbench`, `subtraction_suite`, `sphere_splitting_showcase`, `recursive_boolean_cube`, `csg_architecture_cavity`, `boolean_burrow`, `sdf_cavern_room`, `boolean_eggs`, `booleanvariations`, and the retained `coincident_face` comparison. Visitors pass around the egg plinth using the side aisles.

The core encounter list contains three primaries: csg_compose_workbench, boolean_burrow and sdf_cavern_room. The burrow is configured with carver_profile:level and section:off; it is the bodily counterexample to the visual wall and raised model. The remaining bodies are secondary evidence, because the hall's claim is about scale and inhabitation rather than a complete catalogue. Roles are recorded in `commons/data/artifact_roles.json`.

On 1 October, twelve rows were inserted before the final comparisons for a dedicated egg bay and approach. The egg token is at (7,43), facing the incoming visitor at 180 degrees. The final two artifacts and exit move with their rows. The museum mount suppresses the companion's extra ground and raises its plinth onto the deck; it brings two local lights and no camera or environment. The three CSG results and plinth have collision. The top of the halo fits below five metres. The earlier runtime work remains dated evidence in doc/book/iterations/2026-09-20-boolean-review/.

For the walking encounter here, the visible boundary, support, clearance and collision rules have to cooperate. The map deliberately retains scenes where one or both systems are absent, beside a burrow where support, clearance and collision cooperate. The 1 October desktop capture assembled the actual hall: ten placements, none relocated or refused, with egg meshes of 3,800, 2,422 and 4,374 triangles. Native images and the capture report are in doc/book/iterations/2026-10-01-boolean-eggs-in-the-spine/. Headset traversal remains untested in this pass.
