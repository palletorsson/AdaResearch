# Six shader lessons

## Shaping — Make a mark

Same point, cube and square receivers. Write one distance mask; point height changes radius and trigger changes edge. Predict which addresses lie inside. Carry the rule and its coordinate convention onward.

## Patterns — Repeat its address

Keep the circle and receivers. Multiply and fold the coordinates; move the point sideways to shift the lookup. Predict the direction of movement and its return after one tile. Repetition changes the reading without multiplying bodies.

## Materials — Give light another direction

Keep the repeated circle, now sampled into height data and encoded directions. Compare flat, bump and normal-map readings under one lamp. The point gains coat(); its geometry stays. Explain a changed highlight at an unchanged silhouette.

## Water — Move the rendered surface

Carry the point and rigid cube; replace the two squares with a subdivided plane. Apply a bounded layered field to colour, normals, then vertex height. Compare four corners with a dense mesh. The glass supports the player through its own collider.

## Pink — Choose the composition

Return to rigid point and cube. Their shared recipe combines repeated pigment, a view-dependent rim and emission; local X/Y/Z choose the amounts. Isolate each, then keep a composition. Compare its surroundings without claiming that visible glow is a lamp.

## State — Keep what happened

Use the same point as an XY selector that plants a difference. Read the completed field, update into a separate texture and retain the result. Compare holding, stepping, running and changing ink. Then inspect one model cell and vary feed at an equal update count.
