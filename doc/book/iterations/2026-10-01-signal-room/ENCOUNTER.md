# Different neighbours

A white band reaches your shoes and continues towards the far wall. At the wall it turns upward. The floor holds your weight; the image keeps going.

Change the order of the rows. The bands gather into unequal widths. Change the order of individual cells and they break apart. What had looked like a direction becomes a field of fragments. Your feet have not moved.

Beside the entrance, sixteen squares repeat a small part of what surrounds you. You can count them. Across the room, the same decision has become too large to take in at once. Walking changes which part you can hold in view.

The counter still reports the same number of black and white cells. Its entropy reading has not changed either: one bit per symbol. It counts the shares of the two values and leaves their neighbours out. The measurement is exact. The room has changed in a way it was never asked to notice.

The bright places have moved. The floor will hold you in the dark ones too.
