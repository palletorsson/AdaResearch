## One paragraph per hall

**Evolution.** Encounter the live drifting population and distinguish it from the authored static arrangements. Carry the Graph Theory PointBody as a run/pause control for a small population table. Reconstruct movement, shared food, energy drain, inherited speed, splitting and death. Reset the same seeded opening under finite food and four-second regrowth; compare at the same simulated time. Track ancestry and energy before calling a change improvement. Keep explicit fitness-ranking creatures and flowers as comparisons. Carry the environment's consequential rule into Gradient Landscape, where the objective becomes an explicit surface.

**Gradient Landscape.** Compare losses on the same observations and repeat the starting condition. Follow the consequence of changing the question that a low error answers. A short height-regression extension of the sphere can make the fit tangible: hand measurements control a predicted height through two fitted parameters. This is a proposed tutorial use of a working model, not an artifact move. Carry the distinction between data, objective, parameter and update.

**Classification.** Watch cluster centres regroup an unchanged population. Distinguish grouping by distance from learning labels supplied by a person. Predict what changes when positions or the number of centres change. Carry that distinction to the sphere: A, B and C will be chosen categories, not names discovered by K-means. Focus the hall instead of teaching several classifiers through partial snippets.

**Neural Networks.** Follow one input through connected weights, inspect output error, then compare weights before and after fitting. Distinguish the brief activation from parameters that remain. Predict what falling training loss establishes and what still requires fresh examples. The next hall applies this structure to a real small task; it must not depend on an unprinted network helper from this older manuscript.

**Perception.** Continue the point as a pickable sphere in its museum bay. Turn two tracked hands into six relative coordinates, collect whole labelled poses, fit the complete 6→8→3 network and test fresh takes before enabling its response. Change labels in a new session to show that examples change the mapping. Distinguish this learning from authored float, spin and stretch actions. Carry the point class, representation, data and model into a question about time.

**Sequence Memory.** Make two movements whose mean pose could be the same but whose order differs. Retain timed samples for both hands, fit one whole-phrase prototype, then compare two taught phrases. Test reversal, one stationary hand and changed timing; allow unknown and ambiguous outcomes. The hand-set memory cell remains a separate comparison about what a state keeps. Do not call the prototype a recurrent network or its fixed timing an artistic standard.

**Generative.** Carry the saved ONE phrase and the same sphere into a side study in the Generative hall. Compare a recorded take, the time-aligned mean and a generated variation. Retain the existing GAN diagram as a different, explicitly untrained comparison. Carry source, production method and evaluation as separate records into Synthesis.
