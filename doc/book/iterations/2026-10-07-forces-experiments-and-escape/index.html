The experiment leaves the apparatus

Carry the familiar black point into experiments that work within a limit. Change one relation. The apparatus still follows its rules, but the consequence may leave the job, the receiver or the room we assigned to it.

One instruction: carry the point across the room. A growing capacity to act. A growing awareness of what else acts with you.

Design proposal. Existing source inspected; proposed interactions have not been implemented or tested. Canonical hall order, tutorial.md, final.md and museum code are unchanged.

What cannot this apparatus contain?

Cybernetics has a precise account of limits to regulation. Ashby's requisite variety relates the disturbances a regulator must answer to the distinctions and responses available to it. Under the stated conditions, too little regulatory variety leaves some variation in the outcomes. This is a limit relative to a regulator and a task, not proof of an absolutely uncontainable entity. Ashby, An Introduction to Cybernetics, chapter 11

Our design inference: make the missing capacity playable. A collector cannot distinguish cargo from furniture; a stabilizer measures position but ignores velocity; an outlet cannot process the incoming stock. Ask what the controller can sense, affect and preserve. Increasing power alone need not supply the missing distinction.

The Nature of Code supplies a useful mechanism vocabulary: accumulated forces change a body's motion, spring forces introduce restoring relations, and steering provides another contribution to motion. Ada can let those mechanisms meet in a shared room. The instability is not evidence that a physics bug is desirable. The Nature of Code: Forces

Your Half-Life reading gives the dramatic structure: an experiment opens conditions it can no longer manage. Here the first escaped 'monster' can be the same sphere the student built. It becomes threatening through mass, momentum, attachment or a goal that persists after circumstances change. This is our interpretation and design proposal, not a claim about a historical influence.

The dramatic turn need not be a secret monster. It may be a door that opens too far, a successful delivery that moves its receiver, or a factory whose correct output occupies its exit.

I · Acquire a capacity / Core mechanism

The point does not stop

The instruction

Push the familiar sphere onto a marked place.

Learn

Velocity, force and mass; distinguish a brief impulse from a sustained push.

What else could happen

The push ends, but the point crosses its target and strikes a loose stop block. The stop becomes another moving body.

What the player can do

Use a smaller push, oppose its motion, or arrange a receiver. Compare the same impulse on equal-sized bodies with different declared masses.

What travels onward

The same point keeps its motion after input ends. This is what the launch and flight encounters depend on.

Candidate existing halls: VFM_03_Motion

Implementation boundary and proposed test

Existing point lineage; the controlled push/impulse comparison and movable stop are proposed.

Test to write: In the isolated test, with gravity and damping disabled, ending the applied input does not zero velocity; the same impulse produces the expected inverse-mass change. Contact is a separate test.

Perfect delivery, moving destination

The instruction

Launch the point into a receiving tray; then use a pad to cross the gap yourself.

Impulse, gravity and trajectory; a receiver also has mass and contact.

Two points carry a body

The instruction

Hold a point in each hand and reach a balcony.

Learn

Sum two force contributions; separate input from retained velocity.

What else could happen

One hand stops thrusting while the other continues. The intended straight flight becomes a curved approach.

What the player can do

Aim down/back and hold triggers to thrust up/forward, as already agreed. Release one trigger, compare, then counter-thrust to land.

What travels onward

The point is now a tool that acts on the player's motion. Release still leaves a moving body.

Candidate existing halls: VFM_05_Launch · Vectors_Act5_ForceAsPlace

Implementation boundary and proposed test

Two-sphere flight remains proposed. The adapter is an integration seam, not the completed ability.

Test to write: A single movement owner adds both inputs; release or drop clears only that input. Neither event zeros the player's velocity. Keep firing a separate action or explicit mode.

I · Acquire a capacity / Core mechanism

The brake has a length

The instruction

Release thrust and settle onto a narrow landing shelf.

Learn

Gravity, opposing force and drag; stopping takes time and distance.

What else could happen

Entering a drag zone slows the approach but does not freeze it. A late correction carries the point or visitor beyond the shelf.

What the player can do

Brake earlier, change the declared drag coefficient, or approach with less speed. First observe the ball, then feel the same relation through movement.

What travels onward

A boundary drawn on the floor is not an instantaneous change in a body's state.

Candidate existing halls: VFM_03_Motion · Vectors_Act4a_KineticForces

Implementation boundary and proposed test

Proposed matched ball/player comparison.

Test to write: Name the chosen drag model rather than imply one universal air law. At nonzero relative velocity its force opposes that motion; it vanishes at zero relative velocity.

II · The room participates / Core mechanism

The trolley refuses

The instruction

Move a trolley carrying the point across a floor seam.

Learn

Contact, friction and a threshold for motion.

What else could happen

A wheel catches or a high-friction patch holds the trolley. More push initially achieves no movement; once it clears, the same input is excessive.

What the player can do

Reduce the sustained push, change the contact route or unload the trolley. Later, after the spring lesson, return with an elastic pull.

What travels onward

The floor is part of the experiment. A blocked body is not necessarily an unresponsive program.

Candidate existing halls: VFM_03_Motion · Vectors_Act4a_KineticForces

Implementation boundary and proposed test

The current ramp is a reduced one-axis model. A free trolley/contact encounter must be made and tested.

Test to write: Provide a successful baseline. Distinguish geometric wheel obstruction from friction. In the friction-only comparison change one material parameter; do not pre-script the release.

The drain that will not empty

The instruction

Clear floating debris through a collection opening.

Home comes to the point

The instruction

Pull a tethered sphere aside so you can cross.

Learn

Rest length, restoring force and damping; a fixed versus movable anchor.

What else could happen

With the sphere caught, releasing the anchor's floor lock allows the rolling base to move toward it. The return mechanism changes what 'home' means.

What the player can do

Anchor the base, shorten the extension, damp the return, or use the moving base as transport. The same sphere can be a gate, tool or load.

A restoring relation connects two bodies. The tutorial's fixed anchor was an assumption.

The successful factory

The instruction

Keep the exit clear while the familiar conveyor supplies cubes.

Learn

Production and collection rates, finite stock, contact and accumulated load.

What else could happen

IV · A limit becomes visible / Optional control study / later tutorial return

The assistant that overcorrects

The instruction

Ask a powered platform to keep the point over its centre.

Learn

Position error, feedback gain, velocity damping and limited actuation.

What else could happen

A position-only controller corrects after the point is already moving. Corrections carry it back across the target. A later delay variant makes the consequence of acting on old information visible.

What the player can do

Reduce gain, include velocity in the control rule, reduce the task's demand or stop the controller and balance manually.

What travels onward

More corrective power is not the same as better regulation. This can return in learning and adaptive-control sequences.

Candidate existing halls: Vectors_Act4b_Oscillation · VFM_08_Arena

Implementation boundary and proposed test

New apparatus and optional teaching concept. Do not make a full control-theory lesson a hidden prerequisite for completing Forces.

Test to write: Declare a powered controller and force cap. Establish the no-delay baseline first. Distinguish modeled oscillation from numerical instability; test timestep sensitivity before interpreting an apparent runaway.

    V · Live with the consequence / Capstone / no new force law

    Containment / carry one point across

    The instruction

    Carry one point to the plinth across a laboratory using the tools already learned.

    Learn

    Select and combine known forces; observe state, dependencies and the limits of the task.

    What else could happen

    Use only a few active systems: a feeding belt, a movable receiver, a tether and a passing field. Clearing one obstruction changes what the next system receives. A spill can become a ramp, an anchor or a continuing blockage.

    What the player can do

    Fly, redirect, hold admission, restrain a body, make another path, or preserve an unexpected arrangement. The final condition may deserve a name the instruction did not supply.

    What travels onward

    Leave the scene available to inspect. Later noise, agents or learning can revisit this same apparatus and introduce one new cause.

    Candidate existing halls: Vectors_Act5_ForceAsPlace

    Implementation boundary and proposed test

    Composed encounter is a proposal. Existing artifacts do not yet share a verified force/receiver contract.

    Test to write: An ordinary successful delivery must be possible. Test component rules, retained state, body bounds and deliberate reset. Never require one collapse or comic ending to prove emergence.

    How to keep the lesson clear

    1. Each encounter begins with one ordinary task and a baseline that can succeed.
    2. First isolate the mechanism; then change one declared condition. A funny outcome is possible, not a required scripted ending.
    3. The same black sphere, grey cubes, off-white trolley, tray, tether and receiving plinth recur. Ability changes are readable before new decoration is added.
    4. A source of energy is visible: trigger thrust, powered belt, hand work, gravity or a preloaded spring. Nothing launches merely because the story needs a surprise.
    5. The player can intervene: cut power, restrain, redirect, let go, use the result differently or reset explicitly. Preserve an aftermath long enough to inspect.
    6. Keep body state and force input distinct. Position, velocity and spring extension carry consequences; do not accidentally keep accumulating a past force after it has stopped.
    7. In Godot, declare who integrates motion. Use the engine for free rigid-body contacts; use one receiver owner for XR player movement. Do not apply a second custom integration to the same body.
    8. The tutorial prints the minimal complete continuation and tests its rule. The reflection asks what the rule permitted, omitted and made costly. The museum can contain further experiments.
    9. Technical failure, numerical instability and unexpected lawful behavior are different. Verify the first two before claiming an emergent consequence.
    10. Noise, flocking, learning and soft bodies can return to these apparatus later. They are not hidden prerequisites for this Forces pass.

    For every apparatus, record four things: the intended outcome, the variables it observes, the actions available to it, and the disturbance under which its promise fails. Make that limit something the student can investigate and change.

    The critical opening is specific: which bodies were counted as cargo, which variations were treated as error, and which arrangements must be restored? An unexpected arrangement can sometimes be kept. That offers a place for queer reflection without assigning a moral identity to a force law.

    What we can already build from

    The conveyor you named is in the cube hall

    Point_Animatedcube contains cube_sorting_line. It feeds grabbable rigid cubes on a moving-surface belt; unsorted stock accumulates and can end the round. Settled cubes can be stored as backlog and reclaimed. That storage policy matters: it is not an unlimited pile of simultaneously active rigid bodies.

    Forces already has another useful conveyor

    Change_Choreography contains conveyor_backlog: rigid boxes, a timed receiver, a visible 48-body limit and held admission when full or obstructed. Feed and receiver rates can differ. These are strong foundations for the proposed overflow encounter; an expanded room-spanning cascade is still new work.

    Existing diagrams are not automatically interacting apparatus

    collision_carts shows calculated before/after lanes; tug_of_war directly positions a load; force_vortex assigns player velocity. They can inform the lessons, but they do not yet prove contact-driven collisions, a jointed tug or additive vortex forces.

    The first builds

    1. Prototype 12 first: the conveyor already connects known cubes, rates, contact and a room that changes. Compare matched throughput with backlog, preserving the visible finite bound.
    2. Prototype 9 next: fixed anchor → rolling anchor makes one changed assumption unmistakable and reuses the returning point.
    3. Develop 3 alongside them as the accepted player ability: two trigger-controlled sphere contributions, one movement owner, gravity and retained velocity.
    4. Use 14 to combine only the strongest verified encounters. Keep angular assemblage and the feedback platform optional until the core route is clear.

    Test the rules, not the joke. A spectacular collapse should not be a passing assertion. A successful baseline, causal response, retained aftermath and deliberate reset should be.
