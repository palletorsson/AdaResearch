## Corrections to carry back to Claude

| Passage | Why it matters | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|
| “Find a fold … it drifts, shallows, crosses into its neighbour” before PINS | The current initial capture is nearly flat; prominent folds appear after the corners are selected. Separate strips do not share one connected cloth mesh. | Establish the hanging surface first. Let PINS reveal the fold instead of describing that discovery in advance. |
| “Nothing is driving this” | The cloth responds to gravity and contact; absence of a motor is a narrower claim. | “The strips settle under gravity.” |
| “the cloth does not move. Not a little. Not at all.” | The probe measured fixed vertices, not an entirely motionless body. Runtime confirms the distinction. | “The frame disappears. The strips go on hanging. Their upper edge stays where it was.” |
| “the frame is not a party to the arrangement. It was built for you.” | The pin rule ignores the frame, but the frame has collision geometry and can obstruct a body or meet cloth. | Keep the claim local: the visible frame is not what establishes these pins. Do not generalize that independence to every physical relation. |
| “It stops being an edge.” | The edge is still there; six of its eight points become free. | “Only the corners stay. Between them, the edge can sag.” |
| STIFFNESS immediately after RELEASE | The material comparison needs a useful starting state. | Restore hanging strips with REBUILD before the stiffness comparison, so the reader can distinguish the two changes. |
| “contact is a line rather than a plane” / “holds the form it had” | The rail is a narrow solid bar; the timed stop is not an exact vertex snapshot. | Use “a narrow rail” and describe the scheduled change in motion without promising an exact frozen form. |

Keep the beginner's distinction from the previous hall: an open cloth surface does not enclose an interior like the pressurized cube. One sentence is enough; it was useful and was lost in the rewrite. “A hand's width” is also too small for a 0.3 m strip; “narrow strips” is sufficient without adding a measuring inventory.
