In Primitives_Polythedra, the held form is already an interface. Turn an open corner, compare a closed solid, or pull one of the pyramid’s five vertices. This pass makes those encounters easier to approach and the editable form easier to recover.

A shape you can reach

The pyramid’s apex started about 2.10 m above the floor. Its apparent front faced the crowded side of the hall. It now faces the open aisle on a 65 cm micropod support, with the base grips at 71 cm and the apex at 1.51 m. The smaller support lets the visitor stand closer.

The visible grip radius grows from 1.2 to 2.8 cm inside the existing grab collider. All five handles still directly move vertices. The front corners and apex can be reached from the front; the two far corners have clear standing positions at the sides.

Let go, then try again

A physical RESET SHAPE button sits on a cased backplate. Its lettering is flat text on planes, with no live viewport. The station stands beside the front corner, leaving the pyramid itself as the main control.

Reset restores the authored vertices after a deformation. While any handle is held, the cap dims and the caption asks the visitor to let go first. A reset press cannot pull a vertex out of the hand. The existing free, symmetric, apex-only and base-only rules remain available through configuration; reset preserves the active rule.

Face the aisle

The trihedron and tetrahedron also turn toward the aisle. Their authored geometry and native grab interaction remain intact. Each now has a clear supported approach, a valid standing position and an unobstructed grab ray. No additional console was added to either held solid.

A principle to carry forward

Use the form itself when the action is spatial. Make its grips visible, give the body a place to stand, and provide recovery when an experiment can wander far from its starting state. A separate control earns its place by supporting that action.

The small recovery panel uses the existing casing, baked text and native push button. Other vertex-based artifacts can supply the same held-state and reset methods. The pyramid opts into it with workbench:1; other placements retain their existing presentation. The map and museum plan agree on all three orientations and the new pyramid support. The manuscript and curatorial roles were not changed.
