# Three encounters to calibrate from

These are existing passages, retained for selection and refinement. They are not new rewrites and not yet three approved exemplars. Palle selected cellular automata as the third area; the exact bridge passage is the researcher's nomination.

## A · Embodied encounter: five-row abacus

Source: Random Definition's final as captured for this packet, including its new recording wall. Its earlier opening established the local “we” reference; this revised encounter still needs a reading. This excerpt concerns the visible hand, not bodily travel.

We stand before a small abacus. Five thin rods sit one above another, each a metre long, with a dark bead waiting at its centre. Behind them, a pale wall waits for marks.

We touch the first bead. The hand visible in the room follows it sideways. Our viewpoint stays still. The bead slows into one position, then departs for another. We can see how far it is allowed to go. Its next turn remains uncertain.

At the next row, the hand follows the same path twice as quickly. The third doubles that pace; the fourth doubles it again. The positions repeat. The rods have not grown, but there is less time inside the same distance. At the fifth bead, we return to the second row's pace. The visible hand rises and falls as well as moving sideways. The bead leaves the line that first seemed to contain it.

Small transparent circles gather behind the moving bead. Where they overlap, the wall darkens. We withdraw our hand. Its visible position follows our own movement again; the bead returns alone to its centre. The marks stay. Movement has left something we can examine without continuing to move.

The wall records twelve positions within each step. A faster bead draws sooner; after the same number of steps, the first four rows hold the same pattern. The fifth spreads its marks above and below the rod. SOURCE changes the target distribution: uniform selection gives equal chances to equal intervals; bounded Gaussian selection favours positions near the centre. The marks also include the journey between targets. Their darkness records those choices together.

The positions are kept in the program. We press REPLAY: the paper clears. We touch the first bead again. The turns return in the same order, and the marks gather again. The next movement can still be unfamiliar, even though the whole sequence has been kept.

## B · Epistemological encounter: Geiger

Source: the reference chapter in retained edition `ca4601b6324d2f76cd92`. Palle's earlier response to the Geiger direction was “The balance is closer”; that is local feedback, not adoption of this whole chapter or selection as a calibration exemplar. Footnote markers alone are omitted here; the complete source is retained. The machine synthesizes sound from calculated event times.

The Geiger machine is already clicking. On its screen:

> seed 435

The colour window displays the same number in large figures. Beside the virtual lamps, a capture also carries seed 435. The opening hand’s path began from it. The number recurs; what it sets in motion changes.

Click. A gap. Another click.

This machine detects no radiation. Its waiting times are calculated. We press RESTART.

Click. The same gap. Another click.

All 96 detection times were calculated before this playback began. Each calculation advanced the generator’s state and supplied another waiting time. 435 is the seed—the number that set its starting state. This is pseudorandom generation: numbers produced by a repeatable rule. The pauses were kept before we heard them. RESTART plays the stored trace again.

The beginning has a record of its own.

*Saved acquisition receipt, abbreviated:*

> Received: 13 October 2026, 14:29:02 UTC  
> Provider: RANDOM.ORG · atmospheric noise  
> Requested range: 0–999 · value kept: 435

No new sample of the atmosphere enters with these clicks. The hall opens with the number that was kept. Reusing it preserves a beginning, not an ongoing connection to its source.

Another number can be entered deliberately at the colour window. It becomes the hall’s new seed. Each instrument receives it through its own procedure: a value may become a colour, a waiting time or a destination. Sharing a beginning does not make their sequences identical.

The visible hand’s curve can seem irregular even when its positions are stored. The clicks can remain hard to anticipate after we have heard them return. Knowing that something repeats is not yet knowing its next turn.

A beginning can also be taken from a movement.

## C · Cellular automata: The surface includes you

Source: CA_ExpandingSpace's current final, opening excerpt. A continuously valued cellular field controls tile appearance and support. This is not Conway's binary Game of Life. The installed rule reads proximity to a body's origin, not pressure or weight. The passage's direct address remains visible for judgement; no voice change has been adopted.

The path looks available. Five tiles across, a longer line ahead. Its colours repeat in panels on a free-standing frieze beside the path. Beneath it lies a shallow basin, already drawn with the addresses of tiles that have not yet disappeared.

Begin at the desk. Press SOURCE once to put the pink probe in your place. The readout shows PROBE / NEAR. Press RUN. Follow the gold square W. The number falls. The tile changes colour, then becomes a faint sheet through which the basin is visible. Stop the local experiment. How much of a path is still there?

Use NEAR/FAR to withdraw the probe and run again. Watch the edge of the depleted region before its centre. Something returns through what remained around it. There is no separate instruction ordering this particular patch to grow back into its old outline.

RESTORE prepares a full bridge. Change SOURCE to VISITOR and start RUN. Your position now takes the probe's place. Approach the marked region, wait, move away. The ordinary museum floor continues alongside the basin; recovery ramps rise at either end on the desk's side. On the bridge, lingering can cost you support. The faint tile can still be seen after it can no longer carry you.

You can bring the black point from beside the desk into this relation. Hold it near the surface and use its trigger to give the rule another position. The same object that carried STEP in the previous hall now brings a location as well. Release it and continue stepping: the input has withdrawn, but its consequences remain.

Look up to find W again. A gold frame holds its place on the frieze. Wait above the tile while watching its colour there. The change stays in front of you; the consequence arrives beneath your feet. When the surface gives way, you are still looking at the same address from a little lower down.

# Random Definition: encounter evidence

Source snapshot: 15 October 2026. This account distinguishes inspected implementation from witnessed experience. The images are retained renders, not participant reports. The source manifest is held separately for technical review.

## Selected route and actual placement

The saved Final tags select, in order: **Random Touch → RAND page → Geiger counter → MOVE / KEEP → Out / Back**. The route enters at the top of the planning map and exits at the bottom. Optional instruments remain in the room; the chapter need not describe all of them.

**Placement gap:** MOVE / KEEP is implemented, but the current map marks its component removed (`part_movement_sampler_removed:true`). Its Final tag and textual position remain. The five-stop route is therefore a selected composition with one unresolved placement, not a newly verified complete walk. The details below describe that instrument's implemented behaviour, conditional on its placement being restored.

## 1. Random Touch

A standing frame holds five horizontal one-metre rods. Each has a dark bead, 13 cm in diameter, waiting at its centre. The stand is about 1.3 m wide, 0.55 m deep and 2.15 m high including its title. The source captured for this packet includes a new pale recording wall behind the rods. Six rectangular controls below read REPLAY, PAUSE, KEEP, LOAD, SOURCE and CLEAR INK. This is later than the open-frame abacus shown in the earlier implementation record.

Contact makes one visible virtual hand follow a bead. The viewpoint, tracked physical controller and body are not carried by this instrument. Withdrawing the physical hand ends the following; the bead returns to centre. Desktop right-click holds/releases a bead without moving the camera.

Rows 1–4 traverse the same stored horizontal positions at rates labelled ½×, 1×, 2× and 4×. Each row's motion starts on its own contact. Row 5 uses the second row's pace and adds vertical displacement. Horizontal range is ±0.44 m; vertical range on row 5 is ±0.12 m. Forty-eight positions repeat, with smooth interpolation between them. The X and Y generators start from the hall seed and hall seed plus one respectively.

Twelve positions per segment leave translucent circular marks on the wall. Overlaps darken the image. Marks persist after release; the row retains at most 576 marks. Sampling by path phase means speed changes when marks arrive rather than their positions after an equal number of segments. SOURCE switches between uniform targets and a bounded Gaussian distribution by rejecting out-of-range draws, with the same seed. Marks include interpolated positions between targets; they are not simply a histogram of random draws. REPLAY resets the paths and clears the wall; CLEAR INK clears marks without changing the path. KEEP/LOAD retain the positions, settings, five phases and marks.

Prior automated hand-interaction and hall-seed test logs report PASS for the earlier abacus. They are not evidence that the new recording wall has been tested. The current wall and distribution descriptions are source-inspected only in this packet. No new headset response or report of how each speed feels was collected here.

## 2. Falling RAND page

A large upright display carries groups of five digits. A small stand says “select”. In this placement, rows enter at the top on a 2.5-second cadence and travel downward. Contact toggles a group between flowing and held. A held group turns amber and occupies its row address while other groups continue; another touch releases it. Withdrawal alone does not release the selection.

The displayed digits are generated by this program, not transcribed from RAND's historical tables. The page uses its private generator initialized from the hall seed. Holding a group changes its persistence in the display. The display does not establish a physical source for those digits. Any additional account of the historical RAND production process needs its own source check.

## 3. Geiger study

A cabinet combines a readout, event timeline, flashing indicator, moving needle, comparison rows and controls. It clicks when the room is running. The display names its seed, mean event rate, clock resolution and playback count. **No radiation sensor is connected.**

The program calculates 96 event times before playing them. A deterministic generator supplies values transformed into waiting intervals. The default settings are seed 435, mean four events per second and a one-millisecond comparison clock. Sound is synthesized from the retained event times; it is not a microphone recording of radiation detections. The clip loops. RESTART starts the same clip again.

A rate comparison regenerates waiting intervals using the same generator draws at another rate. A clock comparison keeps the event times and audio identical while reading intervals at one or 100 milliseconds. The comparison takes two gaps from each group of four detections. Equal measured intervals are skipped; unequal gaps contribute a bit, with the comparison direction alternating across emitted bits. The screen shows both baseline and current results. KEEP stores the baseline and current record; replay restores the kept comparison. These are operations on simulated data, not evidence that a new physical random event occurred.

## 4. MOVE / KEEP — implemented, currently removed from the saved room

A small grabbable dark sphere sits beneath six coloured traces for changes in position and rotation. Holding begins collection. Releasing after at least 30 valid samples and at least one measured pose change produces a seed in 0–999; collection is capped at 300 samples. Sampling is attempted at most about 60 times per second and does not invent missed samples after a slow frame.

The recording includes elapsed microseconds, rounded position components and rounded orientation components. Position is encoded in 10-micrometre units; orientation uses a quaternion rounded to millionths. Those are encoding steps, not claims about sensor accuracy. A fixed hash calculation consumes the source label and recorded samples; a part of its result is reduced modulo 1000. Retaining the exact record permits the same seed to be derived again. Making a similar gesture is a new acquisition and need not produce the same record or seed.

The resulting seed can become the hall's new beginning. Subsequent values are still generated by deterministic procedures. Different recordings can share a seed; no retained pair demonstrating such a collision was supplied for this packet. This procedure makes no estimate or certification of physical entropy.

## 5. Out / Back

A larger dark sphere makes repeated horizontal excursions. It waits at the centre for 1.5 seconds, then travels outward for 3.5 seconds and returns over another 3.5 seconds. A private generator chooses direction and a distance from 1.1 to 2.2 m. Markings show direction and distance.

Holding this sphere engages movement of the player's body through the room's collision system. Letting go ends the carry immediately; the sphere continues its own cycle. Unlike the opening abacus, this instrument can move the viewpoint with the body. The sphere's return is part of its fixed route; changing the seed changes destinations, not that rule. Actual body motion can be constrained by collisions.

## Shared beginning and evidence limits

Random-generating instruments share the chosen hall seed but own separate streams and mappings. The hall starts at **435**. Its stored receipt labels the source RANDOM.ORG atmospheric noise and records receipt at **13 October 2026, 14:29:02 UTC**, in the requested range 0–999. This is what the retained receipt reports; it is not a fresh acquisition or an independent authentication of the provider's physical process. Entering the hall makes no new request.

Manual selection, SEED +1, an explicitly restored record or a completed movement recording can change the shared seed. RESET 435 reuses the original receipt. Seed changes are refused while a participating instrument reports an active held interaction or recording. The optional frame study measures timing without supplying a new hall seed. Optional windows and virtual lava lamps also use the shared beginning.

Code inspection establishes procedures, not what a visitor perceived. Prior passing logs cover hand interaction and shared-seed behaviour. They do not establish a successful complete room walk, independent reconstruction, scientific claims about an external entropy source or literary quality. Unpredictability to a person, statistical properties, and physical acquisition are different questions. Offline computation can receive physical input; the relevant limit here is deterministic calculation from fixed state and the same inputs.
