# An invitation to write from evidence

This is preparation for an independent literary chapter about Random Definition in Ada Research. The three passages that follow are **calibration candidates awaiting Palle's selection**, not an approved style or a request to begin a full chapter immediately. Palle has chosen cellular automata as the third area.

Once the calibration set is selected, write one complete chapter for someone who has never played the game. Give it its own spatial continuity, rhythm and discoveries. The final need not explain what the designer intended the artifacts to mean. Scientific precision constrains the account without determining its literary interpretation.

The dossier describes the apparatus and its known limits. Ask for missing evidence; do not invent an implemented action, historical fact or participant response. A speculative possibility may enter the writing as a possibility. The current local voice reference is “we”; the identity of that voice is open, and it does not establish that several people played together. The cellular-automata excerpt's different address is part of the material to judge, not a newly adopted voice rule.

Normally work within 600–1,500 prose words, with length following the discoveries worth developing. Necessary unfamiliar concepts need brief explanation where they matter. Controls and definitions need not become a catalogue. It is legitimate to stay with an object, refuse a proposed strategy, remove an explanatory paragraph or find an unexpected connection.

The selected room/final sequence is abacus → falling RAND page → Geiger → MOVE / KEEP → Out / Back. There is a known placement gap at MOVE / KEEP, described below. If depicting its restoration, identify that dependency outside the chapter and keep the candidate provisional. You may propose a different encounter order, but supply the corresponding room proposal rather than presenting it as the current walk. Flashbacks and recurrences need not change walking order.

Return the complete chapter, followed by factual questions, any proposed apparatus or route changes, and a short account of what additional evidence you used. The first response will concern the chapter as literature; a separate technical reading follows. Both readings return to you before revision. Palle decides whether to adopt a candidate. A disagreement may lead to changing the world as well as its account.
