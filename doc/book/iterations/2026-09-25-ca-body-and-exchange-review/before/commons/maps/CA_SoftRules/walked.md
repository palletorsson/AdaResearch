## The turn (critical)

Two cast members, and between them the chapter's whole critical charge. rule_30_110_gravity takes the two most famous rows of cells in mathematics — Rule 30, whose center column passes randomness tests, and Rule 110, provably Turing complete — and gives them weight: the abstract rows fall, pile, obey a physics they were never written for. The ca_sphere answers from the other side: the same neighborhood rule wrapped on a closed surface, where no edge exists to hide at and every cell's consequence eventually comes home.

Together they state the critique. A rule is never soft *inside* its lattice — it is exact, total, merciless. What is soft is everything around the rule: the choice of grid, of boundary, of which physics the pattern must live with. The room's title is the argument: rules feel absolute until you see the decisions they float on. Computation is exact; its *situation* never is. That is where the chapter's seam artifact wants to stand.
