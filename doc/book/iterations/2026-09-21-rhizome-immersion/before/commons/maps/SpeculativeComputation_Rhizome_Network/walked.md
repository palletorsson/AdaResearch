## The walk

A cave system with no front door worth the name. Four chambers linked by narrow passages, and the tutorial is a navigation exercise you cannot fail: pick any two points and there are multiple routes between them; block one in your mind and the network routes around the absence. The `rhizome_cave_demo` grows this geometry the way mycelium colonizes soil — marching cubes extract solid walls from a density field shaped by local growth rules, branching without plan, thickening where paths cross, no architect anywhere in the pipeline. Walk until you notice what is missing: there is no root chamber, no trunk corridor, no privileged path that the others merely serve. Every point is an entrance; every point is center, which is the same as saying none is. The walls state the grammar you are walking — ANY POINT CONNECTS TO ANY OTHER · NO CENTER · NO HIERARCHY — and your own accumulating disorientation is the measurement: you keep reaching for the map's *top*, and the map keeps not having one.

## The turn (critical)

Deleuze and Guattari proposed the rhizome against the oldest diagram in Western thought — the **tree**: root, trunk, branches, the hierarchy that organizes libraries, taxonomies, org charts, file systems, and every classifier this sequence has indicted. The placement in the chapter is the argument. Two maps ago you stood in a room where tree-structured classification allocated space unequally; the cave is the *constructive* reply — a topology in which the question "which category does this belong under?" cannot even be asked, because there is no *under*. Connection without permission: in a tree, two leaves may only meet by petitioning their common ancestor; in the rhizome, any point connects to any other, directly, and rupture is survivable because meaning never depended on a single line holding. In QFEP terms the cave runs at high λ — many paths, high entropy — with just enough connectivity to cohere: structure that welcomes becoming. But the map's most precise sentence is its last one, and it guards against the easy reading: the rhizome does not *overcome* hierarchy — it **never needed one**. That is a stronger claim than rebellion. Rebellion still orbits the tree it opposes; the cave simply grows otherwise, the way soil always has, indifferent to the org chart above it. After the crisis of foundations, this is what a foundation that never wanted to be one looks like — and you have been walking on it without falling.
