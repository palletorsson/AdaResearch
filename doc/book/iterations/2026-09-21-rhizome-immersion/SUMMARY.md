# Rhizome: built and written

21 September 2026. [Read the chapter with museum views and the original beside it](index.html).

## What changed

The hall now contains an inhabitable branching volume. Its lower passages offer several circuits; a longer route climbs to 3.7 metres, crosses above lower passages and returns to the ground. Three coloured junction lights help a visitor recognise a place approached from another direction. Two open ends join the museum floor. There is no new control panel.

The existing cave artifact supplies the entry point and surface extractor. A new opt-in `passage_study` composes the walkable network. The other stochastic cave placements retain their defaults. The hall grew from 13 by 12 to 31 by 37 cells, with an open roof and cleared central staging. The small grower remains intact beside the approach. Both works are primary, ordered cave then grower across the manuscript and curation data.

The network is authored: fifteen places, twenty links, a volume opened around each link, and a union of the interiors. It is not presented as a cave generated by the companion model or as proof of a theory. Inner and outer surfaces produce thin walls; a stored mesh avoids regenerating the geometry at every arrival. The cache carries source hashes and is written only by the explicit capture path. A 2.5 cm placement lift separates its floor from the museum floor and avoids flickering coplanar surfaces.

`final.md` is rewritten around seeing, entering, returning, and wanting to make another opening. It recalls the early line and triangle encounters. One actual code line explains how interiors join. *A Thousand Plateaus* enters through multiple entryways and a modifiable map. The small grower offers another kind of arrival and keeps the earlier paragraph about growth inside a territory already given. Operational details remain in the tutorial and technical companion. The source and earlier text are preserved in `before/`; [exact text diff](final.diff).

## What the making revealed

The first descending upper passage cut into a lower tunnel and lost part of its floor. A connection in the graph was not enough to carry the body. Keeping the upper route high until it clears the lower tunnel repaired the passage without inventing a hidden floor through the opening. The final walking checks cover that descent as well as the lower circuits.

The upper view also needed more light. Seven local lights and a faint material emission keep the passages readable without spending the mobile renderer's per-mesh light budget entirely at the entrance. The prototype has a visibly faceted surface at its current sampling resolution. The enclosed routes are the intended first experience; surface finish and how much of the branching whole is visible at arrival remain useful aesthetic refinements.

## Verification

- The artifact build and its continuous capsule walk pass 23 waypoints, including several returns, the upper climb, descent and exit. It uses a 1.75 metre capsule with a 0.28 metre radius. `artifact-runtime.json` records the walk.
- The actual endless-museum code places both artifacts correctly. Its existing desktop player passes the continuous 23-waypoint route with no intermediate resets. `museum-runtime.json` records the route and actual placement/bounds. The process exits 0.
- Four actual museum views show the whole, entrance, lower junction and upper return. They are rendered captures, not generated illustrations. The test uses a private museum snapshot/output folder; it does not restart the user's game or replace the live bake.
- `content-checks.json` verifies retained artifacts, manuscript/primary order, the preserved paragraph, the source excerpt, passing reports and capture files.

Headset comfort, Quest timing, and learner response have not been tested. Width and new-opening questions in the prose are invitations to further making; the visitor cannot currently resize or cut the cave. The walk test controls the desktop body's movement with the museum's normal input/catch logic paused. It establishes these continuous physical routes, not every possible movement through the hall.

Existing startup warnings about a UID, certificates, shader-cache writes and time-limited decorative dressing occur in the museum log. They did not prevent the two authored encounters or the tested routes from loading. No unrelated systems were repaired in this pass.
