01 / The work changes what a surface can mean

A black circle is also a room.

In Descent into Limbo (1992), a circular opening in a gallery floor is treated with a deep black pigment. From above, the opening can read as a dark disc laid over the floor. The visible surface gives the eye almost nothing to measure. The work’s force lies in the distance between what the eye can settle and what the body knows it might approach.

This is not simply an image of a hole. It is an actual cavity whose darkness makes its depth difficult to judge. The work asks perception to carry risk and desire before certainty arrives.

For our project, the useful question is exact: can we build two circles with the same outline, one a real recessed volume and one a dark image on an intact plane, then notice what changes as the viewer moves?

03 / Proposed investigation / not a Godot capture

Hold the circle still.
Change what lies below it.

1

Keep the boundary fixed

Use one circular outline, one viewing position, one light, and one dark value. Only the geometry below the outline changes.

2

Compare from three positions

Look from the approach, shift sideways, then inspect the cross-section. Record what parallax, edge occlusion, and the ray hit reveal.

3

Name what the eye cannot answer

At what point can you tell which circle is recessed? Does a stable, legible floor matter more than visual uncertainty in a space someone can enter?

The eye measures a surface. The body asks whether the floor continues.

This is a controlled comparison, not a dare. Keep the visitor on a supported route; reveal the actual section from a protected viewing position. The study is about how an image and a collision volume can disagree, and how a moving viewpoint changes that disagreement.

04 / Placement in the capability ladder

Meet the void first.
Make the illusion later.

Encounter: late in Boolean Surfaces, after the student has seen a real CSG boundary and its collision. The room can present the two circles as a looking exercise. The visitor need not be taught materials or rendering internals to ask what appears continuous.

Making return: later, in Shaders, when a learner can keep geometry fixed while changing the image on it. They can build the flat version and compare it against the already understood cut. This borrows Boolean construction and adds a render/material operation; it does not need a new foundational rung.

05 / Historical grounding and limit

A relation, not a reconstruction.

Kapoor describes the Documenta room as a hole in the floor that could be mistaken for a black carpet. The artist-site essay on the work describes the dark opening and its uncertain depth. These sources ground the perceptual problem; the page does not reproduce the artwork or claim to resolve its meaning.

The digital comparison can make a cavity and a dark surface answer differently to geometry, viewpoint and collision. It cannot carry the scale, material, institutional context, bodily exposure or encounter of Kapoor’s installation. Nor does a visually similar black circle establish influence or equivalence.
