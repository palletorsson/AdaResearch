Draft encounter / read without the code

A pose left

Turn the rose-coloured plate. Three others travel with it. A fifth stays beside the support. The joint gives your movement a place to begin and a limit at which to stop.

Let go. The plates remain where you left them. From this side they shelter a small pocket of air. Look from the other side and the pocket opens. There was more room in the shape than its first outline allowed you to see.

Lygia Clark made metal planes available to the hand through hinges. In her Bichos, handling becomes part of the work.1 Here, five stored angles hold a configuration. Someone has chosen the joints, their limits and the starting pose. Your movement enters that arrangement and leaves it changed.

Choose Return it. Turn the plate again and release it. The object slowly restores its starting angles. What looked like an invitation now has an expiry: you may change the shape while you hold it. Afterward, the room takes the change back.

Choose Leave it. There is no completed silhouette to recover. There are still limits. The fixed support cannot be lifted, and every hinge has its stops. An unfinished form can be made from quite definite decisions.

Follow one corner as you turn a parent joint and then its child. Start again, reverse the order, and give both joints the same final angles. The plates meet the same pose. The corner has drawn another path through the air.

The shape keeps where you left it. The line keeps something of how you arrived.

The shape forgets a path.

The old artifact description claimed that reversing the order of two joint adjustments must give a different final shape. The native test contradicted it. For this hierarchy, identical final local angles produce identical final plate transforms.

The corner trace reveals what the final pose leaves out. Joint 1 then joint 2, and joint 2 then joint 1, take different paths through the room. Both end at the same angles: 50° and 30°. Other joints keep their starting values.

This brings Point Trace back into Rotation. A record of movement can distinguish experiences whose final shapes are identical.

Making / a different reading mode

What happens after you let go?

This study comes after Rotation. It uses the retained values and _process(delta) introduced in Point Zero, local frames from Coordinates, and a turn about an axis. The optional corner record returns to Point Trace. Day Zero supplies a project you can open and run.

The room, plates, hinges, mouse input and camera are supplied. The code you change is score.gd. It is the actual policy used by the study, separated from the fabrication and viewer.

First, keep a change

Open project.godot and run the supplied scene. Select a joint, move its slider, and let go. You can also drag a plate directly. The rose colour identifies the selected plate. Other plates can move because they are descendants of its joint.

The score gives an angle to the existing artifact:

specimen.set_hinge(joint, degrees)

The artifact clamps that angle to the joint’s limits and writes its rotation. No instruction returns it afterward. Remaining still here is a retained value; the program has not calculated the friction needed to hold real metal up.

Then, decide what release means

These lines in the learner score determine what follows a release:

var return_on_release := false

func release() -> void:
    returning = return_on_release

The buttons choose this policy without replacing the shape. Return it sets the policy; the next release starts the return. Leave it cancels any return and keeps the current pose. Change degrees_per_second from 45.0 to 15.0, run again, choose Return it and repeat the same movement. The target stays the same; the time allowed for your intervention changes.

The return uses this line for each joint:

var next := move_toward(float(current[joint]), float(starting_pose[joint]), degrees_per_second * delta)

current and starting_pose are arrays of angles in degrees. Their indices refer to the same five joints. delta converts the chosen angular speed into this frame’s step. move_toward stops at the target rather than overshooting it. The rest of advance() applies those values and stops once they all arrive. Read the complete executable score after making the comparison.

The stored starting pose is a reference selected by us. There is no mathematical requirement for the object to return to it.

Carry the trace into a new problem

With Leave it selected, enable Follow one corner. The marker belongs to the far corner of plate 5. Keep the camera still.

  1. Start again. Set joint 1 to 50 degrees, then joint 2 to 30 degrees.
  2. Keep a screenshot or note of the corner’s path.
  3. Start again. Set joint 2 to 30 degrees, then joint 1 to 50 degrees.

The final local angles are identical. So is the final pose. The intermediate positions and their traces differ. This is not the earlier experiment of applying world-X and world-Y rotations in opposite orders to the same rigid body. Here you assign values to two different joints in a fixed hierarchy.

A first work: keep this joint tree and give it your own starting pose, reachable-angle choices and release rule. Make two versions with identical geometry and ask someone to describe what each allows their action to leave behind. Show their actions before explaining your rule.

What remains supplied

The production specimen builds the meshes and their parent-child transforms. Its local hinges can be inspected later; you do not need to reproduce its material library, initial-pose search, thick XR pickup volumes or caption system for this exercise. The desktop kit intentionally runs without the XR addon.

The model does not solve gravity, plate-to-plate contact or contact with the plinth while a hinge is moved. The initial pose is checked by sampling; a later edit can still pass through another surface. Studying those physical constraints is a later Form Finding task. A typed angle and a valid transform do not, by themselves, make a physically possible object.

## The editable lesson. Plate fabrication, camera and input are supplied.
var specimen
var starting_pose: Array = []
var return_on_release := false
var returning := false
var degrees_per_second := 45.0

func setup(body) -> void:
	specimen = body
	starting_pose = specimen.hinge_angles()

func turn(joint: int, degrees: float) -> void:
	returning = false
	specimen.set_hinge(joint, degrees)

func release() -> void:
	returning = return_on_release

func advance(delta: float) -> void:
	if not returning:
		return
	var current: Array = specimen.hinge_angles()
	var complete := true
	for joint in current.size():
		var next := move_toward(float(current[joint]), float(starting_pose[joint]), degrees_per_second * delta)
		specimen.set_hinge(joint, next)
		complete = complete and is_equal_approx(next, float(starting_pose[joint]))
	returning = not complete

func reset() -> void:
	returning = false
	for joint in starting_pose.size():
		specimen.set_hinge(joint, float(starting_pose[joint]))

Research notebook / source, code, observation, revision

Following Clark into the code

A work, not an artist-shaped category

The documented anchor is Lygia Clark’s Bicho desfolhado (Husked Creature), dated 1960, with the Reina Sofía example produced in 1973. Its recorded materials are aluminium sheet and hinges. The museum connects its organic character to the participant’s interaction, rather than resemblance to an animal. Collection record.

MoMA’s discussion of the Bichos emphasises instability, manipulability and the absence of an ideal shape or privileged front and back. These conditions matter when translating the work into an interface: a fixed root and a reset command are substantial additions. MoMA audio transcript, in Portuguese.

Luis Pérez-Oramas places Clark’s hinge in relation to the seam or interval between surfaces. His account also questions a simple story in which flat painting merely escapes into three dimensions. This gives the study a more precise question: what can a relation between planes do? MoMA, “If You Hold a Stone,” part 2.

These sources establish a working relation, not an exact digital reconstruction. Another catalogue entry, Máquina (1962), describes 24 shaped plates; our existing artifact uses six identical rectangles. A visual resemblance cannot establish equivalence of construction. Clark archive.

What the existing artifact supplied

bicho_hinge already constructs six plates in a branching tree, with five bounded rotations. Child plates inherit their ancestors’ transforms. Releasing a plate retains the last angle. The initial pose is selected by a deterministic search that rejects sampled clashes with the plinth and floor.

It also fixes the root to a support, gives thin plates a larger XR grab volume, assigns angular stops and searches for an opening pose that reads well. The object already carries editorial decisions. They are useful to inspect because they are concrete.

Three possible studies, and the one we chose

  • Widen one stop. Keep the tree and change one angular limit. This exposes what the joint permits, but current kinematics can allow plates to intersect.
  • Remove the fixed support. Allow the whole assembly to be handled. This would move closer to physical handling, but requires a tested arrangement of bodies, joints, contact and pickup.
  • Change what happens after release. Keep the same assembly and compare a retained pose with a return to its starting angles. This can be tested with the existing artifact and makes an interface decision consequential.

We chose the third. It does not require a reward, a target silhouette or another object. One setting changes what the visitor’s action is allowed to leave behind.

The code changed the argument

The previous source and registry described the reachable angles as a transformation group and claimed that adjusting a parent and child in reverse order must produce different final poses. The implementation did not support that claim.

For this model, every plate transform is computed from the same final joint-angle vector. Assigning those independent values in a different order does not change the resulting product. The order of factors in the hierarchy remains fixed. Bounded angles form a configuration domain; they are not a group closed under composition.

The native probe confirms the same final plate transforms for both adjustment orders. Recording one corner reveals different intermediate paths. The trace therefore gives us a stronger connection to the earlier chapter: a final shape does not contain a complete account of the movement that reached it.

The pointer test found a second, practical error. Godot’s positive local-X rotation takes +Z toward −Y. The old atan2(p.y, p.z) reversed that convention, so the mouse gesture and hinge motion opposed one another. A direction-sensitive test failed before the correction; atan2(-p.y, p.z) follows the intended turn and passes the native pointer and actual mouse-routing tests.

The critical opening

“Leave it” permits a range of forms to remain without grading them against a completed silhouette. “Return it” allows a visitor’s action for a while and then reinstates a chosen state. That distinction is our question for the digital study, not an intention retrospectively attributed to Clark.

A queer reading can begin with that absence of a required final body and then inspect the limits that remain. Which relations are permitted? What is fixed so firmly that the visitor cannot put it into question? Five flexible joints still depend on a root we have bolted down. Openness is particular, designed and testable.

What the evidence supports

The native report records 29 focused checks: production scene transforms against their solver; carried and unaffected branches; retained angles; authored limits; return policy; equal endpoints and unequal corner paths; production pointer direction and release; physics-ray picking; real mouse events; and GUI selection. Captures were rendered in Godot 4.6 with GL Compatibility on the available desktop GPU.

The portable project copies the production specimen and three dependencies byte-for-byte. The viewer substitutes a quieter metallic finish, highlights one plate and supplies the room, camera, input panel, return policy and optional trace. Those are declared apparatus, not changes to Clark’s work. The return is a timed interpolation, not a spring simulation.

No headset, main-museum installation, material validation or learner trial was performed. No plate-to-plate collision solver was added. Pose retention lasts within this running scene; it is not a saved record across sessions.

Where it leads next

The next substantial experiment is releasing the fixed root. Keep the existing joint arrangement and compare the supported assembly with a whole object that can be lifted. The test must include its weight, contact and how the hand transfers motion through it. That belongs with Form Finding and physical constraints. It should begin with a small two-plate system before extending the full tree.

The writing can then ask what changed when the support stopped being guaranteed. That question will follow the tested behaviour.
