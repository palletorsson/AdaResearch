Where is the pink?

Let one lamp find the edge of the sphere. Add another. The body keeps its shape, but a boundary you had been using to read that shape has moved. On the floor, its shadow divides and overlaps. The pink reaches beyond the object we intended to study.

We have met this sphere before. Now we can keep its construction still and investigate the conditions under which it appears. The lamp's colour is one value; what we see ranges through dark rose, grey and bright pink.

THIRTY-TWO SETTINGS / READ LEFT TO RIGHT, THEN DOWN

Five on/off switches give 32 settings including all-off. Consecutive frames change one switch. A faint fixed ambient contribution remains. This is our ordering, not the original work's 28-plate arrangement.

Pink light, or a pink body?

Keep the same two lamps in the same places. First let their pink fall on a pale sphere. Then make the lamps white and assign pink to the sphere's material. Look at the floor before deciding that these are the same colour.

This comparison exchanges two assignments. It is separate from the series above, which changes only the switches. The scene uses RGB lights and a material model; it does not simulate a spectrum or coloured indirect light bouncing from the sphere.

The colour of a body is not a complete description of how it will appear.

A return in Color_Flashlight

This fits beside the existing lamps and faceted sphere. Point One gave us a sun we could switch off. The primitive gave us a sphere. Arrays gave us a way to compare a family. Here those earlier operations let us ask a new question of the same body.

In the hall, I would give the live sphere one place and the comparison sheet a wall beside it. A visitor could move around the sphere, then return to the fixed view recorded on the wall. The small station would lead into the white room where the light changes around the visitor. This is proposed placement; the standalone study has not yet been installed in the museum.

The reference

LeWitt's A sphere lit from the top, four sides, and all their combinations (2004) comprises 28 inkjet prints, documented in the catalogue raisonné. Fraenkel Gallery describes the four-by-seven arrangement. Our pink, 32-state experiment is a response to its method, not a reconstruction of its exact lighting or selection.
