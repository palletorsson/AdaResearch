01 / Arrays → the third dimension

A place still held

Change the fragments. Keep every address.

A fragment hangs where the grid says it should. Its neighbours keep the same distance, although their edges no longer agree. The arrangement can remember a place for each piece; it cannot tell us what broke, or whether anything here can be repaired. Move around the rows. A small shift of your body separates what looked like one vertical line into many. Order survives in the distances between things that have lost their regular shape.

02 / Transformation → scale and body

Who fits through?

Change the opening. Keep the visitor the same size.

A wall stands inside the room. There is room around it, and an opening through it. From a distance, either route looks available. Up close, one of them may refuse you. The wall has not moved; only the width of its opening has changed. Your body makes that small difference consequential. A dimension becomes a condition of entry when something tries to pass.

03 / Colour → composition, with a return in shaders

A world from a cone

Change the height. Keep the forest’s sites and parts.

Three cones are enough for us to begin calling something a tree. Forty-eight groups become a forest; a glossy strip between them becomes water. Nothing in a cone knows about a tree. The resemblance gathers from repetition, colour and the things placed nearby. Lower the trees and the world becomes a collection of small objects again. The pieces have kept their addresses. What changes is the scale of the place we imagine ourselves entering.

What the learner brings

Reuse primitives, scale, repetition and materials. The seeded site generator and curved arch are supplied. First change tree height; then exchange one part or one material.

04 / Shaders → patterns across surfaces

The pattern crosses over

Move the pattern. Keep the flowers and room still.

A black square reaches the edge of a petal. The flower does not move, but the square leaves it. The same change carries colour across the floor and the wall. For a moment a surface looks continuous with its surroundings; then another patch makes its edge appear again. We can still name the flower. Following its outline has become less certain. The room holds its shape while the pattern unsettles the places where we thought one thing ended and another began.

05 / Shaders → Queer Rubber and Pink Extravaganza

A body in the plural

Unfold the body. Keep its six arms and three heads.

The arms draw inward, then open. Three heads remain attached to the same trunk. Each part is a familiar surface, but their arrangement asks us to decide what belongs to a body. The pink rim follows our view; it offers no evidence that the material would yield to touch. Even this excessive figure obeys a strict agreement between its left and right sides. One next change could break that agreement. What would we keep recognising then?

Select a state to compare. Continuous control and walking are in the downloadable project.

What the learner brings

Reuse transforms, interpolation and view-dependent shading. Curved limbs are supplied meshes. Alter one attachment, then one side’s material. Rigging, scans and physical deformation would require separate lessons.

Where they are now

These are independent chapter companions, with proposed homes recorded in the chapter manifest. They have not been placed in the main museum halls or inserted into final.md. There is no headset or learner-trial claim.

The exercises identify borrowed apparatus. A learner can change the central rule before knowing how to build every mesh or the viewing system.
