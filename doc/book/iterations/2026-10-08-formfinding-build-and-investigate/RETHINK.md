# Form Finding — purpose before expansion

Status: experimental drafts; not an accepted sequence revision. The author asked to rethink the subject on 8 October before the new layout was validated. The original map layouts and tutorial texts have been restored; proposed versions are retained separately. Existing final.md texts were never changed in this pass.

## The capacity worth adding

After Forces, connect the familiar bodies. Choose supports, relationships and permitted motion, then observe the form those conditions allow. The learner moves from placing every part to authoring a system that can find a configuration.

The object can remain a collection of familiar points and their connections: two anchors and a hanging chain; a connected net; a supported or balanced assembly; a family of possible arrangements. Positions, connectivity and pinned/free states carry forward. The learner can alter a condition, compare results and retain a configuration as their own sculpture.

## Why the existing six halls need another decision

The current collection contains several different ways of arriving at a shape. The cable builder draws Bézier spans from handles. The laundry line and catenoid evaluate formulas. The relaxation bench iterates position constraints. The mobile encodes an ideal balance calculation in a static sculpture. The annealing cabinet searches an objective. The forge visibly approaches a predetermined body while its neighbour applies a success detector.

These are valuable comparisons. They do not all demonstrate a form emerging under physical constraints. The current order begins with a search on an abstract landscape, so the learner can miss the connection between the previous Forces chapter and the shape of a connected body.

## Proposed teaching arc — not applied

1. **Hold the ends, release the middle.** A small hanging chain. Distinguish a drafted curve from positions that settle under a stated model.
2. **Move a support.** Extend the chain into a net; change a pin or rest length. Make the global consequence of a local relationship visible.
3. **Add a load.** Investigate how support, mass and a chosen balance model shape an assembly. Compare a calculated pose with a physical disturbance test without claiming they are identical.
4. **Find another resting form.** Different initial configurations can reach different outcomes. Introduce the objective and local search through a concrete unresolved shape problem. The landscape can serve as a comparison.
5. **Permit a worse intermediate state.** Change the search rule while retaining the same problem; distinguish the current candidate from the best retained result. Annealing belongs where this distinction becomes useful.
6. **Choose what counts, keep what happened.** Write a criterion, construct a counterexample and retain an arrangement the criterion overlooks. A successful stopping test and a worthwhile sculpture make different claims.

The existing names and artifacts can support this arc. The order and the primary implementations are still open. Do not move maps or rewrite all six reflections from this note alone.
