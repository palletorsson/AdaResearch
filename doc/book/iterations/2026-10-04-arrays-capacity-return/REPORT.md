# Arrays — a record and the ways it is read

4 October 2026. First sequence in the requested one-at-a-time follow-up to the whole-spine capacity report. Shaders was revised separately. [Open the illustrated before-and-after review](index.html).

## Decision

Keep all five halls and their order. The main writing is already strong: an empty slot, a place above the head, an apparent ridge, a turning wall and a beat make different consequences of an address available. Concentrate the teaching on a reusable operation: **locate → read → change → interpret through a named receiver**.

The revisions are to full and compact teaching summaries, sequence metadata and their generated reading copies. They do not implement teaching adapters or rewrite full tutorials. The before text is the exact pre-edit snapshot; the after text is the current authored proposal. Museum images retain their original capture dates. There is no new visual before-and-after because the exhibits did not change.

## What changed and why

| Hall | Before | After | What this gives the student |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Array | Two required steps: nested occupancy, then retained random departures | Secure row-to-grid addressing first; Molnár variation becomes optional | A record and reader they can change without confusing absence, reference or spacing |
| Tutorial_3D | Generic distinction between displayed XYZ and layer/row/column | Explicit `[x][z]` → `[y][z][x]` conversion, tested with unequal indices | A visible convention change rather than a hidden transpose |
| Floor | A quarter-turn rule and exception | Same rule, with declared orientation semantics and a supplied image renderer; predict locally, enlarge globally | Zero turns no longer risks being confused with an empty slot |
| Walls | A small upright application and physical comparison | The same learner image returns; the eye chart and outside landing remain a reflective pause | Reuse without compulsory new syntax; source, appearance and access stay distinct |
| Disco | A short score with separate clocks | Explicit track/step meaning, event/tempo variation and a 16-to-12 address comparison | A timed reader and a clear limit on what its floor response can tell us |

The repeated operation develops across different records. It is not one physical array already shared by every exhibit. A function, written convention or manually retained record can establish continuity while museum transport remains proposed.

## Evidence checked

Two parallel readers reviewed teaching lineage and source behavior. The five finals and four existing tutorial companions still match the bytes used by the whole-spine report. Source inspection confirmed the four-entry row, grid storage in X/Z, flight record in Y/Z/X, generated raster tilings, four-by-sixteen score and twelve-by-twelve floor. Exact source paths accompany each revised hall in the illustrated review.

In particular, the floor renderer derives its tiles into an image; it does not maintain the proposed learner occupancy matrix. The sequencer and floor have independent clocks, with sounding steps linked to floor columns by a remainder operation. There is no footstep-to-score recording path in the inspected components.

The original museum photographs are context for those encounters. They are not new runtime checks or evidence that the proposed learner exercise has been installed. No headset or student session was run.

## What is still open

The old **Disco tutorial remains incorrect for the current encounter**. Its 17×17 recording floor has not been repaired by changing metadata. Array still lacks a full companion; Floor and Walls mainly redirect to the main text; the 3D companion does not teach the new mapping. These are the next full-lesson writing tasks after the selected summaries are reviewed.

The first implementation decision is where the small learner occupancy study lives and which named adapter redraws it. The binary table has setters, but updating that display alone does not recreate pickup geometry. Resolve that connection when expanding Array; do not rebuild the museum as a substitute.

Keep the current critical writing and room arrangements. The next relevant neighbour is Colour: reuse addressed assignment there while declaring what a colour value means and what receives it. Mature sequences can receive smaller interventions than this one.
