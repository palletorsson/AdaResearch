# Return visits in Point_Trace

These experiments remain in the room as secondary encounters. The book follows the drawing dots, stick, head-path comparison, measured writing desk, whiteboard and short-memory point; a return can ask a different question of the same hall.

## Give the pause another record

At the paired telemetry monitors, look at the footer and move the corresponding controller. Check that the displayed coordinates follow the movement before interpreting the rows as your hand's record. When the controller supplies the readings, hold it still and watch for another timed row.

Compare that with pausing the drawing dot. Its movement gate can leave the point count unchanged. The telemetry instead appends time and position when its timer reaches the sampling interval. The two instruments make different accounts of waiting.

DEMO FEED labels the synthetic fallback. The current TRACKING label means the script found a controller node; its lookup does not verify an active pose or choose the currently driven rig. A staging controller can therefore make that label too confident. Checking the active source is the next implementation improvement before relying on this comparison as evidence of a visitor's pause.

## A mark that seems to know you

The desk in this hall now records measured head height against time, as a primary encounter. This makes a pause available for comparison with a movement-gated drawing point. It starts empty, omits missing observations and holds the completed page.

The earlier `asemic` mode is preserved in the source and remains the default elsewhere. That mode generates sine-shaped handwriting whose amplitude follows head-position speed, with optional demonstration input. Its marks can still support a later comparison about interpretation, once generators have been introduced. It does not establish a person's attention or intention.
