# What the voice can know here

15 October 2026 · Editorial proposal, with one short passage to try · [Current complete reading trial](../2026-10-15-435-witness-trial/index.html) · [Author strategies](../../course/writing-patterns/AUTHOR_STRATEGIES.md)

Palle supplied a proposal bringing Ahmed, Haraway, Guattari, Hartman and other writers into the search for scientific and poetic attention. Its strongest new possibility is that encounters could change the narrator's capacity to perceive. This note turns that possibility into a local experiment. It does not adopt a new narrator for the book.

## Keep “we”; let its attention change

The working proposal is a continuous shared voice whose account becomes possible through particular encounters. At the spheres, it can compare a path and its pace. At the falling page, it can attend to something remaining visible. At the Geiger, an audible recurrence, a displayed number and a dated receipt support different claims. At MOVE / KEEP, it can distinguish a movement from what a procedure retains. At Out / Back, it can distinguish changing an excursion from changing its rule.

This need not become a story of an intelligence acquiring abilities in a fixed order. We do not have to pretend the voice has no memory before a memory hall, or make every chapter more elaborate than the last. A new apparatus may narrow what can be known. A revisit may make an earlier sentence inadequate. The voice can be revised by the research without revealing a secret human, AI or system identity.

The governing question is: **what in this encounter now permits this sentence?** A sensation needs a situated voice; a historical claim needs its source; an internal operation needs code or a record; an unperformed experiment needs conditional language. Their prose can meet without pretending they are the same kind of evidence.

Ahmed's attention to orientation suggests asking what is within reach and how that changes with contact. Hayles's account of literary materiality suggests asking what changes when motion becomes writing. These are selective Ada applications of [Queer Phenomenology](https://www.dukeupress.edu/Queer-Phenomenology) and [Writing Machines](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262582155/writing-machines/), not a claim that either author proposes our narrator.

Perec's patient observation and Le Guin's gathering are useful working operations already in the library. Flusser's apparatus question can press on the ending. Their functions can change from passage to passage. Bachelard remains useful where a measured relation and a poetic image put different questions to the same object.

## A short bridge trial

This original 168-word passage tests attention to orientation and retention in the shared voice. It is a candidate alternative for the opening through first contact with the page, not a replacement chapter or a participant report. The existing historical RAND paragraph would follow it.

<!-- sample:start -->

Three lines hang in the room. Each is two metres long. A sphere waits at each centre.

We touch the first. The visible hand travels sideways with the sphere; the room stays where it is. The hand has gone some distance from the place where we made contact. We withdraw our tracked hand, and the visible one follows it again. The sphere returns alone.

At the second line, the same turns arrive twice as quickly. There is less time inside the same distance. At the third, the visible hand also rises and falls. The line stays horizontal beneath its journey.

We press REPLAY. On contact, the first sphere takes the same turns again. Nothing on the line marks where it has been. The program has kept the positions.

Further into the room, groups of five digits fall from above, a row at a time. We touch one group. It turns amber and stays. The rows continue around it.

We take our hand away. The digits are still there.

<!-- sample:end -->

The first change is visible displacement, then release; the second is rate and direction; the third is an invisible retained path; the fourth is a visible retained group. The bridge leaves the comparison available without concluding what every act of keeping means. The cost is compression: it loses some of the current opening's pauses and its explicit allowance for unfamiliarity during replay. It may be less compelling. Only a comparison can settle that locally.

## Factual anchors and differences from the supplied example

The supplied example describes a hand sometimes ahead of or behind the sphere. In the current [hand follower](../../../../commons/artifacts/random_touch/hand_follower.gd), contact adds the sphere's displacement to the virtual hand while tracking remains intact. Withdrawal releases the override. The sphere does not exert physical force on the hand. The [artifact](../../../../commons/artifacts/random_touch/random_touch.gd) supplies REPLAY; the actual head and body stay in place. Out / Back is the later body-moving encounter.

The Geiger calculates all 96 event times before playback. Its [extraction model](../../../../commons/artifacts/geiger_counter/geiger_model.gd) divides intervals into clock units and omits a bit for a pair whose measured intervals tie. A 1 ms versus 100 ms comparison can hold the event trace fixed. We have not run or narrated that new comparison here. Coarse measurement is not a demonstration of quantum indeterminacy.

Hartman's work concerns the violence constituting archives of slavery. The useful discipline here is to stop an account from asserting what its evidence cannot support; it does not equate digital reduction with that history. See [Venus in Two Acts, pp. 11–12](https://bcrw.barnard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/Venus-2-Acts-hartman.pdf). Glissant's [“For Opacity”](https://englishes-mooc.org/media/pages/course/module-03/c65603fc2f-1571655966/glissant.pdf) suggests another question: knowing the reduction procedure need not make a person fully legible. Neither prompt requires making the algorithm obscure.

## Smallest useful comparison

1. Keep the current five-stop room order, apparatus, 435 and Geiger trial fixed. Compare the current opening through first page contact with the short passage above; retain the historical RAND paragraph and subsequent trial text for either reading.
2. Record the reader's unprompted account of what changed on contact and withdrawal, what stayed with them, and what they would want to meet or try next. Staying with the spheres, preferring the current passage or finding no useful difference are valid responses.
3. Check mechanism separately: did the prose imply the real body was dragged, physical resistance was felt, or touch generated the selected digits? A clear misunderstanding is a reason to revise even if the rhythm is preferred.
4. Retain, revise or discard one operation. Keep the actual response and exact passage. Do not convert an editorial preference into evidence about headset experience or independent reconstruction.

The first runtime research pilot remains the **48-value bench**. Clock-resolution comparisons and a retained pair of gestures yielding the same seed remain separate possible experiments. This note initiates no new runtime or scene changes.
