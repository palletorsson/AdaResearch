Which body hears it?

A cube, a stick request and a person’s physical steps can share one coloured strip without receiving the same instruction. Forces becomes strongest where we can discover that difference by moving through it.

A small change of address

The drag paragraph now speaks to the visitor holding a controller. It keeps the distinction between the stick request, a physical drag force and the steps taken in the room outside the headset. This distinction already belongs to the work; the text no longer makes VR an aside to the desktop.

What the plank keeps

The field releases its receiver at the boundary. In the air, motion continues under ordinary gravity; the default XR body regains stick steering when its feet reach support. The revised paragraph follows that sequence. Keeping the plank makes both routes available to compare.

Even stillness has a threshold

The old cube could retain a tiny motion arrow after stopping. A small movement followed by zero left the old drawing in place. The lesson now clears it before the redraw tolerance can preserve it, and clears the collapsed component marks too.

At the existing display scale, movements at or below four centimetres per second count as stillness. The text names the choice without turning the encounter into a calibration manual: “Even stillness has a threshold in this picture.” The legacy cube display elsewhere is retained.
