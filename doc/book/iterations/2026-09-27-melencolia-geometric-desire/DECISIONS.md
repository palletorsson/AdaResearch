# Melencolia — geometric desire

The user asked for the Dürer artifacts to match the objects and placement of *Melencolia I*, and for a thread from Platonic abstraction into material reality, desire, mysticism and other kinds of escape.

## Built

The existing `durer_scene` identity now loads a sculptural tableau with the large truncated solid on the left and a seated, winged figure on the right. The smaller figure, millstone, sleeping dog, sphere, compass, book, keys, purse, tools, ladder, scales, hourglass, magic square, bell and a shallow horizon relief establish the principal relationships of the engraving. The surfaces use quiet ivory, stone, dark metal and wood tones. Forms are stylised; their depths and rear surfaces are interpretive.

The former polyhedron was not the closed eight-face solid described by its source. It now has six planar pentagons and two triangular caps, twelve vertices and eighteen edges. The chosen proportions are explicitly not a claim to a unique historical reconstruction.

The map keeps the first court and its distinct geometric manuscript reference. The second deck is enlarged; a clear approach, side aisles and a descent allow circulation around the scene. Nineteen authored artifact placements remain. The decorative cable tray is refused only in this hall because it crossed the new composition at head height above the deck.

## Written

`final.md` now lets the sphere acquire a different relation beside the dog, and the compass belong to a hand that has not begun its next task. Correct geometry and the square's sum remain available without being made to explain the whole image. Walking around an engraving reconstructed in depth exposes authored decisions.

Geometric desire is the project's reading. It is not attributed to Dürer as a proven intention. The historical comparisons distinguish architectural pattern, Escher's study and transformation of repeats, Malevich's painted spatial freedom, Hilma af Klint's spiritual inquiry, and Clark's bodily participation. The last is a reason to keep “escapism” as a question rather than a label for South America. These comparisons are introduced in the chapter and developed in `critical.md`; no additional works by those artists are claimed to have been installed.

The earlier critical research is preserved under `before/`. Its claim that geometry is exhausted, or that the magic square is meaningless, is no longer the chapter's premise. The first court prose and the returning engine counter passage remain verbatim.

## Limits

The photograph is from the running museum. Figures are original procedural sculptures after the print, not scanned reconstructions. Instruments are fixed scenery, not working tools or pickup mechanics. Coarse local collision protects the main bodies; small tools and folds do not have precise individual collision. Headset comfort, label legibility and Quest performance require a separate physical visit.
