Requests and permissions

I. The room refuses

404 — NOT FOUND

The address remains. The room does not.

An intact numbered doorway and its threshold survive; beyond them there is no room, only a vast dark architectural void. The student stops with their black sphere at the edge.

The concept behind the image

A requested resource has no available representation, or its existence is not disclosed. The missing architecture is our interpretation.

403 — FORBIDDEN

You fit. You are not permitted.

A transparent closed door reveals an inviting empty gallery. The student's body clearly fits through the opening but a red lock refuses entry.

The concept behind the image

The request is understood but refused. Exclusion is a permission decision, not a geometric incapacity.

408 — REQUEST TIMEOUT

The room stopped waiting.

A very long entry slot requires the student to insert a rigid black rod; a mechanical shutter closes while the rod is still being offered, with a drained hourglass above.

The concept behind the image

A complete request was not received within the allowed time. The interrupted physical submission translates that deadline.

429 — TOO MANY REQUESTS

Each attempt becomes another weight.

The student repeatedly pulls a request cord at a closed gate. Each attempt adds another small black sphere to a suspended bucket, which now weighs down the gate's lock.

The concept behind the image

Too many requests within a time interval. The accumulation and weight are artistic inventions, not an HTTP mechanism.

Iteration, retention and waiting

II. The rule never ends

INFINITE LOOP

The exit brings you back.

A narrow looping corridor returns the student to the same scratched plinth and black point.

The concept behind the image

An iteration never reaches a terminating condition. A spatial circuit lets the body feel repetition without progress.

MEMORY LEAK

Nothing here is allowed to leave.

Black spheres left by previous visits accumulate in unreachable glass compartments until the gallery is crowded out.

The concept behind the image

Allocated memory is unintentionally retained instead of being reclaimed when no longer needed. The inaccessible balls stand for unreclaimed allocations.

STACK OVERFLOW

Another room inside another room.

Each doorway produces a smaller doorway and its frame. The expanding stack exhausts the available space and wedges against the ceiling.

The concept behind the image

Nested calls can exhaust a finite call stack. Recursive frames are a spatial analogy, not a literal memory layout.

DEADLOCK

I wait for what you hold.

Two locked display carts each hold the key required to open the other. The student looks from one to the other.

The concept behind the image

Each participant holds a resource while waiting for another held by the other; neither can progress.

References, types, order and boundaries

III. The parts disagree

TYPE MISMATCH

Fits the hand. Rejected by the slot.

The familiar sphere meets an intake that only accepts a tightly fitted triangular prism.

The concept behind the image

An operation expects a different kind of value. The shape sorter is an analogy; data types are not geometric shapes.

NULL REFERENCE

The instruction points to nobody.

A heavy instruction arrow directs the student's hand toward the empty mounting holes where a handle should be.

The concept behind the image

Code tries to use an object reference that does not refer to an object. Naming an expected thing does not supply it.

RACE CONDITION

Who moved first changed the floor.

Two independent arms move the same bridge slab in conflicting directions just as the student approaches.

The concept behind the image

The result depends on the relative timing of concurrent actions. The arms translate unsynchronised access into a shared surface.

INDEX OUT OF BOUNDS

One step beyond what was stored.

Four floor cells numbered 0 through 3 end abruptly; the visitor follows the next address over empty space.

The concept behind the image

The requested position is outside the valid index range. A four-element zero-based list has indices 0–3.

Arithmetic, time steps and control

IV. The simulation breaks

DIVISION BY ZERO

The denominator disappears.

Two measured points collapse to the same place; a large mechanical divider has a zero-sized receiving gap and its operation has no defined continuation.

The concept behind the image

A formula dividing by zero needs explicit handling. The resulting exception or non-finite value depends on the language and operation; no physical explosion is implied.

NUMERICAL INSTABILITY

A small error grows a body.

A gently bouncing sphere leaves successively larger trajectory traces; the last bounce reaches the ceiling.

The concept behind the image

Approximation errors may grow under unstable numerical evolution. Growing traces are a visual metaphor, not measured simulation evidence.

TUNNELING

The wall missed the crossing.

A fast sphere appears on opposite sides of an intact thin wall at consecutive sampled positions.

The concept behind the image

Discrete collision checks can miss a fast body crossing a thin barrier between samples; this is not quantum tunneling.

RUNAWAY FEEDBACK

Every correction asks for more.

A motor trying to centre a hanging sphere swings it farther each time, pulling the surrounding supports with it.

The concept behind the image

A feedback system can amplify a deviation instead of reducing it. The scene needs a powered actuator; a passive spring is not an unlimited energy source.

A possible underside of the curriculum

What does the rule make you live through?

Earlier, the point gave the student a way to act. Here the surrounding system exposes the conditions of that ability. The point still fits the hand when an intake rejects it. An address can remain legible after its destination disappears. A repeated operation can consume all the room left for a body.

The next room is forbidden.
The next room is this room again.

These encounters could return beside the concepts they trouble: an absent reference beside the point; a missing cell beside the grid; unreleased copies beside arrays; recursive frames beside recursion; lost collisions and excessive corrections beside forces. They do not need to become a separate lesson in every hall. A single encounter can interrupt the confidence of an operation we thought we understood.

The political opening is in the difference between failure and refusal. A locked entrance may be performing its assigned job perfectly. That does not settle whether the rule should be there. Similarly, a boundary can protect a body or exclude it; its meaning depends on what the boundary does, to whom, and under whose control.

The physical comedy remains available. But we can stay with the missing handle, the overloaded storage and the impossible exchange long enough to feel frustration and dependence as well as the joke. The student might diagnose, wait, ask for help, find another use or challenge the rule.

If developed in Godot, build bounded encounters with a way out rather than deliberately hanging or exhausting the actual application. A simulated failure can be an artifact without making the research environment unusable. None of these images proves the corresponding behavior is present or physically stable.

Terms and limits

403 is a refusal after a request is understood; 404 can mean no representation was found or that existence is not disclosed; 408 concerns an incomplete request not received in time. The architecture is an artistic translation of those distinctions. RFC 9110.

429 concerns request frequency. Adding weight to a lock is our invented physical consequence. RFC 6585, section 4.

The collision and instability plates draw on actual simulation concerns, but their dramatic outcomes are invented. Godot documents limitations involving fast bodies and thin barriers, and options for continuous collision detection. Physics troubleshooting · RigidBody3D continuous collision detection.

The division-by-zero device is symbolic: a zero distance alone is not a division operation. A tutorial would need to show the actual formula and its handling. Likewise, shape mismatch, nested doorframes and locked cases are analogies, not exact implementations of types, call stacks or concurrent processes.
