Borrowed
outlines

A font gives us more than a silhouette. It gives us decisions about the inside, the outside and what remains separate. Here, six bodies put those decisions to another use.

Two things to change

The shape we carry.
The path along which we carry it.

A straight path makes depth. Turn the section as it travels and the body twists. Change its width and it narrows or opens. Bend the path and the same section becomes a channel. Join the ends and the path itself can make a new hole.

The method lets us keep something constant while another decision changes. O returns twice here, first as an upright opening, then as a flared hollow body.

O01 / Straight depth

An opening becomes large

The counter—the empty part of the letter—becomes a space at bodily scale. Its bottom stroke also becomes a sill. A 1.7 m body fits in the raised opening, but meets the rim when placed at floor level.

A hole is not yet a doorway.

O02 / Changing width + a slight turn

The same opening, rising

Narrow the section, then flare it. The letter becomes a vessel-like body with a throat and a broad rim. Its opening continues through the bottom: this is still an open tube, not a cup.

What would we have to close for it to hold something?

B03 / Rise + twist

Two wells in one body

B gives us two separate channels inside one continuous body. The section turns as it rises. From the side it reads as a twisting wall; from above, the letter returns.

Recognition depends on where we stand.

C04 / Follow a curved path

An open channel curls back

C travels around an arc. Its gap becomes an opening along the side of a curved channel. It begins to suggest a shelter or a seat, though neither use has been tested.

Enclosing space visually does not always make a topological hole.

I05 / Close the path

A hole that was not in the letter

I has no hole. Carry its section around a full circle and join the ends: now the body has one. The top and bottom strokes become circular flanges, with a thinner wall between them.

The path contributes form as well as the profile.

:06 / Carry both pieces together

One character, two bodies

The colon supplies two disconnected regions. They bend and turn together, but remain separate. One character, one generator and one mesh resource can still contain two bodies.

Being named together does not mean being joined.

A small vocabulary, many possible uses

The working forms suggest columns and chimneys, open vessels, handles, channels, frames, screens and passages. A symbol font can supply a different starting vocabulary. A hand-drawn contour or a vector drawing could enter the same construction once its region has been triangulated correctly.

Lines also make form. Contours can imply a surface; repeated ribs can describe a shell; woven lines can make a screen; a profile carried along a line can become a tube or ribbon. A trace gives movement a shape that remains. The line-form possibilities connect the working luminous rings and traces to further studies in lattices, straight-line surfaces and branching.

Three further encounters seem especially useful: cut the sill of O until the opening actually admits a walking body; let B’s two wells meet in one room; carry one profile through the scales of a handle, a column and a shelter. These are proposed next experiments, not completed interactions.

There is a critical question inside the convenience. The type designer made these proportions for reading. We borrow that work when we make a room. Which of those decisions becomes visible only when someone tries to enter?

Where the shortcut ends

Here “compound 2D profile” means a region that may contain holes or separate pieces. Simply making it wider, taller or twisted does not change those connections, provided it does not fold through itself.

A branch, a merged channel or a closed bottom is a different operation. We need to construct the new connection deliberately. B cannot smoothly become O just by pairing arbitrary points: one of its holes must disappear. Nor does borrowing a font guarantee that every glyph will triangulate successfully.

The measured topology and construction

The source is a flat TextMesh. Godot supplies the face triangulation; our code builds the sweep from its boundary edges. Vector glyphs are required. For direct access to the original outlines, Godot also exposes glyph contours.

The generator does not solve arbitrary path framing, self-intersections, remeshing between different topologies or engineering feasibility.

Move around the forms

Download the Godot study

Open project.godot in Godot 4.6 and run. Press 1–6 to approach a body, 0 for the gallery. Drag to orbit; scroll to move closer. Hold right mouse and use WASD/QE to fly through the forms.
