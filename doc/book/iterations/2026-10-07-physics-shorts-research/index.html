7 October 2026 / public title study + design interpretation

Will it work? Physics as a small story

The attraction is a physical question with a visible answer. Precision and failure can both complete it. A clean fit, a well-timed catch and a mechanism that unexpectedly defeats its own task belong in the same museum.

This revises the earlier emphasis on catastrophe: every encounter should offer a satisfying ordinary success, a readable near miss, and room for an alternative outcome. Do not arrange failure as the compulsory ending.

From a watched clip to a room you can change

A title can promise an answer. In the museum, the arrangement must make that promise visible: the cup, the gap, the hinge, the approaching body. The visitor supplies the next action. A clean catch is a complete event; a miss offers information for the next attempt.

  1. Show the whole apparatus and an intelligible destination before explaining the rule.
  2. Let the player make a prediction: land, fit, balance, repeat, escape or stop.
  3. Offer one meaningful adjustment and an explicit release or start.
  4. Let the process unfold without hiding the decisive contact.
  5. Keep the result visible: a fit, rebound, jam, missed catch or new arrangement.
  6. Make another trial easy while preserving the option to keep the aftermath.

My design hypothesis is that a readable uncertainty is more useful here than continuous spectacle. If nothing can be predicted, the outcome loses its relation to the player's decision. If nothing can vary, the apparatus becomes a demonstration to watch once.

Garry's Mod's official description places tool use and player-made contraptions at its centre. That is a useful precedent. Ada can provide small initial tasks inside such freedom, then let the same pieces be used otherwise. The Physics Sandbox VR developer description likewise lists domino arrangements, linked bodies and thrusters; this is a genre precedent, not a review of how well that game works.

KNOWN BODY → VISIBLE GOAL → PREDICT → ONE ADJUSTMENT → RELEASE → CAUSAL RESULT → RETRY/KEEP. Offer precision, near miss, surprise. Show decisive contact. Reuse sphere/cube. Test mechanism, not a prescribed gag. Preserve aftermath; reset deliberately.

Proposals based on your examples / no museum changes

Twenty questions to put in the museum

Use an intelligible route through push/fit → launch/catch → fly/land → roll/slide → hinge/slider → spring/contact → attraction/orbit → composed arena. These are teaching groups for selecting encounters, not a new canonical map order. The robot pair, physics dog and complex mechanisms can be supplied sandbox encounters; they do not all require full construction tutorials here.

01 / Contact, friction, pushing direction

One box, one passage

Can I clear the door without blocking my way out?

Precision The box fits its bay and the route stays open.

Other outcome The push solves one obstruction and creates another.

Core; small puzzle using the familiar cube.

02 / Launch velocity, impulse and gravity

The cup across the room

Can I make one clean landing?

Precision The black sphere lands and stays in its cup.

Other outcome A rim strike turns the intended catch into a second trajectory.

Core; catapult/launch family is already present.

03 / Vector sum, sustained thrust, retained velocity

Two points, one landing

Can my two hands bring me to rest on that ledge?

Precision Counter-thrust produces a controlled landing.

Other outcome One hand keeps thrusting and the approach curves.

Core ability already chosen: aim down/back + triggers; implementation still proposed.

04 / External contact impulse and reaction

Push away from the wall

Can I free a platform that has carried me into a corner?

Precision A push against the wall moves the platform out.

Other outcome A poor contact point turns it and wedges it again.

Small skateboard/trolley variant. A paddle in empty air must not supply unexplained propulsion.

05 / Orientation, collision shapes and clearance

Will it fit?

Can this cube pass through this slot?

Precision A deliberate rotation finds the opening.

Other outcome A near fit jams on an edge.

Return to Transformation inside a physical encounter.

06 / Hinge axis, gravity, angular limits

The falling pole becomes a bridge

Where will its far end land?

Precision One released pole spans the gap.

Other outcome It stops short or tips a second support.

Priority prototype; one hinge and one readable destination.

07 / Constraints, rotary-to-linear conversion and timing

The crank and the waiting cube

Will the piston meet the cube while the gate is open?

Precision A cube advances one place each cycle.

Other outcome A badly timed contact leaves a visible jam.

Optional mechanism study after the hinge and slider have passed tests.

08 / Restoring force, damping and contact

The spring catches another ball

Will the returning point catch, miss or strike its neighbour?

Precision Two spring-mounted spheres exchange motion in a readable rhythm.

Other outcome A changed spacing produces a missed contact or an extra collision.

Core spring encounter. Source energy comes from the player's stretch or a declared preload.

09 / Mutual attraction and sensitivity to initial conditions

Three bodies, two beginnings

Will nearly identical starts stay together?

Precision A chosen repeating family holds its visible organization.

Other outcome A different starting condition changes the partnership or sends a body away.

Later Forces comparison; preserve deterministic replay and inspect integration error before claiming chaos.

10 / Release velocity, contact, mass and friction

One ball, ten pins

Can I clear the arrangement with one release?

Precision A legible line of contacts yields a strike.

Other outcome A pin remains standing or rebounds into a different lane.

Priority prototype after the point has a verified dynamic receiver.

11 / Pad impulse, ballistic flight and timing

Poles over the burning void

Can I arrive on the next pole with the right velocity?

Precision A chain of well-timed landings carries the player across.

Other outcome A late or sideways approach misses the next receiver.

Course assembled from the already taught pads and gravity; no new compulsory force law.

12 / Prediction of a trajectory and a visible tolerance

The three-ring mortar range

Can the sphere pass through the rings and reach the cup?

Precision One arc passes the openings cleanly.

Other outcome A ring contact redirects the sphere.

Candidate interpretation of the donut/mortar idea: torus targets return from Primitives. Hollow colliders must match the openings.

13 / Friction, angular motion and rotational constraints

Slide, roll or tip?

Which body arrives first, and in what orientation?

Precision The chosen body reaches its matching receiver.

Other outcome A sliding cube tips and changes its contact.

Priority controlled comparison. First compare the same body with rotation free/locked, then compare shapes.

14 / States, conditions, grasp/release and conflicting goals

Put it away / put it back

Which machine gets the last word?

Precision Each arm completes its own transfer reliably.

Other outcome Together they produce an endless exchange; blocking one changes the cycle.

Sandbox/reflective encounter with supplied controllers; do not smuggle full robot kinematics into the first Forces tutorial.

15 / Support, applied impulse, recoil and control limits

The four-legged turret

Can it keep its aim while its support changes?

Precision It fires from a stable stance.

Other outcome A push or displaced support forces a recovery and opens a crossing.

Later arena opponent; the existing commanded gait is not a physics dog. Telegraph its action and allow diversion/obstruction as well as shooting.

16 / Constraint removal, gravity and redistributed load

Cut one support

What will the remaining structure carry?

Precision A chosen cut makes a pole fall into a useful bridge.

Other outcome A different support falls or the load lands elsewhere.

Optional chopping encounter using authored breakable connections; not a claim of simulated wood grain or general mesh fracture.

17 / Impulse, friction and balance

The skateboard and the point

Can I cross the room and stop on the mark?

Precision One push and a measured brake produce the stop.

Other outcome The board travels farther than its load can remain balanced.

Embodied return to known forces; desktop/tabletop ball-on-board comparison remains available.

18 / Constraint contact, tangential departure and gravity

The transparent pipe

Where does the ball go when the curve stops guiding it?

Precision The exit points at a receiving cup.

Other outcome The ball leaves tangentially where the player expected it to keep curving.

Priority apparatus with a visible whole path. Use a declared spring/impulse source, not unexplained pneumatic realism.

19 / Rates, retained stock, contact and available space

The conveyor fills the room

Can the receiver keep up?

Precision Matched input and removal sustain a steady flow.

Other outcome Backlog occupies the route and changes later contacts.

Core combination from the known cube and existing conveyors.

20 / Composition of already learned contacts and constraints

One release, five transfers

Will the first push ring the final bell?

Precision The whole relay completes.

Other outcome It stalls at a contact the player can inspect and adjust.

Final sandbox: choose a few verified mechanisms, not every exhibit active at once.

Source inspection / no new Godot run

What the project already gives us

Hinge crank

Creates rigid wheel and rod with HingeJoint3D, PinJoint3D and SliderJoint3D. The inspected script sets target speed but does not enable the hinge motor. A runnable mechanism test is needed before treating the comment's powered motion as verified.

  • algorithms/joint/03_hinge_crank/HingeCrank.gd

Drawbridge

Creates a rigid bridge and a limited hinge, changes target velocity from keys/idle oscillation. It likewise does not enable the motor in the inspected script. Review hinge orientation and limits while testing the actual movement.

  • algorithms/joint/09_drawbridge_hinge/DrawbridgeHinge.gd

Slider press

Builds a SliderJoint3D and drives the rigid piston with an applied-force velocity controller. The comment's declared Z guide and the joint rotation need verification; the explicit delta * 60 factor in applied force also needs a timestep review. It is a useful candidate, not a proven stock actuator.

  • algorithms/joint/04_slider_press/SliderPress.gd

Bowling

A small bowling study already creates Mover balls and pins, applies gravity/friction and scores motion/height thresholds. This inspection does not establish full rigid-body ball-pin contacts or working hand release. Reuse its layout intent; verify or replace the receiver mechanism before calling it a playable physics lane.

  • algorithms/forces/games/force_bowling_vr.gd
  • core/mover.gd

Robot arms

Both inspected artifacts construct articulated-looking props in configured poses. The pair that picks up and returns a cube would be a new interaction, not a switch on an existing grasp controller.

  • commons/artifacts/robot_arm/robot_arm.gd
  • commons/artifacts/industrial_robot_arm/industrial_robot_arm.gd

Four-legged opponent

The existing walker has four IK leg chains and a commanded sequential stepping gait. It is a visual/behavioral starting point, not evidence of force-supported balance. A back-mounted turret, recoil response and physical support would be explicit additions.

  • commons/hazards/octapod_crawler/four_leg_critter.gd

Mortar and three bodies

The mortar contains aim/charge, preview and rigid-shell firing logic. Its variable called force sets launch speed; keep speed and force distinct in the tutorial. The three-body study exposes initial-condition families and trails. Neither was run in this pass; do not promise every initial condition becomes chaotic or escapes.

  • commons/artifacts/mortar_vector_siege/mortar_vector_siege.gd
  • algorithms/physicssimulation/threebodyproblem/ThreeBodyProblem.gd

The cube conveyor

The familiar sorting line and Forces backlog apparatus give two concrete precedents for input, accumulation and delayed consequences. Preserve their documented finite-body/storage policies rather than implying an unbounded simulation.

  • commons/artifacts/cube_sorting_line/cube_sorting_line.gd
  • commons/artifacts/timing_machines/conveyor_backlog.gd

Godot documents that a hinge motor is disabled by default and must be enabled to turn the joint. HingeJoint3D reference. The flags above are research findings, not repaired or runtime-verified claims.

The first five builds

  1. Falling hinged pole: one release, one arc, one clear landing.
  2. Transparent pipe: the point remains visible while a constraint changes its route.
  3. Mini bowling: repeatable skill, legible contact, satisfying success and near misses.
  4. Incline comparison: one controlled change makes sliding, rolling and tipping tangible.
  5. Conveyor: the strongest existing example of a small rule accumulating into a changed room.

Keep two-sphere flight as the continuing ability route. Use these small apparatus to make the forces legible around it. The robot dog and robot-arm duet need more implementation and can follow after the basic contacts and constraints are trustworthy.

For teaching, the success criterion is stronger than a funny ending: the student can name the relation, change a relevant parameter, predict a difference and reconstruct the minimal working mechanism. Reflection can stay with the competing intentions and the aftermath.
