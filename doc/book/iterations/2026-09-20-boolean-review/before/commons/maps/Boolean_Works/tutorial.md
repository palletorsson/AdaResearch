# Tutorial · When the Negative Gets Big

This is the scale test. In the first hall a subtraction made a cavity in a small solid. Here the negative is enlarged until it claims to be a room. Walk the hall in order and test that claim with your body.

Begin at the compose workbench. Three operations turn above a table: union, intersection, subtraction. Look for the promised handoff and notice what the scene actually affords. Then cross to the paired subtraction stations. Compare the cut and its material before you trust the word *same*.

The recursive cube is the hinge. Its holes resemble boolean subtraction, but the code never removes a volume; it declines to build selected cells. At the architectural cavity and the SDF cavern, ask the practical question: where is the floor, and what collides with your body? A rendered hollow is not automatically an inhabitable hollow.

Finish at the boolean variations and coincident face. The room has moved from verb to apparatus: a label, a camera, and a mesh can all claim an inside while the player remains outside it. The next sequence, swarm intelligence, changes the question from carving a body to coordinating many bodies.
