The radio has a place

Two full-size wheels, a readable face and a 0.85 m support. The primary lesson and its artifacts remain intact. Reload the updated Point_One map to try the radio.

The hall check uses the actual floor and colliders, then drives both wheels through XRTools pickup. It checks a supported final metre of approach, not a complete visit or human comfort. The grips partly obscure their lower numeric readouts from this standing view; an inclined mount is the next refinement to test. Sound quality and standalone Quest performance remain open.

Same hand, three laws

The old zero lever did not return at all. It now returns immediately on release, including pointer exit or focus loss. It is a return rule, not a simulated spring. The stepped lever keeps the native 5° detents.

One base, meaningful differences

The two variants now inherit the repaired base. Their hand poses and scene identities remain available to existing consumers. Slow pointer drags accumulate across a step instead of losing every small movement to rounding.

The focused reference is interactable_lever_demo.tscn, an opt-in configuration of InteractableDemo. Its 100 checks cover physical pickup, ray dispatch, tilted and rotated mounts, limits, release rules, tiny drag increments and agreement between controls and receivers. The radio hall adds 42 checks. The previous angular and radio suites also pass after the shared changes.

These are actual Godot renders and synthetic input through the real control code. Tracked-hand comfort, seated reach, small-text legibility and listening still need the headset. The two unresolved legacy Nostromo sound IDs remain listed in the previous radio review.
