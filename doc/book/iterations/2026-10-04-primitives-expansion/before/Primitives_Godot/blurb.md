Every engine starts with a lie about roundness. A sphere is not a sphere — it's a budget of triangles pretending. Godot gives you the knobs: radial segments, rings, height slices. Crank them low and the pretense shows. Crank them high and the illusion holds, but the machine pays.

Walk the gallery. A sphere at 8 segments is a gem. At 64, it's smooth enough to forget. The cylinder loses its circle. The torus reveals it was always a folded tube. Each primitive is a negotiation between geometry and cost — fidelity purchased in triangles.

Tessellation is the first question every renderer asks: how much resolution does reality require? The answer is never enough, and always too much.
