# Who becomes easy to address before anyone speaks?

Walk around the paired miniature rooms. Count every chair in each. Compare the head chair with a side chair, then compare two chairs around the round table. Imagine a participant who needs a clear sightline to an interpreter.

The green circle arrives already recommended by its colour. Before accepting that judgement, consider sightlines, hearing, turning and entry. A shared centre can be useful without meeting every body's needs. Geometry sets conditions for participation; it does not contain the participants' agreements or resolve unequal authority. The next design should let those conditions be compared with people using the space.

Six iterations divide a full turn, TAU, into equal angles. cos and sin place each chair on a ring around origin. The little circle carries the arrays, transformations and waves we have already learned. Equal spacing is what this loop guarantees. Equal opportunity to speak would require another kind of evidence.

The material comparison is specific: the long-table model has a head chair and six smaller side chairs. The circle has six equally scaled chairs. Count, scale, colour and position change together; the pair is not an experiment that isolates table shape.

A place at the table does not make every account agree. The next hall keeps two accounts visible.

## A question the next version can test

Build a comparison with equal chair count, scale and colour, changing only the arrangement. Test sightlines and approach routes before claiming the circle is more accessible. Keep the present pair as the openly persuasive first example.
