# What survives the journey?

A change made to a body in Assembly reappears at standing scale in the final hall. The blue starting pose and amber released pose have the same thirteen joints and twelve connections. Their agreement in count does not guarantee agreement in recognition. Walk between them before asking the ledger for an account.

The encounter now has a consequence that survives unloading its source room. The ledger reports an actual saved pose. It still cannot account for the encounter that produced it: neither the movement of the visitor's hand, the reason for the alteration nor the alternatives considered are retained. A destination can survive while the way of arriving disappears.

This is a particular decision about memory. Greater numerical precision at an endpoint cannot recover a path that was never recorded. Adding a path would make another question possible, but would not establish a visitor's intention. A record's usefulness and its incompleteness coexist.

The body's relations also survive. Moving a joint changes geometry without changing the graph. The name `l_hand` remains attached to the same joint even in a pose that troubles its recognition. The comparison makes an unfinished permission available to inspect: another arm would require changing the connections or the inventory, not merely another coordinate.

The record names no person. It can outlast a visit and be overwritten by someone else using this local installation. The empty place on a direct start and the label `LAST KEPT POSE` matter: the scene must not manufacture a past or claim ownership on a visitor's behalf.

Desire can locate the next experiment as clearly as a fault. A form one wants to enter, a relation one wants to add, a movement one wants to remember: each gives a concrete direction for another pass through the museum. The ending offers a body to return to. It leaves open what else that body might become.
