# Where was this body's destination decided?

Walk around the floating inventory and follow the part carrying the amber ring. Keep that instance in view as the body gives way to a chair or another assembly. Its position and visible form can change while the ring remains with it.

The name MolecularDesigner suggests chemistry, but the current controller arranges catalogue parts and draws declared bonds. It does not establish chemical validity. Its constructive question is still substantial: can a new body be made by changing positions and relations, or does it demand a kind of part the catalogue does not describe? The initial inventory is mutable: an assembly can retype an existing instance or create more instances. Reusing an instance while replacing its visible form offers another question about identity. Neither physical emergence nor freedom is established by a persuasive animation.

Here the restoring force returns from the earlier halls. The vector to_target points from a part to its assigned destination. The spring changes velocity and velocity changes position. A moving piece can look as if it has found its place, although another file already supplied the target. dock_damp is applied each frame, which also makes the settling depend on the update rate. The clock remains inside the body we are watching.

The material comparison is specific: the controller reuses part instances, but can replace their mesh, type and colour when assigning a new assembly. The same instance need not keep the same visual identity. Target positions are authored before the parts move.

A useful catalogue is a choice with consequences. The last hall asks us to keep one consequence available for revision.

## The hand can now supply a destination

Beside the automatic catalogue, a body repeats the existing VRBody graph at standing scale. The visitor can grab any joint and leave it elsewhere. The body retains that pose, and a changed release keeps it for the final hall. A changed hand lengthens a connection without dragging the elbow or enforcing a bone's length. This intervention makes the difference between a position, a relation and a bodily expectation available to inspection.

The name `l_hand` persists even when the joint moves somewhere that makes the name awkward. Recognition may falter before the program's classification does. The visitor may also restore symmetry without being asked: the body's expected form is partly in the assembly and partly in the observer. Those are questions for the encounter, not findings about how every visitor responds.

The workshop opens positions while keeping thirteen joints, twelve connections and a bounded working space. It does not yet let a visitor grow another arm. This is a precise next act of making: add or revise a relation, and see which new bodies become possible. The changed pose now survives the loaded visit and returns beside its starting arrangement in the final hall. The record keeps destinations but leaves out the path of the hand. That new permission brings a further question about what remembering a change should mean.
