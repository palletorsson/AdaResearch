## Reading used

Lynn Gamwell, *Mathematics and Art: A Cultural History*, Princeton University Press. Read selected passages from the [Google Drive study copy supplied by Palle](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1CaUhLfk_CvbcpKYPoIcafWBVoxSZvnw8NujHhC2thIQ/edit?tab=t.0): preface; the preface's account of chapter 2 on proportion; the section on mathematical models; discussion of Minimal Art, interpretation, display and patronage. The document is OCR with damaged words and page transitions. No page-exact quotations or claim to have read the whole book is made here.

The preface separates philosophical questions about mathematics from its practice. Its proportion discussion disputes the step from a ratio to a universal guarantee of beauty. The model discussion follows different artistic uses of mathematical objects. The Minimal Art discussion records conflicting interpretations and examines the conditions under which work is displayed. These support our questions, but do not settle an interpretation of Ada.

We do not adopt the book's broad civilisational contrasts or its judgments of individual artists as established facts. For future historical additions, verify the particular work, statement and relationship. A model, a resemblance, a documented influence and our interpretation are different claims.

## What should stay open

Melencolia does not need to expose geometry as a failed promise. Its tools work, and working tools do not choose their own purpose. The student leaves with the ability to make and with questions worth carrying into Transformation.

The controlled column arrangement isolates resolution. The varied gallery permits decisions to meet. For teaching, work from one body to a pair; the full gallery is the larger field of possibilities.
