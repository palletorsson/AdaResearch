## What this map holds (seed)

Three transformations, three ways to die. A corridor where pushing stones shove you over fire pits. An arena where rotation sweeps the floor toward the edge. A room where the centre grows and presses you out. Translation displaces, rotation sweeps, scale pressures — the pit below is the same in every case; only the geometry of your removal changes. The sequence spent five maps teaching the operations to your mind. This map teaches them to your body, and the body's version has stakes.

The `pusher_block`s run the corridor on fixed axes and schedules that never consult you. The `grower_block`s swell from ankle-height to wall-size and reclaim the floor you stand on. Between them, rotation-cube stations turn in moats of fire. And when the crossing is done, the map holds the chapter's theme-event: the transformation catalyst — `becoming_catalyst`, the bracelet, change you wear — lands here, on completion, not at the sequence boundary. This is the chapter's soul map: the fire crossing.

## Why it was built (seed)

Concept: Three geometric transformations — translation, rotation, scaling — experienced as three distinct threats, each pushing the player toward fire pits through a different spatial mechanism. The map makes the algebra of the sequence lethal so that the body learns what the mind categorised.
Sequence role: Challenge map, the sequence's limit case. After earlier maps introduced the three transformations abstractly, this map turns each operation into a physical hazard with its own room, and all three share the same fire-pit penalty for mistimed movement. Surviving it is the theme moment: the transformation mode of the catalyst unlocks on completing this map.
Critical angle: Transformation as violence. Each geometric operation — move, turn, grow — has a victim when applied to inhabited space. The pit does not care which transformation sent the learner into it, and the mathematical neutrality of the operations is only available from outside the space they act on.

## The walk

You spawn against the north wall and the corridor is already moving. The PUSH CORRIDOR: walls above head height channel you into lanes, and three `pusher_block`s slide across them on fixed axes — three metres out, three metres back, at three different speeds with three different pauses, deliberately out of phase so no single rhythm carries you through. Sunk into the lanes, the pits: squares of fire a full storey below the walking floor. The first room is the algebra of translation made carnivorous — T(p) = p + v, and v is a vector that does not consult the body it displaces. You wait, read a pusher's pause, and cross behind its back.

A single-cell throat squeezes you south, and the SWEEP ARENA opens. Two rotation stations turn on their axes here — `rc` rotation cubes, each moated by a nine-cell field of fire, with narrow bridges of true floor between the burning blocks. Rotation becomes sweep: R(p) = M·p, and the matrix does not ask whether the position is currently occupied. You cannot fight an arc; you move with it, matching its period, crossing the moat in the window the turn leaves open. The room teaches angular velocity through your pulse.

Another throat, and the GROWER ROOM — the slowest room, and the cruellest. Three `grower_block`s stand in a line, small as footstools when you enter. Then they swell — three-tenths of a scale unit per second, from 0.3 toward 3.5 — and the floor you were planning to use stops existing. Behind each grower, a fire pit waits for the body being squeezed toward it. Scaling becomes pressure: S(p) = s·(p − c) + c, and the factor does not consult the bodies being compressed. You do not dodge this room. You budget it.

## The turn (critical)

Geometric transformations are taught as neutral. A translation shifts, a rotation turns, a scaling grows; the operations act on objects, and the objects can be recovered by applying the inverse. Trans_Pit refuses the neutrality, and the refusal is precise: the neutrality of an operation is only available from *outside* the space it acts on. Five maps of this chapter let you stand outside — the transformer's seat, the gesture, the demo. This map puts you inside, and from inside, T(p) = p + v is not a shift, it is a shove; the matrix is not a turn, it is a wall coming; the scale factor is not growth, it is the room deciding you take up too much of it. Every operation has a victim when the space is inhabited, and the operations never ask. The asking is the part an ethics would insert.

The chapter's truth — *identity preserved under transformation* — is put to the fire here, and the fire finds the fine print: preserved for whom? Invertibility is a property of operations, not of bodies. The transform survives its own application; the body it acts on may not, and no inverse translates a body back out of the pit. That decoupling is the map's central claim, and it is also where this chapter — the trans chapter — carries its heaviest and most hopeful weight. Because the map is not cruel. It does not stage irreversibility as horror; it stages it as a *crossing* — chosen, walked, survivable, and real precisely because it cannot be undone. The demo offers preservation, the voice that says you can always go back. The fire answers that you can't. And then, only then, the bracelet arrives: not restoration but capability, change you wear on the wrist and carry into every room that follows.

What stays the same when everything changes? Not the body that entered — the one who crossed. Identity, this map says, is not the invariant that survives transformation untouched; it is the continuity of the one transforming. Irreversibility is not what identity loses to; it is what becoming costs, and the map pays it with open eyes and hands you forward — changed, continuous, and equipped.
