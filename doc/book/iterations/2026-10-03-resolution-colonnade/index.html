Rings of Ash

A courtyard of twenty-five totems. Pompeii-inspired red, pale bodies, bronze rims and a distant rose light. Five families of outline rise to different heights: vessels, reeds, bells, spindles and stacked stones. Corners remain visible in the light.

4 · 6 · 8 · 12 · 16

Walk across a row: the number of sides increases. Walk towards the far wall: the curves are sampled with more profile divisions. All twenty-five combinations are present.

Different bodies, a shared vocabulary. Some columns narrow into stems; others gather into swollen vessels or open like bells. Height and outline now change alongside resolution, so the gallery explores their combinations.

Each base names its profile, mesh height, sides and profile divisions. “Rings” here means subdivisions along each curved section. Straight collars retain a single height interval; adding divisions to a straight profile alone would not change its shape.

Construction, placement and checks

The twenty-five columns contain 27,140 triangles in total, excluding their plinths and room architecture. The counts measure geometry, not a device frame-rate guarantee. Flat normals let the actual surface divisions catch the light.

The native checks cover each parameter pair, finite geometry, five genuinely different profiles at equal test height, prescribed heights, plinth and overhead clearance, independent growth of both subdivision counts, solid column collision, floor support, an actual desktop walk down the central aisle, return control and recovery after leaving the floor.

For a controlled resolution comparison, turn off varied_forms on the gallery scene: all columns return to the original Vessel outline at 4.67 metres above their plinths. The reusable column and gallery are registered in Ada. The full room has its own footprint and is supplied as a standalone gallery, with source-identical code in the student download. It is ready to review for later museum placement.
