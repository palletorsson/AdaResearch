Before the hand

A spray can is already moving across the canvas. Wait before taking anything. The line changes direction, and the pigment changes colour as it travels. A painting is underway without your hand.

At the grid, a colour waited at an address until another write replaced it. Here, look back at a place the nozzle has left. Something of its passage remains. Something is already changing.

Thirty spray cans wait on the low tray. Pick up a colour, aim the nozzle at the canvas and hold the trigger. Move across the automatic line. Your gesture reaches a surface that has other business; it does not wait for you to finish.

Bring the nozzle closer. The mark gathers into a smaller area. Draw back and the same colour spreads more thinly. Hold still for a moment: the patch deepens. Movement is only one way to make a difference here. Staying also leaves more behind.

Release the trigger. Your marks stop arriving while the other painter continues. Let go of the can and it returns to its place.

From a held colour to a nozzle

Thirty cans replace the manual pigment balls in Color_Paint. Each has a coloured body, metal rims, a paper label and a forward nozzle. The automatic painter carries the same form. The original hall, canvas, tray and Albers studies remain in place.

Grab, aim and hold the trigger; on desktop, right-click carries or returns the can and the left mouse button sprays. A close nozzle concentrates colour; withdrawing spreads it more thinly. Holding still builds coverage. The shader gives each deposit a soft edge and grain, while angle stretches its footprint. Range and intervening objects constrain what the nozzle can reach. Cans return to their own docks when released.

Both human and automatic input reach the same image. The code now makes their difference through distance, direction, duration and the source of motion. The chapter follows that encounter and retains its question of authorship, ground, forgetting and the neighbouring Albers studies. The preceding Grid chapter now points toward a can rather than a ball.

What changed under the surface

The map opts in with tools:spray. Other placements keep their balls. Deposits use a bounded pool of GPU stamps, with a separate feedback material for each canvas. Gentle settling and fading preserve grain longer than the older wet brush; stochastic rounding prevents tiny changes becoming stuck in the 8-bit image. Explicit shader priming fixes the black initial canvas exposed during development. Idle brush commands clear after their single deposit.
