# Where the next encounter is earned

This is an editorial test of the two adopted finals, **What remains uncertain** (1,748 prose words) and **What the gauge forgets** (1,712), checked against new native views of the installed halls. It is the assistant's reading, not a new human reader result. The chapters, room order, materials and writing-wall layout are held fixed for this test.

My judgement: the additional space helps the objects become imaginable. The two disputed transitions are now intelligible, but the next encounter is still partly supplied by the narrator. **Cabinet → points remains the weakest join. Doors → coffee has a promising question, but its arrival does not immediately pursue it.**

## How the test works

At each boundary I ask: what unfinished action or question travels forward; what can the next apparatus do with it; and what would disappear if that next stop were removed? I also remove the final bridging sentence mentally. If the objects no longer seem related without the narrator telling us they are, the transition needs attention. This is a diagnostic, not a rule requiring every passage to end with a question.

Source checks and pictures establish what is offered. They cannot establish what a person wants next. The earlier reader's objection is evidence about the earlier edition; it is not a second vote against this revision.

## Doors → coffee: the question arrives before its occasion

The new door passage earns its extra length. Reducing the gap, trying zero, keeping the LOW/HIGH history and seeing the leaves reunite give us reasons to stay. The distinction between a shared position and a different past is its strongest discovery.

Then comes:

> What would returning require if the change involved heat passing into something else?

This is a legitimate question, but **heat enters because the writer knows coffee is next**. The visitor has just dealt with angles, records and resetting. The existing doors do not make us encounter heat or a failed physical reversal. That limit should remain visible in our judgement of the bridge.

The cups then delay their answer. Two paragraphs attend to milk and prepared visual stages before the thermal account arrives. We have been directed towards heat, but the first thing offered to the eye is a changing boundary between colours. The passage is carrying two inquiries at once.

My preferred test keeps the order and changes only the handoff: leave the doors with a question about a recoverable beginning; meet the cups as stages held beside one another; bring the printed temperature times into view immediately. Milk can remain a distinct observation whose appearance does not supply the thermal reading. The [small writing trials](trials.html) demonstrate this without replacing the final.

The native approach view strengthens this diagnosis: five small cups and their swirls occupy the table, while the larger thermal explanation is on a separate side wall. The cooler surrounding body has no separate visible object. The thermal connection therefore depends on reading, not simply on seeing the coffee. That can be a deliberate shift in attention, but another connecting sentence cannot make it an enacted reversal experiment.

## Cabinet → points: a connection is explained, but a new task is missing

SAME k is already a productive tension: arrangements differ while the readings remain equal. KEEP and REPLAY add another one: the record must preserve distinctions the dials omit. We can now ask whether those omitted differences matter to another process.

The revision redirects that attention to viewpoint:

> A record of positions says where the objects are; what lines those positions make available to us still depends on where we stand.

The claim is sound, but the cabinet can already be viewed from another side. Moving our viewpoint does not yet require the point enclosure. Removing the points still leaves a direct and compelling cabinet → stones route: a relation omitted by the dial can become a relation that carries weight.

The enclosure does offer something the cabinet cannot: **we can occupy a position within the arrangement**. That is the best reason to retain it in this route. The new native views show a field surrounding the camera, with different alignments from two positions; the cabinet remains an object in front of us. The two artifacts do not share a saved configuration or exchange data. The text must not imply that we have enlarged or entered the cabinet's actual saved state.

The next test should give this embodied difference a precise activity: follow one visible alignment, step sideways, and try to recover it without changing a point. Keep that brief. If a person still wants to go straight from SAME k to the stones, the points may work better as a short interlude or optional investigation. That is a future curation choice; this pass preserves their current place.

## The chair shaders are part of the argument

The pink/black chairs use a shared, moderately rough StandardMaterial3D surface; the frosted pane uses a separate screen-sampling shader. The relevant question is whether colour, armrests, the lower back rail and the loss of their edges remain available to attention.

In the native views:

- The clear pane shows the arms and lower rail. Intermediate panes spread their edges. C and D retain the same declared colour channel, while D obscures the form more strongly. E is opaque.
- Pink remains recognisable as a colour patch after some structural details become difficult to distinguish. This is a strong candidate gem: **a record can keep the same answer while the image changes the work of seeing it**.
- The final table's black armchairs can be distinguished in the inspected close view, but their dark seat and arm surfaces lose shading against one another. This is a visibility concern to test at normal headset distance, not proof that the category is unreadable.
- The five installed panes face different backgrounds and lighting. The central doorway adds objects behind C. Their images therefore do not isolate frosting alone.

I added a controlled render comparison on **one installed pane**: same chair, same camera and same colour record; only obscurity changes from 0.40 to 0.78, then restores. The separate pictures are labelled as an in-memory test, not as a new installed state. Decorative time-based background effects remain live. The blur difference persists without moving to another pane. See the [visual evidence](visuals.html).

The shader does not compute the printed conditional entropy from pixels or a person's recognition. The printed values follow the declared record channels. The prose should let the visual discrepancy be encountered before explaining that distinction. Screen-space frosting also needs a headset check for both eyes, movement and sampling grain; these desktop images cannot establish that result.

My next visual changes would be a consistent quiet backdrop for the five panes and a small neutral fill for the black chair forms, tested against the current versions. Avoid adding changing colours or extra shine that competes with the category distinction. These are recommendations, not changes applied in this test.
