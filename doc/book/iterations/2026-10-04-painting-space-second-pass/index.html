Another family of possibilities

Lines make form

A line can outline a body, become its structure, or acquire a body of its own when given thickness. We can leave space between lines and still see a surface. Walking around it makes those gaps return.

ApproachWhat it can make
Contours and ringsCross-sections, wireframes and open shells. The latitude and longitude study already makes a sphere legible this way.
Ribs and woven linesLattices, screens, cages and canopies. Spacing and crossing become decisions about how much remains open.
Straight lines between curvesA curved envelope built from straight segments: a string-like surface whose form changes when either guiding curve moves.
A profile along a lineTubes, ribbons, rails and knots. The path chooses where the form goes; the profile gives it width and section.
Traces and branchesDrawing accumulated into a body, or paths growing into a network. Order and duration contribute to the form.

The rings and moving traces are working studies here; the brush also lets us draw a path in space. Woven structures, straight-line surfaces and branching networks are possibilities to test next. The profile atlas already explores carrying a section along a path.

A visible line needs a width. Our luminous rings use thin cylinder segments; the brush’s light view uses a chain of points. Neither automatically fills the gaps or creates a collision boundary. A crossing may look joined while the parts remain separate. That difference belongs to the form we are making.

Where the mark is

The cursor now shows brush width. A guide drops to the floor, giving its position another reference. Changing depth and drawing from another viewpoint lets a stroke leave the flat plane of the screen.

Moving quickly fills gaps between pointer samples. Holding still deposits more marks in one place. On the sphere, intermediate marks follow a spherical arc; in free space, they connect successive positions.

F5 saves both drawings and their settings in Godot's local user-data folder, replacing the previous save. F9 brings them back. This is an explicit save, not autosave. Z can undo a stroke, clearing, restoring an example or loading a save.

Construction and verification

52 native checks passed, including mouse drawing, continuous strokes, material switching with retained positions, undo, disk save/load, invalid-state rejection, the paint seam and pole, bounded brush memory and rendered views. The native renderer reported no errors.

Soft colour uses camera-facing stamps with radial falloff. Shells use additive colour and a view-dependent sheen. These suggest volume and glass; they do not simulate filled fog or physical refraction. The space brush holds at most 900 stamps, replacing the oldest after that. Undo retains up to 12 states.

The camera flies freely without walking collision. This remains a standalone desktop study; museum placement and tracked-hand testing on Quest are still ahead.
