This is a much more useful literary experiment than the previous trial. The three candidates now do something genuinely different with the same encounter. They change the order in which knowledge arrives, what the reader attends to, and how the Geiger machine relates to the rest of the room.

My first reading, without consulting the proposed tests, produces a fairly clear distinction:

- Repetition changes the experience of duration.
- Montage changes who or what is allowed to speak.
- Recurrence changes the relationship between artifacts.

Of the three, I find montage the most promising transformation of the Geiger passage itself, but recurrence the most consequential experiment for the map as a whole.

The original reference still has the best immediate encounter with the machine.

## 1. Repetition: It becomes a performance, but is it enough?

The repeated sequence is the most conspicuous formal intervention:

> We press RESTART.
>
> Click. A gap. Another click.
>
> We press RESTART.
>
> Click. A gap. Another click.

The first recurrence confirms something. The second begins to establish a ritual. By the fourth, I am no longer primarily interested in whether the generator reproduces its results. I know it will.

I begin to wonder why we are still pressing RESTART.

That is a productive change in attention. It recalls Beckett without needing to imitate his syntax.

But the repeated words are not equivalent to repeated sound. On the page, I can glance over all four repetitions almost instantaneously. In the VR apparatus, their duration cannot be skipped in quite the same way.

The question for this strategy is whether it produces duration or merely represents duration typographically.

I wouldn't adopt this passage yet, although it suggests that repetition may become more powerful when the actual instrument, rather than the prose alone, performs it.

## 2. Montage: The most successful local experiment

I like the separation:

> On the screen:
>
> At the machine:
>
> In the program:
>
> Elsewhere in the room:

These are distinct sources of evidence, rather than successive explanations from one narrator.

The receipt has a historical date. The screen has a seed. The machine has intervals. The program has a procedure. Elsewhere, the same seed participates in other operations.

The reader has to assemble their relationship.

The most interesting addition is actually this:

> At the falling page, the selected digits remained while the rows moved on. Here the screen's number remains while the clicks pass.

This creates a relationship with an earlier encounter, but without pretending that keeping a five-digit group and retaining a generator's seed are the same process.

It passes both our artifact and map tests.

I have one technical/editorial reservation: In the program currently introduces a prose explanation, not a program excerpt. I'd either use an actual relevant code fragment or call this register something like The calculation. Otherwise the documentary form suggests more direct evidence than it provides.

And I would be careful to distinguish replaying sound generated from stored event times from replaying an audio recording.

## 3. Recurrence: The most interesting map experiment

This passage makes 435 move through the chapter.

It has been a starting input, a displayed number, a source for colour and movement, a saved atmospheric-noise result and now the beginning of audible intervals.

The number is repeated, but its function changes.

That is a strong literary operation because it makes the map readable as something other than a collection of independent demonstrations.

However, I notice two problems.

First:

> We have already travelled through one of its consequences: the sideways turns of the first sphere.

In the opening artifact, the visible hand moves while the viewpoint remains fixed. Travelled risks confusing this with the later whole-body excursion. This could be changed to followed, preserving the difference.

Second, the final sentence:

> So far, the number has entered the movement. At the next sphere, a movement will supply the number.

This is logically elegant, but it announces the inversion before the reader encounters it.

I would rather allow MOVE / KEEP to make that discovery possible. The next artifact can deliver the reversal, instead of the Geiger passage preparing its interpretation.

This is an important finding for the map-writing system: a connection may be structurally valuable without needing to become an explanatory transition.

## What this tells us about dynamic composition

The experiment has now demonstrated something useful.

The same material can support at least three different compositional functions, each justified by the apparatus. But applying them together would likely weaken the chapter.

We need a system that can decide not only which strategy fits an artifact, but where in the sequence its effect is needed.

For this particular chapter, I would currently allocate the operations as follows:

Provisional composition — Randomness

1. RANDOM TOUCH — Patient observation

   Allow the reader to discover the bounded path, speed difference and new axis without rushing to explain them.
2. RAND BOOK — Selection and retention

   Give stopping a group of digits its own duration. No need to introduce a second archival interpretation prematurely.
3. GEIGER — Montage

   Let instrument, receipt, sound and calculation provide different kinds of evidence. Retain the reference's concise audible repetition.
4. MOVE / KEEP — Inversion

   Let movement becoming a number reverse the earlier relationship. Make the collision experiment an open research possibility.
5. RANDOM RETURN — Rule and consequence

   Preserve the Beckett/Borges-like inevitability of returning. Finish with the actual constraint rather than explaining the chapter's theme.

I would not treat this as the permanent assignment of literary strategies. It is a composition hypothesis for this particular sequence.

## The next improvement is not another rewrite

I would introduce a simple consequence test into the literary metadata.

For every proposed strategy, record:

| Question                          | Example: Geiger montage                                        |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What changes in the reading?      | Evidence appears through distinct registers                    |
| What becomes newly perceptible?   | A dated acquisition is not the same as a repeating calculation |
| Which other artifact is affected? | RAND's retained digits                                         |
| What does the strategy risk?      | Becoming a labeled technical manual                            |
| What can remain unspoken?         | The philosophical meaning of preservation                      |

These are more valuable than a generic literary-quality score.

And I would ask the writing system to preserve the reference until an alternative demonstrates a meaningful consequence.

## My decision on this trial

I would retain the reference, mark montage as a promising local candidate, and keep recurrence as a map-level operation to develop further. I would not adopt the four repeated RESTART sequences yet.

This judgment is based on the passages supplied here; it is not a completed reading of all four full chapters.

What interests me most, though, is a larger discovery: the literary strategy does not have to be visible in every sentence. A motif can develop through the physical artifacts themselves, through the way the reader remembers an earlier encounter, and through what the writing deliberately does not explain.

That suggests a strong principle for the system:

The map composes relationships. The artifact realizes them. The prose attends to what happens.

Sometimes the best literary operation is to let a relationship remain implicit until the next encounter makes it unavoidable.
