# Scientific and poetic attention — authors' strategies

15 October 2026 · A working collection for Ada Research · [Writing roles](../WRITING_ROLES_AND_DECISIONS.md) · [Research loop](../ENCOUNTER_RESEARCH_LOOP.md) · [Existing source studies](PROVENANCE.md)

Palle asks for strategies we can use at will, with attention to the actual encounter. None of the three earlier sphere openings was a clear winner. This collection does not choose a narrator or rewrite the chapter. It supplies operations to try when a passage needs something specific.

The author associations below are selective editorial readings. The Ada instructions and examples are our proposals. They are not comprehensive accounts of an author's method, nor a claim that these different philosophical projects agree. Existing passage studies in craft.json remain separate from these broader strategy prompts.

## Bachelard: two demands, and a useful tension

In Chapter I of *The Formation of the Scientific Mind*, Bachelard locates obstacles within acts of knowing: familiar ideas can prevent a question from being properly formed. For Ada, an explanation should remain revisable through a particular intervention. “The faster sphere seems more random” can become an investigation of identical targets played at different rates. Speed changes; the source need not. [Primary excerpt, Chapter I](https://classiques.uqam.ca/collection_methodologie/bachelard_gaston/obstacles_epistemologiques/obstacles_epist_texte.html).

In the introduction to *The Poetics of Space*, Bachelard distinguishes the work of scientific ideas from the emergence and reception of a poetic image. This complicates any easy instruction to combine them. Our proposal is to let a measured relation and an imaginative experience question one another while retaining their different evidential status. A felt image does not verify an algorithm. A correct algorithm does not exhaust an encounter. [Primary introduction, especially I–II](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bachelard_gaston/poetique_de_espace_3e_edition/poetique_de_espace_intro.html).

The library's existing study of *The Poetics of Space*, Chapter I, pp. 3–5 in the Jolas translation, follows a movement from describing a house to inhabiting it. Our current local transfer is from seeing a two-metre line to seeing the contacting virtual hand carried along it while the viewpoint stays still. The measurement stays fixed while its relevance changes. This is an Ada adaptation, not Bachelard's own example. See the source locator and limits under “bachelard-house” in [craft.json](craft.json).

*Air and Dreams: An Essay on the Imagination of Movement* also belongs on the reading list for this artifact. Its focus suggests attending to rising, falling and suspension as active relations. This is a reading lead based on the book's stated subject, not a completed passage study. [Publication description](https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780911005134).

Use the collection by the job a passage needs: Perec for patient particulars; Calvino for a description unsettled by closer observation; Ahmed and Haraway for position and reach; Flusser and Guattari for the conditions of action; Le Guin and Beckett for gathering and recurrence. Hartman and Glissant help question the extent of an account in their distinct contexts; Barad brings a distinct question about measurement. These jobs are editorial proposals, not definitions of the authors.

## Keep scientific and poetic attention active

### Local trial: let the operation produce the strangeness

The [435 reading trial](../../iterations/2026-10-15-435-witness-trial/README.md) responds to later supplied feedback. It combines three existing operations selectively: follow the consequence of a rule; let a display and dated record speak alongside the encounter; let a number recur through different uses. Its criterion is that an unusual observation remain accountable to the apparatus. The Geiger’s 96 event times are calculated before playback, for example; replaying the sound is distinct from regenerating the record. Palle’s 15 October response to the Geiger passage, “The balance is closer”, supports preserving this local direction. It does not settle the whole trial or establish a universal Ada voice.

Scientific attention asks what happened, under which conditions, what was held fixed, and what would change our account. Poetic attention can make a scale, interval, absence or relation newly noticeable. A passage can do both through the same detail. Doubling playback speed preserves the geometric span and changes the time available before the next movement.

Do not use technical accuracy as an excuse to explain every available fact immediately. Introduce a definition when it enables the reader to distinguish or investigate something. Equally, do not let “force”, “memory”, “choice” or “freedom” silently switch between a mechanism and an image.

Our concrete anchor is the current three-line artifact:

- Each line is two metres long; parallel lines are two metres apart in depth.
- Each sphere waits for contact and moves only the contacting virtual hand. The head, body, tracked controller and other hand stay unchanged.
- The second uses the same X targets at twice the rate. The third adds Y to the slow X sequence.
- Withdrawing the tracked hand releases it from the virtual path; the sphere returns alone. The later Out / Back encounter moves the body.
- The saved path can be repeated. That does not establish that a person's experience repeats.

These claims come from [motion_record.gd](../../../../commons/artifacts/random_touch/motion_record.gd) and [random_touch.gd](../../../../commons/artifacts/random_touch/random_touch.gd). The [hand-and-interface revision](../../iterations/2026-10-15-hand-and-interfaces/README.md) distinguishes tested behaviour from headset experience still unobserved. The earlier literary sample below describes the superseded body-carry version and is preserved as a historical trial. It uses an imagined observer, not a reported participant.

## Earlier trial of attention — before the hand-only revision

> A line two metres long hangs in the room. At its centre a sphere waits. I can see the whole distance before anything happens.
>
> I touch it, and the room begins to pass sideways. The sphere carries my avatar through a stored sequence of positions. I know how far it is allowed to go; I do not yet know where it will turn.
>
> At the next line, the same positions arrive in half the time. The line has not grown. There is less time to meet each change.
>
> I withdraw my hand. The sphere returns to its centre without me.

This uses measured extent, a change of viewpoint and exact repetition as its material. It does not claim that a visitor felt fear, pleasure, loss of control or resistance. The latter would be misleading if it suggested physical force against a hand. The useful reading question is what the passage makes someone notice or want to try.

A possible next encounter is already present: touch sets the first sphere in motion, while touch holds a falling group still on the RAND page. The gesture has acquired another consequence. That is a candidate bridge to test, not proof that the reader should now leave the spheres. A wish to examine time, repeat a turn or stay with the ride can revise the transition.

## Use and record a strategy

Choose a live writing problem first. Pick one operation that addresses it; add a second only if it does different work. This collection is not a required paragraph order, a quota of authors, or a recipe to apply to every map.

A small trial record needs:

- The artifact/build and exact passage being tried.
- The strategy, the detail it brings forward, and the proposed change in attention.
- Which sentences describe implementation, report an observation, or offer an image.
- The reader's words: what remains with them, what they understood, and what they would do next.
- A local decision: retain, revise, combine or set aside, with the next comparison if useful.

A strategy earns reuse when it makes a particular encounter more perceptible and leaves the mechanism intelligible. Check technical truth and reader response separately. A poetic reading may produce an association, a wish to linger or a useful uncertainty; it need not end in another explanation.

Tutorial use: let the question earn a definition, trace and reconstruction; keep necessary prerequisites explicit. Final use: develop attention and implications without turning every artifact into a complete lesson. Either text can initiate a revision to the apparatus.
