# THE OFFICE FOR ALTERATIONS OF DESTINATION

The visitor applied to the Office for Alterations of Destination.

“I should like the sphere not to return,” they said.

“Certainly,” said the clerk. “What seed would you prefer?”

“Does the seed decide whether it returns?”

“No. The seed decides where it goes before returning.”

“Then I would prefer a seed that sends it somewhere it cannot return from.”

“That is not among the available destinations.”

“Could you give it a longer distance?”

“We offer distances between 1.1 and 2.2 metres.”

“And beyond 2.2?”

“That is a different application.”

“Could I remain where it takes me?”

“Of course. Release it.”

“And the sphere?”

“Returns.”

The visitor considered this.

“So the sphere is not free, but I am?”

“The sphere has a route. You have a release control. We do not make claims about freedom.”

“Then I wish to change the route.”

The clerk pushed a form across the desk. Under SEED, somebody had already written 435.

“No,” said the visitor. “The other form.”

The clerk looked under the desk, in the drawers, behind the drawers, and at last in the instruction governing the desk itself.

“There is no other form,” the clerk said. “That would require changing the office.”
