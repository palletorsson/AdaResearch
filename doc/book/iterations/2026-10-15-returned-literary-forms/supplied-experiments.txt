Here are five radically different literary experiments to send back to Astra. These are actual writing samples, not instructions for changing a few paragraphs.

They all draw on the same apparatus evidence: the five-rod recording wall, falling RAND page, simulated Geiger counter, seed 435, movement sampler and Out / Back sphere.

But I am deliberately abandoning the obligation to describe all five objects, visit them in order or maintain one narrator. I want to show how far literature can move while remaining accountable to the computational world.

These are original experiments inspired by different literary methods, not attempts to reproduce the authors' prose.

## 1. THE INSTRUCTIONS WERE FOLLOWED

Beckett / procedural theatre · A performance, not a walkthrough

A short performance for one sphere and one person

A dark sphere at the centre of a room.

It waits.

(One and a half seconds.)

It travels outward.

(Three and a half seconds.)

It returns.

(Three and a half seconds.)

It waits.

The direction changes.

The distance changes.

The waiting does not change.

A person approaches the sphere. The person holds it. For a time, they travel together.

The person releases it.

The sphere continues.

The person is no longer carried.

The sphere returns to the centre.

The person is elsewhere.

(One and a half seconds.)

The sphere waits.

A new beginning is supplied. A new direction is calculated. The distance is selected within the permitted range.

The sphere departs.

The sphere returns.

A different beginning is supplied.

The sphere departs.

The sphere returns.

The person approaches the centre again, although nothing in the procedure requires this.

(The procedure does not wait for the person.)

This is almost a theatrical score. There is no explanation of pseudorandomness. The distinction between the sphere's return and the person's freedom to remain elsewhere becomes the drama.

What it tests: Can a rule become literature through its performance, without being converted into an argument?

## 2. THE CASE OF NUMBER 435

Borges / fictional scholarly investigation · A story organized around one number

The investigation began with a receipt, which is unusual, since the event under investigation occurred many times after the receipt had been issued.

The receipt reported the acquisition of a whole number on 13 October 2026, at 14:29:02 UTC. The source named was RANDOM.ORG, the requested range zero to 999, the delivered value 435. There was no record of anyone having requested this particular number rather than any of its 999 alternatives.

In the room, 435 was found in several places. It stood above a colour window. It attended a cabinet making clicks. It had furnished a beginning for the movements of a hand. The investigator initially supposed the same sequence was circulating through the apparatuses, translated into different materials. This proved too simple. Each machine kept its own procedure, its own requests, its own method for turning numerical results into an event.

The clicking cabinet offered ninety-six times, calculated in advance. The investigator listened. Then pressed RESTART. The intervals returned. The cabinet was not measuring radiation; it was playing from its own retained calculation.

A preliminary report described 435 as the cause of the clicking. A correction was entered: a starting input, not a complete account of the causes. Nothing in the receipt recorded the cabinet's algorithm, and nothing in the sound authenticated the atmosphere.

The investigation remains open, despite the investigator's ability to reproduce the evidence at will.

What it tests: Can one ordinary integer support an entire story about recurrence, provenance and the inadequacy of records?

## 3. THE OFFICE FOR ALTERATIONS OF DESTINATION

Carroll / logical comedy · An invented conversation with the rules of the simulation

The visitor applied to the Office for Alterations of Destination.

“I should like the sphere not to return,” they said.

“Certainly,” said the clerk. “What seed would you prefer?”

“Does the seed decide whether it returns?”

“No. The seed decides where it goes before returning.”

“Then I would prefer a seed that sends it somewhere it cannot return from.”

“That is not among the available destinations.”

“Could you give it a longer distance?”

“We offer distances between 1.1 and 2.2 metres.”

“And beyond 2.2?”

“That is a different application.”

“Could I remain where it takes me?”

“Of course. Release it.”

“And the sphere?”

“Returns.”

The visitor considered this.

“So the sphere is not free, but I am?”

“The sphere has a route. You have a release control. We do not make claims about freedom.”

“Then I wish to change the route.”

The clerk pushed a form across the desk. Under SEED, somebody had already written 435.

“No,” said the visitor. “The other form.”

The clerk looked under the desk, in the drawers, behind the drawers, and at last in the instruction governing the desk itself.

“There is no other form,” the clerk said. “That would require changing the office.”

What it tests: Can the constraints of an actual algorithm generate comedy, institutional absurdity and an argument about agency—without requiring the narrator to explain that argument?

The office is fictional. The sphere's distance range, release behavior and compulsory return are grounded in the implementation.

## 4. NOTES ON A PATCH OF INK

Perec / Ponge · Radical concentration on a single material detail

There are five rods. Each is a metre long. Behind them is pale paper.

On the first rod the bead moves, and the hand that touches it moves with it. The room stays still. The paper acquires a pale circle, then another, then a third. At first they are separate. Later a circle falls partly over another. The overlap is darker than either circle alone.

The bead reaches a position and moves on. A person can stop following. The bead returns to its centre. The patch does not vanish.

The temptation is to read the patch as a picture of where the random destinations occurred. But the paper was marked along the way, twelve times during each segment. It has received the travel between destinations too. A dark place may have been crossed often without having been selected often.

On the next rods the same horizontal targets are played at different rates: half speed, ordinary speed, twice speed, four times speed. The marks do not need to be in different places merely because they arrived at different times.

We can try to remember the sequence by looking at the patch. We can find places of accumulation. We cannot recover, merely from this darkness, the order in which each circle arrived.

REPLAY clears the paper.

For a moment the rods are precisely where they were, and the evidence of having followed them is gone.

What it tests: Can concentrated observation of one small surface produce a scientific discovery and a literary event without a conventional story?

Notice that this version abandons almost the entire room. The patch of ink becomes sufficient material for a piece of literature.

## 5. FIVE DOCUMENTS FROM ONE ROOM

Eliot / archival montage · A composition without a continuous narrator

1955\. A published table.

A million random digits. Electronic generation. Tests found unwanted patterns; the published supply underwent correction and transformation. A number could be located on a page and used elsewhere. The historical table did not descend from the ceiling.

SELECT. A falling page in the room.

Five digits under a finger. Amber. The rows continue falling around the selected group. The displayed digits come from this installation's own generator. No page of the 1955 book has been torn loose.

13 OCTOBER 2026. A saved receipt.

Value: 435. Range: 0–999. Reported source: atmospheric noise. One acquisition kept; no new request on entry.

The cabinet.

Click. A gap. Another click.

RESTART.

Click. The same gap. Another click.

Ninety-six calculated event times. No radioactive material, no detector, no fresh atmospheric sample. A sound can recur without the event that its name ordinarily suggests.

The absent station.

The movement sampler exists in the program but not in this saved arrangement of the room. Were it restored, a held sphere could record positions, rotations and times, then reduce their encoded trace to one of a thousand seeds. An empty place in the itinerary is not a gesture that someone made.

Another sphere, elsewhere.

Out: three and a half seconds.

Back: three and a half seconds.

A released person need not return with it.

What it tests: Can documents, controls, sounds, dates, absences and movements compose a chapter without being translated into a single explanatory voice?

## What I would send to Astra

All five literary experiments — Markdown

Complete examples, literary operations, experimental questions and factual safeguards in one handover file.

I would emphasize that these are not necessarily five candidate final chapters. They are five demonstrations that the same scientific material permits fundamentally different literary architectures.

The important differences are:

| Experiment        | What becomes the organizing principle         |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Performance    | Time and repetition                           |
| 2. Investigation  | One number and its provenance                 |
| 3. Logical comedy | Conflict between intention and rule           |
| 4. Observation    | One material surface examined closely         |
| 5. Montage        | Heterogeneous records and their relationships |

My strongest candidates for further exploration are 3 and 4, precisely because they are so different.

The logical comedy introduces characters, conflict and absurdity without abandoning computational precision. The ink study nearly removes storytelling altogether, trusting material observation to carry the writing.

This is the degree of difference I would want Astra to explore before returning to the complete literary chapter.

The goal should not be five polished versions of a walkthrough. It should be five works that could hardly have been written by the same method, despite originating from the same simulation.
