## The walk

Motion, finally — but every motion in this room is an argument about its cause. The newton_cradle passes one impulse down a line of stillness. The collision_crasher shows five tethered blocks exchanging momentum with arrows drawn at each impact: nothing lost, everything redirected. The friction_ramp slows what gravity started; fluid resistance does it smoothly; the bouncing_ball loses a little to every floor it meets. The combined_forces_demo stands in the middle like a thesis — many pulls, one resultant — and the flocking_controls generalize it: a whole crowd steering by summed forces, one boid's arrows exposed so you can read the mind of the flock.

## The turn

The turn is the chapter's central sentence taken literally: *position and velocity are states; force is what changes them.* Watch the cradle long enough and states stop feeling like possessions — they are balances currently unchallenged. Motion isn't what things do; it is what forces have most recently decided.
