# An interruption can acquire a schedule

Paint a difference into the little source. It enters every copy. The system accepts the new mark and immediately makes it regular. This is a specific tension in the station: permission to edit a shared source gives considerable reach, but no control here singles out just one occurrence.

The neighbouring fields also complicate what counts as sameness. Both read the same stored cells; alternate columns in the reflected field face differently. Identity at the source does not require identical orientation at every appearance. Conversely, a symmetrical source can hide an operation. An image that looks unchanged is weak evidence that nothing happened.

These are opportunities for queer reading through an actual intervention. Add an asymmetry that makes a formerly hidden reversal legible. Ask whether a difference belongs to a mark, its facing, its neighbours, or the position from which it is encountered. The apparatus makes some of those relations editable and holds others fixed. It does not assign a degree of queerness to the result.

Keeping the floor and two reference panels together makes the scope of a decision inspectable. The station's P1 and PM buttons change the floor while its fixed wall references remain available. FLIP X instead rewrites the source and reaches all three fields. The apparent size of a change does not tell us where it was made or what authority it had.

The source offers sixteen addresses and eight paint values. Its display uses nearest-cell sampling, making the encoding conspicuous. That is a useful choice for this comparison, not a claim that sharp squares are more truthful than filtered edges. A curve, a brush pressure or an irregular seam would require other storage and rendering decisions. There is no unencoded original hiding behind this apparatus that a sufficiently fine grid must finally recover.

The next environment adds another condition: a repeating image can cover a surface whose corners, supports and route do not repeat with it. The rule that organises appearances and the geometry that admits a body may cooperate, or they may disagree. Their disagreement is material for the research.
