# Why the encounter became uncertain

Author-supplied account, received 5 October 2026. Selected passages from `a036ccc3-7ab3-4fc2-a964-e9ee899ffbde/Pasted text.txt`. These are the author's account of practice, not externally established claims about all programming education.

> When I started the project, I wanted to investigate how algorithms can organise beauty and make visual arguments about 3D bodies and virtual spaces. I imagined building on the tutorials and courses I was teaching at the university in programming for artists and 3D world exploration. The plan was to make exploratory tutorials, visual arguments and rooms of pleasure, starting from the basics.

> As AI capabilities increased, I began to believe that the project's milestones could be reached with AI and my guidance. Things I had only dreamt about seemed within reach. At first, that felt great: I wanted to fulfil the research goals. But something was happening to creative time. I was no longer coding much myself. Helping others understand code and make beautiful things had been a foundation of the project, and that foundation was slipping under my feet.

> The work was now getting done through written instructions. Before that, I had paused YouTube tutorials to code; before that, I had copied and edited code from forums; before that, I had worked from books. The AI model could take on longer and longer tasks.

The preface draws out the consequence: the maker could receive a working object before understanding how to construct it. A prospective student might arrive by the same route. This changes the expected relationship between the course, the student and the teacher; it does not establish that learning or understanding is unnecessary.

The original account describes the encounter as something that “would never occur.” The revised preface retains this as an experienced doubt about its anticipated form: “might never happen in the form I expected.” It leaves actual future educational arrangements open.
