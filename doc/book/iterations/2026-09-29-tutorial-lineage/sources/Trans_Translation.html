# Translation: seven journeys

The far bank is familiar now. In the last hall, a moving surface carried you across. Here seven carriers link a chain of islands, rising to an upper route and descending again.

Before boarding, follow the small waiting cubes with your eyes. Some hang over the fire basin. Others mark the upper route, beyond the reach of a hand from the floor. The carriers give you a way to meet them.

<!-- @tc -->

The first carrier travels into depth. Step aboard and let the room pass. Look at the cube under your feet: it keeps its facing and size. Along this journey, only its z coordinate changes.

At the next island, the route turns sideways. The carrier moves along x. You can keep looking towards the far end of the hall while travelling across it. Direction of travel and direction of attention have separated.

Then the way goes up.

Stand on the lift and watch the lower landing descend from view. It is still where it was. The carrier changes y; the room gives that change a consequence. You arrive beside an upper platform, three metres above the ground route.

Near the entrance, three small axis markers show x, y and z separately. Along the route, those directions have become sideways travel, a lift and a passage into depth.

The transport script calculates a destination from a starting position, a direction and a distance:

```gdscript
target_position = initial_position + (move_direction * move_distance)
```

The direction is normalised: its vector is given a length of one. Multiplying it by `move_distance` sets how far the carrier will go. Adding that displacement to `initial_position` gives its destination. Three metres up and three metres forward use the same addition, with different directions.

On the upper route, another carrier travels along z. It repeats a direction you have already travelled near the floor. Now you can look down past its edge to the route that brought you here.

A sideways return carries you back across x in the opposite direction. Then the next carrier moves down and forward together, like an escalator. Its surface stays level while its path slopes: three metres down, two metres further along z. Watch the pickup approach as the lower landing comes towards you.

<!-- @pick_up_cube -->

The small pickups have been waiting along these journeys. They rise and fall without turning. Their small vertical loop continues as the carrier brings you into contact with them, and each collected cube disappears with a sound.

Take one, then look back while the carrier moves on.

The carrier brings your body to a pickup by sharing its displacement:

```gdscript
var movement_delta = global_position - previous_position
_translate_rider(carried_player, movement_delta)
```

For each small move of the carrier, your position moves with it. A pickup detects the arriving body and disappears. Ride back to its place. The carrier can repeat the journey; the cube is gone.

<!-- @ -->

At the last crossing, the waiting cubes run diagonally over the pit. Watch an edge of the carrier as you travel. It does not turn.

This direction changes x and z together. The destination lies eight metres across and eight metres further into the hall. Both coordinates change during the same journey. We have combined directions without adding rotation.

Thirteen pickups mark this passage through seven rides. By the final terrace, the cubes you collected have left empty places along the route. Another arrangement of carriers and pickups could lead you elsewhere using the same operations.

<!-- @translation_cube_demo -->

Step onto the final terrace. Two pairs of small doors stand on plinths, a red door and a blue door in each frame. One pair has rails; the other shows translucent panels where its doors can end up. Try the same upward-and-sideways gesture on a square handle in each frame. A pale line stays behind the moving handle.

At the rail-guided door, the line rises. The sideways part of your gesture has gone missing. Beside it, the other mechanism lets the handle move in both directions together. Its line can cross the opening diagonally.

Lift the guided handle to the corner of its rail, then move it sideways. A bend leads to the place the other handle could reach directly. The translucent panel beside the second door marks its destination. Compare the two handles in their frames: they have the same distance to travel up and across.

The guided door retains the part of a displacement that runs along its current axis:

```gdscript
var t = (knob_pos - start).dot(phase1_dir)
knob_pos = start + phase1_dir * t
```

Here `phase1_dir` points upwards. `.dot()` extracts the part of your movement along that direction; the next line puts the handle there. The full implementation also limits how far it can go.

The trail records the handle after this correction. Your hand can propose a diagonal and leave a vertical line.

In the trace hall, movement left positions behind. Here the handle has already been corrected before its position is kept. The line can be an accurate record of where the handle went without showing everything your hand tried.

Bring the free handle back towards its starting corner, then try a curved route to the translucent panel. The recent lines remain together as you try another way. There is room to take the long way within the frame.

![Two door mechanisms leave different traces for different permitted paths.](/book-review/doc/book/iterations/2026-09-23-translation-paths/translation-paths.png)

*A guided bend, a direct diagonal, and a curved route to the same relative endpoint. Each panel records a separate gesture at the handle.*

Up followed by sideways reaches the same position as sideways followed by up. Translation itself does not impose this order. The first mechanism does. The second gives both freedoms together. A destination can be shared by journeys with very different permissions.

<!-- @ -->

The torus beside the course already turns while its cylinder rises and falls. It offers a glimpse of the next question. Our carriers have kept their facing throughout; this neighbouring object combines the operations. Keep the difference between travelling and facing in mind. In the next hall, the turn itself will become the question.

# Trans Translation

Moving. Displacement. Position as coefficients.

Translate a single object.

```gdscript
func move_to(node: Node3D, target: Vector3) -> void:
    node.global_position = target
```

Direct assignment. One frame, the object is there.

Translate smoothly over time.

```gdscript
func tween_to(node: Node3D, target: Vector3, duration: float) -> void:
    var tween := create_tween()
    tween.tween_property(node, "global_position", target, duration)
```

The tween interpolates linearly. Duration in seconds.

Translate along a local axis.

```gdscript
func move_forward(node: Node3D, distance: float) -> void:
    var forward: Vector3 = -node.global_transform.basis.z
    node.global_position += forward * distance
```

Local translation respects the node's current orientation. Forward is -Z in Godot.

Apply a translation impulse to a rigid body.

```gdscript
func nudge(body: RigidBody3D, impulse: Vector3) -> void:
    body.apply_central_impulse(impulse)
```

Impulse is velocity times mass. Physics integrates the impulse into velocity on the next step.

Translate relative to another node.

```gdscript
func move_relative_to(node: Node3D, reference: Node3D, offset_local: Vector3) -> void:
    var offset_world: Vector3 = reference.global_transform.basis * offset_local
    node.global_position = reference.global_position + offset_world
```

Local offset is transformed through the reference's basis. The result is a world-space position.

Chain multiple translations.

```gdscript
func move_sequence(node: Node3D, steps: Array, duration_per_step: float) -> void:
    var tween := create_tween().set_parallel(false)
    for target in steps:
        tween.tween_property(node, "global_position", target, duration_per_step)
```

Sequential tweens move the node through a sequence of positions. Useful for scripted paths.

Interpolate along a curved path.

```gdscript
func move_along_bezier(node: Node3D, curve: Curve3D, duration: float) -> void:
    var tween := create_tween()
    tween.tween_method(
        func(t): node.global_position = curve.sample_baked(t * curve.get_baked_length()),
        0.0, 1.0, duration
    )
```

Sample the Bezier curve at interpolated positions. The node follows the curve's shape over the duration.

You can now translate a node instantly or over time, locally or relative to another node, along straight lines or curves. The seven rides in this hall already separate and combine those components. Trans_Rotation follows in the active sequence.

Check identity.

```gdscript
func is_identity(t: Transform3D) -> bool:
    return t.is_equal_approx(Transform3D.IDENTITY)
```

Identity preserves the input. Useful as a test for whether a chain of transforms cancels out.

Invert a transform.

```gdscript
func invert(t: Transform3D) -> Transform3D:
    return t.affine_inverse()
```

Undo the transform. Composing t with t.affine_inverse() produces identity.

Compose with multiplication.

```gdscript
func combine(a: Transform3D, b: Transform3D) -> Transform3D:
    return a * b
```

Right-to-left application order. a * b applies b first, then a.

Extract the origin.

```gdscript
func get_origin(t: Transform3D) -> Vector3:
    return t.origin
```

The origin is the translation part of the transform. Ignore the basis to get just the position.


## Same destination, different permission

On the final terrace, `translation_cube_demo` is instantiated twice: `course:lift_lateral` with a rail guide, and `course:free` with ghost endpoints. Their knobs begin 0.5 m above each artifact root; the 0.8 m plinth brings that to 1.3 m above the deck.

The guided mechanism projects hand displacement onto a permitted axis, clamps the distance and admits the next axis only after the lift threshold. The free mechanism admits both components at once within the same bounds. In `constrained_door.gd`:

```gdscript
var t = _clamp_run((knob_pos - start).dot(phase1_dir), target_y, 0.0, 0.05)
knob_pos = start + phase1_dir * t
```

Pure translations commute: adding the same two displacement vectors in reverse order gives the same endpoint. This puzzle imposes an order through its constraints; that is a property of the mechanism, not a limitation of vector addition.

## Watch the accepted movement

The exit mechanisms now leave a pale trace when a handle is held. Try the same small upward-and-outward gesture on each. The guided door records the lift after discarding the sideways component; the free door keeps both components within its bounds. Then follow the rail to its corner and outwards. Compare that bend with a direct diagonal in the other mechanism.

The free handle can be brought back towards its starting corner to try a curved route. The short recent history remains visible, with older samples eventually rolling off. This returns to Point_Trace with a changed receiver: the record belongs to the constrained knob, not to the whole hand gesture. Release does not reset the mechanism or erase its trail; the artifact’s existing reset method clears it when called, and reloading the hall starts a fresh record.

# Translation as transport

The carrier normalises its direction and defines its target:

```gdscript
target_position = initial_position + (move_direction * move_distance)
```

Each frame advances towards the target by at most speed times elapsed seconds. The same displacement is added to its detected rider:

```gdscript
global_position = global_position.move_toward(target_position, move_speed * delta)
var movement_delta = global_position - previous_position
_translate_rider(carried_player, movement_delta)
```

The rider helper handles a desktop body or the VR tracking origin and its collision body; it applies an incremental displacement rather than teleporting the visitor to the destination. Contact with each pickup's Area3D calls collect(). Thirteen pickups use motion=slide: a vertical sine oscillation with the default 0.2 m amplitude and bob speed 2, without spin or a scale pulse. The carrier supplies the longer approach while each pickup rises and falls.

The escalator token is `tc:3.6055513:0,-3,2#deck:3`: direction (0,-3,2), normalised, and distance sqrt(13). ## A trace after the constraint — 23 September

Both exit studies use `#trace:on`. This opt-in passes `trace_motion` to their existing constrained doors. `accepted_motion_trace.gd` records the knob position after projection and clamping, and only while the pickup signal marks it held. It does not capture the tracked hand’s rejected displacement. The pale trace is displayed 9 cm forward of the sampled position to keep it visible in front of the door panel; the stored points are unshifted door-local coordinates.

Samples require 6 mm of movement. At most 256 positions remain, with old samples rolling off; reset clears the record. The tube radius is 4 mm, with six sides and one unshaded mesh per handle. No collider, new control, or movement constraint is added. Other placements retain their default untraced form.

# The same displacement, another encounter

The upper crossing repeats a direction already travelled near the ground. The displacement remains a translation along z; the view below the rider changes its significance. The coordinate operation does not, by itself, account for the encounter.

A transport cube becomes usable through several agreements: its top meets the landing, its detection area recognises the rider, and its movement is added to that body. The simulation's representation of contact participates in what becomes reachable.

The pickups make another distinction visible. Reversing a displacement returns the visitor to a position. It does not restore a collected object. Position is one part of the scene's state, and changing it cannot reverse every other process that occurred on the way.

The final diagonal is translation with two changing coordinates. It leaves the carrier's facing alone. This separation prepares a question for rotation while retaining a remainder here: which possible journeys did this arrangement of islands and pickups leave untried?

# Translation: seven journeys

This 15 x 55 m hall merges the former Translation and AxisDecomposition maps. Seven transport cubes carry the visitor through depth, sideways movement, ascent, a high crossing, reverse sideways movement, descent and a final diagonal. Thirteen pickups wait along their paths. The route climbs three metres and returns to ground level before the exit.

The carriers do not rotate or scale. Pickups rise and fall without spinning or pulsing in size until collected. Entrance markers make x, y and z explicit; the transport and pickup objects carry the book's primary thread. At the final terrace, two grabbable door mechanisms compare the same destination under sequential axis constraints and free combined movement. These are a third primary encounter. The next active hall is Trans_Rotation.

Original maps and texts are preserved in doc/space/translation-merge-2026-09-09/before. The active Trans_AxisDecomposition stop was removed from the sequence, not deleted from the repository.
