# What does a value permit?

The same zero can mean a pale cube, an empty cell or a prohibition on entering. At this station it means the first. The array does not decide that meaning on its own; a rendering rule gives the number a body. Changing that rule would change the kinds of space the arrangement could offer.

The added height index permits independent positions above a source cell, but our two recipes constrain their values. Repeating and shifting a source do not exhaust the volume's possibilities. A capability and the particular use we make of it should remain distinguishable.

Slice view is another authored decision. It makes an interior available by hiding forty-eight bodies, retaining their data. That reduction helps us learn. Its visible/stored counter also admits the cost: what the reader can inspect now is less than what is there. A useful interface keeps that difference recoverable.

The book asks what bodies become possible with each added capability. Here the question lands on addresses, palette indices, display visibility and the rule that assigns a body to a value. Material response, deformation and inhabitation still require their own encounters.
