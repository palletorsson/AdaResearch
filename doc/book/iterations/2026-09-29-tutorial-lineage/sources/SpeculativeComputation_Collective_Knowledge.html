# More contributions than contributors

When many marks accumulate, what makes the result knowledge rather than a pile?

<!-- @peer_production_tool -->

Follow a packet from one of the rim nodes into the central structure. Watch the new facet appear. During one growth cycle, compare a contribution from a node you have already seen with one from a new source. The number of contributions and the number of contributing sources need not increase together. Press FLOW to hold the arrivals. The work can wait while you look.

The exhibit turns contribution into an arrival event. A packet comes from one of twelve positions and adds a piece to a shared form. At sixty pieces this workbench holds its form until you choose VERSION; FLOW starts the arrivals again. Its visible coherence comes from an assembly rule that knows where to place each arriving facet.

Press COLOUR. The smooth progression from purple to teal gives way to the colours of the sources. Nothing has moved. Some pieces that looked like neighbours now belong to different contributors; distant pieces may share one. Press PIECE to follow a piece back to its numbered rim node. The common form has kept an address for each arrival.

The source makes the counting operation inspectable:

```gdscript
_placed += 1
_edits += 1
# count distinct contributors this version
if peer_idx >= 0 and peer_idx < _seen_peers.size() and not bool(_seen_peers[peer_idx]):
    _seen_peers[peer_idx] = true
    _contributors += 1
```

Every accepted facet increments the addition count. The Boolean array _seen_peers lets the same source count only once within a version. On the next version those flags clear while the totals persist. Read PARTICIPATIONS again after a VERSION. It can pass twelve, because it adds together each version's distinct sources. Call that number CONTRIBUTORS and a label has quietly turned repeated attendance into an expanding community. SOURCES counts distinct nodes in this version; EVER counts distinct nodes across versions.

That is a simulation of accretion, not a live collaborative editor. The peers are generated positions rather than people logged into the museum, and the incoming material is accepted without a dispute over its content. The animation demonstrates one way many sources can feed one object while leaving the harder question of validation open.

Collective knowledge needs more than participation counts. A contribution may repeat an error, correct an earlier claim, supply a missing case or disagree with an accepted account. Those actions can add little to a simple count while changing the usefulness of the shared result dramatically.

The previous room kept four viewpoints separate. Here the common centre can make their differences disappear into a continuous object. Switch COLOUR back to ORDER, then recover a piece with PIECE. Its source remains in the record even when the surface no longer shows it. Does the shape preserve a reason for accepting that contribution, or only the fact that something arrived?

Choose a piece and press DISPUTE. Its frame turns red. Press REVISE and watch that same piece turn and change height. The pile has not gained a piece; the revision count has increased. The source is still named, and the change is attributed to VISITOR. The red frame stays. A change has happened, but who agreed that the dispute was settled?

Here revision is a geometric operation we have supplied. It has no evidence to weigh and no argument to answer. The original and previous poses remain in the record, but there is no complete history you can browse. Keeping a name and a trace makes reconsideration possible; it does not perform the reconsideration for us.

Before pressing VERSION, look once more at the disputed piece. The next form starts empty. Its current source count begins again while additions and participations carry on; EVER does not count an old source as a new one. The device keeps only the previous version in memory. What would have to survive for someone else to understand why you changed that piece?

<!-- @the_commons_table -->

Leave the growing helix for the round table. There is no object at its centre to receive another contribution. A small ring of light surrounds an opening. Through it, the floor is still there.

Take a place at the rim, then walk to another. No one position at this table addresses every account at once.

One station holds three labelled trays and another marked OTHER. The small spheres have been assigned by a rule. The counts belong to this batch; OTHER holds the specimens that did not meet the three tests. Its name says almost nothing about what they share with one another.

Beside it, five bars stand in order of height. We know how to make that order:

```gdscript
heights.sort()
```

The instruction acts on a list. The bars give its result a place on the table. From the entrance, the arrangement may have looked settled before you knew what had settled it. The card says which comparison was used. It does not ask whether height was the comparison you needed.

Continue around. Two pointers face different ways inside one bracket. A model camera aims towards the middle. Cords join small points by several routes. Four little blocks recall defaults the engine supplied. These are familiar operations and incomplete instruments, gathered into a common piece of furniture. Sharing the table has not made them agree.

The table has no head. The classification still has an author.

Look back at the helix, where every arrival found a place through one central rule. Here the middle has been left open, but the rules have moved to the rim. Which of them could somebody joining you change? Which would they first have to notice?

Perhaps a commons begins with making room for that question. An empty centre gives us somewhere to look through. It does not, by itself, distribute the power to revise.

# Contribute, preserve a source, revise a piece

This hall follows Situated Computation's four separate claims and leads to Rhizome Network's changing connections. Its primary is `peer_production_tool`; the [scene](../../../commons/artifacts/peer_production_tool/peer_production_tool.tscn) and [source](../../../commons/artifacts/peer_production_tool/peer_production_tool.gd) implement the encounter.

## First comparison

The hall uses twelve generated sources, sixty-piece capacity and a 0.7-second interval between packet launches. A packet advances with `delta * 4` and retains its sender index when it lands. The program chooses senders; they are not people choosing to participate. Piece positions follow an authored helix.

FLOW holds or resumes both packet motion and new launches. PIECE also holds flow and selects the next landed piece. A connecting line and P1–P12 identifiers let the visitor recover the selected source without relying on colour alone. COLOUR switches between the original purple-to-teal arrival-order gradient and source colours without changing geometry. Each landed record retains its source in either view.

## What the counts count

```gdscript
_placed += 1
_edits += 1
# count distinct contributors this version
if peer_idx >= 0 and peer_idx < _seen_peers.size() and not bool(_seen_peers[peer_idx]):
    _seen_peers[peer_idx] = true
    _contributors += 1
```

The historic `_contributors` counter remains cumulative source-version participation. It is labelled PARTICIPATIONS, correcting the earlier CONTRIBUTORS display without discarding its history. PIECES is the current form's occupancy; ADDITIONS is the lifetime arrival count. SOURCES counts distinct sources in this version; EVER counts distinct sources since this artifact's last rebuild. REVISIONS counts visitor revision operations separately. None counts unique humans.

VERSION retains one previous version in memory, clears the current records, pending packets and per-version flags, and waits for FLOW. Lifetime counts persist. At capacity, interactive placements hold the form; passive placements retain their automatic release behaviour. Configuration rebuild starts a fresh session, so these are not persistent saved totals.

## Dispute and revision

DISPUTE toggles a red outline on the selected existing record. REVISE rotates that piece another thirty degrees and alternates its height between original and 1.5 times original. Its source, location, addition count and current piece count do not change. Original and immediately previous transforms remain available in the record; `revised_by` records VISITOR and a revision number increments. Revision does not clear the dispute. This is a geometric editing demonstration, without semantic validation, agreement or a full history browser.

The central form uses a MultiMesh. Selection/dispute outlines share another MultiMesh so marking many pieces does not create twelve draw calls per outline. Source tracing is one cylinder. Dynamic counters use depth-tested Label3D text instead of caching an unbounded set of baked counter textures. Source records, packets, current geometry and retained previous version are bounded by the configured capacities. Rebuilding frees only owned children, preserving the museum's attached nodes.

## Placement and evidence

The full-size workbench remains at (6,5), now facing arrival. Two central pier cells become floor so that its front controls and central form can be reached and seen; four side piers remain. An explicit spawn at (6,1) and local dressing exclusions support approach. The other three authored tokens remain exactly placed.

The controlled museum probe tests actual button signals, repeated-source counting across versions, packet sender identity, held motion, colour-independent geometry, disputed revisions, capacity, safe empty operations, rebuild ownership, legacy behaviour and selected walks. Rendered desktop views are evidence of these selected states, not headset comfort, Quest performance or learning. See the [pass report](../../../doc/book/iterations/2026-09-20-collective-review/SUMMARY.md).

The existing wiki fragment and two other supporting tokens remain an ecology to develop. A single NORTH fragment still points to an absent EAST panel; this pass does not claim to complete that separate reading circuit. The earlier theoretical/design material remains in the [preserved proposal](../../../doc/space/postcrisis-pass-2026-09-15/before/commons/maps/SpeculativeComputation_Collective_Knowledge/technical.md).

Concept: Gödel's incompleteness proved that individual formal systems cannot contain themselves; this map asks what happens when several such systems share a commons. Four independent reasoning agents, each running a different logic, report on the same question, and a mediation stage reads their disagreements as data.
Sequence role: Fifth map in the Post-Foundations Crisis sequence. Follows SpeculativeComputation_Situated_Computation, which named standpoint as a design constraint; leads to SpeculativeComputation_Rhizome_Network, which converts non-hierarchy from a reasoning posture into a topology.
Technical angle: Four agent stations — classical first-order logic, paraconsistent logic, probabilistic inference, constraint solving — each with a visible specification of the kinds of statement it cannot prove. A central mediation station collects their outputs, records disagreements rather than resolving them, and reports the set of claims the commons can support. A second display highlights claims no single system could reach alone.
Critical angle: The map argues for reasoning infrastructures that are plural by design rather than by accident. No single system is complete; a commons might cover more than any single system and remain incomplete in a different and more tractable way. Pluralism is the engineering discipline rather than the rhetorical posture.
Key artifacts: the four agent stations carry the individually incomplete reasoning systems; the central mediator carries the collective protocol; the coverage display tracks the claims the commons supports but no single agent does.

Gap: No gap identified.

# Does more activity mean more voices?

Follow a packet from a rim node to the central form. Look for a second contribution from a source you have already seen. Hold the arrivals with FLOW and switch between source and order colour. The geometry does not change, yet what it lets you notice does.

The shape accepts every arriving contribution. Its peers are generated positions, and one central rule assembles their pieces. A growing form is a useful image of collective activity, but its coherence does not establish agreement. The counter exposes another loss: attendance across versions is not the same as a growing community. The old CONTRIBUTORS number could exceed the twelve source positions; its new name, PARTICIPATIONS, makes the repeated counting explicit.

Choose a piece, mark it disputed and revise it. A revision changes an existing form without adding another arrival. The source survives; the visitor's intervention gains a name. The red dispute marker stays until someone explicitly clears it. Nothing in the operation decides whether the revision was justified.

A commons needs ways to preserve disagreement, trace a revision and distinguish repair from accumulation. These controls make some of those differences inspectable, while leaving evidence and agreement unresolved. We have supplied a geometric change, not a deliberative community. The record retains the original and immediately previous poses, not every step of a history. The device keeps one previous version in memory and loses it on rebuild. What must another person inherit to reconsider your choice?

Many sources can still feed a single centre. The next machine changes the rule of connection.

The earlier theoretical and design text is [preserved for further development](../../../doc/space/postcrisis-pass-2026-09-15/before/commons/maps/SpeculativeComputation_Collective_Knowledge/critical.md). This encounter does not turn an illustrative scene into empirical evidence about people.

OTHER records the failure to meet three tests; it does not establish a coherent identity for the excluded specimens. The chapter asks who may change a rule after noticing it.
