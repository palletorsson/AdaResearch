# The choosing hand

What changes when a colour becomes part of the body you act through?

The lamps changed what reached a surface. Here a hand has outgrown the furniture. A small manicure table stands before it, complete with bottles, stools and another hand small enough to rest between the tools. Which of these bodies are you about to dress?

<!-- @nail_color_controller -->

Find the movable point in the RGB box on its open plinth beside the manicure table. Take it along one axis, then bring it back. A colour you left can be found again at the same place.

Look up at the giant nails, then down at the petite hand on the table. Both answer the same choice. A movement small enough for your hand to make crosses two very different scales. Does the colour feel equally delicate on both?

![The giant hand and the petite hand on the manicure table, seen from inside the museum salon.](/book-review/doc/book/figures/Color_Nails/salon-red.png)

Raise the hand you are choosing with and turn its nails toward you. Compare them with the petite hand, the giant nails and the swatch. The colour has reached the hand you use to choose it. What seemed like dressing an object now includes the body doing the dressing.

The nail material adds its own response to light. A colour selected in the box can look different on a small glossy surface from the way it appears on the flat preview.

The point's three coordinates are constrained to the box and converted into red, green and blue channel values, each between zero and one. Moving along one axis changes one channel. Moving diagonally can change several at once. The box turns a colour choice into a position that your hand can revisit.

This is a three-channel description of colour. It does not divide every difference you can perceive into three equally noticeable kinds. Equal distances in the box need not feel like equal changes in the preview.

<!-- @hand_color_controller -->

At the controller for the hand surface, leave the nails at the colour you chose and move the point for the skin. Look again at the nails against this new background. Their assigned colour has stayed fixed. Do they still look the same?

The two stations use the same position-to-colour mechanism but target different surfaces. The salon hands are two copies of the same source mesh. Their nail materials receive the nail-colour signal; their skin materials receive the hand-colour signal. Each receiving material is assigned the selected value:

```gdscript
material.albedo_color = colour
```

The scale difference lives in the geometry's transform. It does not require a second colour recipe. A nail can become monumental while its material still receives the same three channels as the small one on the cushion.

The two points move with the same ease. Changing polish and changing skin can feel like neighbouring choices. Yet changing a rendered skin colour does not give us another person's lived history, or undo the racism through which skin is read. Polish is read through the body too: a colour may be called elegant, excessive or defiant according to whose hand wears it.

Audre Lorde asks us to recognise difference while confronting the power that turns it into inferiority.[^color-nails-lorde] Look again at the hand you have chosen. Which colours feel available to you? Which seem to require an explanation, and to whom?

The bottles never need opening. No brush crosses these nails, and no polish has to dry. The room keeps the cushion, the stools, the promise of a hand being attended to. The application has become an assignment. What remains of the manicure in the pleasure of choosing?

The colour leaves with you. Your hands carry it into the halls ahead.

<!-- @ -->

Choose a colour because you want it, without requiring a naturalistic match. Try a nail or hand that interrupts the palette you expected from this body. Reach for the point again. The hand making the next choice is already wearing the last one.

You have chosen their colour. The fingers have kept their length. What else might you want to change?

Carry one of those chosen colours toward the rainbow. Where would you put it among the bands? The next room gives colour a familiar order; look for the decisions that hold that order in place.

[^color-nails-lorde]: Audre Lorde, [“Age, Race, Class, and Sex: Women Redefining Difference”](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/institute-intersectionality-studies/media-library/intersectionality-readings/lorde-age-race-class-and-sex.pdf), in *Sister Outsider* (Crossing Press, 1984), pp. 114–123, especially 115–116 and 120–121. Lorde examines the unequal power attached to difference and refuses to let one aspect of a person stand for their whole life. The comparison between this salon's skin and nail controls is the book's application of her argument.

5. Compare the same selected value on the swatch, the small hand and the giant. Equal channel values do not promise identical viewed appearance.

# One colour address, display hands and the player

Color_Nails follows the illumination comparison in Color_Flashlight. This time the operation changes material albedo.

The map's nail_salon at (7,9) uses the existing ArrayMesh from `commons/body/hands/right_hand.tscn`. The mesh is shown in its rest pose at 4.3 metres and 0.32 metres high. It is not a tracked skeleton. Surface names identify `Hand` and `Nails.001`; each display mesh receives its own material overrides, leaving the shared source resource intact.

The primary controls stand on separate plinths: `nail_color_controller` at (9,4) and `hand_color_controller` at (5,4). Both use `commons/interfaces/nail_color_controller.gd`; target_surface distinguishes nails from skin. Their half-metre ValueMapper3D boxes convert point coordinates into RGB. In the salon mounting, root y=1.2 places each box from y=0.9 to 1.4, above an approximately 0.8 m plinth. Front and top access remain open. The optional `#mount:salon` hides the generic canopy/light and reduces the swatch; other placements retain their existing presentation.

The existing controller writes its selected colour through GameManager. The salon connects once to `nail_color_changed` and `hand_color_changed`, then updates only the appropriate material list:

```gdscript
func _nail_colour(colour: Color) -> void:
    for material in nail_materials:
        material.albedo_color = colour
```

The matching skin handler uses skin_materials. There is no per-frame salon loop. Geometry scale is independent of the material's RGB values. Gloss, lighting and the size of the viewed patch can still make the two appearances differ.

The table, plinths and stool seats have collision. Brass legs, cushion, tiny bottles and file are set dressing; the bottles are not extra controls. The map leaves a route along either side and reserves this room against automatic sculpture placement. The neighbouring colorballs uses `#case:salon`, constructing a closed glass case before spawning its balls so they cannot bounce into the plinth approach. Other colorballs placements retain their open physics display.

Verification uses `commons/testing/probe_nail_salon.gd`: actual museum loading, dimensions, two controllers, named material surfaces, standing reach, supplied pickup motion through each RGB box, colour propagation and side-route capsule sweeps. The probe restores the user's saved colours after its samples. Tracked controller ergonomics and headset comfort remain untested.

# A manicure too large for the table

The table offers a familiar scale of care: a cushion, bottles, somewhere to sit. The hand behind it exceeds that arrangement. Another hand fits on the cushion. They receive the same colour choice, but it is difficult to regard the same patch of polish in exactly the same way when it becomes large enough to dominate the room.

This is a specific use of the shared computational substrate. The two hands use one source mesh. Their transforms differ; their nail and skin materials receive the same selected channel values. Enlarging the hand does not require inventing another colour mechanism. A familiar operation can reach an unfamiliar body.

The connection had to be made. Two objects being written in code does not, by itself, mean that a control will address them both. Here the salon subscribes to the existing nail and hand colour signals, then assigns the selected value to named material surfaces. The possibility is located in that connection, and can be inspected there.

Try a colour that makes the hand stranger to you. Then return to a familiar one without changing its scale. What remains unfamiliar? The controller can change a surface while leaving the fingers, pose and division between nail and skin fixed. Its adjustable channels do not contain the whole question of what bodies are possible.

The furniture also leaves a remainder. There is no salon worker at this table. The bottles do not need opening; the material changes without a brush crossing the nail. The scene borrows the signs of manicure while replacing its application with an assignment. That omission is available to notice, rather than a reason to call this a complete simulation of a salon.

Both display hands work without a tracked player body, making their material comparison available on desktop. In the museum's standard VR controller setup, the player hand is another receiver. That connection leaves another difference open: dressing a figure before you and recognising an appearance as yours. The later mirror can return to that question.


## Colour and permission — 28 September 2026

The two RGB controls have equal mechanical reach while their targets carry different social histories. Nail colour also acquires meaning through the body wearing it. The chapter now brings this tension into the act of choosing, without treating an avatar's changed appearance as possession of another person's experience. Its invitation to enjoy an unfamiliar hand remains.

Audre Lorde's [“Age, Race, Class, and Sex: Women Redefining Difference”](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/institute-intersectionality-studies/media-library/intersectionality-readings/lorde-age-race-class-and-sex.pdf) supplies the reference for questioning how difference becomes a hierarchy. The salon comparison is our application. Keep the argument answerable to the two controls and the visitor's sense of permission; avoid adding a general survey of identity theory to the encounter.
