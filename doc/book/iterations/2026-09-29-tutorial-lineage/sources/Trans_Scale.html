# A door, at last

<!-- @scale_me -->

In the rotation hall, you tried the openings against the size of your body. Here, something waits to be taken.

A figure stands behind a tower of food. Crystal grows through her suit; broad panels lean around her body. The food has begun to flower. Porcelain plates climb above it, and smaller serving plates wind down the side. These are familiar enough to approach, strange enough to keep looking at. In the far wall, beside the figure, a little gold frame surrounds a door.

Try to put yourself through it.

You can see the opening. You can point to the space beyond. Your body will not follow. For the moment, calling it an exit describes somebody else's possibility.

There is a bottle on the highest plate. DRINK ME. Take it.

A cut. Now the plate is under your feet. The figure rises beyond you, her leaning panels large enough to seem architectural. The table has become a place to stand. Look down along the serving plates. What could be read at a glance now asks for a journey.

The bottle is gone. You have not watched the room swell through the sizes between. The room and its contents are suddenly four times as large: the food, the figure, the spaces between them. Your body has kept its size. The effect may feel like shrinking. The program has made that feeling by changing the other side of the comparison.

```gdscript
room.scale = Vector3.ONE * FACTOR
```

`FACTOR` is four. The food and the figure belong to `room`, so the same multiplication reaches their positions as well as their dimensions. The figure is farther from the table now. Increasing only their dimensions would have left the distance between them unchanged.

The arrival on the plate is another operation. Scaling the room would not put you there. The program takes the top plate's local position and asks where it has arrived in the enlarged world:

```gdscript
var feet := table.to_global(landings[0]) + Vector3.UP*0.08
```

Then it places your feet there, eight centimetres above the porcelain. The room has changed size, and you have been given a new place from which to meet it.

Follow the plates down. Their winding descent was already present when they were small. Nothing in their arrangement needed to be invented after you took the bottle. Yet the distance from one to the next now belongs to balance, gravity and the time spent falling. The program did not multiply your walking speed or the gravity with the room. Uniform scaling has left a very uneven experience.

Find the little door again. It was forty-five centimetres wide and sixty high. Now it is 1.8 metres wide and 2.4 high. Walk through.

Just beyond it stands a second gold frame, also 1.8 metres wide and 2.4 high. Pass through that one too. It stood at this size before you took the bottle.

![The tiny gold doorway and its enlarged version viewed from the same standing height and distance.](/book-review/doc/book/iterations/2026-09-23-scale-door-and-body/door-and-body.png)

*The camera stays 1.65 metres above the floor and 2.5 metres from the opening in both views. On the right, the second gold frame beyond the doorway has stayed at its original size.*

The little doorway has kept its proportions. There is a difference between preserving a shape and preserving what someone can do there. If the bottle had enlarged you by the same factor, would that doorway have become an exit?

The figure has not become hostile by becoming enormous. Her unfamiliar body remains with you through the change. The same is true of the food: its strangeness preceded its usefulness as a landscape. You can learn a route through something without exhausting what it is.

# What changed enough to let you through?

Enter the room and inspect the tiny door beside the standing figure. Try it before taking the bottle. Look at the food tower's plates and at the distance between the table and the figure.

Take the DRINK ME bottle on the top plate: VR grip/pickup, or the museum's desktop right-button carry gesture. The room changes size in a cut, and your next position is on the enlarged table. You do not see the sizes between. Find the figure and door again before descending the porcelain steps.

Only after comparing the two views, inspect the implementation:

```gdscript
room.scale = Vector3.ONE * FACTOR
var feet := table.to_global(landings[0]) + Vector3.UP*0.08
```

The first line multiplies the room and its children's dimensions and relative positions. The second computes the arrival point; moving the body is a separate operation. `FACTOR` is four. The assignment changes the scale at once. After a physics frame, the program relocates the body to the enlarged table; it does not animate growth. The museum, body, gravity and walking speed are outside that multiplication.

Descend the serving plates and walk out of the room through the former tiny door. It has changed from 0.45 by 0.60 metres to 1.8 by 2.4 metres. Pass through the equally sized gold frame beyond it: that frame remained fixed throughout. Then turn toward the museum exit. The fixed wall at the room entrance makes this a route through the experiment; it does not scale with the room. Which proportions survived? Which distances became tasks? If the body and its movement had also scaled, would the door have become usable?

# A bounded fourfold change

The source token is `scale_me:0:0:1#encounter:drink_me#plinth:0`. `algorithms/primitives/scaleme/scale_me.gd` detects that configuration before binding the legacy target and builds `drink_me_room.gd`. Unconfigured scale_me instances retain their legacy behavior.

`Room` owns the architecture and the two seeded dream figures. It begins four metres wide and deep, with a 3.2-metre ceiling. Its floor sits 4 cm above the museum deck to avoid coincident surfaces. Its far-side door is a genuine gap between colliders, 0.45 by 0.60 metres. The entrance is 1.4 by 2.4 metres.

The food tower uses spread=blooming and service=tower; the crystal suit uses crust=spire and panels=leaning. Sculpture meshes are combined into one static concave collision shape per sculpture. Thirteen visible porcelain serving plates have matching cylinder colliders. Their tops descend from 1.76 m in 0.135 m increments, becoming drops of 0.54 m after scaling.

`drink_me_bottle.gd` extends XRToolsPickable. VR picked_up resolves the grabbing controller's player body. Desktop uses the shared pointer's optional on_desktop_grab callback and resolves its owning CharacterBody3D. Both defer consumption, release the hand, hide/disable the bottle, and call the same encounter operation. Mere proximity does not trigger it.

```gdscript
room.scale = Vector3.ONE * FACTOR
var feet := table.to_global(landings[0]) + Vector3.UP*0.08
```

FACTOR is 4. The scale assignment is instantaneous; there is no interpolation through intermediate sizes. After the physics frame updates, XRToolsPlayerBody.teleport moves the VR body and origin together; desktop moves the character transform. Velocity is cleared. The body, museum, locomotion and gravity remain unchanged. There is no timed reversal; the encounter resets when the hall is freshly loaded.

The source map reserves a 21-by-22-cell hall for the 16-by-16-metre expanded room. Its fixed structure-4 wall spans row 2 except cells 9–11, where the small room's own entrance remains accessible. The wall joins that room to the hall sides, preventing a forward bypass around its exterior. It intersects the room front only, leaving the expanded interior and far exit clear. This wall is outside the scaled hierarchy. The standalone spawn is `s` at (10,0), with `utility_definitions.s.properties.player_rotation = 180`; `sp` would create a score point, not a spawn. Its continuous museum floor remains underneath, 4 cm below the room floor, and supports entry and exit at both states. This is a gallery, without an embedded grid basin. An optional em_visibility_bounds metadata AABB tells museum distance culling to measure from the expanded room boundary instead of its now-distant placement anchor.

A separate `UnscaledDoorway` is a sibling of `Room`, never a descendant. Its 1.8-by-2.4-metre opening lines up just beyond the expanded exit. Both admit the same body after the change, but only the room doorway has been multiplied. This fixed comparison makes the transform hierarchy visible.

Verification: commons/testing/probe_drink_me_room.gd loads the actual museum hall, takes the bottle through the desktop pointer, checks both door sizes against a body capsule, scale boundaries, arrival clearance, landing collision, descent and visibility near the exit. Headset comfort and learner discovery remain to be walked by a person.

# An opening for whom?

A doorway can remain geometrically similar and change its practical meaning. Here a 0.45 by 0.60 metre opening becomes 1.8 by 2.4 metres while the visitor's body remains fixed. Uniform positive scaling does not change the topology of the room's mesh. It changes the space available to a body with particular dimensions and movement capacities. Those claims should remain distinct.

The change is a cut followed by relocation onto the enlarged table. The experience therefore combines a new room scale with a new viewpoint; the sudden sense of shrinking is not evidence of a continuously changing body. The two lines of code let that construction be questioned.

The simulation chooses its boundary: room and contents belong inside the multiplication; the museum, visitor, speed and gravity remain outside. This exclusion makes the encounter work. It also prevents the effect from being a complete physical model of shrinking an organism. The scene is a specific visual and bodily argument whose assumptions can be examined.

The crystal figure's difference precedes the visitor's new scale. Her enlargement supplies no reason to make her an enemy. Food that has opened into flowers can become a landscape without becoming fully familiar. The encounter lets the unfamiliar host movement and attention.

The serving steps are an authored accommodation. They are visible at both scales, and do not imply that the original sculpture was naturally traversable. A different body or movement system may require another route. Return to this question with sine, noise and the later discussions of terrain and existence.

The wall across the hall is also a choice: it makes the doorway experiment part of forward passage while allowing retreat. Its demand to pass through this room is an authored teaching condition, rather than a necessity of scaling itself.
