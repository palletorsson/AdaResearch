# Keeping time, keeping a place

The score is exact about the cells we select. A footfall between its steps remains outside that account. We can move with the pattern, anticipate it, miss it or ignore it. The body can inhabit a rhythm without being reduced to the sequence that plays it.

The current floor gives us three things to distinguish: the stored score, the changing light, and our movement. Stopping one clock lets another continue. A selected sound reaches the floor as a brief flash, but a flash does not preserve everything about its source: the first and thirteenth steps can arrive at the same column. What survives the translation becomes visible through the comparison.

An unexpected beat can be kept. Its address gives us a way to return to it, to listen again before calling it wrong. The grid limits the places where we can put an event, yet those places do not settle what we can make of the event when it returns. The chapter lets precision and pleasure ask different questions of the same arrangement.
