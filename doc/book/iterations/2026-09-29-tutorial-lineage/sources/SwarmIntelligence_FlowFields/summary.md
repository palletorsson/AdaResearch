# SwarmIntelligence_FlowFields — Summary

The flow field assigns direction to space. A vector field defined on a grid — generated by Perlin noise or curl noise — gives every point a direction. Particles read the local vector at their position, move accordingly, and trace paths that reveal the field's hidden topology: vortices, laminar streams, saddle points, and divergence zones. No pathfinding, no goals, no memory. Just local obedience to arrows.

The field is the mathematical abstraction underlying all swarm navigation. Physarum's trail gradients, boids' steering forces, and ant pheromone maps are all vector fields. This map isolates the concept by presenting the field pre-built — no agent authored it, no feedback loop shapes it. Curl noise produces divergence-free flow where particles follow contour lines without accumulating or depleting, mimicking atmospheric and oceanic currents. Bilinear interpolation smooths the discrete grid into continuous trajectories. Time evolution drifts the noise parameter, moving vortices and shifting streams so particles must continuously adapt.

The map's open channel geometry with a central ridge creates a topological feature where flow patterns bifurcate — streams split around the ridge, making convergence and divergence behavior spatially legible. The `FlowFieldMain` artifact renders thousands of particles streaming through invisible currents.

This is the second map in the Swarm Intelligence sequence. After Physarum demonstrated biological flow networks built by agents, this map strips away the feedback loop and presents the field itself as the primary object. The separation clarifies what is agent and what is environment, preparing the learner for boids (next map), where agents will compute their own vectors from neighbor queries rather than reading a pre-existing grid.
