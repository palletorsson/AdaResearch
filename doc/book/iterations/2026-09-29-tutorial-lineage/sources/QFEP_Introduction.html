# Four controls, one result

In the previous room, the formula waited to be questioned. It hangs again at the back of this hall; on the console in front of it, it returns a number.

Can two different arrangements of a model produce the same number?

<!-- @qfep_balance_workbench -->

Move one control on the workbench and watch both the numerical result and the shape of the four-axis display. Press KEEP. A pale outline holds that arrangement while your next movements redraw the coloured shape.

Now try changing two controls to return the result towards the kept total. Look for two configurations that look different on the axes but give nearly the same number. The outline remembers what the total cannot.

The console combines four adjustable quantities. F names a modelling or free-energy contribution,[^friston] E an entropy contribution, lambda their relative weighting, and phi the weighting attached to change. Its current calculation is F minus lambda times E, plus phi times a fixed change value of 0.3. Raising F raises the result. Raising E lowers it in proportion to lambda. Raising phi adds more of the fixed change contribution. When lambda is zero, moving E does not affect the total through that term.

Try the special case with lambda at zero before comparing more complicated settings. Watch what happens to the total when E changes, then give lambda a nonzero value and repeat. The contrast reveals a dependency: E’s contribution passes through another control. A slider can move through its full range without affecting one particular result when its weight is zero. That is a property of this calculation, not evidence that entropy is absent from every surrounding object.

This is an operational introduction to the project’s QFEP expression, QFE = F − λE(S) + φΔE(S,t). The console lets you inspect that arithmetic. It does not measure these quantities from a living system, and its fixed change value is not an observed rate.

QFEP stands for **Queer Free Energy Principle**. Ada uses the name to ask something of a model as well as something through it: whose persistence does it support, which differences can it notice, and who may change its terms? In the project’s research documents, Q keeps those terms open to revision while protecting the capacities of what the model leaves out to live, act and answer back. More entropy is not, by itself, more freedom. A boundary can shelter a body; opening it can also expose that body to harm.[^qfep]

All four rails on this console run from zero to one. Its phi is a nonnegative weight; the separate phi rail nearby, and the later Phi hall, also allow negative values. A familiar symbol does not guarantee the same range. Read the rail as well as the letter.

The display says “nearest project reference.” Its name and colour come from comparing your arrangement with seven authored examples. A name can change while you are still moving the same four handles. Watch when it changes: the border between two reference examples is a choice made in the comparison, not an experimentally detected physical phase transition.

Return to your two nearly equal totals. What distinction did the total lose? One number can summarise several contributions while concealing how they compensated for one another. Keeping the separate values visible prevents a convenient summary from becoming the whole account.

The documents call the return of this question to the maker **Self-Q**. A structure teaches its maker what to notice next. After enough time with these four rails, we may begin looking only for things that fit them. What have they made thinkable? What have they made harder to think? What continues beyond their reach? The museum must be able to ask this of its own rooms, including this one.[^self-q]

For a more demanding version, the room could receive measured quantities from one clearly specified simulation. It would then need to show how those measurements were defined and how changing the weights affected a particular decision. That is the route from a manipulable formula to an accountable model.

The next room starts with a smaller, computable question: what makes an outcome easy or difficult to predict?

[^qfep]: The project’s [QFEP development notes](../../../doc/research/qfep/README.md) and [Entropic morality](../../../doc/research/qfep/entropic-morality.md) develop this ethical commitment. It is a research proposal, not a moral law derived from entropy or a result established by this console.

[^self-q]: [Project entry: The Self-Q](../../../doc/ENTRY.md#the-self-q-recursive-qfep) names the maker’s own history and the three questions used to examine a design decision.

[^friston]: Karl Friston, "The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory?", *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 11 (2010), 127–138. The console's F is a rail from zero to one named after that quantity; it computes none of it.

# QFEP Introduction

The formula arrives as four grabbable spheres on a plinth. Pick them up, read their names, feel the weights.

Declare a term sphere.

```gdscript
class_name TermSphere
extends RigidBody3D

@export var symbol: String = "F"
@export var description: String = ""
@export var base_color: Color = Color.WHITE
```

Each sphere is a RigidBody3D that carries a symbol and a description. The formula is a bag of four such spheres. Physics treats them as objects; the learner treats them as ideas.

Instantiate the four terms.

```gdscript
func build_formula(parent: Node3D) -> void:
    _add_term(parent, "F", "free energy", Color(0.9, 0.7, 0.3))
    _add_term(parent, "E", "entropy", Color(0.3, 0.6, 0.9))
    _add_term(parent, "λ", "order-chaos mix", Color(0.8, 0.4, 0.7))
    _add_term(parent, "φ", "rate sensitivity", Color(0.5, 0.9, 0.5))
```

Warm gold for F. Cool blue for E. Magenta for λ.

Green for φ. The colour key stays stable across every map in the sequence.

Lay the spheres on a plinth.

```gdscript
func _add_term(parent: Node3D, sym: String, desc: String, col: Color) -> void:
    var sphere := preload("res://commons/artifacts/qfep/term_sphere.tscn").instantiate()
    sphere.symbol = sym
    sphere.description = desc
    sphere.base_color = col
    parent.add_child(sphere)
```

The helper loads the prefab and configures it from the scene. Changing term data means changing exports, not rewriting the artifact.

Render the symbol on the sphere.

```gdscript
func render_label(label: Label3D) -> void:
    label.text = symbol
    label.modulate = base_color
    label.billboard = BaseMaterial3D.BILLBOARD_ENABLED
```

Billboarded labels stay readable at every angle. The sphere is both a body and a sign.

Speak the description when grabbed.

```gdscript
func _on_grabbed(_hand: Node) -> void:
    var panel: Label3D = get_tree().get_first_node_in_group("term_readout")
    panel.text = "%s — %s" % [symbol, description]
```

Grabbing a sphere publishes its meaning to a shared panel. The learner reads by touching. The formula becomes a conversation with the body.

Display the assembled formula.

```gdscript
func show_formula(label: Label3D) -> void:
    label.text = "QFE = F − λ·E(S) + φ·ΔE(S,t)"
    label.font_size = 64
    label.modulate = Color(1.0, 1.0, 0.95)
```

The full line hangs above the plinth. Learners see the whole before the parts. Later maps will isolate each term.

You have met the formula. The next map, F Term, zooms into free energy and the trap of pure order.

func colorize_panel(panel: Label3D, sym: String) -> void:

The panel tints to match the sphere the learner just grabbed. Name and colour arrive together.

# Keep a reading; change its arrangement

The previous hall let you question a formula. This workbench evaluates one small implementation of it. Its four rails supply F, E, lambda and phi, each between zero and one. None is a sensor measuring the surrounding room.

Move one rail. Watch its point on the four-axis diagram and the number below. Press KEEP: the pale outline and retained total stay while the coloured shape follows your next movements. The instrument holds one arrangement at a time. Pressing KEEP again replaces it.

The implemented calculation is:

```gdscript
return _F - _lambda * _E + _phi * rate_delta_e
```

Here `rate_delta_e` is fixed at 0.30. Its historical name does not make it a measured rate. F and E are supplied numbers too; this workbench neither estimates a probability distribution nor evaluates free energy from a model.

Try F = 0.4, E = 0.2, lambda = 0.5 and phi = 0.5. The result is 0.45. Keep it. Change F to 0.6 and E to 0.6. The total returns to 0.45, but the two outlines differ. Two increases have compensated in this calculation. The saved arrangement makes their difference available to inspection.

Then set lambda to zero. Moving E still changes the diagram, but no longer changes this total. Restore a nonzero lambda and repeat. A control’s consequence depends on the expression receiving it.

The name and colour are selected separately: the script compares your four values with seven authored reference tuples, using the sum of absolute differences. The closest example supplies the label. Equal totals need not receive the same name. These are project references, not physical phases detected by the instrument.

The nearby formula and separate lambda/phi rails remain secondary comparisons. They are not wired to the workbench’s four controls. The separate phi rail runs from -1 to +1; this console’s phi runs from 0 to 1. Read the particular instrument’s domain.

The current hall preserves the earlier term spheres and other works. Proposals for remembered scenes, a responsive room and a measured receiver remain in [design-proposals.md](design-proposals.md), clearly marked as development work. Next, the F hall can ask what a prediction would actually be compared against.

# What the total cannot keep

The coloured shape changes. The number returns. Without the retained outline, this return could look like nothing happened.

Try it before giving it a larger name. Find two arrangements that nearly agree in their total. One may have more F, another less E. Their equality belongs to the calculation you are using. Your hand can still distinguish the paths that brought them here.

This is where the critical question enters the tutorial. A summary gives you something useful to compare, but it decides which differences can compensate for which others. If this number later selects a body, a route or an action, what differences would that selection leave behind? The present console does not make those decisions. It gives the question a visible beginning.

KEEP preserves an arrangement without requiring it to win. You can return to it, disagree with it, or pass through the same total from elsewhere. There is room here for an account of difference that does not immediately turn difference into a ranking. That possibility is part of Ada’s queer inquiry; a high entropy setting does not acquire it automatically.

The rail also draws a boundary. Phi cannot go below zero on this console, though the separate rail nearby permits it. Neither handle is the whole concept. A limit becomes available to thought when we can meet it, name it and build a comparison beyond it.

Even the reassuring names are choices. The instrument calls your setting the nearest of seven project references. Move across a boundary and its colour changes. Nothing in that colour change proves a natural division between kinds of life. It exposes a comparison rule, which can itself become material for another experiment.

The earlier proposal imagined the laboratory recalling scenes from your walk. That remains a promising encounter to build. It would be stronger if those returns could resist the formula as well as illustrate it: an earlier work might contain something this new summary cannot keep.
