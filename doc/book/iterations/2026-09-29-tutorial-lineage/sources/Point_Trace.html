# Duration and Residue

Bring the detour.

There was time between the ends. We passed through it. Let the next line try to keep something of the passage.[^point-trace-klee]

## Let the hand leave a line

<!-- @draw_dot -->

Three coloured pens wait on their plinths. Take the red pen and draw a short loop in the air. Pause with it still in your hand. Beside the line, a small instrument tilts its face towards reading: a count above a table of positions.

The nib is the point being followed. Turn your wrist while keeping your hand almost still: the tip can travel while your grip barely moves.

Watch during the pause. Does another point enter merely because time passes?

Move again. Give the loop a tail, then release the pen. Your hand can rest while the line stays. Something of the movement has become available to look at from another side.

The count comes from a list:

```gdscript
var point_count = _trail_points.size()
```

`_trail_points` holds positions in the order they were kept; `size()` counts them. The recorder joins each position to the next with a straight segment. Repeated in small enough steps, the two-point construction from the last room lets us see a curve. The gesture has acquired joints. Between those joints, the program draws a connection; your hand may have taken another way.[^point-trace-sampling]

The recorder waits for at least five millimetres of movement before considering another position. A still pause can leave the count unchanged while Point Zero's counter keeps running. You spend time holding the line still; the position list does not say how long you waited.

Pickup, tracking, drawing, the letters on the screen: more code is running than we can read here. We follow the movement gate because it answers the question our hand just asked.

Each row gives three coordinates for a retained position. As the drawing grows, earlier rows leave the small table. The drawing still has its beginning. What has left the screen has not yet left the line.

## Another resolution, another gesture

The other pens give the line a coarser grain. From red through green to blue, their grids grow wider.

Repeat a small bend with the blue pen. Then enlarge the movement until another turn appears. Your wrist is negotiating with a spacing. A gesture that one recorder can distinguish may need to become larger for another.

The program rounds each coordinate to the nearest grid position. Several movements of your hand can arrive at the same saved address. Until a different address is kept, the line has no new corner.[^point-trace-quantisation]

The saved positions lie on the grid, but the segments between them can cut across it diagonally. A grid of corners does not require a drawing made only of right angles.

Now give the coarse recorder something it might do well. Make a corner you can return to. Let your hand wander while one address stays steady, then find the edge where it changes. The same interval that swallowed a small flourish can help you repeat a shape. What kind of drawing do you want to make with it?

There is also a limit to how many positions a pen can keep. Near the exit, another dot has a shorter memory. We will reach its limit while drawing.


## After the hand lets go

<!-- @trace_ball_ramp -->

A black ball rests on a pale plinth. A plank slopes from its edge to the floor. Pick up the ball, set it near the top of the plank and let go.

The ball continues. A thin black line stays behind it.

The point has acquired weight. Your hand chose where to release it; gravity, the slope and contact with the plank carry the movement further. Here the recorder follows the ball's centre. The mark hangs a radius above the surface it seems to draw on.

Wait until the ball settles in the shallow tray. The line stops growing. Walk alongside it: the slope has become part of the drawing. Carry the ball back and release it from another place. The next release begins a new trace.

You have set a line in motion and let something else continue it. How much of the drawing did you make when you placed the ball?


## Touch the page

<!-- @whiteboard -->

Four pens and an eraser wait on the shelf beneath the wall-mounted whiteboard. These belong to the board. Take the red one and touch its nib to the face. Make a short stroke, lift the pen, cross a little space, and touch down again.

Your hand travelled across the gap. The picture leaves it white.

The pen writes within the board's edges and close enough to its face. Lift beyond that contact allowance, and the next touch begins a new stroke. Your hand keeps moving across the gap; the rule stops admitting it to the picture. You can use that interruption to give two marks some space.

The board turns the accepted contacts into a painted image. The coloured pens have the familiar spacings. A lift of the hand gives the drawing a gap.

## A line with a short memory

<!-- @trace_short_memory -->

The amber point near the exit has room for thirty-two positions. Draw a loop, then lead it away. Small beads mark the positions the cord joins. Watch its far end.

At first you make more line. Then you begin to unmake it.

Hold your hand still. Does the remaining line wait with you?

This recorder uses the same movement gate as the red pen. We have given its list less room. When a new position takes it over that limit, the oldest goes:

```gdscript
if _trail_points.size() > trail_max_points:
    _trail_points.pop_front()
```

Waiting does not spend another place. Continuing the drawing can. Thirty-two positions are neither thirty-two seconds nor a fixed length of line.

Now try drawing with the loss. Give yourself a tail. Curl it around your other hand, then lead it elsewhere. The space you just marked can open again. What would you make with a line that follows you without keeping all of where you have been?

<!-- @ -->

Return to a line you want to keep. Release the drawing pen after making it: that release sends a copy onward. With the amber dot, only the surviving positions travel.[^point-trace-carry]

We can carry the record forward. The movement that made it has already passed.[^point-trace-inscription] The copied positions can enter another program and be given another task.

The recorder was made to keep a gesture. Its list can now become something to compose with. A bend of the wrist may return at the size of a room, inviting another movement. The first use is still there, but it no longer tells us everything the record can become.[^point-trace-hauntology]

In the next room, look for your bend. It may have grown.

[^point-trace-klee]: Paul Klee, [*Pedagogical Sketchbook*](https://openendedgroup.com/field2/assets/Klee_Paul_Pedagogical_Sketchbook_1960.pdf), translated by Sibyl Moholy-Nagy (1960 edition; German original 1925), opening section. Klee begins with an “active line on a walk” and a moving point. Ada makes the recording conditions part of that encounter: which point moves, when a position is accepted, and how retained positions become a line.

[^point-trace-sampling]: Claude E. Shannon, [“Communication in the Presence of Noise”](https://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/S62.12/docs/Shannon_noise.pdf), *Proceedings of the IRE* 37, no. 1 (1949), 10–21, Theorem 1, gives a reconstruction result for band-limited signals sampled uniformly in time. It is a technical neighbour, not a guarantee for this trace: frame timing, a movement threshold and spatial rounding do not meet those assumptions. Joining retained positions with straight segments cannot by itself recover the intervening gesture.

[^point-trace-quantisation]: Godot’s [`snappedf`](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_%40globalscope.html#class-globalscope-method-snappedf) returns the nearest multiple of a spacing. Here this quantises spatial components; it does not set a fixed temporal sampling rate. The separate movement gate determines whether to consider another reading. Changing either operation can alter the record, for different reasons. The [technical companion](/book?map=Point_Trace&section=technical) gives the spacings, recording gate and list capacities.

[^point-trace-carry]: A drawing pen must retain at least two positions to send a copy onward. The whiteboard's image and the ball's path stay in this hall. The [technical companion](/book?map=Point_Trace&section=technical) distinguishes these records and their transfer routes.

[^point-trace-inscription]: Sigmund Freud’s [“A Note upon the ‘Mystic Writing-Pad’”](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Freud_WritingPad.pdf) (1925) and Jacques Derrida’s “Freud and the Scene of Writing,” in [*Writing and Difference*](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/W/bo27619783.html), translated by Alan Bass (1978), open a neighbouring inquiry into inscription, retention and the relation between a mark and what is no longer present. Here they open a question to pursue: what can a retained mark tell us about a movement that has already passed?

[^point-trace-hauntology]: Jacques Derrida's [*Specters of Marx*](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781040559963_A63760427/preview-9781040559963_A63760427.pdf) (1993; English translation by Peggy Kamuf, 1994), “Exordium,” connects inheritance with responsibilities towards those no longer or not yet present. Ada extends this question to technical use: a tool can carry an earlier purpose into an unanticipated connection. The [project afterword](/book-review/doc/book/iterations/2026-09-23-book-frame/index.html#afterword) develops this proposal alongside entropy as difference and Ashby's requisite variety.

# Point Trace

Point_Lines gave us a segment between two positions. Here we keep intermediate positions in order. The lesson is how a reading becomes part of that list, and how the list becomes a drawing.

## Watch a position enter

Draw a short loop with the red pen. Pause, then move again. Compare the point count with two neighbouring rows on the tilted display.

The installed scenes use a 5 mm movement gate. The script can inspect the held point during process callbacks, but a callback need not add a position. This excerpt reduces the recording branch in draw_dot.gd by leaving out display updates, fading and progress bookkeeping:

```gdscript
var current_global = _draw_sphere.global_position
var dist = current_global.distance_to(_last_global_position)

if dist < min_segment_distance:
    return

_last_global_position = current_global
var rec: Vector3 = _shape_sample(current_global)

if not _trail_points.is_empty() and _trail_points[_trail_points.size() - 1].is_equal_approx(rec):
    return

_trail_points.append(rec)
```

The first return declines movement below the threshold. Passing that test updates the comparison position before shaping the sample. The second return declines a shaped position that repeats the preceding saved one. `append()` puts a new accepted position at the end of the list.

The comparison position therefore need not be the last saved position. Movement can pass the first test and still produce a duplicate after shaping. While the tool is released, recording is disabled and the comparison position follows the resting tool.

## Change where a sample can stand

The red, green and blue pens use 10, 40 and 80 mm spacing. In free space, the shaping operation rounds each world-coordinate component:

```gdscript
var s: float = resolution_mm / 1000.0
rec = Vector3(snappedf(rec.x, s), snappedf(rec.y, s), snappedf(rec.z, s))
```

These lines show the free-space rounding operation with the position named `rec`. The complete helper also handles nearby whiteboard surfaces. A recording lattice changes spatial grain; it does not set a fixed number of samples per second.

Repeat a small bend, then enlarge it for the coarse recorder. Try deliberately returning to an address. Only consecutive duplicates are rejected: an ordered trace can revisit an earlier position after visiting others.

## Join what was kept

The count is the size of the retained list:

```gdscript
var point_count = _trail_points.size()
```

The mesh uses `Mesh.PRIMITIVE_LINE_STRIP` and supplies the retained positions in order. Each adjacent pair gives a straight segment. The list supplies endpoints for that segment; it does not establish the intervening route of the hand.

The instrument displays the last ten positions while the larger count includes the whole retained list. The three drawing pens keep at most 4096 positions each. Beyond that capacity, `pop_front()` removes the oldest position. This is a bounded array maintained by removal, not a circular-buffer implementation. Fading is off in these placements.

## Meet the limit sooner

The amber `trace_short_memory` point inherits the same recorder with `trail_max_points = 32`. Its plaque names the capacity. Keep drawing until the beginning is removed, then pause. Fading is off: elapsed time alone does not delete a point. The limit counts accepted positions, not seconds or metres. On release, only the surviving positions are copied onward.

## Move the observed point

The pen uses the same recorder and threshold, but `WritingTip` is the observed point. Turning the barrel moves its nib even when the grip changes position very little. Its fixed XRTools grab point aligns the nib with the hand's forward direction on pickup.

At the whiteboard, lift a pen between two marks. The board's separate pen script writes only while held, within the surface bounds and contact allowance. It starts a fresh stroke after the nib leaves. The drawing can omit the journey across the gap.

## Carry the record onward

Releasing a drawing pen calls `TraceData.add_trace(_trail_points)`. The store copies lists containing at least two positions. It survives room changes during the running game; it does not save these traces to disk. Whiteboard pixels follow another storage route.

The copied list contains positions without per-point timestamps. Grid can display and reshape those positions, but cannot recover the timing of a pause from them. That is the record we take into Point_Line_Grid.

See [technical.md](technical.md) for exact source paths, surface rules and the optional timed recorder. Earlier extension sketches are retained in [the previous tutorial](../../../doc/space/point-trace-focus-2026-09-16/previous/tutorial.md).

# Point Trace — implementation reference

The current book develops the selected-position recorder, its spatial grain, its sampling point, and the whiteboard's contact rule. [tutorial.md](tutorial.md) gives the short programming route. This reference distinguishes those installed behaviours from optional comparisons and possible extensions.

## Drawing dots and stick

Both scenes use [draw_dot.gd](../../primitives/point/draw_dot.gd). Their scene properties set `min_segment_distance = 0.005` metres and `trail_max_points = 4096`. Recording requires the pickable to be held. The dot observes its GrabPoint; the stick observes `GrabStick/Blade/Top/TrackBall`.

In the recording branch of `_process()`, the script reads the observed world position and compares it with `_last_global_position`. A reading below the movement threshold is declined. Passing the gate updates that comparison position before calling `_shape_sample()`. If the shaped result approximately equals the immediately preceding saved position, it is declined; otherwise it is appended.

The gate's reference is therefore the last gate-passing reading, not necessarily the last retained point or the previous frame's position. A released tool updates the reference without extending the trace. Returning to an earlier position after other saved positions is allowed.

The three drawing pens use 10 mm, 40 mm and 80 mm spacing. In free space the helper rounds world X, Y and Z to multiples of the spacing. Near a whiteboard it can shape the sample in the board's frame, then convert it back to world coordinates. That capture zone extends 0.05 m from the board plane, with 0.02 m around its bounds; positions are clamped to the face and offset 0.004 m for visibility. This behaviour is separate from the board's own pens.

## Reading the line

The retained coordinates enter an ImmediateMesh line strip. Adjacent positions are connected directly, including diagonals across the recording lattice. These segments are constructed geometry, not additional measurements.

The cased display tilts 35 degrees from vertical. Its count is read after capacity enforcement; the table shows the last ten retained positions in world metres, with at least three decimal places. Eighteen retained points produce rows 9–18. These numbers are current list positions, not permanent sample identifiers.

The three panels are fixed to their plinths and stay visible at zero points and after release. Appending or evicting points does not make it chase the newest endpoint. The casing and display do not change the data being reported.

When the list exceeds 4096 positions, its oldest position is removed. Live fading is off here. The optional `_trail_times` array is populated only when fading is enabled, so it is empty under these placements' default settings. The live position list does not encode the duration of stationary pauses.

## The whiteboard's pens

The map enables separate pens with `#pens:1`. [whiteboard_pen.gd](../../artifacts/whiteboard/whiteboard_pen.gd) reads the nib of the held tool; [whiteboard_drawing.gd](../../artifacts/whiteboard/whiteboard_drawing.gd) converts its world position into the drawing surface's local frame.

A nib outside the bounds or more than 0.035 m from the plane ends the stroke. The next valid contact starts another stroke. The four pens use 0/10/40/80 mm positional spacing before conversion to canvas coordinates. NO GRID omits that rounding; the image still consists of pixels.

At this board's 2.0 by 1.2 m size, the canvas is 1024 by 614 pixels. A brush with a three-pixel radius connects valid contacts; the eraser paints white with a 22-pixel radius. The dedicated `whiteboard_canvas.gd` retains a bounded pixel image and uploads changes once per frame when dirty. It has no wet-paint feedback, diffusion or idle fading. Its image stays with the board during this visit.

# Point_Trace — current intent

Question: What has to happen before movement enters a record?

The book follows the detour from Point_Lines through four related decisions: whether movement passes the recorder's gate; where a retained position may stand; which part of a tool is being tracked; and whether contact admits movement to a drawing. An ordered list of selected positions, joined by straight segments, is the main new capability.

Three drawing pens share the draw_dot recorder and a 5 mm movement gate, with red, green and blue grids of 10, 40 and 80 mm. The plain nib is the sampled point; the grip aligns forward on pickup. The wall-mounted whiteboard keeps four surface-writing pens and an eraser. The ball ramp offers a movement continued by gravity. Telemetry and the science screen remain secondary.

The neutral dot, draw_stick, head_trace_projection and automatic_writing_desk have been removed from this hall at the author's request. Their code is retained; [retired encounter passages](trace-retired-encounters.md) preserve the former manuscript material.

The recording rules can hide a small difference and make another position easier to return to. The critical text should ask what a particular practice needs from its record, who chooses the rules, and how those rules could be challenged. It should not equate a sampled position list with preserved intention, complete duration, or freedom.

Release a drawing pen to copy its retained positions into TraceData. Grid receives that record, centres and scales its display, and applies another lattice. The source is already sampled and may already be spatially rounded; Grid makes addresses and changes of representation the next explicit question.

# Point Trace — what enters the record?

The question carried from Lines is concrete: two endpoints did not retain the detour. Here a list of positions keeps more of its shape. What work must happen before a movement becomes one of those positions?

## A pause without another point

Hold the drawing dot still and watch its count. The process loop continues, but movement below the threshold does not add a position. In these placements the retained list contains no per-point timestamps. The drawing can therefore remain unchanged through a pause whose duration matters to the person holding the tool.

This is a limit of a particular recorder. The telemetry nearby can append timed rows during stillness. Adding a clock to a record makes other comparisons possible; it does not make intention or the experience of waiting readable automatically.

The body has undergone something that one instrument has no field for. We can ask whether this absence matters to a drawing, to a performance, or to someone making a judgement about the person who moved. The purpose of the record changes the consequences of what it omits.

## A curve partly constructed

The program joins neighbouring saved positions with straight segments. That line lets us see a shape, including across intervals where no further position was retained. Its apparent continuity can make the record feel more complete than the measurements establish.

A segment between two samples may be a useful account of their connection. It may also cross a space the hand did not cross. There are two operations to inspect: selecting positions and constructing something between them. Neither operation can establish the whole event on its own.

The claim that a line erases a prior gesture also needs care. A segment can be defined without first recording a journey. Erasure becomes an actual operation when a program deletes a record or replaces it with a smaller one. A representation's limits matter without requiring an invented history of what was once inside it.

## A grain to work with

The coarse dot moves readings onto an 80 mm lattice. A small flourish may disappear into repeated coordinates. That same range can let the hand wander while an address remains steady, making a chosen corner easier to repeat.

The useful question is what this instrument lets someone make. A visitor can enlarge a movement, work with the stepped form, change tools, or decide that the missing difference matters. The grid has become part of the practice rather than merely a defect to overcome.

Queer possibility can appear in that change of use: an interval treated as inadequate for faithful capture becomes material for another drawing. This does not make coarseness inherently queer or finer recording inherently oppressive. It keeps open the question of who gets to define the task, choose the grain, and refuse the instrument's account.

## A limit that changes the task

The amber companion keeps only thirty-two positions. It makes the loss of a beginning encounterable during a short drawing, while the original dots still offer longer records. A pause leaves its tail intact; further accepted positions make room by removing older ones. Time passing and a record changing are different events here.

A visitor may want to preserve a loop, or discover a use for a line that clears space as it travels. Neither purpose settles which capacity is right. The comparison makes the question answerable through another gesture. Its small memory is a chosen limit we can change, not a measured limit of the headset and not an entropy measure.

## Which point stands for the body?

The stick keeps the same movement threshold while observing a point farther from the fist. Turning the wrist can move that point through an arc. The resulting line describes the trajectory of the chosen tip; it does not become a complete description of the hand.

The head-trace square follows a point the visitor is already carrying. Its floor projection can be a useful account of a route while different heights coincide. Lowering the head and returning makes that choice available to inspect. The suspended version keeps another difference but still has no shoulders or intention. Neither representation needs to become a complete body to be useful; the question is which account a particular purpose requires, and whether its omissions remain available to question.

At the board, proximity to the face and the board's edges decide where a held pen can write. Lifting makes an interval of movement absent from the image. The visitor can use that absence to separate marks. Here the recording boundary supports an action the person wants to perform.

These choices become political when their outputs acquire authority over someone: when a selected point stands for a whole body, or a missing mark becomes evidence that nothing happened. The museum can make the choice inspectable and invite another use. It should leave room to question the interpretation as well as adjust the parameters.

## The authority of a legible instrument

Eighteen retained positions can be displayed beside ten coordinate rows. Both readings can be accurate while the small window leaves earlier positions out of view. The casing makes the instrument easier to read and gives its report a finished appearance. It does not widen the report's scope.

Ask where row 1 has gone. Compare the surviving line with the list operation. Ask what a pause contributes, then inspect the movement gate. The display and the code answer related questions, and the visitor's purpose helps decide which answer matters.

The writing desk now keeps head height against time. A level stretch can record a wait, where the drawing dot's spatial list adds nothing. An interval without measurements leaves blank paper. These forms of apparent quiet have different conditions. Neither a steady height nor an empty interval can establish attention, reluctance or absence of thought. The default generated handwriting remains available elsewhere as a further comparison: what makes a mark feel as if it knows you?

## Reduction and the possibility of reply

The project's [entropic-morality essay](../../../doc/research/qfep/entropic-morality.md) asks how to make necessary reductions while remaining answerable for their consequences. Trace gives that question an instrument: which differences reach its list, which can be recovered from its display, and how can a visitor work differently with the rule?

The point count is not an entropy measure, and no numerical QFEP parameter has been established by this encounter. The connection is methodological. Make a bounded account, expose its conditions, and keep open the possibility that another purpose needs another account.

The copied positions can travel to Grid and take on a new use. Their usefulness does not require us to mistake them for everything that happened. What might we want from them there that this recorder has not kept?

Earlier philosophical associations and drafts remain in [the previous critical text](../../../doc/space/point-trace-focus-2026-09-16/previous/critical.md). They are retained research material, not evidence that the current tool preserves a complete duration or intention.

## A record can acquire another task

The author's 28 September connection to hauntology concerns inherited purposes and uses that have not yet become actual. The drawing record retains a prior gesture while becoming available for another construction. In Grid it can acquire another centre, scale and quantisation; following the enlarged bend remains a visitor's invitation, not an automatic walking route. This gives the final chapter's short passage its material occasion.

# Point Trace - Map Summary

## Overview
Point Trace introduces duration and embodied gesture into geometry. Where previous maps dealt with discrete abstractions (points, measured lines, grids), the trace accumulates over time as a visible record of continuous movement. This map foregrounds gesture, repetition, and the residue of action - revealing geometry as a lived process rather than instantaneous calculation.

## Spatial Layout
- **Dimensions**: 7x14 grid (medium corridor)
- **Architecture**: Fragmented platform with raised ridge at row 4 (heights 1-2), tapering southern section
- **Entry**: Type "I" - immersive spawn into dark space
- **Atmosphere**: Very dark background [0.05, 0.05, 0.1] - intimate, focused

  - Creates persistent visual marks in space
  - Records gesture as accumulating geometry
  - Sunken to height 0 (fillhole group) creating focused drawing pit

- **Mood**: Intimate, contemplative, focused on gesture
- **Visibility**: Hidden tiles except corners - space revealed through exploration

## Learning Sequence
1. Player spawns into dark, fragmented space
2. Encounters dark_sphere creating intimate enclosure
3. Discovers draw_dot tool in sunken area
4. Experiments with continuous gesture - moving controller traces visible line
5. Observes how trace accumulates over time - unlike discrete points
6. Compares grab_sphere_point_snap (discrete) with draw_dot (continuous)
7. Experiences duration and embodiment - the time of drawing matters
8. Exits having encountered geometry that remembers movement

## Design Intent
The **sunken drawing area** (fillhole group at height 0) creates a "pit" that focuses attention on the act of tracing. The very dark background makes the glowing trace lines highly visible. The fragmented platform architecture mirrors the concept - incomplete, accumulating, not predetermined.

Unlike Point_Line which reduces gesture to endpoints, Point_Trace preserves the entire path. The draw_dot tool resists discretization - it cannot be compressed to two coordinates and a distance. It must be experienced as duration.

## Key Contrast: Trace vs. Line

**Line** (Point_Line):
- Two endpoints
- Instant calculation
- No memory of path
- Pure abstraction

**Trace** (Point_Trace):
- Continuous accumulation
- Duration required
- Records entire gesture
- Embodied residue
