# Limits of Geometric Knowledge

You left a cube whose connections could outlast its name. Now try the name sphere. What are you accepting when you recognise something as round?

<!-- @sphere -->

Begin with the ordinary sphere near the entrance. Look at it from a distance, then approach. Follow its silhouette against the room beyond it. Where does the outline give you something to inspect that the name had allowed you to pass over?

Above the entrance: **Let No One Ignorant of Geometry Enter.** It recalls the motto traditionally associated with Plato's Academy.[^ignorance-inscription] Here we have entered with a little geometry. We will use it to inspect the limits of the forms that seemed to admit us.

<!-- @geometry_courtyard -->

Inside, five solids have been given five places. Tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron, icosahedron. Off-white and almost black, each rests on a plain block. You can lift one and bring a face toward you. Its edges remain sharp. There is nothing unfinished about that sharpness.

Columns surround the group. Near the entrance their sides are easy to count; farther along, the corners draw closer together. Four divisions, then five, six, eight. Eventually the column looks round. Walking along the court, you pass through several constructions of the same architectural name.

The solids do not take that journey. An icosahedron has twenty faces because it is an icosahedron. Adding more would give you something else. In the next room, the sphere keeps its name while the number of faces changes. What did the name ask us to preserve?

<!-- @sphere_high -->

The next room holds three pairs of spheres. Start with the first pair in the resolution display. One sphere wears lines along its triangle edges; the other has plain shading. Predict whether removing the lines also changes the sphere's outline. Walk around the pair and compare.

The two meshes have the same vertex positions and triangle connections. The lines are an added overlay. They make a construction easier to follow without rebuilding its surface.

Choose one place where the silhouette changes direction. Step back until that distinction becomes difficult to see, then approach again. Your distance changed. The mesh did not.

<!-- @sphere_mid -->

Continue to the middle pair. Inspect it from a comparable distance. Which distinctions are easier to find now? Compare the marked example with its plain neighbour before comparing it with the finer pair behind you.

The finer pair uses sixteen rings and sixteen radial segments; this pair uses seven of each. Those two settings divide different directions of the surface. Changing them changes the positions and connections available for making it.

<!-- @sphere_low -->

At the coarsest pair, follow one flat patch toward its edge. Is this merely a sphere missing something, or is there a feature here you would choose to keep?

Its construction uses one ring and ten radial segments. One direction is divided more often than in the middle example, the other much less. Resolution has a distribution. It does not arrive as a single quantity called detail.

The script gives the two choices separate names:

```gdscript
sphere_mesh.radial_segments = segment_count
sphere_mesh.rings = ring_count
```

<!-- @sphere_resolution_atlas -->

Across the room, twelve smaller spheres spread the two choices out in space. Across each row, more rings divide the height. Along each column, more segments divide the circumference. Their triangle edges remain visible. You can follow one change while the other holds still.

A crowded equator can belong to a sparsely divided body. One figure has a broad flat face where another gathers many narrow triangles. Both answer to sphere. Before deciding which is more convincing, look for a shape you had not expected to find there.

A mathematical sphere consists of points at a fixed distance from a centre. The mesh joins sampled positions with flat triangles. Adding triangles can improve that approximation; no finite collection of flat patches acquires the sphere's continuous curvature. Shading can soften their appearance while the polygonal outline remains.

![The existing high, middle and low sphere artifacts in matched views: triangle edges visible above, hidden below. Each column keeps the same mesh.](/book-review/doc/book/figures/Primitives_Ignorance/sphere-visible-construction.png)

*The lower row keeps every triangle in the upper row. The lines disappear; the construction remains.*

This is where ignorance turns around. Plato distinguishes the drawn figure from the geometric object considered in thought. Here we turn our attention toward the constructed figure: what its polygons leave out, and what their particular arrangement makes available.[^ignorance-plato]

The formula specifies a sphere. The mesh gives us one finite construction through which to encounter it. The ridge is available too. We can learn from its shape without requiring it to disappear into a smoother answer.

<!-- @budget_of_smoothness -->

At the far end of this room, a counter makes the cost of these choices countable. Four numbered spheres turn at the same rate. Keep the counts hidden at first. Choose one for a distant background and one for someone to inspect close to their face. The spheres stay on their stands; you are choosing possible uses.

Press **SHOW / HIDE COUNTS**. Did the numbers change your choice? Then press **SHOW / HIDE EDGES** and follow the lines toward a pole.

Each tag gives two triangle counts. Even counting depends on what the instrument agrees to include.[^ignorance-counts]

Hide the counts while keeping the edges visible, then try the reverse. What becomes easier to judge? Both controls alter what is shown while keeping the sphere meshes in place.

A triangle count does not measure frame time or tell us which object deserves to exist. For your proposed use, name the distinction worth spending geometry on. You might spend more on a close silhouette, or keep a facet because it makes this body recognisable.

<!-- @capsule_reliquary -->

At the centre, the five-sided capsule turns above a stepped pedestal. Eight pale tiers carry it: cylinders with four divisions through eleven, each smaller than the one beneath. Corners shift around the stack. The support is making its own argument about roundness.

The capsule is taller now, nearly the height of a person. Its dark surface receives the light slowly as it turns. A small, awkward construction has been given the place of an object worth lingering before. When a ridge faces you, picture the other side. Would the same ridge be waiting there? Follow the silhouette as another side comes into view.

Five segments divide the body around its long axis. Opposite a ridge is the middle of a facet. Half a turn does not bring this cross-section back onto itself. The divisions are evenly spaced, but their odd number interrupts that particular symmetry.[^ignorance-capsule]

You cannot fold your understanding around this corner by simply repeating the front at the back. The shortcut fails. You have to follow another side, or learn the rule well enough to work it out. The object remains knowable; recognising a capsule was not yet knowing this one.

Stay with what the five divisions produced. Between the familiar pill and its polygonal construction, a body appears that the name did not prepare you for. Its surprise is made within the restriction. What would you miss if you corrected it into the capsule you expected?

This gives ignorance another direction: a limit in our expectation becomes somewhere to look. The same small set of instructions that confines the form can make more available than we first knew to ask of it.

![The five-segment capsule raised on eight cylinders, with resolutions four through eleven.](/book-review/doc/book/iterations/2026-09-27-ignorance-courtyard/five-on-four-to-eleven.png)

*The capsule keeps its five divisions. The pedestal changes the attention we give them.*

<!-- @primitive_mesh_library -->

The third room has another collection of beginnings. A wedge, a ring, a plane. A pair of letters with depth. The engine supplies these constructions before we decide what to make of them. Each has been wrapped in a reusable scene, like the boxes in the previous hall. The mesh supplies a surface; the scene can bring its material, collision and behaviour along. Choosing a shape and choosing what an object can do are still different decisions.

Here, even the word primitive has practical habits. A plane offers a surface to subdivide. A quad supplies two triangles. Move beside them and their broad faces narrow toward an edge. The letters already carry a language; their geometry lets that language cast a shadow.

One stand holds a point drawn twenty-two screen pixels wide. Approach it: its mark keeps that pixel width while the stand grows in your view. This is another way of giving a point a visible body. It does not behave like the sphere we first called Point One.[^ignorance-prefabs]

Two longer forms belong to the engine's trail meshes. Here they are shown at rest, before particles or moving bones would shape them into trails. Some of the engine's simplest forms are beginnings of movement.

The old studies remain through the side opening: the rock, the star, the choir, the extra cubes. The court has made a clear arrangement without exhausting what these forms might become.

<!-- @ -->

Carry a more specific question than whether the sphere is good enough: **enough to hold which distinction, for whom, at what distance?**

The polygonal model has a limit; our first understanding of its possibilities has one too. The next room follows a finite sequence of round-looking objects toward a boundary none of its examples reaches.

[^ignorance-inscription]: The Academy motto is a later tradition, not a securely documented inscription from Plato's lifetime. See [The Academy of Plato](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Societies/Plato/), MacTutor History of Mathematics. The museum stages the familiar wording as an instruction whose authority we can question.

[^ignorance-plato]: Plato, [Republic, Book VI](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/republic.7.vi.html), Benjamin Jowett translation, the divided-line discussion: drawn figures serve reasoning about geometric objects rather than being its ultimate objects. Ada's reversal is methodological: stay with the implemented figure and investigate its limits and possibilities. This does not establish that Plato was unaware of imperfect drawings, or that geometry cannot describe curves.

[^ignorance-counts]: Some generated triangle triples collapse at the poles. The counter distinguishes all submitted triples from those with measurable area in [Godot 4.6 SphereMesh](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_spheremesh.html) arrays. “With area” excludes triangles below a local squared-cross-product tolerance of `1e-16`; it is a practical threshold. The [technical chapter](/book?map=Primitives_Ignorance&section=technical) records all four measured pairs of counts. Overlays and counter furniture are excluded. Analytic sphere descriptions and this finite polygonal rendering are different representations.

[^ignorance-capsule]: The central installation reuses `capsule.tscn`, enlarged uniformly to 1.6 times its authored size. It uses a [CapsuleMesh](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_capsulemesh.html) with five radial segments and five rings. Its regular pentagonal cross-section repeats after 72 degrees, but not after 180 degrees about the long axis. The absent half-turn symmetry does not make all symmetry disappear. The scene turns at 45 degrees per second. These are the authored mesh and spin settings, not controls available on the plinth.

[^ignorance-prefabs]: Godot 4.6 [PrimitiveMesh](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_primitivemesh.html) is a family of generated mesh resources. The new room wraps PrismMesh, TorusMesh, PlaneMesh, QuadMesh, PointMesh, TextMesh, RibbonTrailMesh and TubeTrailMesh in reusable scenes. Box, cylinder, sphere and capsule have already appeared; a cone is a CylinderMesh with one radius set to zero. QuadMesh inherits PlaneMesh. The [PointMesh](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_pointmesh.html) display uses a point-size material: its 22 pixels describe the rendered mark, not a world-space diameter. Trail specimens show their undeformed rest geometry.

# Inspect the sphere that the name leaves out

The cube hall separated positions, connectivity and presentation. This room carries that distinction into finite models of a curved surface. The Academy motto supplies a question about knowledge; the work is to inspect the construction that knowledge has made familiar. The historical and interpretive references are in the [book text](/book?map=Primitives_Ignorance&section=final).

## Recognition, then inspection

Begin with the ordinary `sphere` near the entrance. Approach and inspect its outline. Treat it as an example of what the name leaves unspecified. It is smaller than the later resolution pairs: its default radius is 0.25, while those pairs have radius 0.5. Do not interpret this initial encounter as a controlled resolution comparison.

Proceed to `sphere_high`, `sphere_mid` and `sphere_low`, at rows 18, 21 and 24. This follows the actual order of their placements. At each pair, compare the edge-marked surface with its plain neighbour, then compare with the preceding pair from a similar distance.

| Pair | Rings | Radial segments | Radius / height |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| High | 16 | 16 | 0.5 / 1.0 |
| Middle | 7 | 7 | 0.5 / 1.0 |
| Low | 1 | 10 | 0.5 / 1.0 |

Within a pair, the generated vertex and triangle-index arrays match. Both have `inspection:1`; `inspection_edges:1` adds the overlay to one member and `inspection_edges:0` hides it on the other. The base material also matches. Comparing the pair separates mesh geometry from its presentation.

Between pairs, the geometry changes. The low example is especially useful: it has more radial divisions than the middle one, but far fewer rings. A smaller total does not mean every direction was reduced equally.

## Follow the two parameters

The low sphere's builder reads the selected configuration into these variables:

```gdscript
sphere_mesh.radial_segments = segment_count
sphere_mesh.rings = ring_count
```

The first controls division around the axis; the second controls the ring structure along it. The current room leaves their defaults in place. It does not provide buttons for rebuilding these pairs at arbitrary resolutions.

The source generates positions and joins them with triangles. The definition of a sphere specifies a fixed radius from its centre; a finite surface of flat triangles approximates that curved boundary. Smooth lighting can make a facet less conspicuous while leaving its geometry unchanged. Other ways to represent or render a sphere exist; the limit examined here belongs to these meshes.

Choose one facet or ridge you would keep for a particular visual use. This is a design proposal about an existing form, not a request to operate an editing control that the display does not have.

## Continue to the counter

The counter sits at (9,26), near the rear of the second room. Choose one of its four numbered spheres for a distant background, then one for close inspection, before revealing the counts.

| Control | What changes | What stays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SHOW / HIDE COUNTS | The information on the tilted tags | The generated sphere meshes |
| SHOW / HIDE EDGES | The extra triangle-edge overlay | The generated sphere meshes |

The spheres remain fixed on their stands and rotate at the same rate. The two controls are independent. The four radial settings are 4, 8, 16 and 64, with rings generated by `maxi(seg / 2, 2)`.

Each tag gives two counts. One reads every index triple:

```gdscript
var arrays = mesh.surface_get_arrays(0)
var triangle_entries = arrays[Mesh.ARRAY_INDEX].size() / 3
```

The other looks up the positions and excludes patches whose squared cross product is below `1e-16`. At the poles, some listed triangles collapse. The full [measured invoice and area test](/book?map=Primitives_Ignorance&section=technical) remain in the technical chapter. This distinction offers a second inquiry for readers who want to follow the instrument itself.

A count is not a frame-time measurement. Identify a desired feature, viewing distance and proposed use before treating a larger or smaller number as an improvement. Headset performance requires its own measurement.

## The capsule: the side you have not seen

The `capsule_reliquary` at (9,22) contains the enlarged original capsule. Its long axis is vertical and it turns at 45 degrees per second. Watch a ridge face you and predict what is opposite it. Observe the changing silhouette while the object turns; no pause or grab control is assumed.

The scene's authored resource contains:

```ini
radial_segments = 5
rings = 5
```

Those are separate parameters. Five radial divisions give a regular pentagonal cross-section through the straight middle of the body. Each angular interval is 360 / 5 = 72 degrees. After that rotation the cross-section fits itself again. A half-turn is 180 / 72 = 2.5 intervals: a ridge arrives opposite the middle of a facet, so this rotation does not preserve the cross-section.

The unevenness is the odd count and the mismatch of opposite sides, not irregular spacing between five segments. This is one failed symmetry test, not the disappearance of every symmetry. A regular six-sided cross-section would pass the half-turn; that comparison is calculable without adding another exhibit or suggesting a segment button here.

Try to describe what you expected before naming what the geometry does. The name "capsule" does not tell you how the hidden side is divided. Learning the rule gives another route to knowing it. Palle's question concerns the form this restriction makes available between familiar categories, and whether we can encounter it before treating its facets as defects.

## Return visits

The following studies remain available alongside the main sphere and capsule encounters. They were moved here from the longer book draft so the collection can retain its depth without requiring every argument on the first walk.

### The ideal set

Choose a tetrahedron and a cube from the held solids. Turn each until you have inspected more than one face and corner. What kind of sameness repeats within each object? Then compare with another member of the family.

The five convex regular polyhedra have congruent regular polygonal faces and the same arrangement at every vertex. They do not look identical from every possible viewpoint. Their regularity is a specified symmetry of the object, not a promise that the eye sees the same picture everywhere.

Why only five in this family? Regular face angles must fit around a convex vertex with a positive angular defect. Triangles permit three, four or five faces there; squares permit three; pentagons permit three. Six equilateral triangles or four squares already fill a flat turn. Those constraints lead to the tetrahedron, octahedron, icosahedron, cube and dodecahedron.

The list is finite because the question is restrictive. It does not list every useful solid, every beautiful object, or every possible way of making a world.

At the held octahedron, count six vertices, twelve edges and eight faces. Compare those numbers with the cube's eight, twelve and six. Vertices and faces exchange roles in their dual relationship. A different way of organising the same comparison reveals a relation the individual names conceal.

Look at what cutting the tetrahedron's corners adds. New faces appear where vertices used to be. A construction can leave the five-member regular family while remaining precise and usable. Exclusion from a catalogue is not exclusion from geometry.

### Other ways to recognise a form

Compare the star's five-point pattern under the same imagined half-turn. A familiar outline can answer one symmetry test and fail another. Name the transformation before saying an object is symmetrical.

Look for a feature on the irregular rock that you would lose by replacing it with a regular solid. Choose a use for that feature: recognition, shelter, a grip, a distinctive silhouette. What resists your first abstraction may be exactly what another task needs to keep.

The right triangle offers an invariant to compare: move or turn a rigid right triangle and the right angle remains. An invariant says which changes you are considering and which relation survives them. It is not a ranking of the objects that possess it.

The cones mark a place that invites you to look for something absent. A marker can preserve an expectation even when the thing it names is no longer there. Read the marking, then check the place.

### Further instruments in the existing collection

The snap-octahedron puzzle, two cube scenes, cylinder ring/radial display, primitive pillar, tiled science screen, seven-words choir and diamonds remain placed. They are available for later encounters and development; their existence does not add another compulsory step to this chapter. The two dark-sphere placements remain decorative.

## Scope of this pass

The sixteen older secondary/decorative placements remain in the side alcove. Native desktop approach and supplied pickup were tested; headset reach and legibility remain unverified.

## What the polygonal representation cannot retain

An analytic sphere is defined by a fixed distance from its centre. The finite surface mesh connects sampled positions with planar triangles. That triangle surface cannot coincide everywhere with the exact curved boundary. This is a limitation of the chosen representation; it is not a claim that code or mathematics cannot specify a sphere analytically.

Keep geometry, presentation and use distinct. Changing rings or radial segments changes the generated mesh. The inspection overlay changes how a fixed mesh is shown. Viewing distance and the observer's purpose change which of its features matter. The room's reference to Plato directs attention to those distinctions; its historical and interpretive sources are footnoted in final.md.

## Two directions of subdivision

First compare silhouettes from the same distance. The current default configurations are:

| Artifact | Rings | Radial segments |
|---|---:|---:|
| sphere_low | 1 | 10 |
| sphere_mid | 7 | 7 |
| sphere_high | 16 | 16 |

All three use radius 0.5 and height 1.0. Their scripts expose other configuration choices, but this room retains the defaults above. Rings and radial segments divide different directions of the surface. Their distribution matters as well as their quantity.

Each resolution is placed twice. The room adds `inspection:1` to both members and `inspection_edges:1` or `inspection_edges:0` to distinguish them. Their generated vertex and index arrays agree. The inspection mode supplies the same plain material to each pair and adds actual triangle-edge lines to one member.

The room-specific overlay is built from the mesh's index triples. It does not assume that `VERTEX_ID % 3` produces distinct barycentric coordinates for every indexed triangle; shared indices can violate that assumption. The shared inspection helper reads the indices explicitly. Existing non-inspection appearances remain available in other maps.

## Two triangle counts

Read the buffers of each generated sphere:

```gdscript
var arrays = mesh.surface_get_arrays(0)
var triangle_entries = arrays[Mesh.ARRAY_INDEX].size() / 3
```

This counts triangle entries, including collapsed entries at the poles. The helper also examines their positions:

```gdscript
if (b-a).cross(c-a).length_squared() < 1e-16:
    continue
```

The cross product is twice the triangle's oriented area vector. This squared-length tolerance excludes zero and tiny-area triangles in local coordinates. The label **with area** means triangles retained by that test, not a proof of exact nonzero area at arbitrary scale.

| Segments | Rings | Triangle entries | Retained by area test |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 2 | 24 | 16 |
| 8 | 4 | 80 | 64 |
| 16 | 8 | 288 | 256 |
| 64 | 32 | 4,224 | 4,096 |

These values were measured from meshes produced by the project's Godot 4.6 runtime. The previous formula `2 * segments * rings` matched the area-bearing count in these examples. It did not include the collapsed pole entries in the index buffer. The updated counter exposes both descriptions.

An overlay is built from the three edges of each retained triangle. Shared edges may be drawn twice. It is slightly enlarged by a factor of 1.002 to reduce coplanar flicker. Overlay geometry, stands, screens and the counter itself are excluded from the sphere counts.

## A count does not measure the whole frame

The display inventories mesh geometry. It does not time the GPU, estimate headset frame rate or measure attention. Materials, screen coverage, lighting, shadows, batching and other running work affect performance too. Use the numbers to specify a design choice, then test its perceptual and runtime consequences separately.

The low-resolution form may preserve the distinctive outline a visitor wants to recognise. The high-resolution form may be useful for a closer inspection. Neither judgment follows automatically from an integer or from the name sphere.

## Five segments: a particular symmetry fails

The installed `commons/primitives/capsule/capsule.tscn` authors a CapsuleMesh with radius 0.25, height 1.0, five radial segments and five rings. The root script defaults to `facture = "cast"`, which leaves this mesh unchanged. The reliquary instantiates this scene without a facture override, gives the visible mesh an off-black material, and scales it uniformly by 1.6. Its bottom sits at the 1.04 m top of eight 0.13 m cylinder tiers, with radial segments 4 through 11. The original rotation script remains inside the source scene. The capsule has no pickup or collider; the solid support prevents walking through its base. Its child uses `commons/primitives/prismes/rotation.gd`, with angular speed `(0,45,0)` degrees per second.

The straight middle has a regular pentagonal cross-section. Its equal angular intervals are 72 degrees. A 72-degree rotation preserves the cross-section's corners; a 180-degree rotation does not. Opposite a corner's radial direction is a side midpoint. The absent half-turn symmetry should not be described as total asymmetry or as an inability to infer the back from the full generative rule.

The capsule supplies a second limit: a familiar name or an assumed front/back correspondence does not specify the unseen side. The constraints generate an inspectable form between what "rounded pill" suggests and what these facets actually do. "Transcendental inside restrictions" is Palle's research formulation for exceeding an expectation within a generative system, not an additional property measured by the mesh API.

A direct Godot mesh measurement is archived at `doc/space/ignorance-focus-2026-09-16/capsule-return/`. It extracts a ring from the actual authored mesh, tests the 72-degree and 180-degree rotations, and compares a six-segment copy as a control. This verifies cross-section geometry, not visual legibility in the room.

Concept: Ignorance names the room's reversal of the Platonic association between geometry and knowledge. We use the geometry we have learned to inspect what this polygonal model cannot retain, and the particular forms its limits produce. The learner's lack of mastery is not the only question; the scope of the representation becomes an object of inquiry.

The library also distinguishes a generated mesh resource from the reusable scene that gives it material, collision and behaviour.

# What the polygons cannot hold

The room carries the motto traditionally associated with Plato's Academy. Its reversal asks what the geometric representation leaves unaccounted for. The visitor has learned enough to inspect the method, not only to submit to its catalogue of named forms.

In the divided-line discussion of the [Republic, Book VI](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/republic.7.vi.html), drawn figures assist thought directed toward geometric objects. Our method turns toward the particular implementation and asks what happens there. The approximation's ridges, distributions and costs become material for investigation. This is a change of research direction, not a demonstration that Plato mistook a drawing for an ideal object.

The limit must remain specific. Flat triangles do not reproduce a spherical boundary exactly. That does not mean geometry cannot describe curvature, or that every polygonal construction fails to support bodies and uses. Nor does a rougher object necessarily escape a norm: a deliberately faceted style can become another default. Ask which distinction this particular construction preserves, which it loses, and what a different encounter might require.

## Understanding does not simply fold around the corner

The capsule belongs in the main walk. Its five radial divisions let a ridge face a facet across the body. An assumed half-turn correspondence fails. The learner cannot obtain the unseen side merely by repeating a familiar front. Another view, or closer attention to the generative rule, becomes necessary.

This is a precise restriction on a shortcut. Five equal angular divisions still permit one-fifth-turn symmetry; the geometry remains describable. The productive uncertainty lies in what recognition had not yet taught us to look for. We should preserve that opening without declaring the body unknowable.

Palle calls this "transcendental inside restrictions": a form can exceed the categories and expectations with which we entered while remaining entirely produced by the available rules. The capsule matters as something found between familiar forms, before its facets are dismissed as unfinished smoothness. That is an artistic research proposition to pursue in the encounter: the geometry is finite, while our investigation of its possibilities remains open.

A teaching method that sends the capsule to the margin would close this inquiry prematurely. The sphere comparisons teach us to specify an approximation; the capsule asks whether we have mistaken that specification for an exhaustive understanding of what the form offers. There is more to uncover inside the model's limits.

## The instrument had an assumption too

At first the counter offers four spheres and a question: which one would serve the use you have in mind? The prices are hidden. There is time to decide where a visitor might stand and what they might need to notice before a number begins to organise the answer.

When the counts appear, there are two for each sphere. The mesh's index list contains triangle entries which collapse at its poles. Another count excludes triangles without measurable area. The same object supports two answers to a question that initially sounded complete: how many triangles?

The counter itself used to calculate only the smaller count. Inspecting the generated mesh revealed what that formula omitted. This does not make the earlier count meaningless. It makes its scope specific. We had to examine the instrument that was supposed to teach us to examine the sphere.

## A remainder can be an entry

At a pole, a triangle can remain present as indices while providing almost no surface to see. It is neither a hidden room inside the sphere nor an alien substance outside computation. It is a consequence of how a repeated construction arrives at a place where its rows meet.

This is a useful kind of strangeness because we can follow it. Read three indices, look up their positions, calculate an area, compare that result with the rendered mark. Each step answers something, and the answers do not become interchangeable just because the same word triangle appears in all of them.

The area test also has a threshold. The helper excludes a sufficiently small squared cross product. Its practical count therefore brings another decision into view: at what scale do we treat a patch as collapsed? The calculation helps us inspect the mesh while retaining an assumption of its own.

## Smoothness is not a confession

The paired spheres keep their geometry and change the edge overlay. A surface can become easier to inspect without becoming more geometrically detailed. Conversely, removing an overlay can make a shape easier to recognise without erasing its triangle list.

The faceted version is not inherently more truthful. The shaded version is not inherently a lie. Both are ways of making a finite construction available to a task. Ask what each reveals, what it makes harder to notice, and which encounter benefits from that choice.

A deliberate ridge might be the feature by which we recognise an object. A smoother silhouette might help us attend to movement instead of tessellation. There is no obligation to recover a supposedly original appearance before these uses can begin. There is an obligation to be specific about what the construction supports.

## Who decides what is enough?

The counter measures mesh entries and area-bearing patches. It does not measure beauty, usefulness, frame time or the freedom of a visitor. A number can inform those questions without settling them.

Viewing distance matters. So do the task, eyesight, movement, display and other demands on the running system. A choice made for a distant background may become conspicuous when someone approaches. The difference was not absent at the doorway; the conditions for noticing it changed.

The queer possibility can begin with changing the criterion. Instead of asking how cheaply the object can pass for an ideal sphere, ask which facet, seam or silhouette we want someone to encounter. That choice may spend more geometry or less. Its value belongs to what it lets us perceive and do, not to its position on a scale from coarse to fine.

## A small catalogue leaves room

The room's other forms remain available: regular solids, the faceted capsule, a truncated tetrahedron, an irregular rock, an absence marked by cones. They offer further questions without all becoming prerequisites for leaving the hall.

The five convex regular polyhedra answer a tightly specified geometric question. They do not exhaust useful forms. Cutting a corner can leave that family while producing another precise construction. Likewise, the capsule's five-sided cross-section can fail a half-turn symmetry test without becoming a failed object. Name the operation and the requirement before calling the result deficient.

We cannot inspect every source line or test every viewpoint before moving on. Choosing a path through this room is part of the research, and leaves something for another passage. The next hall approaches a limit with a finite sequence. We carry forward the habit of asking what each count includes.

## Implementation anchors

`mesh_inspection.gd` reads generated triangle indices and applies the local area tolerance. `budget_of_smoothness.gd` exposes the two counts and independent overlay control. The three sphere scripts enable matched marked/plain pairs through room configuration. These encounters support specific comparisons; they do not establish a universal geometry budget or a numerical measure of queer possibility.
