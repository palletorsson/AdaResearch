# More detail cannot change a function's contract

A height field permits variation while keeping one rule stable: each horizontal position receives one height. Its smoothness is secondary to that contract. Roughening it can make movement difficult without making a ceiling possible above a separate floor at the same position.

A volume offers another description. Different vertical positions can change classification independently. That capacity does not guarantee shelter, an entrance or a route. The distinction matters to the research question: what bodies are possible depends both on the representation's expressive capacity and on the particular space made with it.

The column instrument is selective. It samples a finite lattice and begins with the strongest observed example. It helps us find a difference, while also arranging our attention. Its count cannot speak for all positions, and it cannot determine whether an opening accommodates the player. Making its selection rule visible lets the learner question what the demonstration has privileged.

The composition desk also carries expectations in its vocabulary. An “arch” term is not an arch by declaration. Its contribution can be obscured by other terms or lost in sampling. The held surface and the change of viewpoint give us ways to inspect that mismatch. The possibility of inhabiting an interior begins with a representation that can express one; it remains a question for bodies, entrances and contact.
