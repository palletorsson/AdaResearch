# Primitives Melencolia — forms in company

The final hall of the eleven-map Primitives sequence follows Portals and leads into Transformation. Its two arrangements offer a movement from recognisable solids to the unsettled purposes of making.

The first court keeps four blue corner pyramids, a taller pink centre, and four ochre cubes. These follow an anonymous sixteenth-century geometric manuscript, not Dürer. A connection puzzle beside them tests a graph more narrowly than the complete geometric definition.

On the second platform, a new sculptural tableau follows the principal objects and image relationships of Dürer's *Melencolia I*: stone left, seated figure right, child and dog between, sphere and tools below, ladder and instruments above. Its invented depth can be inspected by walking around it. The geometry of its stone and the sums of its square are checkable; those results do not settle the scene's meaning.

The hall is 13 × 29 cells, with an entrance at (6,0) and an exit at (6,27). Its decks stand one and two metres above the floor. The original connecting ramp remains; a new descent ramp leads off the tableau platform. All nineteen authored placements remain, with the supporting torus collection and code display moved clear of the composition. The returning counter at (3,26) keeps the link to Point Zero's engine clock.

The chapter names geometric desire and opens distinct paths through Islamic geometric ornament, Escher's tessellations, Malevich, Hilma af Klint and Clark's participatory abstraction. These are written comparisons for later development; only the Dürer reconstruction was added here. The historical links and interpretive limits are set out in critical.md and the final.md notes.
