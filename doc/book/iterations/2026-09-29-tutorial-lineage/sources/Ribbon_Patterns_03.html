# What can you take with you?

Look down before choosing another appearance. In the museum, your garment grows as you enter new sequences. Returning to one does not add another layer. If you chose a nail colour in the salon, your virtual hands have already carried one decision here. A dress can carry part of the walk too.

The mark has crossed a floor, reflected, slipped along a row and acquired depth. In the previous room its values stayed fixed while the surface changed how they appeared. Here the surface is waiting beside a body. Find the bent red mark on both. What would have to stay the same for you to call this the same fabric?

*The small source reaches a flat wall and a skirt. The crown and sleeves keep the look’s own materials.*

Press **UV / FABRIC**. The familiar mark disappears into a coloured field. Walk around the preview skirt. Where does the colour return to its beginning? This view draws the surface's own two coordinates as red and green. A curved body can carry a flat address system. It also has to decide where that system joins, ends or starts again.

Return to the fabric and press **OFFSET**, then **REPEAT**. Which operation moves the mark? Which makes more copies fit? The wall and skirt both begin with four horizontal repeats and two vertical repeats in their own UV coordinates. Their physical widths differ. The wall is three metres wide: four copies sit 0.75 metres apart. Four copies around this skirt do not promise that same spacing.

```glsl
vec2 address = UV * vec2(repeats, 2.0) + vec2(phase, 0.0);
ALBEDO = texture(motif, address).rgb;
```

The source texture is four by four pixels, read with nearest filtering. `UV` supplies an address from the mesh; multiplication repeats it, addition shifts it, and the texture wraps. A small statement keeps finding new places to speak. The skirt's side and caps bring their own UV layout. We have not made a tailor's continuous cutting pattern, or promised that every seam will match.

Try **CUT OUT**. Narrow strips disappear from the wall and skirt. Look through a strip, then try to move through the sample wall. The view opens. The collision still refuses you.

```glsl
if (cut_out && fract(address.x) > 0.8) discard;
```

Here `discard` stops drawing a fragment. It does not cut a passage into the collision shape. There are already two accounts of the wall: one receives your gaze, another receives your attempt to cross. Finding their disagreement gives us somewhere precise to work. A visible opening has made a promise that the movement system does not keep.

Turn the cut-outs off. Press **FINISH** and compare a pale patch on the plane with one turning around the skirt. This control changes roughness and metallic response in the engine's material model. It leaves the source alone. Now try **VERTICES** and look along an edge.

```glsl
VERTEX += NORMAL * amplitude *
    sin(UV.y * 18.849556 + UV.x * 6.283185);
```

The shader moves rendered vertices along their normals, by at most 0.04 local units. The wall's subdivisions provide vertices to move. The garment has its own mesh and scale, so its result differs. Collision stays at the old shape; this deformation does not simulate cloth, gravity or a moving hem. Even the shading normals retain the original mesh directions. An edge has changed, but several systems still remember the earlier body.

# From source to skirt

Three primary placements: `fabric_shader_studio` (22,26), `super_drag_mirror` (31,27), `drag_catwalk` (58,24). The ordinary museum hall retains the fourteen earlier exhibits as secondary material. RotatingLaser moves from (58,39) to (58,50), with six-metre beams and a four-metre rig. Three older demos opt into `embedded:true`: no scene-wide environment or screen overlay; coloured-line paths are fitted together inside a three-unit source radius before animated sway and shader expansion.

## One recipe, explicit receivers

`fabric_recipe.gd` validates a versioned 4x4 array of palette indices (0..3), repeats (4/8), phase (0/0.125), cut-out, matte/metal finish, static displacement and UV inspection flags. It normalizes JSON numbers before making an RGBA8 nearest-filtered texture and a spatial ShaderMaterial. Both wall and skirt receive new materials from the same recipe, with their own UV coordinates. Repeats are per UV domain, not equal distances in metres.

The console supplies sixteen source cells, four paint choices and twelve pointer-operated actions. Editing updates only the wall and posed preview. SAVE writes `user://ada_studio_fabric_v1.json`; LOAD validates that file (maximum 16 KB). WEAR SAVED and RUNWAY SAVED read the file, not the current preview. Missing/invalid saved recipes leave recipients unchanged.

The garment adapter targets only the named `Skirt` mesh in SuperDragOutfit. It stores the recipe across `dress()` calls and retains the original skirt material for restoration. CostumeWardrobe persists the outfit and skirt recipe together in `user://super_drag_outfit.cfg`; progression data is untouched. Runway recipes are session-only. The mirror shares the live World3D and reflects the actual player geometry through its own SubViewport, with a head-centred rather than stereo-per-eye view.

## Rendering and movement are separate

The wall is a 3x2 metre subdivided QuadMesh with a full rectangular StaticBody collision. Fragment discard removes the last fifth of each horizontal repeat from drawing. Finish changes roughness (0.92/0.22) and metallic (0/0.85). Static vertex displacement is bounded by 0.04 local units along the original normals. CPU meshes, normals and collision are not rebuilt. This is not cloth physics; the skirt's existing UV seam/cap layout and mesh density remain visible limitations.

The studio keeps at most 32 undo entries. They restore edited recipes/look, the wearer's previous outfit/recipe, or each recipient performer's previous recipe. SAVE is not file-version history, and UNDO does not rewind the saved source file. Weak references tolerate a receiver disappearing when its hall unloads.

## Verification

`commons/testing/probe_fabric_studio.gd` loads the actual Ribbon_Patterns_03 museum, sends XRToolsPointerEvent presses/releases, validates source and shader changes, save round trips, garment-only assignment, isolated appearance persistence, undo, live mirror and walking performers. Tests redirect saves to `res://ada_run/probe-fabric-*`; they do not write the player's saved appearance. Desktop captures and the report are linked in the possible-bodies guide. Headset legibility, tracked-hand reach, stereo comfort and continuous gestures remain open.

## Journey-driven garment growth (21 September)

The wardrobe reads all 24 sequences from curriculum_spine.json. Completed sequences restore through the existing progression manager; the endless museum also grants one stage when the visitor enters a new sequence. A revisit cannot grant another stage. Arrival-based stages last within the current visit; no new visit-history save or completion credit is written. Desktop uses its walker wardrobe. VR uses the wardrobe whose costume follows the active origin's camera, so the menu rig cannot receive the visitor's growth.

The base walk dress adds a bounded tier. Selected super-drag looks now receive the same stage: their existing Skirt CylinderMesh lengthens from 0.50 to 0.90 m, while its waist stays at local y=-0.515. Its lower radius grows by a factor of 0.65 to 1.15 of the look's base radius. Pearl hem decorations follow that geometry. New arrivals ease towards their target over 1.4 seconds; waiting creates no additional stages. Quiet restoration and look switching apply the current stage directly. Preview and runway outfits retain their original dimensions unless explicitly given a growth stage.

Growth retains the worn Skirt node, mesh and material instance. The saved recipe and its UV repeat count remain unchanged, so the marks cover a changing physical extent. This is authored geometric growth, not cloth simulation, biological adaptation, new collision or a change to locomotion. The costume's chosen finish and the salon's hand-colour receivers are separate systems.

# A surface's permissions

A strip disappears under `discard`; the body still meets an intact wall. The disagreement is reproducible. Visibility and traversability have different implementations. Asking which one defines the opening exposes a decision the attractive surface could otherwise conceal.

The same source can dress a wall and a skirt because a material can read both meshes' UV coordinates. That shared operation permits a crossing between architectural and bodily surfaces. It does not erase their differences: UV seams, dimensions, silhouette, normals and collision all contribute. The experiment offers a way to investigate a relation rather than announcing that everything is interchangeable.

The garment adapter gives the crossing a boundary: the mesh called Skirt can receive the recipe; the crown and sleeves cannot through this control. A name in the implementation becomes a permission. This is a useful place for the question "what bodies are possible?" to land. We can point to the adapter that makes one possibility available, then ask what another adapter would allow.

The wearer chooses when to apply a saved fabric and can recover the prior outfit through studio undo. Editing a public sample does not silently rewrite the worn body. The runway makes another distribution visible: one recipe across four performers whose silhouettes and positions differ. Their scripted route remains a limit of this encounter. Apparent material freedom does not yet grant another way to move.

The next sequence can ask what these configurations do over time. Here we make the conditions of appearance inspectable enough to carry that question forward.
