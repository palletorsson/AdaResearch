# Sweep: queer, feminist and critical pedagogy of games, 3D and computing

2026-09-30 auto-research, for the braid. [V] = structure checked against a real page (publisher, syllabus, open text) this session. [V-2] = checked only through a search index or secondary summary. [mem] = from memory, not checked. All descriptions are paraphrase.

## Headline

**No course found in this sweep teaches queer/feminist theory together with *procedural 3D or VR programming for beginners*.** The queer and feminist courses either make in no-code or text tools (Twine, Emotica, paper, zines, HTML), or they only read. The art-school 3D/VR courses make, but with Maya, Blender or Unity, and have no queer lens. Ada's niche looks unoccupied, at least as far as this sweep reached (the search was not exhaustive).

What the sweep did find are **ordering moves and exercise formats** that carry over directly (list below). They back the three-layer shape (foundation, then debt, then ontology), and one course shows the "related, not forced" braid already working.

## The ten most transferable moves

1. **Two strands on separate clocks** (Gaboury, CCS 382): the theory topics and the making workshops run in parallel and in *different* orders, and meet twice a week. This is the braid's "related to the spine, not forced": the spine is one strand, the code ladder is another.
2. **Failure goes before futures.** In *Queer Game Studies* Part IV (Failures) comes before Part V (Futures). CCS 382 runs Failure (Halberstam and Menkman's glitch) and then Futurities. *Glitch Feminism* puts "error" before "anti-body" and "skin". All three agree with Palle's order: DEBT, then ONTOLOGY.
3. **Make before the tool.** UCSC AGPM 129 has students make a paper prototype in week 5 and only then meets Twine in week 6. Digital Love Languages writes poems in *folders* before any syntax. For Ada, the first body could be scanned or drawn in Tilt Brush and imported before any GDScript. That makes import_assets a day-one strand, as the 2024 Queer 3D course already did with 3D scanning.
4. **Share in chapter 1.** Anthropy's Twine book uploads a game to the web in chapter 1. Ada could export and share a build of the first body in week 1, not at the end.
5. **A weekly micro-response in three parts: a question, a comparison, a provocation** (CCS 382 group responses). It is cheap to run and fits the ARGUMENT strand, because the comparison matches the comparison image.
6. **A collective body built from the class's own data** (Blas, *Facial Weaponization Suite*): scan, aggregate, fabricate, wear it in public. This is a ready template for a short-track mini-project. The family axis is "how many faces are aggregated", and the PLACE is "become it".
7. **Use the system's own architecture as the syllabus.** *QueerOS* runs interface, user, kernel, applications, memory, I/O, warning. *Image Objects* runs hidden surface, frame buffer, teapot, object orientation, GPU. *Feminist Data Set* follows the ML pipeline stage by stage. Each part of the system gets a technical description *and* a critical one.
8. **Name units after art lineages, not tools** (Claudia Hart, SAIC). Intro to Experimental 3D is modelled on Itten's Bauhaus basic course and goes 2D, then 3D, then 4D (time). Courses are named after art histories, not "modelling" or "rigging". This is the nearest precedent to "Klee's Pedagogical Sketchbook in drag".
9. **A shared core with local nodes** (FemTechNet DOCC, 2013, the "anti-MOOC"). Video dialogues are shared, and each school runs its own course around them. Ada's halls would be the shared core, and each track or teacher a node with its own order.
10. **Round-robin coding** (Digital Love Languages, week 7). A file passes from hand to hand and each person adds one change. In Ada terms, each student adds one value to a shared axis.

## Cautions that apply across the sweep
- Reading load: CCS 382 asks for 50-75 pages a week. That will not fit a three-week Konstfack course.
- The tools avoid space. Twine and zines skip geometry, and the "anyone can make games" stance plays down how much tool debt 3D and VR carry.
- Glitch as a *visual style* is the thin reading. Russell's glitch is social and embodied, not a shader effect.
- Biometric scanning (Blas) needs consent and retopology. Aggregating meshes requires matched topology, which is a hidden debt of its own.
- The Critical Engineering Manifesto is written in a hacker/exploit register. It is a stance, not a pedagogy.

## The works in detail

### 1. CCS 382 "Critical Unmaking: Queer and Feminist Technologies" [V]
- **Who, when:** Jacob Gaboury, Stony Brook University, Fall 2016. It is based on Zach Blas's Queer and Feminist Technoscience course at Goldsmiths. Undergraduate, one semester, two sessions a week.
- **Units, in order:** Intro "Critical (Un)Making" (feminist hacking, critical making).
  - UNIT I, HISTORIES: Beginnings/Methods I-II (Fox Keller, Keeling's Queer OS, Haraway's Situated Knowledges, da Costa); Cyberfeminism (Plant, VNS Matrix, subRosa); Queer Histories of Computation (Gaboury's Queer History of Computing part 1, Halberstam's Automating Gender, Turing, Link's Love Letters); The Cyborg and the Posthuman (Haraway, Hayles); an in-class Wikipedia edit-a-thon.
  - UNIT II, PRACTICE: Play (Anthropy, Nooney, #Gamergate); The Transgender Body (Stone, cárdenas's Becoming Dragon, Cheang); Race (Chun, Nakamura, Satterwhite); Code & Software (Chun, Mackenzie, Mez); game testing and play.
  - UNIT III, TACTICS: Obfuscation (Blas's Queer Darkness, Temkin's esoteric codes, transCoder); New Materialisms (Grosz, Bennett, Yoldas); Failure (Berlant, Halberstam's Queer Art of Failure, Menkman's Glitch Moment(um), Jodi); Futurities (Preciado, Xenofeminism); rough-draft presentations.
- **Pedagogy:** each week has a reading day, then a lecture plus workshop day. The workshops follow their own ladder: Game Design 101, then mechanics/frustration/failure, then branching narrative/tools, then testing. The deliverables are a Wikipedia edit-a-thon (month 1, a public contribution), a queer/feminist game (October, made in Twine or Anthropy's Emotica, then playtested, with a 3-page write-up), and a "critical unmaking" final project (an essay or a made piece). The weekly three-sentence team response is a question, a comparison and a provocation.
- **Ada can learn:** the three-part arc Histories, then Practice, then Tactics. Making sits in the *middle* unit, and the failure/glitch unit comes late. The workshop strand keeps its own order. Also the question/comparison/provocation response, and a public contribution in the first month.
- **Caution:** it is written for humanities students, reads heavily, and makes only in Twine, with no 3D or code.

### 2. Queer Game Studies (Ruberg and Shaw, eds.), University of Minnesota Press 2017 [V]
- Anthology, 26 chapters in five parts: **I. Defining queerness in games** (Clark; Chang's Queergaming; Burrill on the body; Street), **II. Queering play and design** (Wonica's board game; Alexander; Brady; Gabel; Brice on kink; Hjorth and d'Amazing), **III. Reading games queerly** (Yang; Phillips; Harper; Bagnall on normativity built into hardware; kopas on Gone Home), **IV. Queer failures** (Shaw; Richard; Cross; Halberstam's "going turbo"; Halberstam and Juul; Youngblood), **V. Queer futures** (Stockton; Goetz's "queer growth"; Macklin; Howe/Rocca/Schoemann; Ruberg's 48-hour utopia).
- **Ada can learn:** failure as a whole part that comes before futures. Bagnall's chapter shows that normativity is inscribed in *hardware*, which is a DEBT reading for a VR headset (IPD defaults, a standing height, two hands). Goetz's "queer growth" is a counter-model to levelling up, for a course that does not climb a ladder.
- **Caution:** it is a reader, not a course, and says little about geometry or code. It is used as a course reader (UCSC, below).

### 3. AGPM 129 "Queer and Trans Games", UC Santa Cruz, micha cárdenas, Spring 2020 [V]
- 11 weeks. W1 play Anthropy's *Queers in Love at the End of the World* and post a 10-second game concept. W2 Ruberg/Shaw intro and Porpentine. W3 "Not Gay as in Happy" and Monsterhearts 2. W4 play Monsterhearts and Gone Home. **W5 a character map and a paper prototype** that cites three sources. **W6 Twine is introduced** (Anthropy, electronic literature). W7 Clark and studio time. W8 playtest, Shaw "Trouble with Communities". W9 Kara Stone's "Time and Reparative Game Design". **W10 final digital game and playtest.** W11 reflection paper and celebration.
- **Pedagogy:** play, then read, then prototype on paper, then learn the tool, then make, then playtest, then reflect. Grading is self-assessed. The discussion rule is "move up, move back".
- **Ada can learn:** a day-one exercise where students play a work and answer it with a 10-second response. Paper comes before the tool. Self-assessment.
- **Caution:** text and paper games only. It was taught online during the pandemic.

### 4. Anna Anthropy: Rise of the Videogame Zinesters (Seven Stories 2012) [V-2]; Make Your Own Twine Games! (No Starch 2019) [V-2]
- *Zinesters* in 8 chapters: The Problem with Videogames, The History of Magic, What Is It Good For?, Changing the Game, The New Videogame, Making the Games, By Your Bootstraps, Growing Up. Appendix A "What to Use" and B "Zinester Games". CCS 382 assigns chapters 1, 4 and 6.
- *Twine Games*: each chapter is one small named game that teaches one idea. Interview with a Cat (basics, *including uploading the game online*), then Spider Milk (links), Ghost Burgers (scripting, hooks, variables, stylesheet), Pebble Economy (counting items with variables), then Where to Go from Here.
- **Ada can learn:** a chapter is a named tiny work, not a topic, which is what the Point Zero/Point One kits already do. Sharing happens in chapter 1. Variables arrive only after a whole thing exists.
- **Caution:** the anti-craft "anyone can" stance is useful for courage but sits in tension with Ada's goal of geometric capacity. Twine has no space.

### 5. Videogames for Humans (merritt kopas, ed., Instar 2015) [V-2]
- 27 Twine games, each read by another writer, critic or author.
- **Ada can learn:** pair each student body with *another* student's written reading of it (a claim written by someone else), rather than self-review.
- **Caution:** an anthology, with no making in it.

### 6. Pixelles Game Incubator (Montreal, 2013-) [V-2]
- Grew from the Hand Eye Society's Difference Engine Initiative. It began as 6 weeks for 10 women making a first game, and is now a free 8-week workshop series for about 12 gender-marginalised first-time makers. Mentors, non-programming tools, and a **public showcase** at the end.
- **Ada can learn:** the short-track shape: a cohort, mentors, a first-work scope and a fixed public date. This matches Queer 3D's mini project shown to the class.
- **Caution:** no programming, entry by application, and it is a community programme rather than a school.

### 7. Digital Love Languages (Melanie Hoff et al., School for Poetic Computation, 2020-24) [V]
- 2020: 10 weeks online, 30 people. Orientation, Folder Poetry I-II, Codes Words Spells I-II, Zines as a Loving Practice of Abolition (Neta Bomani), a Hand-Coding Round Robin, sharing plus glitch text input plus NLP, then presentations. Small personal software in Python and JS. Later runs: 2022 "Communion, Consent, Refusal, Renewal" and 2024.
- **Ada can learn:** start from a material the student already knows. In Godot that could be the *scene tree as folders* before any script. Round-robin coding, and software made *for* a named person.
- **Caution:** web and text scale. The care framing depends heavily on who facilitates.

### 8. QueerOS: A User's Manual (Barnett, Blas, cárdenas, Gaboury, Johnson, Rhee; Debates in the Digital Humanities 2016) [V]
- Sections: Getting Started, Interface, User, Kernel, Applications, Memory, I/O, Warning. It extends Kara Keeling's "Queer OS" (Cinema Journal 2014). Each component of an operating system is given a poetic and theoretical description of what it does and where it falls short, and the text calls itself speculative vaporware that assumes its own obsolescence.
- **Ada can learn:** a "user's manual in drag" for Godot. SceneTree, Node, lifecycle, Resource, Input, Renderer and XR could each get a technical page and a queer one. The closing "Warning" section fits the Sieve's third question (what lives in the dark spot).
- **Caution:** it was never implemented, and poetic descriptions do not teach a beginner to program.

### 9. Jacob Gaboury: Image Objects: An Archaeology of Computer Graphics (MIT 2021) [V]; A Queer History of Computing (Rhizome 2013, 5 parts) [V-2]
- *Image Objects*: 1 Culling Vision (hidden surface algorithms, the problem of visibility). 2 Random-Access Images (the frame buffer, memory, the screen, the line, the grid). 3 Model Objects (the Utah teapot as standard and icon). 4 Object Paradigms (the origins of object orientation). 5 Procedure Crystallized (the GPU). Coda: After Objects.
- *Queer History of Computing*: five parts about five men, starting from Turing and the limits of computation. It questions the assumption that the technical and the sexual can be kept apart.
- **Ada can learn:** the order of *Image Objects* is almost a Godot learning order, and each step carries a debt. Visibility maps to normals and culling (Godot winds front faces clockwise). The frame buffer maps to Image, pixels and the grid. The teapot is the "default test body", read critically as the capsule player or Suzanne. Object orientation maps to classes and inheritance. The GPU maps to shaders and MultiMesh.
- **Caution:** it is a history centred on Utah and the US, not a course.

### 10. Legacy Russell: Glitch Feminism: A Manifesto (Verso 2020) [V-2]
- Introduction, then Glitch Refuses, Is Cosmic, Throws Shade, Ghosts, **Is Error, Encrypts, Is Anti-Body, Is Skin**, Is Virus, Mobilizes, Is Remix, Survives.
- **Ada can learn:** every title is a predicate ("glitch is X"), which is a model for the one-sentence CLAIM. The run error, encrypts, anti-body, skin is a DEBT-to-ONTOLOGY move for bodies.
- **Caution:** read as a shader effect it becomes the thin reading. It is rooted in Black and femme online selfhood, so using it for procedural geometry needs care and citation.

### 11. Feminist Data Set (Caroline Sinders, 2017-) [V]
- The stages of an ML pipeline, each interrogated through intersectional feminism in public workshops: data collection, labelling, training, choosing an algorithm, the model, and the interface (a chatbot). Also a Toolkit, and TRK, a wage calculator for labelling labour.
- **Ada can learn:** a pipeline can be the course order, with critique at every stage. For the "how are LLM layers activated" station, students could collect and label a tiny data set of *bodies* themselves before training or prompting anything. Data collection is slow on purpose.
- **Caution:** a multi-year art project. In three weeks it can only be one workshop.

### 12. The Critical Engineering Manifesto (Oliver, Savičić, Vasiliev, 2011) [V]
- Tenets numbered 0-10 (paraphrased). Engineering is the most transformative language, so study it. Any technology you depend on calls for study of its insides. Be suspicious of rich user experience. Look past the awe of implementation. Engineering engineers its user. "Machine" includes bodies, forces and networks. Watch the gap between production and consumption. Take strategies from art and activism. Code spills into social life. The exploit is the most desirable form of exposure.
- **Ada can learn:** an "exploit" exercise. Find where the engine or a supplied script shows its seams, for example walking through a CSG wall, a seed that repeats, or float jitter far from the origin. Tenet 3 is a DEBT question for VR "presence".
- **Caution:** a hacker register and exploit culture. A stance, not a pedagogy.

### 13. Zach Blas: Facial Weaponization Suite (2011-14) [V]
- Community workshops lasting up to a month (the UCSD 2013 run with Ricardo Dominguez took one week). Readings and discussion of biometric recognition, then participants' faces are scanned, the scans are aggregated into a "collective mask", the mask is fabricated, and the group makes a public intervention. The Fag Face Mask is the best-known example.
- **Ada can learn:** a short-track mini-project template. Scan (Queer 3D already scans), aggregate in code, and produce a body that a recognizer cannot read. Its family axis is how many faces are blended (1, 2, 4, 8, all). PLACE is to wear it or become it. This is DEBT (bias in recognition) and ONTOLOGY in one piece.
- **Caution:** biometric consent, and mesh aggregation needs matched topology or retopology.

### 14. Claudia Hart: Experimental 3D (SAIC, 2007-) [V]
- Intro to Experimental 3D is modelled on Itten's Bauhaus basic course. It uses Maya and poses formal problems of increasing complexity: 2D, then 3D, then 4D (time), covering light and dark, form, rhythm, colour, proportion and volume. Other courses are named for art-historical paths: Painting in the Virtual Environment, Atmospheric Animation, Digital Bodies (character animation drawn from avant-garde dance film, natural and unnatural movement), Virtual Installation, Mixed Reality.
- **Ada can learn:** the closest precedent to "Klee in drag". A Bauhaus basic course redone in 3D software for art students, with units named by lineage rather than tool. The chapter titles can be Klee's and the art lineages', with the code ladder running underneath.
- **Caution:** keyframe work in Maya, not code. The course pages show no explicit queer lens. A Bauhaus order risks repeating Bauhaus universalism, which is exactly what the "in drag" is meant to trouble.

### 15. VLST 2390 "Virtual Reality for Artists" (Gregory Vershbow, Penn Summer, 2022 syllabus) [V]
- Four studio assignments, each paired with readings and a writing prompt. A 360 photo made with a normal camera (what is lost when you can no longer frame; Crary, Grau). A VR gallery built in Blender (artist, curator and architect at once; Fried, Summers). An AR site-specific intervention (bringing out what the site represses). A personal project. Unity interactivity and Tilt Brush come late, and it ends with a final critique.
- **Ada can learn:** every studio assignment has a paired "what does this medium take away?" prompt, a clean DEBT question per unit.
- **Caution:** no code, interactivity comes last, and there is no body or queer lens.

### 16. FemTechNet DOCC 2013 "Dialogues on Feminism and Technology" [V structure; V-2 theme list]
- A distributed open collaborative course, which FemTechNet calls a "feminist anti-MOOC". Local "nodal" courses at many schools share video dialogues. The themes (search index) are Labor, Sexualities, Race, Differences, Body, Machine, Systems, Place, Infrastructure, Archive, Transformation.
- **Ada can learn:** the spine as a shared core, with each course (short, long, each teacher) as a node that chooses its own order. Declared departures are what keep the nodes legible to each other.
- **Caution:** a theory course from 2013 with heavy coordination.

### 17. Freire and hooks in creative-tech teaching [mem, plus V-2 for SFPC]
- Freire's *Pedagogy of the Oppressed* (problem-posing rather than "banking" knowledge into students) and hooks's *Teaching to Transgress* (engaged pedagogy, in which the teacher is also at risk). In creative tech, SFPC's "Learning to Teach, Teaching to Learn" conference (Taeyoon Choi and Tega Brain) is the visible venue [V-2]. I did not verify that SFPC explicitly cites hooks or Freire.
- **Ada can learn:** PREDICT is problem-posing. The student states what they expect before being told. The teacher's own planted bug, and their own failed prediction, is hooks's vulnerability made operational.
