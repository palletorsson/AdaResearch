# Counting what could happen

How quickly can a few choices become many possible futures?

<!-- @entropy_fan -->

Start with CYCLE. Follow the fan with your eyes, from its narrow origin to a leaf. Count how many choices appear at each level. As the display cycles through its branching arrangements, predict the number of final leaves before checking the readout. ONE holds a single path; OPEN holds the widest fan so you can take your time.

With one continuation at each of four levels, there is one final path. Two continuations per level give sixteen possible leaves. Three give eighty-one. The number of outcomes grows by multiplication because each earlier choice can be followed by several later choices.

Pick two leaves that share the beginning of their paths. Trace backwards until you find the last common branch, then forwards to see where their futures separate. The leaves are different outcomes even though much of their history is identical. This gives you a way to count without treating every end as an unrelated event: shared beginnings organise the possibilities, while later branches multiply the distinct completions that can follow from those beginnings.

The display reports an entropy based on the logarithm of the number of available outcomes. It says “equal leaf weights”: every ending has been assigned the same probability. Its natural logarithm gives nats; the bit workbench used bits. The unit changes the number, not which endings are counted. The logarithm turns multiplication across independent choice stages into addition, making the total easier to compare across different depths of branching.

The assumption about equal possibilities matters. Eighty-one drawn leaves do not tell you that a traveller is equally likely to arrive at each one. A strongly preferred branch could make the actual distribution much more concentrated. To model that situation, we would need probabilities as well as an inventory of paths.

Compare this with the bit workbench. There, the probability changed while the possible bit values remained the same. Here, the fan changes the number of continuations that the diagram offers. Both affect uncertainty, but they are different operations on a model.

Press PRUNE. Amber caps appear where branches stop. The fan has twenty-seven leaves now, where OPEN offered eighty-one. Eighteen closures remove fifty-four endings: a cut made earlier takes several later possibilities with it. Follow one of the amber stops. You can see where continuation was refused, even though the missing subtree is no longer drawn.

Press OPEN again. Here the same endings return at a touch. Outside this little machine, reopening a route may not restore the lives or histories lost when it closed. The ease of the button is part of the experiment’s limit.

Distinguish an option that was available and then prohibited from an option never drawn at all. A picture of the permitted future can hide the history of how the permissions were chosen. Counting its leaves cannot recover those omitted alternatives.

This is where QFEP’s Q reaches into the diagram. Who drew the tree? Who can request another branch, or refuse the condition for reaching it? The project’s research documents ask that what a model leaves out retain the capacity to challenge and change its terms.[^q] Our four buttons still belong to a tree someone else specified. You can reopen it. You cannot yet ask it to grow otherwise.

Keeping every branch open is not automatically care. A closure might protect a place in which a more vulnerable body can continue. The count cannot decide that for us. Stay with the difference between more endings on a screen and more ways to live.

Lambda’s hall will turn a balance between repetition and variation into a spatial invitation. Ask what its preferred route leaves to either side.

[^q]: See the project’s [QFEP development notes](../../../doc/research/qfep/README.md) and [Entropic morality](../../../doc/research/qfep/entropic-morality.md). QFEP means Queer Free Energy Principle; revisability and responsibility for excluded capacities are explicit commitments of the project, not quantities measured by this fan.
