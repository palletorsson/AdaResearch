# What the gauge forgets

<!-- @shannon_entropy_meter -->

A row of coloured tiles waits on a desk. We have come from arrangements prepared for comparison. Here a button says SORT.

We watch the neighbours as we press it. The tiles gather into blocks of colour. Some neighbours part company. Above them, ten bars retain their heights and the Shannon reading stays still. Across the desk's front, an enlarged strip keeps the first forty tiles as they were drawn. We can still find their old neighbours there.

The desk holds two hundred tiles. Its calculation reads how often each colour occurs. Sorting the display changes none of those shares. The front strip gives us somewhere to look for the relation the number leaves out. Pressing SORT again restores the original order.

CONTRAST brings a different sample, with one colour occupying much of the row. Now the reading falls. A change of places and a change of shares have different consequences for this gauge.[^ledger]

Beyond the desk, four glass jars rise above a shared pale plinth. Their glass bodies are 2.3 metres tall. Each holds forty lavender beads and forty coral beads, but the beginnings differ: layers, an overlapping boundary, a mixture, a lavender core inside a coral rim.

On approach they hold for eight seconds. The last jar gives us time to look through the coral towards the lavender inside. Its height reading leaves colour uncertain. Its core/rim reading makes colour predictable. Both readings concern the same beads at the same moment.

Each is a conditional colour entropy: how much colour uncertainty remains when a region is known. Horizontal bands and core/rim regions retain different addresses. The labels make those choices visible.

Then the beads begin to move. The four beginnings continue under the same motion rule, along separately seeded paths. Lavender crosses the first jar's middle. Coral enters the last jar's core. After a while, the names on the plinth describe a past that is harder to find in the beads.

PAUSE holds their positions. RESTART brings back the beginnings and their paths. While motion continues, clusters can gather again and the readings fluctuate. The jars may come to look alike without arriving at identical positions. Their resemblance leaves a question for another observation.

<!-- @entropy_jar -->

Beside the plinth stands a jar small enough to lift. Red beads sit below blue ones. We pick it up and shake it. Beads enter new height bands; the reading changes with their distribution. The motion now follows an intervention we can repeat or vary.

The whole jar still holds forty of each colour. A colour chosen without its location would remain uncertain by one bit. The height reading asks for something more particular. It does not measure the heat of shaking.

We put the jar down. Its positions would take a long list to preserve. How much can a shorter account leave open?

<!-- @entropy_sorting_table -->

A narrow tube delivers four cubes onto a pale table: pink, black, pink, black. Then it stops. Six little pictures show the possible colour arrangements of these four places, with two cubes of each colour.

We add a divider through the middle. No cube moves. Four pictures remain. Each fits the new account: one pink and one black in each box.

Now we exchange the middle cubes. Pink sits with pink, black with black. Only one picture fits the counts in these two boxes. The divider first changed what the description retained. Our hands then changed what that description could report. Four cubes are enough to inspect every surviving possibility.

<!-- @entropy_compartments -->

The next cabinet enlarges the problem. Sixteen places lie in two glass-sided trays, with orange A spheres on the left and blue B spheres on the right. Two round dials face the arrangement. One reads one bit; the other reads zero.

The colour shares are still equal. The second dial uses the number of arrangements compatible with k, the number of A spheres on the left. At k = 8, only one colour assignment fits. We press A LEFT −. An orange sphere exchanges places with a blue one. Four exchanges bring four of each colour into each tray. Now 4,900 assignments fit that description.

The dial shows S_B/kB = ln Ω, the Boltzmann expression from the chairs, divided by Boltzmann's constant. For 4,900 possibilities it reads about 8.497. The fixed places and retained population define this counting model. It contains no temperature or heat exchange.

We press SAME k. Colours move within their trays. Neither dial responds. A different arrangement has appeared in front of two unchanged readings.

KEEP preserves the site lists and generator states. After another change, REPLAY restores the saved arrangement. The record had to retain distinctions that the dials omitted. During a seeded run, some swaps exchange equal colours; others move the configuration reading up or down. Motion continues without a promise that the number will rise.

Beyond the cabinet, points occupy a square glass enclosure. At the blue end, rows line up. Farther along, offsets draw the points away from their lattice positions. We step inside and move sideways. Lines that held from one position loosen from another.

The displacement grows in a chosen direction through the enclosure. This apparent disorder has a spatial rule. There is no entropy gauge to settle what we see. We have changed the viewpoint while the arrangement remains available to another view.

<!-- @entropy_ruin -->

At the stone bed, changing position brings four columns and a lintel into view. A low brick wall stands behind them. This time we can alter a relation that holds something up.

ONE STONE draws a supporting drum towards us and releases it. A drum above the gap descends; other stones can tip or strike the bed. We selected one removal. Contact and gravity carry its consequences beyond the selected stone.

The display keeps two counts: PULLED and MOVED. They can part company. All 116 stones remain in the apparatus, though they no longer do the same work. A loose drum can also be taken in hand. Its old place becomes a gap while its new place waits to be decided.

The bottom two courses stay fixed. Pausing automatic selection leaves gravity active. RESTORE rebuilds the starting arrangement and restarts the selection seed. Repeating that seed alone cannot preserve an exact landing of all the rigid bodies.

Beside the columns, the museum wall follows another procedure. Its top pieces leave for arranged landing places. A low course remains while the view opens above it. Here the fall has been composed; at the columns, the contacts have consequences to resolve. Neither structure supplies a thermodynamic entropy reading.

We look through an opening that was occupied by stone. The inventory still includes what stood there.

Through the doorway, black and white extend over the floor, up the wall and overhead. The small colour question has become large enough to surround us.

We change ROWS to CELLS. The counts stay at 2,048 of each symbol. H stays at one bit. A four-by-four witness brings part of the arrangement within reach of counting; the number of unlike neighbours changes. The record can be asked another question without drawing a new population.

We walk across the pattern. A black patch supports the next step as firmly as a white one. After the stone bed, that continued support matters: changing this image leaves the floor intact.

The side passage leads towards the next hall, where the eligible objects for a removal will be laid out. We have seen one chosen stone move others. Next, the choice itself will need a boundary.
