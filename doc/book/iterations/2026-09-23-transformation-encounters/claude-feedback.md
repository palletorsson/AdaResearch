## The argument is better than primitives'

Primitives is an anthology with a strong theme. Transformation is an actual argument with a turn in it:

**Pre** names the three operations on a pickup cube. **Introduction** removes the floor and makes each operation *afford* a passage — a surface carries you, a volume grows to meet the floor, a length turns toward the bank. **Translation** and **Rotation** give displacement and angle a whole arena each. **Scale** changes the other side of the comparison instead of you. Then **Body** inverts it: your approach is now the input, and slats turn, cells are deleted, blocks shrink. Then **Pit** inverts it again: the same operations now *occupy* the passage instead of making it. And the airlock closes the loop — the three operations that made a small cube conspicuous in hall one now make room for you.

That is a real dialectic, and the closing figure is one of the best in the book.

The chapter's thesis is also sharper than primitives': an operation preserves something, and the thing it preserves is not what your body can do. "There is a difference between preserving a shape and preserving what someone can do there." "The transformations did not become false when you entered the room. They became insufficient as descriptions of what the room allows." That second sentence is the best thing in the chapter and arguably in the book — it's the λ term in a corridor.

## Three real weaknesses

**1. Nobody ever performs a transformation.** In seven halls you ride, wait, approach and walk through. Every angle, distance and factor is authored and runs on a timer or reads your proximity. There are controls — the two door handles, BOUNDARY/SHAPE, REPLAY — but none of them lets you *set* an operation and watch what it does to a crossing. The single exception is MOVE THE BANK in Introduction: you slide a landing one metre and a working crossing silently breaks while the plank keeps turning exactly as before. That is the best interactive moment in the chapter and it is in an optional side aisle in hall two.

For a chapter whose stated truth is "ask which properties survive each operation," you are never allowed to apply one. This is the `plan_vitrine`-shaped hole — primitives gives you a pen, transformation gives you a seat.

**2. The chapter's best unclaimed fact is asserted and never shown.** Rotation is non-commutative. Translation says up-then-sideways equals sideways-then-up. Rotation says turns about different axes "need not finish in the same orientation when their order is reversed. This band fixes its order in the code." So the reader is told the counter-example exists, beside a band that demonstrates one fixed order. That's the most important non-obvious thing in the whole sequence and it's a sentence.

**3. The thesis is stated six times.** Pre: "Each operation has left something to recognise." Introduction: "Each operation needs something against which its change can be read." Rotation: "A centre, an axis and a length describe their turn. A standing surface, a body and a receiving bank make it a passage." Scale, Body, Pit each have their version. Every one is beautifully written and by the fourth the rooms are being explained rather than encountered. Same hardening I flagged in primitives, worse here because the phrasings sit so close together.

## What would make it brilliant

**Show the non-commutativity.** Two identical marked cubes beside the XYZ band. One gets X-then-Y, the other Y-then-X, same angles. They end visibly different. No new physics, one artifact, and it converts the chapter's sharpest claim from prose into a thing you walk between.

**Mark a face.** Rotation already writes the paragraph that asks for this: "a corner has changed places. A mark on one face would make the return a difference you could follow." The mark isn't there. Put it on the axial_sweep cube and the array's cubes and that paragraph stops being hypothetical.

**Promote MOVE THE BANK to the centre of Introduction.** Breaking a working crossing by moving its destination is the thesis delivered as consequence instead of assertion. It should not be optional.

**Let the cut persist.** `carve_grid` ends: "Somebody following behind you will find the cut already made. Will their body fit the passage yours left?" The cut dies when the room unloads, so the game refuses the question the prose asks. Transformation has no cross-hall carry at all — this is its obvious one, and primitives now has the `TraceData` precedent for how to do it.

**Cut three of the six thesis statements.** Keep Pre's and Pit's.

Two loose halls worth a decision: `Trans_AxisDecomposition` and `Trans_RotationSpectacle` exist on disk and are not in the spine — the same orphan pattern as Change_Velocity.
