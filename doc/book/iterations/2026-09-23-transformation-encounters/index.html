The distance part of the rule can be written simply, for a visitor within reach:

var t: float = absf(slat_x - visitor_x) / aperture_m
var d: float = absf(visitor_z) / reach_m
var openness: float = (1.0 - t) * (1.0 - d)

Two distances, multiplied. The implementation smooths this value and opens a neighbouring run of slats far enough for a body to pass. The first is how far this slat is from you along the wall; the second is how far you are from the wall. The slat does not know whether you meant to come. The next throat holds a low block of small cubes, about 1.3 metres high. You can look over it. The lattice reaches across the way, but it does not enclose your head.

Press into it. The cubes your body touches stop existing.

They are removed. You advance a shell at a time, leaving an open-topped cut through the block. For a standing visitor this is closer to a trench than a tunnel. Your view was above it before your legs had a way through.

The test is one line, and the interesting part is a number:

@export var bite_m: float = 0.16

The lattice is solid and so are you, so the physics engine stops you at the surface and your collider never actually overlaps a cube. A lattice that deleted only what it strictly intersected would delete nothing, for ever, and would look exactly like this one. bite_m is the margin by which you are allowed to reach past the place you were stopped. At zero the room is a wall. At 0.16 against a 0.34 cell it clears about one shell per press.

Your movement supplies the input, but the operation on the lattice is deletion. Translation alone would leave every cube somewhere. Here the count changes. The whole behaviour also hangs on a tolerance: the difference between a body being stopped and a position allowed to reach beyond it.

The cut stays while this room remains loaded. Look at its stepped sides. It records where the lattice met the collision shape used for your body, expanded by that small margin. The cubes can only retain the contact at their own resolution. Some of you passed above the entire record.

Transformation: decisions on Claude’s review

23 September 2026. Read against the current seven canonical halls and their implementations. The feedback is a useful encounter audit; its strongest suggestions warrant action. It is not an instruction to make every hall perform the same editorial formula.

Claim or suggestion Decision and action
A strong sequence argument Agree: the familiar cube becomes a crossing, a relation of size, an opening responding to the body, and an obstruction. Preserve that arc and the welcoming airlock. Calling Primitives merely an anthology understates its accumulating capacities.
Nobody performs a transformation Too absolute. Introduction retains matrix, gimbal and composition interactions; Translation has operated exit mechanisms; Body takes the visitor as input. The main route nevertheless needed a clearer opportunity to vary a relation and compare consequences.
Show rotation order Accepted. Restored the existing transform_composition_workbench beyond the combined array at source (29,39), as a primary encounter. Pair four compares X30° then Y45° with the reverse order; trace mode shows intermediate forms. Pair two supplies a useful commuting counterexample. No duplicate demonstration was invented.
Mark a face Accepted. The axial cube carries a pale asymmetric L on a dark side patch. Arrays carry it on their top face: the first render showed that side marks were buried by the surrounding floor. Marks follow actual transforms and add no collision. The prose now describes a visible mark.
Bring MOVE THE BANK into the main lesson Accepted with a distinction between prominence and compulsory access. Controls move to source (4,15), at the third crossing; the landing, rails and artifact root retain their positions. The text asks the reader to compare its initial and displaced bank. Safe side aisles remain available.
Let the cut persist A possible later development, not a correction forced by the text. Trans_Body already limits the cut to the room’s loaded lifetime; a following visitor is a question, not a claim that cross-hall persistence works. No new persistence system or simulated social visit is claimed.
Cut repeated thesis statements Accepted selectively. Introduction loses its abstract recap and ends the bank encounter by resetting the particular crossing. Rotation loses one recap after the blades. Body loses the repeated three-threshold summary. Pre’s framing, Scale’s relational reversal and Pit’s conclusion remain.
Two orphan halls Keep the source files. AxisDecomposition was absorbed into Translation, and RotationSpectacle into Rotation. Neither belongs back in the spine merely because its folder remains.

Implementation discovery

The grid parser initially read #initial_pair:4 as tutorial shorthand, turning the station by four degrees and passing a boolean in place of the pair number. Adding initial_pair to its recognised numeric configuration keys fixes both the initial comparison and the intended 180-degree placement. The workbench itself did not require a rewrite.
