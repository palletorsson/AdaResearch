# GT_Pathfinding

**Desire.** Take away a necessary-looking stone and still get someone across.

**Route / writing move.** Lead with the removal's consequence. Follow the altered route back into the part that survived, treating replanning as reuse as well as replacement. A returned old stone can then complicate the apparent story of steadily vanishing possibilities.

**Encounter to lean on.** `impatient_river` says “some connections can survive a local change”. The button, unavailable stone and resulting crossing make the claim specific.

**Explain / linger.** Explain that the spark performs a route found earlier; its animation does not reveal the search's deliberation. Give the reader time to propose another crossing. NO ROUTE can remain an informative, occasional outcome rather than the compulsory destination of the chapter.

**Rhythm beside its neighbors.** Layout changed appearances without changing adjacency; the sinking stone changes the available graph. Network Analysis will ask about structural importance, so the memory of one disrupted crossing can precede any ranking of vertices.
