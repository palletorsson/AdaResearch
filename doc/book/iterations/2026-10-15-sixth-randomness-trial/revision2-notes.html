Sequence-context revision (r2)

The comparative reading's revision, applied on top of the 15 October small revision. The question it answers: what does the definition hall owe as hall one of fourteen, and what belongs to its siblings? Three additions, one cut, one replacement, one door. Three further proposals were dropped because the small revision had already achieved them (hash de-jargoning, 'joins an excursion', withdrawal before REPLAY).

Contract: the first and final paragraphs are unchanged. The door enters before the final line, so r1's last-three-paragraphs contract is deliberately not carried; the final return to centre is.

What leaves, and where it goes: the bit extraction (and the bits vocabulary r1 introduced for it) hands off to Random_Entropy — 'What the gauge forgets' — the next hall, whose own text already links back to this one. The instrument roster thins to one witness. What arrives: a prediction failure the visitor can run and count; the doubles most five-digit groups carry (RAND's correctors worked against bias, not repetition); the relational definition the room demonstrates three times — travel against destination, clock against sound, traces against seeds — and never names; and a half-sentence door: chance acted inside rules it did not write, and the rooms ahead give it more to decide.

R3-replace-clock-with-definition · replace (geiger_counter)

Random_Entropy, the next hall, is titled 'What the gauge forgets' — the bit extraction is its opening argument and inherits r1's bits vocabulary with it. What stays funds the chapter's one definitional move, which the room demonstrates three times and never names.

Old:

The cabinet has another way of reading the intervals. It compares pairs of gaps to produce bits, zeros or ones. We keep the ninety-six events fixed and make the comparison clock coarser, measuring in larger steps. Two gaps that had different durations may now receive the same measured value. Such a pair is skipped. Nothing disappears from the timeline or the sound. One procedure has lost a distinction that another record still holds.

We can hear the gaps once more. For this comparison, the tied pair still yields no bit.

New:

The cabinet can also read its own intervals against a coarser clock, measuring in larger steps. Two gaps of different lengths may then receive the same measured value, and a distinction the sound still carries goes missing from that account. Nothing in the gaps has changed — only the resources brought to them.

That is the definition this room has been circling. Random is not a property a sequence owns. It is a relation between the sequence and whatever meets it: a clock, a rule, a listener. The same ninety-six times are a riddle to our ear and a recipe to the generator.
