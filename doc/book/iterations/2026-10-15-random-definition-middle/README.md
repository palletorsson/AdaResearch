## A small judgement loop

Test one join first, not all thirteen stops in one session.

1. **Attend.** Read or encounter the first stop. Before revealing the next, ask: “What are you attending to now? What would you like to find out or try?” Record the person's actual words. A wish to remain with an object, confusion or an unexpected use is legitimate evidence; they need not guess the next artifact.
2. **Develop.** Show the next stop. Ask what changed, whether it answered, complicated or abandoned their attention, and where they wanted more or less time. Prefer a concrete reference to a sentence or action over a general rating.
3. **Disturb.** Remove one stop, swap a pair, or revise one bridge in a working comparison. Ask what is lost or gained. A stop can earn its place through rhythm, pleasure, resistance or duration, not only through a factual prerequisite.
4. **Steer.** Record one decision and its reason; make one change and repeat. Keep the previous version available. Report version order: familiarity can make a second reading easier. Where possible let another reader encounter the versions in the reverse order; otherwise treat the comparison as provisional.

Read and walk as separate checks. A smooth paragraph may hide awkward positioning. An evocative object may need more duration than its paragraph gives it. Apparatus correctness is a third check. None substitutes for another.

## Turn evidence into a next action

| Observation | First revision to try |
| --- | --- |
| Nothing concrete holds attention at the first stop. | Strengthen the object, action or opening sentence before explaining more. |
| A specific question arises; the next stop ignores it. | Rewrite the bridge or try a more relevant next encounter. |
| Both stops do the same work; removing one loses little. | Compress, combine or make a stop optional. |
| The connection is understood but feels rushed. | Allow time, a gesture, a sound or a silence. |
| Only the explanatory prose creates the relation. | Test an actual comparison or intervention in the room; do not claim a shared data path unless one exists. |
| A different, productive question or use appears. | Preserve it as an alternative branch or gem candidate. Do not correct the reader back to the planned interpretation. |

The purpose is not perfect compliance with the author’s route. Look for an encounter that develops attention and leaves a consequential next possibility. A pause, refusal or return can be the right movement.

## In the writing wall

Every map's text comparison now has **Judge a transition · then choose a revision**. For each tagged stop that has a successor, **Test this transition** creates a research draft naming the two stops. It captures the current context, identifies the originating text and attaches to the first artifact. Prediction is marked as an editorial hypothesis; observation remains empty.

Record the response under Observation; record the version and source of evidence; record the adopted edit or rejection under Changes actually made. Save wall retains the record. The control does not reorder the map, score a reader or automatically validate the hypothesis. Proposed reorders remain experiments until adopted in both the room and final.
