## My reading of the three examples

### A. The five-row abacus — Embodied observation

The sentence I would preserve is:

> The rods have not grown, but there is less time inside the same distance.

This has the quality we're looking for: geometric precision becoming strange through experience.

But the new recording wall introduces something potentially more interesting. Movement leaves overlapping transparent circles that accumulate darkness. The hand withdraws, the bead returns, and the marks stay.

The wall changes the artifact from an experience of following to an experience of leaving evidence.

I would give this greater literary attention. Perhaps the most compelling question is not where the bead went, but what its accumulating marks make visible that could not be perceived while following it.

The technical explanation of Gaussian and uniform distributions might then arrive because the reader has something specific to investigate in the marks.

### B. The Geiger counter — Epistemological encounter

The strongest feature remains:

> Click. A gap. Another click.

And then the same gap returning.

I particularly like the historical receipt interrupting the simulated events. There are two different temporalities: an acquisition on 13 October 2026 and a sequence that can be replayed indefinitely.

But I would still reduce the explanatory paragraph. We do not necessarily need to explain every aspect of pseudorandom generation immediately.

My proposed calibration principle: Let a documentary fragment complicate an encounter instead of making the narrator explain everything.

### C. The cellular-automata bridge — Material consequence

The supplied bridge image shows a gridded walkway, a separate frieze repeating its cells, translucent depleted tiles, and a pink probe.

This is the most exciting of the three passages to me.

The important moment is:

> The faint tile can still be seen after it can no longer carry you.

And especially:

> When the surface gives way, you are still looking at the same address from a little lower down.

This is a remarkable literary possibility because a scientific distinction has become an event that happens to a body.

The cell retains its position and remains visible, but loses its capacity to support someone. Its numerical state becomes architecture. The frieze allows the same event to be observed from outside the immediate physical consequence.

I would preserve this kind of writing almost as a guiding example. It does not require metaphorical elaboration. The simulation produces the situation.

One caution: the passage currently uses imperative second-person instructions. That works well as a playable score but may not be the right voice for the finished literary chapter. I'd preserve the encounter while leaving the narrative address open.
