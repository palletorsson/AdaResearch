# A room with two dates

<!-- @random_touch -->

**Present tense.**

Five beads wait on five rods. The rods are a metre long. Behind them is pale paper, and below them one large button: REPLAY.

We touch the upper bead. The visible hand follows it sideways while the viewpoint stays still. Circles appear on the paper. They are faint enough that an overlap matters. We withdraw. The hand resumes its own place; the bead returns to the centre; the circles remain.

**Recorded positions.**

The first four rows use the same horizontal destinations. Their rates double from row to row. Twelve marks are taken within each segment of motion, so the quicker bead writes its account sooner. Given the same number of segments, those four rows retain the same pattern. Row five adds vertical movement at the second row's pace.

The paper records positions on the intervening journeys too. A dense patch cannot, by itself, identify the distribution from which the destinations were drawn.

**REPLAY.**

The paper clears. Nothing has yet happened on its surface. The positions required to make it happen are already stored.

**A page.**

Five digits arrive together. Another group follows. The descending rows belong to a display named for RAND's random-number book; the digits here are produced by the program.

We touch a group and its figures turn amber. They stay at their row address. Our hand goes elsewhere. The page continues around this small interruption.

The mark left by the bead had to accumulate. These five digits were available before we selected them. Their new condition is that we can keep reading them.

**Production note.**

RAND's published account describes testing an electronic source, finding biases and transforming the resulting table. Preservation followed a process that itself needed examination. [Production account](https://www.rand.org/pubs/monograph_reports/MR1418.html).

A complete page would give us more material for examining patterns. It would still leave questions about how the material had been acquired. The amber group makes no claim about that history. It has survived our selection, which is another event.

We touch it again. It rejoins the fall.

**Display.**

**Saved receipt, abbreviated.**

**The calculation.**

Ninety-six detection times are generated before the sound begins. No radiation detector is connected. The seed sets the starting state of a repeatable calculation. The calculated intervals become synthesized clicks. Pressing RESTART returns playback to the beginning.

Click. The same gap. Another click.

**The date again.**

It remains 13 October on the receipt. Our listening happens later. Restarting has not sent a request to the atmosphere, and the repeated sound cannot authenticate the receipt's account of its source. The document and the clicks have different jobs.

The number 435 also begins the colour window, the lamps and the abacus. Their procedures put it to different uses. If we deliberately select another seed, the instruments begin again from that choice. The original receipt can still be recalled with 435.

**Clock comparison.**

The same event times can be read with a coarser clock. Two measured intervals that become equal are skipped by the bit-producing procedure. Some distinctions disappear from that result while the clicks remain unchanged. The machine can let us hear what its other account has ceased to distinguish.

**A record made during the visit.**

We take the MOVE / KEEP sphere, move it and turn it. Six traces register changes in position and orientation. We release after a sufficient recording. A number appears within the range zero to 999.

The retained measurements include times. Rounded positions and orientations become an encoded list; a fixed hash calculation reduces it to a seed. Reusing that exact list permits the number to be derived again. Repeating the gesture makes another list.

This record can supply a new beginning to the hall. It is not the old receipt with its date replaced. Its source is a measured movement, and its subsequent random-looking outputs still come from repeatable calculations.

Two recordings may lead to the same seed. No such pair is attached to this account. There is a place for it among the evidence, should we find and keep one.

**A body in the record.**

The larger sphere carries us through the room while we hold it. A direction and distance have been chosen; the excursion has an outward leg and a return. Our viewpoint moves. Releasing ends the carry while the sphere continues.

The return is required by its route. Another seed changes the excursion available under that rule. The rule itself is not selected by the draw.

We could write down the seed and describe where the sphere went. We could add the moment at which we released it. These would be different records, each available to someone who was not here.

The sphere is back at the centre. The receipt still bears its earlier date. A document of this visit would need another one.
