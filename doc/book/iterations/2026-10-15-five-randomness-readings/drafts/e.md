# A small constitution for chance

<!-- @random_touch -->

We begin with a modest jurisdiction: a metre of metal, a bead, and the hand visible in the room.

Contact grants movement along the first rod. The bead's next horizontal destination comes from a retained numerical sequence; the visible hand follows. Our body and viewpoint remain where they were. Withdrawal restores the hand to our own movement and sends the bead back to centre.

Here is the first clause we could write: we may end the contact without erasing what it has done.

Circles have accumulated on the pale wall behind the bead. Their overlaps make darker places. They remain when the bead goes home. The picture contains selected positions and places passed between them. A patch of darkness therefore cannot testify alone to how the destinations were selected.

The next three rods preserve the horizontal positions and successively double the speed. The fifth uses the second row's pace and permits movement vertically as well. Those permissions belong to the apparatus. The number drawn at a particular step has no authority to lengthen the rod or add another axis.

REPLAY clears the marks and restores the paths. The hand may start again. A fresh-looking sheet can have a fully determined next journey waiting behind it.

The next clause needs an amendment. Ending contact does not always end an intervention.

On the falling page, touching a group of five digits turns it amber and holds it at its address. Our hand can withdraw while the group remains. Other rows continue downward. We have given one result a longer stay.

The page is named for RAND's random-number book. This display makes its own digits. Our choice does not authorize a claim about the source of the historical tables, or even about the rest of this generated stream. It authorizes another look at these five figures.

That is already a consequential permission. We can inspect something without racing its departure. We can bring another thought to the same group. A further touch releases it; the authority to hold is also an authority to let it go.

We could keep choosing what resembles a pattern we expect. The resulting collection would then bear the history of our choices. A rule for generating numbers and a practice of selecting them can both participate in what eventually becomes convincing.

The Geiger cabinet requires a distinction between a beginning and what may be done from it.

Its screen reads 435. Its clicks have irregular gaps. RESTART brings back the opening intervals. No radioactive source is connected: the machine calculates ninety-six event times and synthesizes its sound from that retained list.

435 is the seed that sets the generator's starting state. The generator's calculations then advance that state. With the same procedure and conditions, the beginning can be used again to reproduce the sequence. That is the operative promise of pseudorandom generation here.

The beginning has a document. A saved receipt reports RANDOM.ORG atmospheric noise, 13 October 2026, and the value 435. The hall recalls it without a new acquisition. The receipt's account of origin does not confer a different source on each later click.

We may choose another seed deliberately. The window, lamps and other generating instruments receive it through their separate procedures. We may also restore 435. We do not need another random choice in order to choose a beginning for a random-looking sequence.

The comparison clock has a smaller jurisdiction. Making it coarser can make unequal event intervals receive equal measured values. The extraction procedure skips those ties. The sound still plays the retained events. One procedure's inability to distinguish two durations does not cause their difference to vanish everywhere.

MOVE / KEEP permits our movement to enter this arrangement as data. We carry and turn a sphere beneath six traces. After sufficient valid recording, releasing it supplies a seed between zero and 999.

The instrument has recorded positions, orientations and times. It rounds these measurements, hashes the encoded record and reduces the result to a thousand possible values. Keeping the record permits the same derivation later. It does not preserve every distinction of the bodily event.

Two different recordings can share the number that the hall receives. We have no demonstrated pair to place before this account. Such a pair would be useful evidence precisely because the movements could remain different in their records while having the same consequence at this input.

Our permission to supply a seed is therefore limited. It lets an event during the visit change the generators' beginnings. It does not let the gesture rewrite their procedures. The distinction leaves a task available: decide whether a different consequence requires another input or another apparatus.

At the last sphere, the scale of the permission changes. We hold it and the body is carried through the room. This time our viewpoint travels. We may release at any point and end the carry while the sphere continues its excursion.

Direction and distance vary. Return is compulsory for the sphere. A different draw has no power to repeal it.

We can now name something the room does not let chance decide. The journey may vary inside its route; the requirement to return was placed there by the makers. If we want a sphere that sometimes stays away, we must alter that requirement and encounter what follows.

This small constitution is still a draft. Its last clause would concern who may revise the preceding ones. We are standing close enough to the moving object to begin asking what such a revision should allow.
