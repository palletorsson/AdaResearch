At first there is time to wait. Five dark beads sit at the centres of five thin rods. A pale surface stands behind them. Nothing begins because we have arrived.

We touch the upper bead.

The hand visible in the room goes sideways with it. Our viewpoint stays where it was. A segment of the journey takes two and a half seconds. The bead slows towards its destination; a new segment begins. Faint circles gather on the paper. We have time to lose track of which circle came first.

Withdraw.

The visible hand returns to our own movement. The bead goes home alone. The circles have stopped arriving, and the marks already made stay there.

The next rod allows the same horizontal journey at twice the pace. A segment takes one and a quarter seconds. The third halves that time again. The fourth halves it once more. The paper fills sooner. We can stop and look for a difference that these speeds have left behind, but after equal numbers of segments the horizontal marks agree. The wall samples twelve positions within a segment, however quickly that segment passes.

The fifth row returns to the second row's pace. The visible hand begins to rise and fall as it moves sideways. The rhythm is familiar while the space has changed.

REPLAY clears the paper. We are back at a beginning the program can supply again. The waiting bead does not retain our impatience.

Two and a half seconds elsewhere in the room admit a row of digits from the top of a page. This cadence has its own mechanism. It has not been listening to the bead.

A row arrives. Another is already travelling downward. The figures come in groups of five; we touch a group and it becomes amber. The group stops at its address while the flow continues around it.

We leave our hand in the air, then lower it. The selected figures remain. Their persistence no longer requires that we stay in contact.

The display carries the name of RAND's random-number book, but these digits are generated here. The reference to a book offers one expectation of duration: numbers waiting on a page. This page has first made them pass, then given the hand a way to keep some of them available.

We read the group once, and then once more while another row descends. Nothing in the five figures records how long the second reading took. A further touch releases them. The page resumes deciding when they leave.

At MOVE / KEEP, duration enters with our hand. We pick up the sphere and move it beneath six traces. The instrument records position and orientation with elapsed times. Too little valid movement leaves the previous seed in place. A sufficient recording yields a number when we release.

Rounding and a fixed hash procedure turn the retained measurements into a seed from zero to 999. The exact record can produce the number again. We can spend another interval trying to repeat the movement, but that attempt makes another record.

The new seed can restart the hall's separate procedures. What we did during the visit has reached their beginnings. It has not made their subsequent calculations cease to be repeatable.

The larger sphere waits at centre for one and a half seconds. Then its outward leg takes three and a half, and its return takes three and a half more. Direction and distance vary within the route.

Holding now carries the body. The viewpoint travels with it. We can release before the return, and the sphere completes the remaining time alone.

It comes back. It waits. Another destination becomes available under the same rule.

We are still free to remain beside it through the next cycle. No counter on the sphere says how long we should take before leaving.
