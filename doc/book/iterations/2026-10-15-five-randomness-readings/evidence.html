Apparatus for the five candidates

Random Touch

Five horizontal rods are one metre long. The first four use the same X targets at rates 0.4, 0.8, 1.6 and 3.2 segments per second; their labels are ½×, 1×, 2× and 4×. The fifth uses 0.8 segments per second and adds Y. X is bounded by ±0.44 m and Y by ±0.12 m. The record contains 48 points, starting at centre, with smooth interpolation between successive points.

Contact moves the contacting visible virtual hand, not the viewpoint, body, tracked controller or other hand. Withdrawal ends the following; the bead returns to centre. Twelve phase samples within each segment draw translucent circles on pale paper. Overlaps darken. Ink persists after release, with a maximum of 576 retained marks per row. Waiting and return motion add no ink. Equal segment counts yield the same X marks at different speeds.

The installed source is uniform. Marks include interpolated travel positions, so a dense centre does not establish Gaussian target selection. The current public interface has one large rectangular REPLAY button. It resets the paths and clears the paper after release. Bounded Gaussian comparison and KEEP/LOAD helpers exist in reconstruction/research code; they are not public room controls. Source, hand and ink checks in the ink-wall iteration report PASS; these are prior automated results, not a fresh participant trial.

Falling page

The upright page displays its own generated groups of five digits, not a transcription of RAND's book. Rows arrive from the top at a nominal 2.5-second cadence. Touch toggles a group: selected groups are amber and stay at their address while other digits continue downwards. Hand withdrawal does not release a selection; another touch does. The private generator uses the hall seed.

Historical claims used in A and B were checked against RAND's publication and production account on 15 October 2026. It describes producing tables for random-selection calculations and finding biases in an electronic source before transforming the published table. The drafts make no claim that keeping a table alone establishes physical provenance.

Geiger and common seed

The cabinet has a readout, event timeline, indicator, needle, comparison rows and synthesized clicks. No radiation detector is connected. A repeatable generator calculates 96 event times before playing them. RESTART replays the retained times; the clip also loops. Default rate is a mean four events per second. The seed sets the starting state, while the generator's state advances.

The comparison procedure reads two gaps per group of four detections, skips equal measured intervals and emits bits from unequal comparisons with alternating comparison direction. A clock comparison uses the same events and sound while switching resolution between one and 100 milliseconds. Changing rate is a different intervention that changes waiting intervals.

The hall's generating instruments start at 435 using their own procedures and streams. A stored receipt reports RANDOM.ORG atmospheric noise, acquisition at 13 October 2026, 14:29:02 UTC, range 0–999 and value 435. This is what the receipt reports; its provider claim is not independently authenticated here. Opening the hall reuses the receipt without a fresh request. Manual seed choices, SEED +1, restored records or a completed movement recording can choose a new beginning. Reset recalls 435. Active interactions can block a seed change.

MOVE / KEEP, conditional on restoration

The grabbable sphere supplies position and orientation beneath six traces. A sufficient recording requires at least 30 valid samples and a measured pose change; at most 300 samples are collected. Timed poses are rounded, encoded, hashed with a fixed procedure and reduced to a seed in 0–999. Retaining the exact record permits the same derivation. Repeating a similar gesture creates a new record. Different records can share a seed; no demonstrated collision pair is supplied. The input changes the hall seed; subsequent generator outputs remain pseudorandom. This instrument does not certify entropy.

Out / Back

The larger sphere makes horizontal excursions: 1.5 seconds waiting at centre, 3.5 seconds outward and 3.5 seconds returning. Direction and distance in the range 1.1–2.2 m are selected by a private generator. Holding engages collision-aware body carry and can move the viewpoint. Release ends carry; the sphere continues its cycle. Collisions can constrain actual body motion. A new seed changes destinations but does not remove the authored return. The sphere's return does not force a released player to accompany it.
