# The carrier participates

The value has stayed. Has the colour?

Pause between making a pattern and putting it on a body. Look at what happens when a value reaches a surface and meets its surroundings.

<!-- @SimultaneousContrast -->

Two grey squares wait against different backgrounds. Do they appear alike? Look before touching the controls. There is no required answer to report.

Exchange their surrounds. The targets keep their numerical values. Does your judgement follow the square, the background, or neither?

Now make both backgrounds the same grey as the targets. Their faces may almost disappear, while the raised edges still give them away. Restore the unequal backgrounds and connect the targets with their grey bar. Notice what becomes easier to compare.

![Equal target values against dark and light surrounds inside the museum](../images/215f0559-contrast-neighbours-museum.png)

*One grey value, two different surrounds.*

Numerical equality can be inspected in the program. It does not oblige every viewer or viewing situation to produce the same experience. Keep your observation beside the stated condition, even if it differs from what you expected.

<!-- @MetamerismLab -->

At the next station, the bent mark covers a plane and a curved wall. Find its blue corner on both. The repeats begin with equal widths measured along the two surfaces.

Walk sideways. Follow a boundary around the curve. Its distance along the surface stays fixed while its width in your view changes. The carrier has entered the appearance before you alter the source.

Change from flat colour to shading, then move the supplied light direction. Follow a pale part of the mark. The plane keeps one facing; the curve turns through several. One texture receives different treatments because the surfaces meet the chosen direction differently.

This small shader comparison does not model cloth fibres or wavelengths. It gives us one contribution we can isolate and return to.

Imagine the mark on a sleeve. Which part would you call the pattern: its source, its spacing along the cloth, or the appearance that reaches someone across a room? Keeping one fixed does not keep all three fixed.

The next studio gives the pattern a garment and a wearer. Take this pause with you: before correcting an appearance, find out which condition has changed.
