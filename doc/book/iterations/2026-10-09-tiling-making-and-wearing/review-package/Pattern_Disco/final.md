# The same floor, another palette

Do you recognise the floor in its new colours?

The checker is navy and pink now. Its squares return in the same arrangement. Step onto one and wait for its colour to change. It keeps supporting you. After the coloured rooms, this familiar floor gives us somewhere to try what we have learned.

Hold the floor and stop the sequencer. Choose CHECKER, then change its palette. Keep your eye on one square. Which colour seems to come forward? Does the square still seem to belong to the same figure?

Nothing has moved. The square keeps its calculated zero or one and receives another colour. Leave the palette alone and press ONE STEP. Now the checker exchanges its values. Two different actions can change the picture. Follow one place long enough to tell them apart.

![Two palettes on the same held checker, seen from the same place on the museum floor.](../images/755f5b7c-checker-pair.png)

One palette choice reaches every square carrying the same value. We cannot use this control to leave just one of them pink. The reach of a choice belongs to the way the instrument has been made.

Start the floor and walk towards an approaching band. Let it pass underneath you. Its passage does not push your body. Walk with it, then across it. Can an invitation to move be felt without a force being applied?

Return to black and white while it runs. Notice whether you follow the same parts. Changing back cannot undo the coloured versions you have just seen beside it.

<!-- @step_sequencer -->

Bring in a repeating note. The score and the moving pattern have separate clocks. Let them run, look away, and keep moving. When you look back, where do you find the beat?

Your footsteps are not written into the score. You can move with its pulse, miss it, or keep another rhythm. The room repeats its invitation without knowing what you make of it.
