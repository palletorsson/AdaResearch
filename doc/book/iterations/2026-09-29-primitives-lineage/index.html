Primitives: what each next room needs

Working agreement, 29 September 2026. Read against the twelve current final.md texts, their intentions, tutorial files, map placements and the tutorial atlas. This tightens the existing order. It does not certify twelve completed making lessons.

The thread is what we have to add before a position can become something we encounter, alter and use again. A new operation changes both the construction and what a body can do with it. The critical question belongs at that change, rather than arriving afterwards as a separate interpretation.

Two connected lineages

The geometric construction is short:

point → segment → triangular face → tetrahedral enclosure

Two distinct points determine the segment. A third point outside their line allows a triangle with area. A fourth outside their plane allows a tetrahedron with volume. Connections and faces still have to be specified. Adding points alone does not render any of those constructions. The sphere used as a point marker is already a three-dimensional object; it is not the mathematical point growing dimensions.

The working lineage is longer:

running scene → visible and physical attachments → reference frame → relation → ordered record → repeatable address → face → enclosure → editable connectivity → reusable scene / generated mesh → refinement and stopping → composition

This is the museum's learning route, not a claim that software must always be constructed in this order. Trace and Grid teach recording and repeatable placement. A triangle does not mathematically require either. We place them before Triangle because the visitor can bring a drawing, understand its selected positions, and use snapping to make corners meet.

Transformation then changes the pose of a known object. The Cube hall has already shown a different operation: editing the positions inside a mesh. Keep that distinction available when a whole cube moves, turns or scales.

Give the later halls different jobs

First Polyhedra asks whether faces enclose a region. Cube asks what stays connected when its corners move. A mesh already exists with the Triangle hall's single face; we do not wait until Cube to acquire one. Cube makes shared construction more inspectable. Its square-looking face returns the earlier quad's diagonal at a larger, familiar body.

The Cube shelf adds another branch: instance a reusable scene and change its neighbours. That is different from choosing a PrimitiveMesh class and its parameters. A mesh resource describes a surface; a scene can combine that surface with materials, collision and behaviour. The next hall brings both into view. Neither is simpler in every sense merely because the engine supplies it.

Ignorance compares constructions. Portals follows a rule through a sequence of constructions. Melencolia asks what we want to do with the resulting vocabulary. Those are different returns to limitation; they do not need to repeat the same warning about finite computers.

What actually travels

The released coloured pen supplies a saved position list through TraceData. Grid reads it, centres and scales a display copy, and shows a snapped comparison. Triangle independently reads the saved trace, selects up to six corners, scales and flattens them, and leaves the carried outline open. Grid's green display is not the input handed to Triangle. The original saved list remains available.

The whiteboard's image and the ball's path do not travel by that route. A conceptual return is not evidence of a shared data object. The face, tetrahedron and cube are currently separate artifacts; their construction ideas recur, but the visitor's particular face does not automatically become the next hall's solid.

For the student kit, the intended continuity is stronger: reuse the known positions, segment helper and list rather than replace the framework at every chapter. That is a delivery requirement for the making pass, not something the existing downloads already guarantee.

Three selections, one reason to continue

  • Hall: choose the encounters that let a difference be felt. A second object earns its place by changing a relation, offering a comparison or opening another use. Pleasure and atmosphere can matter without each becoming a coding lesson.
  • Main text: follow the encounter long enough for the new distinction and its stakes to become legible. Keep the mystery of what it might become; avoid mystery about what the installed object actually does.
  • Tutorial: teach the smallest usable operation that lets the student make another choice. Supply surrounding machinery explicitly. Give one variation that works beyond the original demonstration.

Each handover should answer: what is being kept, what is newly added, and what can we now change? These questions guide our editing; they do not need to become a repeated formula in the prose.

Where work is still needed

  1. Coordinates → Line is the next making task. The coordinate download has no portable XR rig; the existing Line tutorial starts with an 8×8 grid and nested arrays. Deliver the held point and one measurable segment before extending the grid exercise. The museum already has the two-endpoint encounter.
  2. Trace needs a small list before the recorder machinery. Teach append and one distance gate with Line's measurement. Introduce rounding, capacity and transfer afterwards. The amber short-memory passage currently has no corresponding placed artifact in the cleaned hall. Preserve the idea and resolve its encounter deliberately; do not silently restore another dot after the requested cleanup or describe it as installed.
  3. Triangle's first tutorial should stop at a usable face. Keep the large twin and fan experiment in the hall and main inquiry. The six-corner concave comparison is a second investigation after the student can deliberately move three corners and fill them. Grid is an optional placement aid, not a prerequisite for the first face.
  4. Polyhedra → Cube should reuse one vertex/face representation in the student kit. First compare an open corner and its closed base. Then follow one moved cube corner through the triangles that name it. Avoid introducing a new unexplained mesh helper at this join.

The main texts can receive small handover edits now. The remaining tutorial gaps need runnable work, not more transitional prose. No new room is required to make this lineage clear.
