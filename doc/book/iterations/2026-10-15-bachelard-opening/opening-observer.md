# From the moving spheres to the RAND page

<!-- @random_touch -->

Three thin lines hang horizontally in the air, one metre apart. Each is two metres long, with a dark sphere waiting at its centre. The room is visible behind and between them.

A hand meets the first sphere. It begins to move sideways, carrying the avatar with it. The line stays in place while the room slides across the view. The sphere slows into one position, then departs for another. The line marks the extent of the journey. Nothing on it marks the next turn.

At the next line, the same sequence of positions unfolds twice as quickly. Its reach has not changed; there is less time between changes of direction. The third sphere restores the slower pace and adds rising and falling. The space above the line becomes part of the journey.

A repeatable calculation—a pseudorandom generator—supplies these positions. Its seed sets the starting state from which the sequence is produced. REPLAY returns the spheres to their centres, ready for another touch. The same turns remain available for another journey.

The hand withdraws. The avatar stops travelling with the sphere, which returns alone to its centre. No trace of the journey remains on the line.

Further into the room, numbers descend across a large page. They arrive from above, one row at a time, in groups of five digits. A hand touches one group. It turns amber and stays while the surrounding numbers continue downwards. The hand withdraws; the selected digits remain.

The gesture that set one thing moving has made another remain. The five digits have outlasted the contact that selected them.

RAND's *A Million Random Digits*, published in 1955, made a supply of numbers available for calculations using random selections. Producing that supply required testing and correction: its electronic source introduced biases, systematic departures from the intended even distribution. The published table preserved the results after further processing. Random numbers could be returned to at a numbered page. [RAND's production account](https://www.rand.org/pubs/monograph_reports/MR1418.html).

This falling page uses its own generated digits. Touch selects a group already present and changes how long it remains in view. Five amber digits stay in place while the surrounding rows continue to fall.
