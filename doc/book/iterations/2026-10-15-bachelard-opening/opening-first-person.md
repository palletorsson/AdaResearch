# From the moving spheres to the RAND page

<!-- @random_touch -->

Three thin lines hang in front of me, one metre apart. Each is two metres long, with a dark sphere waiting at its centre. I can see the room behind and between them.

I place my hand on the first sphere and begin to move sideways. The line stays where it was; the room slides past. The sphere slows into one position, then departs for another. I know how far it is allowed to go. I do not know where it will turn.

At the next line, I make the same journey twice as quickly. The positions repeat, with less time between changes of direction. The third sphere returns to the slower pace and carries me upwards as well as sideways. I rise and fall above the line. A strip of room that was only visible has become somewhere I travel.

I withdraw my hand. The sphere returns alone to its centre. No trace of the journey remains on the line.

The positions are kept elsewhere, in the program. A pseudorandom generator—a repeatable calculation—produced them from a starting state set by a seed. I press REPLAY and touch the first sphere again. Its turns return in the same order. There is time to meet one of them again.

Further into the room, I stand before a large page of falling numbers. They arrive from above, one row at a time, in groups of five digits. I touch a group. It turns amber and stays while the surrounding numbers continue downwards. I take my hand away. The digits remain.

At the sphere, my touch began a movement. Here it has made a small still place in the fall.

RAND's *A Million Random Digits*, published in 1955, made a supply of numbers available for calculations using random selections. Producing that supply required testing and correction: its electronic source introduced biases, systematic departures from the intended even distribution. The published table preserved the results after further processing. Random numbers could be returned to at a numbered page. [RAND's production account](https://www.rand.org/pubs/monograph_reports/MR1418.html).

This falling page uses its own generated digits. My touch has selected a group already present and changed how long it remains in view. The rows move on. I read the five digits again.
