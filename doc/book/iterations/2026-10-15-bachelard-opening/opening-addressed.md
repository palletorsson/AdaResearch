# From the moving spheres to the RAND page

<!-- @random_touch -->

Three thin lines hang horizontally in the air, one metre apart. Each is two metres long, with a dark sphere waiting at its centre. The room is visible behind and between them.

Touch the first sphere. It carries your avatar sideways. The line stays in place while your view of the room shifts. The sphere slows into one position, then departs for another. You can see the limits of the journey without knowing its next turn.

At the next line, the same sequence of positions unfolds twice as quickly. Its reach has not changed; there is less time between changes of direction. The third sphere restores the slower pace and adds rising and falling. The space above the line becomes part of the journey.

A repeatable calculation—a pseudorandom generator—supplies these positions. Its seed sets the starting state from which the sequence is produced. REPLAY returns the spheres to their centres, ready for another touch. A turn that caught your attention can be visited again.

Lift your hand away. You stop travelling with the sphere; it returns alone to the centre. A moment ago the line was a journey. Now it is a line you can look at again.

Further into the room, numbers descend across a large page. They arrive from above, one row at a time, in groups of five digits. Touch a group. It turns amber and stays while the surrounding numbers continue downwards. Take your hand away: the selected digits remain.

The gesture that set one thing moving has made another remain. There is time now to read what passed under your finger.

RAND's *A Million Random Digits*, published in 1955, made a supply of numbers available for calculations using random selections. Producing that supply required testing and correction: its electronic source introduced biases, systematic departures from the intended even distribution. The published table preserved the results after further processing. Random numbers could be returned to at a numbered page. [RAND's production account](https://www.rand.org/pubs/monograph_reports/MR1418.html).

This falling page uses its own generated digits. You did not produce the group you touched; you selected it. The other rows continue past. You can notice a repetition, wonder why your hand stopped there, or leave those five digits waiting while you look at what is still falling.
