## Turn-reversal experiment

The reviewer proposed returning to Grammar Lab, keeping its sentence and rewrite rule while reversing every turn. We extracted all four complete files from the current tutorial. A second copy changes only the two turn handlers in `grammar.gd`; its host, PointBody and renderer remain identical.

**Prediction:** for this planar reader, starting at the origin with heading +Y, corresponding endpoints become (−x, y, z). The sentence, ordered segment lengths and segment joins stay the same. This describes raw model coordinates; the separate display origins must be accounted for when comparing meshes.

**Result:** 30 native Godot checks passed, including 20 model cases: generations zero through four at 30°, 60°, 90° and 120°. Both complete programs were instantiated under a translated and rotated parent. The fitted cylinder positions reflect about each display's own origin; a held reading remains independent when the live angle and generation change.

The first straight line cannot reveal the difference. `F+F` at 90° supplies an asymmetric witness: the final point changes from (1, 1, 0) to (−1, 1, 0). An unchanged reader fails that geometric expectation even though its segment lengths and counts pass. Counts alone would miss the error.

This tests the supplied unbranched reader and this edit. It does not add a third reader to the museum, test arbitrary 3D turtle frames, or repeat all eight halls' runtime checks. The independent exercise remains open in the tutorial; this worked reviewer example is kept in the review appendix so it does not prescribe the student's choice.
