# Procedural Generation — response to the review

I agree with the assessment's central reading and its recommendation to keep the seven-hall order. The most useful addition is to name a practice that already runs through the work: **preserve something, change one operation, compare the consequence, decide what to carry forward.** This gives the different algorithmic families a common purpose without pretending they are the same technique.

This response records proposed revisions. It does not change the seven canonical tutorial/final pairs, their programs or the museum's layout.

## What is already present

| Hall | What the learner can keep or repeat | What can change | Important limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genetic Evolution | Candidate descriptions; a deep-copied leader | Target or estimator | HOLD keeps the numerical leader; it is not free selection of any favourite |
| Space Colonization | A field fixture and a held geometric specimen | Eligibility policy or direction weighting | The witness is positions and parents, not a resumable simulation |
| Branching Growth | The same eight targets and a held node record | Whether a parent remains active | Held nodes do not include the reached flags or a clock |
| Percolation Network | Occupancy, seed and boundary policy | Permitted neighbour offsets | Reproducible comparison is available; a persistent multi-state archive is not implemented |
| Caves and Mazes | Nine declared centres, including a repeated event | Carving headroom | A fixed route is not an automatic hand-trace import; the miniature is not walkable |
| Sculpted Forms | Captured local centres and their derived sample | Marked extent, or originals moved after capture | The sample omits orientation and collision history |
| Mirrored Patterns | An independent held array | Copying rule or result before committing it | Equal cell totals do not imply equal arrangements |

These are several forms of comparison, not one universal undo or save system. Calling all of them a saved history would obscure useful differences. The tutorial should say exactly what each witness preserves and which earlier decision can actually be revisited.

## Four revisions worth making

1. **A short sequence opening.** Link the L-system description/reader distinction to the new questions: what does an evaluator notice, which sites can respond, what counts as contact, and what survives reconstruction? Repeat a small comparison prompt at the relevant exercise rather than introducing a second theoretical explanation in every hall.
2. **Protect the two difficult teaching steps.** In Colonization, read one site's next decision before expanding the field. In Sculpted Forms, follow one captured centre to an address, one occupied cell and one exposed face before reading the complete mesh. Keep the full runnable programs; make the new operation easy to locate within them.
3. **Trim repeated instructions from the first two reflections.** Preserve their encounters and critical questions. Let tutorial.md carry most button sequences, numerical walkthroughs and code explanations. Keep any operational distinction needed for the reflection's claim to remain true.
4. **Fix the documented room entries before a continuous VR visit.** Branching, Percolation, Caves, Sculpted Forms and Mirrored Patterns have recorded front-desk entrance obstructions. The Sculpted Forms dome also needs a placement review. A side bypass tested from beside a desk is not evidence of arriving successfully from the previous hall. Verify the actual entry and onward passage after moving anything.

A new fitness criterion is a useful optional independent exercise. It should not expand the opening into a whole genetic-algorithm course. The present evaluator is a sufficiently clear teaching focus.

## Two distinctions to retain in the reading

**The visitor's encounter and the student's reconstruction are different scopes.** In Sculpted Forms the installed museum supplies twelve falling cubes and lets the visitor choose the sampling moment. The learner program uses three fixed cubes to reconstruct the sampling operation. Both experiences matter; the tutorial does not secretly contain a physics simulation or reconstruct the whole fall.

**Selection and aggregation need their actual assignment rules.** Colonization first assigns each request to a nearest eligible site, then combines that site's assigned directions. Branching lets each active site choose one nearest unreached target. Calling them merely 'average all targets' and 'choose one' would lose the eligibility and assignment decisions the pair makes visible.

## What the larger claim can be

L-systems made the separation between a description and its reading available. Procedural Generation makes another separation available: a generated appearance, a stored relationship and a usable passage may disagree. Its methods become useful research tools when the student can preserve the relevant input, locate a changed decision and inspect the consequence.

The review's phrase 'selective forgetting' is productive where the omission is specific: a score omits a ring; a sampled centre omits rotation; an end volume omits the number of repeated carving events. It should remain attached to those operations instead of becoming a general claim that every representation forgets in the same way.
