# The student who hasn't arrived

*Eight-panel comic synopsis · Endless Museum · 5 October 2026*

An imagined student walks through a world whose code can already run. Their teacher is also a figure of anticipation: the author preparing for a role whose conditions are changing. The student's needs are assumed; the sphere's construction and behaviour can be tested. Neither person is reducible to that test.

The student wears rose; the teacher wears blue. Faint doubled contours give them an unsettled presence. The small white sphere has weight of line, a definite surface and a consistent size. It is the same point carried through the teaching sequence. A large, polished black sphere awaits the student at the end: the object of the future. The characters are fictional figures, not portraits or recorded participants.

The revised rooms follow the museum's straight off-white partitions, rectangular passages, terrazzo floor and simple plinths. Point One begins with three podiums: an empty position, a visible white point, then a matching physical point. Each room bears the same spaced serif wall title, **ENDLESS MUSEUM**, with **always another room** underneath.

The sequence dramatizes an actual editorial correction: stop rebuilding the sphere in each lesson, and test what must be added to carry the student's earlier work forward. The code in panel 5 is real. The native result in panel 6 belongs to the [bounded continuation pilot](../2026-10-05-lesson-state-chain/REPORT.md), not a human learning trial. The incoming file is an explicit reconstruction from Point One's lesson and source. It is not a recovered student's program.

The comic treats the point as one continuing work. The pilot checks preserved geometry, placement, material and node/resource identity through the interaction; it does not establish persistence of one live instance across all museum halls or sessions.

The teacher's uncertainty concerns the value and direction of their continuing labour. The comic leaves open what the student will need, what the teacher will become, and what learning programming will mean when they meet.
