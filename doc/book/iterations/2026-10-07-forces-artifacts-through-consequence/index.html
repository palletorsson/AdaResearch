Artifacts through consequence

The same point gains abilities. A plinth, a tether, a field and another body give those abilities somewhere to meet resistance.

Keep the main experience legible: an ordinary task, a clear operation, then another relation that changes its consequences. The strange result should arise from what the bodies do together.

Design proposal grounded in current source inspection; no museum/runtime/manuscript/order changes, no new Godot tests. Images below are earlier concept illustrations. Edit or reuse the full proposal.

Start small

First: the plinth, the retrieving point, the tether.

The reluctant plinth is the strongest first study of the new direction: everyone understands moving a ball to its place. First test the trolley and friction; add the elastic continuation only where spring behavior has been introduced. The retrieving point is closest to existing force code. The tethered point gives the same body a return, an obstruction and a possible adversary.

Keep the agreed flight controls and the earlier proposed route: push → launch → two-hand flight → release/brake → vortex → deflect → tethered return → combination. These artifacts can develop that route. This page does not replace or reorder the canonical thirteen halls.

A failed delivery is an outcome to inspect. It is not the only correct ending.

Reuse a small physical kit: the black point, an off-white movable support, a tether with a visible anchor, a bounded field and a few loose blocks. The mobile is an optional reflective encounter. The final room adds no new compulsory force law.

Continuing object / ability spine

The point that can carry you

Earlier concept art, reused as a reference; not a new native museum capture.

The task

Push the familiar sphere; later use two instances to reach a ledge yourself.

What the student learns

Finite push → retained velocity → two contributions to thrust. Teach these in successive encounters.

What else could happen

One hand lets go while the other still thrusts. You continue drifting; the remaining contribution changes the journey rather than cancelling it.

What the player can do

Release, counter-thrust or use the landing court; camera orientation remains level.

Ability / room fixture

The launch pad and the landing tray

Earlier concept art, reused as a reference; not a new native museum capture.

The task

Send the point to a nearby tray, then make the crossing yourself.

What the student learns

Launch state, gravity and contact at landing. Distinguish setting velocity from applying an impulse.

What else could happen

A successful landing strikes a loose tray, which slides into another body. Arrival becomes the start of a second event.

What the player can do

Adjust launch direction, restrain the tray or change the landing surface.

Existing foundation and work still needed

Installed launch pad exists; a movable receiving tray and freely interacting landing encounter are new.

Priority prototype / small task

The reluctant plinth

Earlier concept art, reused as a reference; not a new native museum capture.

The task

Move the point on a small trolley to its display plinth.

What the student learns

Begin with contact and friction. Return after spring force is introduced to add an elastic pull.

What else could happen

The wheel catches; pulling stores energy. When it comes free the trolley reaches a second object before the player has caught up with the consequence.

What the player can do

Change route, reduce the pull, move the obstruction or brake. A quiet successful delivery must also be possible.

Existing hall candidates: VFM_03_Motion · Vectors_Act4b_Oscillation

Existing foundation and work still needed

These are reference studies, not a reusable interacting trolley. Friction ramp integrates a prescribed lane, spring tower uses a one-axis solver, collision carts display ideal outcomes. A coupled room-scale receiver must be built.

  • commons/artifacts/friction_ramp/friction_ramp.gd
  • commons/artifacts/spring_tower/restoring_forces.gd
  • commons/artifacts/collision_carts/collision_carts.gd

Check: The held tether stores energy; removing the pull does not erase motion. No collision animation determines the ending. First test one catch and one free run before adding loose blocks.

The retrieving point

The task

Bring a loose sphere back to your hand using the held catalyst.

What the student learns

A position-dependent attraction acts on declared receivers; the source does not understand the player's preferred target.

What else could happen

The field also pulls a doorstop or movable support. The requested sphere comes closer while something else changes the passage.

What the player can do

Move the source, shorten the radius, use a target filter or release attraction and watch the remaining motion.

Field / accumulated consequence

The collecting room

Earlier concept art, reused as a reference; not a new native museum capture.

The task

Gather scattered spheres into a shallow central basin; later enter the field yourself.

What the student learns

Radial and tangential field contributions act on existing velocity. Change one term at a time.

What else could happen

Gathering becomes orbiting or crowding. The same rule catches loose supports as well as the intended collection; entry speed changes the result.

What the player can do

Alter the field, move its centre or counter-thrust through it. The field is not guaranteed to tidy the room.

Existing hall candidates: Vectors_Act3_FieldsAndMotion

Spring / force-based adversary

The point that insists on returning

Earlier concept art, reused as a reference; not a new native museum capture.

The task

Deflect a tethered sphere to open a passage.

What the student learns

Restoring force, damping and a visible anchor. Reuse the same target body from the shooting study.

What else could happen

The point swings back. In a later version the anchor sits on a movable plinth, so the place it returns to also moves.

What the player can do

Time a crossing, move the anchor, damp the return or wedge the target aside. Destruction need not be the only solution.

Optional reflective encounter

The body you keep adding to

Earlier concept art, reused as a reference; not a new native museum capture.

The task

Hang the point so that it rests level; add one familiar object.

What the student learns

Support, torque and mass distribution. Add a rigid wind-catching plate only after the balance is legible.

What else could happen

The addition shifts the balance. A powered fan then acts on the added plate and sets the rest moving; the body now responds differently because of what it carries.

What the player can do

Move an attachment, add a counterweight or keep an asymmetry. No perfect balance is required for the object to be worth keeping.

Final combination / no new compulsory law

The delivery room

Earlier concept art, reused as a reference; not a new native museum capture.

The task

Carry one point across a room using the abilities and tools already learned.

What the student learns

Composition and observation: launch, brake, gather, redirect and return remain the same operations.

What else could happen

An automated conveyor keeps delivering props while the receiver slows. Moving one jam changes what meets the next body. A blockage may become a support or just remain a blockage.

What the player can do

Stop a feed, take another route, retrieve the point or preserve an interesting arrangement. A manual reset starts a new experiment; it should not silently erase the aftermath.

Existing foundation and work still needed

Existing conveyor_backlog uses actual rigid boxes, a moving-surface collider, a timed receiver and a finite pool. A complete carrying task combining the selected artifacts remains a proposal.

Check: Reuse the learned rules; bound the number of bodies visibly. Validate restart and retained state. Do not demand a particular collapse or funny ending as the success criterion.

What the source inspection changes

There is a real difference between a model showing a result and bodies able to affect one another. Collision carts currently renders ideal one-dimensional before/after outcomes. Tug of war sets the load position from a changing vector sum. The vortex replaces player velocity. Those studies are useful references, but do not already supply the new encounters.

Two stronger starting points are the held point's bounded attraction of rigid bodies and the conveyor's actual rigid parcels and timed receiver. Reuse what they demonstrate without assuming their models, controls or collision masks automatically fit every new receiver.

The current resolution catalyst returns home after release. A force encounter needs an explicit choice about that return: silent resets must not erase the consequence the student is supposed to observe.

One object, three kinds of work

Museum: do a small task and meet what the room adds. Tutorial: reconstruct one mechanism, test it and carry the same body onward. Reflection: ask what happened to the intention, what a body now carries, and whether a consequence has to be repaired.

For opponents, begin with the same sphere as a free receiver, then a tethered obstruction. A pursuing variant can follow later. The player can redirect, restrain, lure or go around it; an enemy need not require a new creature or a new damage system.

Keep noise and flocking as later returns when their rules have been introduced. Forces already has enough material for strange situations through mass, contact, attraction, damping and constraints.
