# Hand-learning pilot — proposed, not run

Purpose: find where unfamiliar visitors cannot teach or test the sphere as the text promises. This small formative study can expose problems; it cannot estimate recognition quality for a population.

Invite four to six willing people who did not construct the system. Let each choose comfortable poses and movement phrases. Include different preferred seated/standing positions and reaches where people wish to try them. Do not require a participant to disclose a diagnosis or perform a movement they cannot comfortably make. The current two-hand requirement is a constraint of this exercise; record when it prevents participation instead of describing that person as a recognition failure.

Use one participant per empty local teaching session. Preserve any existing visitor files before a session; do not silently overwrite another person's saved model. Keep recordings locally under a participant-chosen code, with permission to retain or delete them. No names are needed in the numerical record. Stop whenever the participant wants to stop.

## First: teach poses

Let the participant choose three distinct comfortable poses. Explain the six coordinates, the fixed 0.65-metre normalization and the difference between a label and FLOAT/SPIN/STRETCH. Teach at least three separate examples per label, fit once and freeze that model for the first check block.

Before each fresh test, write the intended label in the session sheet. Ask for three new takes per label and three intentionally unfamiliar poses. Do not coach a failed pose into passing while counting it as the same test. Keep the predicted/accepted answer, rejected capture, tracking interruption and the participant's account separate. Perception's small in-memory test display is not the versioned Synthesis journal: use the session sheet and retain the corresponding model snapshot.

Ask the participant which results surprised them and what distinction they thought they were teaching. Inspect a mismatch together before changing anything. If they choose to revise teaching, save the first model separately, fit the revision and start a new check block. Earlier results still belong to the earlier model.

## Next visit: keep ONE phrase

Teach one four-second phrase with both hands. First ask the participant to explain their recording versus the fitted replay. Then test three fresh takes, a reversal and a deliberate timing change. Record whether the participant experienced a mismatch as useful, confusing or irrelevant to their intended movement. Check whether the miniature replay is understood as a display rather than a command to move the body into it. Save ONE.

Do not rush this visit to reach A/B. Inability to complete or explain this step is a finding about the encounter. It is not missing data to remove from the account.

## Then distinguish A/B and inspect variation

On a later visit or after a break, teach a second comfortable phrase and save A/B separately. In Synthesis, declare intention before each new capture: three fresh A takes, three B takes and three OTHER takes chosen by the participant. Export the journal before any revision. Keep UNKNOWN, AMBIGUOUS, disagreement and invalid capture separate. Report the small number of checks explicitly; repeated takes from one person are not independent participants.

Import ONE into Generative, fit, and compare SOURCE, MEAN and three named seeds. Ask the participant what seems to have been kept or changed. There is no recognition accuracy measure or dance-quality score for these samples. An uncomfortable or uninteresting generated path is something to discuss, not something the participant must perform.

## What to retain

- Session code, engine/version, controller setup, chosen position and alignment, model file/version, number of teaching takes and whether preview was used.
- For each attempt: intended label, actual answer, capture validity, tracking events and whether any coaching or refitting occurred.
- The participant's explanation of a surprising result, in their own words if they permit it.
- Missing steps, fatigue, unclear controls and inability to enter the exercise. Keep these in the account.

Use the blank `ML-HUMAN-PILOT.csv` for manual observations. It contains no fabricated results. Compare within each participant first; do not pool different personally taught labels as if they described the same gestures. Keep hardware/tracking failures separate from valid takes the model declined. Report what changed in the tutorial or artifact as a result, and which question still needs another encounter.
