The wood is unfinished on this side. A knot above my shoulder has split, and a little sky shows through it. When I move my head, the sky disappears. I move back. There it is again.

The panel in front of me is still in place. For a while I prefer the knot. It gives the sky an edge I can approach.

Across the inside of the crate, a brace joins two corners. The boards are thin; the brace is doing much of the work. I follow its grain with a finger to where it meets the upright. Someone drove the nails from outside. Here the timber has lifted around a point of metal. I move my hand around it.

I strike the panel. The boards part with a short crack, taking the brace with them. The upper frame stays where it was.

Through the open face, a number is changing.

I watch until its last digits have changed too often to retain. Then I look down at the packing floor, and back. The number has moved on. My first number was not its first number.

I step out.

The roof extends well beyond the crate. Columns stand in two rows, with short lengths of reinforcement projecting from their tops. Service rails run between supports. Farther along, the concrete stops around an opening and the columns continue below it. There is another floor down there. The building is further advanced beneath my feet than above my head.

ADA RESEARCH stands on a steel frame against the sky.

This is not the address on my consignment record. I have the other address. I could begin the business of getting there, but the number is visible from here and I have not finished looking at it.

I turn to inspect the crate from outside.

CARE UNIT — MODEL B.

The lettering runs across several boards. Narrow gaps interrupt it. Below it, an arrow indicates which way the crate should stand. So far, the delivery has complied with the arrow.

The opening has no door. I walk around to the back, where the panels are still in place. The crate can prevent an approach from this side while admitting one from the other. Its usefulness as a container is doubtful now. As a small room it is quite good.

I return to the number. Its panel says *Frames*.

A count of updates can begin before anything is available to look at. The machinery that makes this roof visible has its own work to do before I see the result. I know the kind of work. I do not know everything this particular count includes, and nothing on the panel gives me the hour the crate was delivered. I could investigate the counter. First I want to see what happens to its view from the packing.

At the opening, the upper board cuts across the display. I lower my head and the tops of the digits return. I try to keep the whole number visible with as little sky above it as possible. This requires an unexpectedly exact position of my knees.

I hold it for a moment.

There is no reason to make a permanent viewing station here. I would have to decide a height for someone, and I have only tried mine. Still, I would like to keep the rest of the crate until I have seen the roof from elsewhere.

Near the centre of the slab, a black sphere sits within a wireframe cube. Its label gives this place three zeroes. The number behind me continues to increase.

On the floor, YOU ARE HERE has been printed with the first word crossed out. I read it from one side, then the other. The lettering does not turn to meet me.

I leave it where it is.

From beside the shaft, I can see the outside of the crate and a narrow portion of its interior. The knot that held the sky is too small to find. Below me, light reaches the next floor.

I move along the edge, looking for somewhere to land.
