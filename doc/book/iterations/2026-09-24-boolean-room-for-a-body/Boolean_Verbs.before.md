## What a solid is here

Before the verbs, the thing they operate on. These demonstrations describe solid regions with primitives and generate a boundary for us to see. They do not fill the middle with little pieces of material. Yet the region is not empty of meaning: we can still ask whether a point belongs to it.

Try passing through one of the small results. These exhibits have not enabled collision. Your body can ignore a boundary your eye has already accepted. A surface, a rule for membership and a rule for contact can agree; here one of those rules has not been supplied. Keep that missing agreement in mind when the objects become architecture.

## What the grammar cannot say

Three verbs, three ways of deciding membership. Union can retain separated regions. Intersection keeps what is shared. Difference leaves the region of A outside B and gives its new boundary something to show.

Membership is a yes or a no, but that does not make every change abrupt. Move a cutter and the cavity can widen continuously; move it far enough and two spaces may become connected. The grammar can compose such changes. What it does not decide by itself is who fits through the result, or what it feels like to be kept on one side. Those questions wait for a body.

The room next door is where you find out that even the four settings on each of these dials are a claim somebody published, and that the published claim and the object do not always agree.
