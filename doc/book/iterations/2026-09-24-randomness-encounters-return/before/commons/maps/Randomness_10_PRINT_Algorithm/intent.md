Concept: One line of BASIC, `10 PRINT CHR$(205.5+RND(1)); : GOTO 10`, prints one of two diagonals per cell and the diagonals join at their corners into something that reads as a maze. The hall separates what the line supplies from what a reader adds. 205.5 is the coin: it sits halfway between character 205 and 206, so moving it changes how often each diagonal is admitted. The trailing semicolon is the neighbourhood: without it every character starts its own row and no mark lies beside another. A maze needs both an open choice and a rule that lets one choice meet the next; take the first away and you get stripes, take the second away and you get a column.
Sequence role: Fourth hall of Randomness, after Random_Remove and before Random_Cubes. Random_Remove made chance subtract; here chance constructs, one binary decision at a time. Random_Cubes widens the coin to six faces and asks what a growing record can establish that one throw cannot, so this hall ends by pointing at the floor as a record of every two-sided throw. The hall is 13 by 14 cells.
Technical angle: The printer (`ten_print`) runs the line with a seeded, visible coin: its console shows the program and carries BIAS, SEMICOLON, RESEED, STEP and RUN. In this hall the default coin breathes: its threshold swings 0.2 to 0.8 over every 540 characters, so the opening field is forward-heavy near the top and backward-heavy near the bottom, about seven in ten each way. The floor (`ten_print_structure#form:floor`) links to the printer on channel "hall" and lays the screen's newest seven rows down as low ridges, half a metre a cell; it scrolls with the screen and has no collider. A second `ten_print_structure#form:wall#solid:on` stands the printer's first twelve characters, same seed, up as 2.4 m solid slabs four to a row, with channels about 70 cm wide. Those twelve flips divide the ground into eight regions, seven open and one sealed. Every open region is a corridor with two mouths, so nothing forks: four are shallow V-shaped bays, two are cut-off corners, and one is a way through. Four `composition_stochastique` grids after François and Vera Molnar (1959) turn exactly 1, 5, 30 and 50 of 100 cells; RESEED moves positions and keeps counts. The three `ten_print_maze_3d` fields stand upright, ten metres on a side, with a backtracking ant; they have no colliders, so a body walks through them but cannot travel along their passages. The ant moves on its own navigation grid, which shuts every channel between parallel diagonals.
Critical angle: 10 PRINT is the subject of a ten-author book (Montfort et al., MIT Press 2012, free at 10print.org) about randomness and regularity in one line of code. The hall does not treat the maze as secretly designed or as meaningless. It asks which relationships the rule supplies and which the reading adds. Two readings sit side by side: the upright fields and the floor read as textile, the walkable block as passage. Whether twelve flips leave a route is decided cell by cell and checked with a body. The breathing coin is a second authored decision printed where the visitor can change it, and the Molnar grids show that a probability and a count are different instruments.
Key artifacts: `ten_print` is the teaching work, the line made operable. `ten_print_structure` appears twice: as the floor that receives the screen, and as the walkable maze printed from the same coin. `composition_stochastique` sets fixed counts against the printer's free coin. `ten_print_maze_3d` is the upright field and its ant, seen but not entered. The clipboard (`ten_print_axioms`), science screen, `remove_random`, `pickup_cube_placer`, `nature_system_demo` and `dark_sphere` remain in the token layer. The chapter does not rely on them, and INTEGRATION.md lists what is wrong with the clipboard and the screen.
