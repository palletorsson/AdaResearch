Concept: One throw against a growing record, and what each record forgets. The die (dice_throw) and the coin (coin_toss) are physical: no random number decides a face, the hand and the physics do, so fairness is a question to investigate, not a property of symmetry. The die's glass keeps counts as shares and a running mean and forgets order and place; the coin's ribbon keeps the order of the last thirty tosses; the spawners keep nothing; the fence field keeps one deal and shows its rule (fixed ends, bounded middles) across forty-eight samples.
Sequence role: Fifth hall of Randomness, after Randomness_10_PRINT_Algorithm (whose floor forgot every row but the newest seven, and whose console printed 205.5 beside a breathing coin) and before Random_Rotate_Random_XYZ (an arrangement whose pieces all stay while their turns accumulate).
Technical angle: dice_throw reads the face whose normal points most nearly up once the same face has stayed on top at a steady height for 0.8 s (a landed die keeps turning about the vertical and never sleeps on this felt); reward balls, 16 per pip, spawn in local space with continuous collision so they land on the felt. coin_toss#disclosure:origin adds SPIN/DROP/TILT gauges read at release; frozen coins (tray or hand) are never read, and a coin lying clearly face up or down and holding still counts as settled. Both carry desktop hooks: on a screen nothing is thrown, the carried body turns to face the view and falls without spin. random_object_spawner: arrivals at a fixed point every 0.3-1.3 s, capacity 20 per spawner, random removal after that, unseeded. random_edge_profile: five points per 1 m slab, ends at 1.0 m, middle within 0.30 m and neighbours within 0.25 m, unseeded, no collider.
Critical angle: A reading is a record of a moment: the glass keeps the face it read even when the reward rain knocks the die over. Numbers printed beside a process (3.50, 0.5000, like 205.5 before them) are the model's claims, not the count's promises.
Key artifacts: dice_throw and coin_toss (both along the entry wall, played facing back toward the door); random_object_spawner (two points above the raised block); random_edge_profile (eight rows of six). dark_sphere is a remainder. The hall builds from its map (artifact_placement map).
