Close the boundary before asking for a face

The puzzle retains its three movable edges. Closing them makes Fill available; it does not create a surface automatically. The physical cap turns green when the fill is visible. An open boundary leaves it dimmed.

One outline, different fills

The drawing station brings NEW OUTLINE, SHIFT START and SHOW / HIDE FILL onto the same plate, with 25 cm between button centres. The screen reports corners, triangles, the starting corner and the drawing tip’s coordinates. Corner numbers are flat labels in space.

The fan’s limitation remains part of the experiment. With a supplied six-corner L-shaped outline, Shift Start changes the generated fill while leaving the boundary untouched. Some starts produce triangles outside the intended region. Six shifts return to the original fill. Hiding the surface preserves the edges; starting a new outline preserves completed faces.

Mount the interface without lifting the work

The shared data instrument provides a 76 × 42 cm screen. A separate backplate carries native 3D push buttons beneath it. The console tilts 30 degrees, while its support remains upright. It follows the artifact’s position and orientation, retains full-size controls when the artifact is scaled, and is excluded from the museum’s calculation of the exhibit’s lowest point.
