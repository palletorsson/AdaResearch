## The walk

A long corridor that puts randomness on trial before letting you believe in it. The prng_crank_machine and seed_replay_demo make the prosecution's case: turn the crank twice from the same seed and the "random" sequence repeats to the digit — determinism wearing a costume. The trng_vs_prng station stands the impostor next to the real thing, chance harvested from the world's own hiss; the random_number_book_page_1955 shows entropy's strangest artifact, a RAND Corporation print run of a million digits — disorder proofread, bound, and cited. The slot_machine gives the stakes a handle; the entropy_jar and entropy_axiom hold the concept still; the butterflies scatter through the far end as the first things in the chapter that nothing placed.

## What it opens

The threshold splits the word "random" in two and makes you hold both halves: the computable kind, which is really a very long lie with a short seed, and the harvested kind, which cannot repeat itself even if asked. Entropy is not decay — it is freedom, and the corridor's whole length is spent establishing that the freedom is *expensive*: it must be gathered from the world, one unrepeatable event at a time.
