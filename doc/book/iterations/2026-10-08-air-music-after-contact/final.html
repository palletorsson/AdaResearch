After the strike

In the previous hall, a score could keep an event's place before it sounded. Here you give the note its beginning. Your hand can leave while its consequence continues.

A stick lies in a cradle at the left end of a table. Eight metal bars lie beside it, the longest nearest the stick. Take it and bring the tip down on that bar. Lift it away at once and keep listening.

The contact is over. The bar glows and rings; the plate above the row names a voice and counts its age. As the glow fades, decide whether what you hear is the strike or what the strike began.

Strike the same bar again before it finishes. There is another attack, but no additional voice. Its age returns towards zero. Now strike a shorter bar while the first is still fading. The plate names two voices, each with its own age. The first was allowed to continue.

You can withdraw from the encounter without ending it. A second action arrives in a room still occupied by the first. Try a long gap, then a short one. Reverse the order. Listen for the moment when one note makes the other difficult to hear, although both remain present in the readout.

These bars have chosen notes. Their lengths were arranged to follow those choices; the program does not discover a pitch by calculating the vibration of the metal. There is a resemblance to an instrument, and a working instrument within that resemblance. The seam is available to us: we can change the body without changing its stored note.

The row offers C, D, E, G and A, continuing into another octave. That is a decision about which pairs your hand can reach. It does not settle which pairs are good. What sounds at ease here has already been given a particular company to keep.

Try one bar slowly and then quickly. The stick's speed reaches both the sound and the glow through different mappings. Brightness is not a direct measure of loudness. The room makes a gesture legible in more than one way, and those accounts do not quite coincide.

The ribbon adds a short piece of the sounding voices. Its sum is not a third bar. Nor is it everything that reaches your ears: distance changes the encounter, while these stored samples stay the same. A representation can tell us that two voices continue even when listening makes one seem to disappear.

Set the stick down away from its cradle. Left alone, it returns. The notes have their own endings. Their mathematical decay would continue indefinitely; the stored sounds finish after seven and a half seconds. Someone has decided how long the consequence may last.

For a moment, do nothing more. Let the room finish answering. Then carry this distinction onward: an action has ended, and something it began is still happening.
