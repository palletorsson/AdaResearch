# A mark in the room

In the disco, a choice of colour reached every square carrying the same value. Here you can make the little arrangement that a larger pattern will repeat. One mark, then its many appearances. How far can a decision made within reach travel?

*Mamma Mönster* records a hope behind Kristina Torsson's textile practice: soft, colourful clothes might change people. Making a pattern began with something wanted from the world. Keep that hope beside this small editor. What would you want your mark to make possible?

There is already pattern underfoot. It climbs the walls. At DRESS THE ARCHITECTURE, choosing another source changes what the hall wears. Its floor still supports you, and its walls still stop you. Leave the larger image running and go to the small tile, where the beginning is easier to follow.

Take a coloured cube and release it over an off-centre cell. Find the mark in the repeated preview, then locate the one source cell you changed.

Add a second mark beside it. Predict the repeated pair before looking across the field. Many visible changes have followed one local edit. The size of the result can make the action seem more complicated than it was.

The tile keeps a finite array of colour choices. The larger field repeatedly reads positions in that array. Its many appearances share a source; they do not each contain a separately authored decision.

Now try to make the mark appear only once while the rest stays unchanged. Editing the shared source makes it recur. This instrument makes it easier to change every appearance than to keep just one different.

<!-- @ -->

A repeated field can be economical, recognisable and pleasurable. It can also make a wanted exception difficult to express. Choose a reason for keeping one copy different: a direction marker, a mistake you want to retain, or a place that should resist the general pattern.

For now, the shared source cannot grant that exception. You have made a decision whose reach exceeds your hand. Which parts would you want to keep together, and where should another decision begin?

The source stores the colours you chose. It does not store why you chose them. The wish behind the work still needs someone to carry it.

In Tutorial Pattern, keep the painted cells as they are and change how a copy reads them. Later, at the hand loom, a cell will ask a thread to cross another. We will have to learn what a different kind of instruction makes.
