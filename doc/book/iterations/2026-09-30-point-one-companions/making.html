Point One — give the point a body

Point Zero gave us an instruction that runs when the scene is ready. Here we will use that moment to place a marker. The supplied scene already gives us a floor, a light and a view. We will keep those while changing what appears in front of us.

Open the Point One study project, then study.tscn. Its root is PointStudy. The three station nodes, Position, Visible and Solid, have position.gd, visible.gd and solid.gd attached. These are the three scripts we will change. Each station has its own “Point one” label.

Run the study. Above the first label there is nothing; above the next two are white spheres. Three small pink feelers move from left to right. Two cross their stations; the third stops at the solid sphere. The sweep repeats. Space pauses or resumes it; R starts the sweep again. contact_probe.gd supplies this contact test. Its movement is borrowed equipment, like the camera. We will change what it can cross.

At the first label, nothing appears

Open position.gd. This is the whole script:

extends Node3D

func _ready() -> void:
    var point_position := Vector3(0.0, 1.12, 0.0)

We know _ready() from Point Zero; -> void says the function returns no value. Here we define a Vector3, a value containing three numbers, and name it point_position. For now, this is the supplied position at our station. Point Coordinates will make its three components the subject of a lesson.

Change 1.12 to 1.7 and run. Nothing appears at this station. We have changed a value without attaching a visible body to it. The editor's unused-variable warning describes that condition. The pink feeler still crosses the empty place. It belongs to the supplied test, not to the position we have just defined. Restore 1.12.

Give the position a surface

Open visible.gd. Its complete construction runs in the same familiar function:

extends Node3D

func _ready() -> void:
    var point_position := Vector3(0.0, 1.12, 0.0)

    var radius := 0.14
    var sphere := SphereMesh.new()
    sphere.radius = radius
    sphere.height = radius * 2.0

    var marker := MeshInstance3D.new()
    marker.mesh = sphere
    marker.position = point_position
    add_child(marker)

point_position is the location. sphere describes a spherical surface; marker is the node that displays it. .new() creates each part. Assigning marker.mesh gives that node its surface; assigning marker.position places it. add_child(marker) joins it to the station. A position and the object placed there are different things.

Change 1.12 to 1.7 in this script. Now the white sphere rises. Restore the height, then change radius from 0.14 to 0.3. The sphere grows around the same centre. Its height follows because the script makes it twice the radius.

Watch the feeler cross the larger sphere. A visible surface has still given it nothing to collide with. We can change how much the sphere hides without changing what can pass through it. Restore the radius to 0.14.

Give the body a boundary

The third station looks the same. Its feeler stops. Open solid.gd:

extends Node3D

func _ready() -> void:
    var point_position := Vector3(0.0, 1.12, 0.0)

    var radius := 0.14
    var sphere := SphereMesh.new()
    sphere.radius = radius
    sphere.height = radius * 2.0

    var marker := MeshInstance3D.new()
    marker.mesh = sphere
    marker.position = point_position
    add_child(marker)

    var solid := StaticBody3D.new()
    solid.position = point_position

    var sphere_shape := SphereShape3D.new()
    sphere_shape.radius = radius
    var collider := CollisionShape3D.new()
    collider.shape = sphere_shape

    solid.add_child(collider)
    add_child(solid)

The first part constructs the same marker. The added StaticBody3D participates in collision. SphereShape3D describes its boundary, and CollisionShape3D attaches that shape to the physics body. The visible marker and this body are both placed at point_position.

Find sphere_shape.radius = radius. Change only that line to:

    sphere_shape.radius = 0.35

Run again. The white sphere stays small, but the feeler stops earlier, leaving a visible gap. The empty space resists it. Debug → Visible Collision Shapes can also reveal the larger boundary; the contact test works without that overlay.

We have chosen whether the surface we see and the boundary we encounter coincide. In the museum, it is your movement that meets this difference. The fixed-camera study supplies another moving body so we can test the same relation. Restore sphere_shape.radius = radius.

Try a different disagreement: in solid.gd, set the visible radius to 0.3 and sphere_shape.radius to 0.07. The feeler disappears into the white surface before the contact label reports “stopped”. The boundary is now inside the body we see. Return both to the matching 0.14 when you finish.

Remove the light, keep the body

In the museum, use the wall switch. The long shadows disappear while a dim fill leaves the spheres visible. The collision boundary stays where it was.

The decision in the switch is one line:

sun.visible = false

Here sun is the museum's directional light. This is a reading of that source, not a line to paste into visible.gd, which has no variable named sun. The portable study keeps its supplied light. Building the switch and its lighting rig is outside this lesson.

What we can carry forward

We can now place a marker, change its surface around the same centre and give it a different physical boundary. The large sphere at the end of the museum corridor asks us to return to those choices: it still says “Point one”. Knowing its construction gives us another way to change the encounter.

Before this pass / revised tutorial

Point One — give the point a body

Point Zero let us recognise an instruction that begins, and one that continues. Now we follow the corridor. Each label says “Point one”, but something different happens above it.

The supplied study lets us inspect the first three conditions together: an absent point, a visible sphere and a solid sphere. Its three scripts are already complete. We will change a few lines as we encounter what they do. The held point and coordinate frame have their own next chapter, Point Coordinates.

At the first label, nothing appears

There is a place to look, but no visible object. A mathematical point has a position and no extent. How can we keep its position before giving it a surface?

Open position.gd. This is the whole script:

extends Node3D

func _ready() -> void:
    var point_position := Vector3(0.0, 1.12, 0.0)

We know _ready() from Point Zero; -> void says it returns no value. Here it keeps a different kind of value: a Vector3, containing three numbers. For now, this is the supplied position at our station. Point Coordinates will make its three components the subject of a lesson.

We name that value point_position. The middle number places it above the floor. Change 1.12 to 1.7 and run. Nothing appears. We have changed a value without attaching any visible body to it. The editor's unused-variable warning describes that condition.

The other stations in the study still show their spheres. This change belongs only to the empty station. Restore 1.12 before continuing.

Further along, the point has a surface

The next label has a small white sphere above it. Its centre gives our eyes a place to find.

Open visible.gd. The same position is there, now used by a visible object:

visible_point.position = point_position

This line is already inside _ready(). visible_point names the mesh node created by the supplied script; assigning its position puts its centre at our coordinates. We have given the point a body through which it can appear.

Change 1.12 to 1.7 in this script. This time the white sphere rises. Restore the height, then change the radius:

var radius := 0.14

Try 0.3. The sphere becomes larger while its centre stays where it was. The supplied SphereMesh uses this radius and a height twice as large. We can inspect its construction later; for now, we can change a position and a surface separately.

In the museum, try crossing that surface. It is visible, but it does not stop you. Restore the radius to 0.14 before meeting the next sphere.

The next point stops us

The next white sphere looks the same. In the museum, approaching it produces a different result: a body meets resistance.

Open solid.gd. It keeps the visible construction and adds a separate physics body and collision shape. Both are placed at point_position. The boundary's size is set here:

sphere_shape.radius = radius

Change only that line to:

sphere_shape.radius = 0.35

The visible sphere stays small. Its collision boundary grows. In the desktop study, turn on Debug → Visible Collision Shapes to see the boundary; the fixed camera does not let you walk into it. In the museum, the distinction is something your movement meets.

We have chosen whether the surface we see and the boundary we encounter coincide. The full physics construction is supplied; changing this one relation already gives us something consequential to author. Restore the matching radius.

Remove the light, keep the body

Use the museum's wall switch. The long shadows disappear, while a dim fill leaves the spheres visible. The collision boundary stays where it was.

The decision in the switch is one line:

sun.visible = false

Here sun is the supplied directional light. This is a reading of the museum source, not a line to paste into visible.gd, which has no variable named sun. We borrow the switch and lighting rig for this encounter. Building shadows is not another task hidden inside learning the point.

Before leaving

The large sphere repeats the same name. Return to the radius we changed: giving a position more surface did not require a new kind of position.

We can now change appearance and resistance separately. Point Coordinates begins with a point we can hold, then reads its movement. It will prepare two positions for the line.
