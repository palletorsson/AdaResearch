# Selected Forces artifacts

15 selections; 279 passing checks. See index.html for actual museum photographs.

## Bubble Blaster — VFM_05_Launch
Grab the pink blaster. Its bubble stream makes the output velocity visible.

Existing hand prop. The drawn launch vector and animated stream return the vector lesson in a carried object.

## Catapult — VFM_05_Launch
Set the angle and launch speed, then throw a ball along the displayed arc.

The selected existing catapult is added beside the earlier physical lever experiment. Its arm swing is prescribed; the released ball carries the launch.

## Human Catapult — VFM_05_Launch
Step into the basket, choose the launch, and press FIRE from the basket. Now you are the projectile.

The launch now goes through the museum’s desktop and XR movement system so normal locomotion cannot immediately erase it. Gravity and player collisions remain active.

## Firework Burst — VFM_05_Launch
Forty-eight coloured projectiles rise into a canopy more than five metres high. Sometimes the whole fountain holds still.

NEW: ballistic motion, coloured trails, a 2.4-second automatic pause near the apex, and adjacent FREEZE / RESUME and AUTO controls. Only this exhibit’s clock stops.

## Force Mower — VFM_03_Motion
Push the mower by its handle; watch the signed work accumulate along your displacement.

W = F · d. The drawn force is a nominal value; the controller does not measure the pressure of your hand.

## Wind Room — Vectors_Act3_FieldsAndMotion
Pick up and aim the rose point to redirect the wind. Push a cube into it. Walk into it yourself.

NEW: relative-velocity drag pushes eight physical cubes and points, with real position trails. Windsocks and cylinder arrows reveal direction. WIND / CALM, TURN and RESET are beside the hand control. Visitor resistance uses a declared comparison mass.

## Force Vortex — Vectors_Act3_FieldsAndMotion
Enter the spiral, approach its core, and be thrown outward.

The selected older vortex prescribes a velocity field. Its ejection now retains momentum through the museum’s movement system. Compare it with the acceleration-based primary vortex in the same hall.

## Viscosity Layers — Vectors_Act4a_KineticForces
Drop the three identical points together. Compare their speeds through air, water and honey.

Existing numerical drag model, raised onto a museum plinth. These are three resistance coefficients, not simulated liquids.

## Centrifuge Ring — Vectors_Act4a_KineticForces
Hold the rose handle and travel around the ring. Press the held trigger to change speed. Let go into the tangent.

NEW: a grabbable ride, 2 / 4 / 6 m/s speeds, and velocity and inward-acceleration cylinders. This is a prescribed circular ride. It moves the player collision body without rotating the tracked head; release coasts and slows.

## SpringSuspension — Vectors_Act4b_Oscillation
Pull the rose point on the wheel and release. The spring and its travel limits respond.

Actual Jolt Generic6DOF joint. Added a force-transmitting hand grip and a visible coil; removed the standalone camera and environment in museum mode.

## Spring Tower — Vectors_Act4b_Oscillation
Stand beside the large coil and follow the mass as the spring length changes.

Existing cosine animation illustrating spring period. The coupled physical PointBody row remains the tutorial anchor and the hand-led experiment.

## Orbit Walk — VFM_07_Gravity
Stand beside the barycentre while the two bodies circle their shared centre.

Existing prescribed two-body diagram. Compare it with the primary mutually attracting PointBodies, whose trajectories are integrated.

## Momentum Cradle — VFM_08_Arena
Change LIFT and read the posed input and output together.

Existing static explanation of an ideal equal-mass exchange. It is deliberately presented beside a running model.

## Newton’s Cradle — VFM_08_Arena
Release one ball, two balls, or both ends. Follow how the motion travels through the row.

Existing integrated pendulum-angle model with idealized velocity exchange. It does not use the Jolt contact solver used by the nearby bowling lane.

## Launch Arc — VFM_05_Launch
Change the launch and follow what its velocity becomes under gravity.

Already in this hall, and still its tutorial anchor. The additional launchers compare different receivers of a launch.
