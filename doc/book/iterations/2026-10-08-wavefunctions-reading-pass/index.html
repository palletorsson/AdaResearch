Four mathematical distinctions

Return to these names as the encounters change. A similar outline can come from a different operation.

Operation What changes Where we will meet it
Oscillation A quantity changes periodically in time. This returning cube.
Spatial pattern A periodic rule places geometry across space; it can remain still. Sine Space and Bernini.
Propagation through coupling A disturbance affects neighbouring locations through their relations. The coupled lattice.
Composition Components combine to produce a result. Two signed waves in Beats; five weighted harmonics in Synthesis.

These are distinctions to inspect, not four separate blocks of rooms. A composition can oscillate; a moving pattern can be prescribed without neighbour coupling. Ask what the program reads to produce its next value.

Assessment decisions and proposed optional extension. Room Memory is an optional artistic investigation. The five-harmonic study completes the formal tutorial sequence. The proposed shared visual-and-sound composition is not yet implemented.

What changed

  • The opening names the three recurring questions. The middle makes the returns to spatial construction, saved events and overlapping voices explicit.
  • Effect Sound keeps phase depth and comparison at the centre. Audio encoding and envelope detail follow the experiment as supporting construction.
  • Cage keeps a small, bounded introduction to chance, with replay as its main task.
  • Sky Stairs and Beats begin with an event and ask fewer consecutive instructions. Their mathematical and material distinctions remain.
  • Room Memory becomes a clearly marked listening interlude, followed by a return to the synthesis bench.
