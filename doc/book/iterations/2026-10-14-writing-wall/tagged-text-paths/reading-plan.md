## Tutorial path

**1 → 2 → 4 → 6 → 7 → 8 → 9 → 11 → 12**

The tutorial begins with a comparison the learner can make, opens the mechanism needed to reconstruct it, and then varies source and measurement. Its recurring operation is to hold a baseline, change one condition, record the consequence, keep the required configuration and state, replay, reconstruct independently, and use the observation to propose another change.

The first pilot remains the 48-value seed bench. The paired 4 × 4 RGB grids in the current tutorial also use 48 displayed scalar draws per grid, but this numerical coincidence does not make them the same experiment. The shifted RGB procedure consumes an additional draw. Keep the bench record and the paired-grid recipe distinct.

The scope is substantial for one chapter. Use the first two stops as the initial reconstruction, with later stops as experiments that extend it. Do not require building all nine instruments before a learner can complete a research loop. The crank's adjacent register remains optional support.

## Final path

**1 → 2 → 3 → 6 → 9 → 10 → 11 → 12 → 13**

Follow a cell, a colour patch, a journey, an irregular trace, a gesture and a kept or disappearing record. The lava and Geiger encounters return to the question of what an apparatus lets us observe. The slot machine opens the next hall's question about counting. This gives the essay concrete encounters rather than a second implementation guide.

The text can propose an experiment before its result is known. It must distinguish a proposal, a visitor report and a verified result. A failed comparison or an unhelpful difference belongs in the research record. Replaying a stored outcome establishes something different from acquiring a fresh unpredictable outcome; the simulated lava and Geiger exhibits do not establish physical entropy.
