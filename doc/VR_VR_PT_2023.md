### **Ada Research**  Purpose and Aims		

With the increasing role of algorithms in our daily lives, there is a growing need to understand the binary operations of code and how it filters our world. Even if ‌digital resolution increases, the world seems to gravitate to the stereotypical. This is especially prevalent in ‌content in 3D libraries, apps, and platforms where there is a lack of diversity[^1]. The potential of algorithms is vast but the usage is predictable. Based on fixed taxonomies the reinforcement algorithm tries to keep us using the apps. The artistic research project Ada Research seeks to challenge this predictability by exploring alternative visions and expanding the understanding of 3D worlds.

Ada Research is a virtual reality (VR) world and a meta-quest into 3D computer vision. The name originates from Ada Lovelace, the world's first programmer, who wrote about the relationship between computers and generative art in 1842[^2]. The project seeks to explore ‌the digital margins and simulate patterns to help negotiate how digital algorithms create invisible fences. The project aims to enhance our understanding of how algorithms, as digital tools, shape our bodies, creativity, and subjectivity.

By creating an immersive virtual reality world that guides the player through a landscape of algorithms, the research aims to provide new insights into the hidden complexity of these mathematical constructs. This exploration describes and visualizes a world with increasingly complex control structures. With four ambitious work packages, the research starts with basic algorithmic concepts[^3] like noise and vectors and eventually progresses to topics such as generative reality and Artificial Intelligence (AI). Each package creates new game items, fantastical spaces, and game logic based on the algorithms researched. Over time the VR world gets populated with strange realities that the player is guided through. The game extends into anamorphic and exploratory spaces reflecting and negotiating the margins of the ongoing research. 

​​Central to the investigation is how fluid queer[^4] potential is coded. The world-building procedure seeks to show how complex algorithms, for instance, drag celebration and fluid simulation, inherently challenge us to find new ways to see the world. However, in the apps, applications, and platforms the binary stereotypes such as heteronormative gender beliefs and other cultural boundaries have a strong pull and content seems to gravitate towards the normative. The more algorithms increase in complexity, the more tools, platforms, and apps seem to take shortcuts. By recognizing algorithms as performative elements that modify different passages, the queer perspective can offer a critical lens through which to explore the power dynamics and social implications of algorithms.

The project draws inspiration from the history of art, particularly the utopian interplay between abstract and surrealist movements. It extends that relation by looking at the role visual thinking plays in mathematics and seeks in the margins to find hidden queer forms. Building on the margins of algorithms, the project explores the concept of entropy to hopefully find a queer ontology at its heart. 

The project is a collaboration between Konstfack, The Royal Institute of Technology Stockholm and Design Academy Eindhoven, and several individual artists. I will lead the project and produce new insights through artistic methods and exploration. To identify the margins the project manages a complex pipeline between art, science, and future industry. KTH will push the insights with deepening knowledge about systems and mathematics alongside DAE who also provides insights into the applied complexity of VR installations. This collaborative approach brings diverse perspectives and expertise to create a more comprehensive and nuanced understanding. The expected outcomes include the VR world, exhibited installations, a book, and a game wiki documenting the game and explaining the algorithms of simulations and geometry. The body of work is a fantastical world and a playground of joy, beauty, subversion, and exploration. An updated and expanded version of Paul Klee's Pedagogical Sketchbook (1953) in drag, in VR, a one-of-a-kind open-source resource to help researchers, artists, and students understand and think with algorithms to learn from, imagine with, and build on.

## State of the arts

The emergence of modernism challenged hegemonic power with utopian ideas that were often related to rethinking realism, manifesting in abstract and surreal art long before digitalization. Recent development has reopened a quest beyond the real. Driven by algorithms, increasingly realistic simulations challenge the borderland between the real and synthetic, fake and original[^5]. DeepFakes[^6] are fake videos, images, and audio voice cloning that are difficult to distinguish from the original. Artificial intelligence improves the mimicking of human creativity, and new large language models like ChatGPT write code and produce scientific writing​​. This algorithmic blur of the real opens up both threats and opportunities. On the one hand, manipulation and fake news have poisoned the political realm in the last decade \- reinforcing bias and misinformation on social platforms. On the other hand, the space beyond the real has queer and surreal potential. The procedural algorithms and virtual cameras can morph into new spatialities, metaphors, and queer cyborg subjectivity. Contemporary art that explores algorithms has a unique possibility to reflect and transform new realities. Here I want to mention four fields that make up contested possibilities where I situate the research. 

#### 1 Machine Learning

The release of the first convolutional neural network (CNN) AlexNet (2012) marked a significant advancement in machine learning (ML), with numerous subsequent contributions to the field. One notable is Leon A. Gatys' paper, A Neural Algorithm of Artistic Style[^7], (2015) Implemented in Google's Deep Dream[^8], the algorithm demonstrates the surreal and queer potential as any content can take an alternative form. Machine Learning (ML) algorithms gained recognition as strange portraits made by the French collective Obvious using GauGAN were sold at Christie's auction house for $432,500. A Study of Invisible Images (2017) by Trevor Pagleno offers insights into how ML algorithms perceive the world and how it shapes our understanding of reality. Ian Cheng's BOB (2018-2019) explores contingency and unpredictability in an AI simulation. In recent years, Deep Learning text-to-image platforms like Stable Diffusion (2022)  and Midjourny (2022), have opened up a new way to make images using text prompts, models for Music like in Riffusion (2022) and 3D objects like Goggles DreamFusion (2022). The models make content creation more democratized but also destabilize human creativity. 

#### 2 Critical Technology \- exploring the hidden

AI and ML models image generators propagate gender and race stereotypes. Here one possible scenario is endless gravitation towards more of the same. Joy Buolamwinis's work The Coded Gaze (2016) raises awareness about the need for more diverse data sets in AI technologies and opens up the discussion of bias and representation in computer models. Other artists shed light on new surveillance protocols and explore the logic behind hidden power structures. In Loophole for all (2013) and Derivatives (2019), Paolo Cirio uses hacking strategies to explore the power behind the selective use of datasets. Adam Harvey's CV Dazzle (2010) shows how to avoid facial recognition with beautiful camouflage. Hito Steyerl's How Not to Be Seen: A Fucking Didactic Educational. MOV File (2021) examines the politics of visibility. Harun Farocki analyzes the inherent power and violence of technology. "Eye/Machine" (2001) is a trilogy of films that investigates the role of weaponization of images, and as Farocki phrased it, “\[T\]hese are images that do not represent an object, but rather are part of an operation.”[^9] Recent social technology has substantial weight in defining our subjectivity, and software steers our cognitive patterns more than ever. The artistic research into computer vision and new digital realities is essential for the project because they lay the foundation for alternative queer becoming. 

#### 3 Algorithmically inspired art 

Computer vision informs the expansion of algorithmic art in various fields, including painting, sculpture, and large-scale installations. Some contemporary examples are ‍Petra Cortright and Ken Okiishi's screen-based paintings and the blobby nature of David McLeod's 3D worlds. Rachel Rossin's expanded paintings, the anamorphs of Zach Lieberman, and the hallucinatory world of Refik Anadol. How Mariko Mori places herself in a sci-fi future and how Lynn Hershman Leeson's works explore the intersection of mind and machine. Sougwen Cheang’s, fluidø (2017) explores augmented robotics. These are all works of growth and point to queer morphologies of the future. 

#### 4 Exploratory 3D 

The immersive environment of VR allows for the creation of interactive environments that challenge viewers' narratives of history, space, and time. Examples of artists who have utilized VR to explore these themes include Jacolby Satterwhite, whose 3D animations delve into queer identity and the body, and Sondra Perry, who has used VR technology to create installation and immersive environments that explore themes of race and identity.

The steady stream of new 3D content creates residue from works as a base material in Jon Rafman and Ed Atkins's work \- assemblages situated in the endless archive. Ongoing research in computer vision and the development of new algorithms, for instance, queer fluid simulation and soft bodies are repackaged in an alternative surreal form in Albert Omoss’s Undercurrents (2016) and Nikita Diakur’s Ugly (2017). 

The dimensional and queer desire has sister fronts in other disciplines. In architecture, notably Zaha Hadid’s Heydar Aliyev Center (2003). And in computer games, the dimensional drive challenges the limitations of three-dimensional space, for instance, Fez (2012), Miegakure (ongoing). An adjacent dimension drive is found in a game pop culture on youtube, bugs, glitches, Machinima, and the tech Demo Scene. The dimensional desire feeds on open-source tools and platforms such as Processing, more accessible game engines like Unity 3D and Unreal, other 3D software like Blender with all its addons, and 3D sculpting tools like Tilt Brush. These platforms lower the threshold for digital creativity and create new intricate pipelines. Central is also the ecosystem of software studies and tutorials like Daniel Shiffman, youtube channel The Coding Train, his book Nature of Code (2012), and the coding adventures of Sebastian Lague. As the line between tools and creativity blurs, Faster GPU:s, Ninites, world creators, AI objects, and disturbed render networks are a few examples of the way we create content in the future.

## Significance and scientific novelty

#### Alternative Visions undercutting the Binary Code

Ada Research aims to visualize alternatives to streamlined stereotypes, surfaces, and images. The project examines the boundaries of digital worlds and platforms by pushing ‌content, that is driven by norms and cliches, to its technological margins into unseen forms. As our digital literacy increases, we start to see the otherwise hidden operating forces of algorithms all over; Perlin Noise can explain the procedural and blobby world of art, architecture, and computer game landscapes, vectors explain painting and financial markets, and the math behind entropy explains contemporary sculpture and cryptography. 

By working in a unique, VR-integrated, and exploratory way the project is able to present vision algorithms in a new way in order to articulate and widen the spectrum of art and what we see. The project probe for ‌the queer within the technical, algorithmic, and mathematical ontological object itself. 

#### Transdisciplinary collaboration and new research tools 

In a field where multiskilled exploration is essential, the project uses a collaborative approach, involving workshops with the Royal Institute of Technology Stockholm, Design Academy Eindhoven, and several individual artists. These explorations open up new intersections between art, science, and technology, with great potential for generating new insights and perspectives into the digital world. Through computer game development the research shows we can use tools of play to advance our understanding. The world-building process can foster hands-on design, layering, exploration, implication, articulation, and documentation in the same VR space in a unique way.

## Project description  Theory 

The goal of the project is to understand how algorithms affect the way digital tools and systems work and how they control our bodies and creativity. To help with this exploration, an investigation into complexity and entropy is central.

##### Queer Embodiment 

In the age of AI, digital materiality is algorithmic and performative. To embody this, I find great inspiration in the theoretical work and life of cryptographer and mathematician Alan Turing, as I find that different areas of inquiry intermingle in his work: entropy, questions of AI, mathematical paradoxes, spatial desire, generative queer morphology, topology and geometry, and queer joy and death. 

* Turing proposed the Turing machine, which laid the theoretical foundation for modern computers in 1936\. The Turing machine was an answer to the "Entscheidungsproblem," or decision problem, which questions the fundamental law of mathematics, and furthered the undecidable foundation of mathematics.  
* Based on the mathematics of entropy, Turing is known for cracking the encrypted messages of the Nazi Enigma in 1942, which helped the Allies win World War II.  
* In 1950, he formulated the Turing test, a test of a machine's ability to exhibit intelligent behavior indistinguishable from humans. Turing's concept of the "B-type unorganized machine," which he described in 1948, was a precursor to modern neural networks.  
* In 1952, Turing posed and solved the leopards' spots problem using mathematical morphology.  
* The same year, the UK convicted Turing of gross indecency due to his homosexuality, which was still illegal. He chose chemical castration over imprisonment, but the treatment made him impotent and depressed, and he took his own life in 1954\.

##### Entropy

The key to finding cross-connections between the points above is entropy, which simply put is a measure of disorder or uncertainty in a system, and a fundamental concept in thermodynamics and information theory. In information theory, difference requires more complex encoding than sameness. As we step towards more entropy, noise \- information, difference, and diversity increase. 

Hito Steyerl's concept of the poor image explores how images can be heavily compressed and disseminated quickly. Poor images are often of lower quality and may not accurately represent the original content, but they are still influential due to the reduction of speed of communication.  
Roger Penrose, a theoretical physicist, has suggested that the universe protects us from the inherent unpredictability of singularities by hiding them behind event horizons or margins. From Turing's cryptic letter "Messages from the Unseen World”[^12] Penrose argues that human thought is too advanced to be algorithmic and that consciousness must have a quantum physics basis that can escape entropy. The world must be abstracted and the process can risk suppressing elements that do not fit. 

In AI there is a trade-off between overfitting and underfitting. If a model is overfitting, it may be too complex and have a high entropy cost, while if it is under-fitted, it does not capture the problem at hand. The prediction of computer models has a complexity cost that goes to infinity and incompatibility. Behind the world of digital discreteness, there is a theoretical black hole. On the margins of a queer quest, the project seeks new comprehension. 

##### Misframing

Even if digital resolution/complexity increases, procedural algorithms are further developed and hybridization is possible, the world seems to gravitate to the stereotypical. John Law writes about Othering (2003), everything that is not independent, prior, definite, and singular, which refers to excluding or marginalizing certain groups or individuals based on perceived differences. Othering can lead to the suppression of diversity and the reinforcement of monocultures, which have low entropy and resilience. Judith Butler's theories of Precarious Life (2001) point to how some life is less valued, and Nancy Frasers writes about misframing (2008) \- certain issues or problems are framed in a way that excludes or marginalizes certain groups.

##### Algorithmic misframing

As technology strives to become natural and invisible, it often abstracts and simplifies complex processes behind interfaces, which can lead to the algorithmic enclosure of our social systems of knowledge. This enclosure is an expression of power that threatens the core values of democracy. Evgeny Morozov describes it as "invisible barbed wire" (2013), and Shoshana Zuboff as the "Big Other" (2015). In Algorithms of oppression (2018), Safiya Umoja Noble examines how algorithms and search engines can reinforce and amplify existing inequalities and biases. Atlas of AI: Power, Politics, and the Planetary Costs of Artificial Intelligence (2021), Kate Crawford writes about the hidden costs of AI and how AI damages the environment, fuels inequality, and shifts toward undemocratic governance. In a society controlled by technology, transferring control to simplified interfaces obscures reality. 

##### Queer forms, grammar, and morphology

The research draws from Donna Haraway's ideas on "making kin, not population" (2018) and her cyberfeminist thoughts. Haraway writes: "The cyborg is a creature in a post-gender world; it has no truck with bisexuality, pre-oedipal symbiosis, unalienated labor, or other seductions to organic wholeness through a final appropriation of all powers of the parts into a higher unity." [^13] (1986) She argues that the cyborg and other margin-creatures and critters offer a way to move beyond traditional dichotomies and create new ways of understanding ourselves and our place in the world. The queer studies of Sara Ahmed, Queer Phenomenology (2006), and the affinity of Happy Objects (2010) are vital to understanding queer objects and their orientation and disorientation. John Allan describes ‌stretched, folded, or twisted space as a way to transcend power in Topology of Power (2016). In A Thousand Plateaus (1980), Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari write about rhizome, the body without organs, the war machine, and the plane of immanence \- all topological metaphors pointing to open-ended processes and systems that are fluid, dynamic, and non-hierarchical. We can relate these arguments to the 3D world and modeling, topology, and entropy \- the trade-off between several polygons (polycount) and the possibility of deforming a body smoothly.

##### Hacker ethics

The project tries to implement a visual world of care and repair that aligns with the principles of the open knowledge ecology of free software (Stallman 1983). Ivan Illich's ideas on the social materiality of technology in Tools for Conviviality (1973) ‌highlight the transparency and repairability in promoting collaboration beyond the dominance of experts. Paulo Freire's concept of reflection and action directed at transforming structures in Pedagogy of the Oppressed (1968) is also relevant to this research as a way to avoid the instrumentalization of technology. The research explores the potential of technology to empower individuals to be more autonomous drawing from the concept of commoning (Caffentzis & Federici, 2014). Feminist and queer perspectives can show ways to create a new grammar that avoids the capture of stereotypes and shows creative strategies toward a reparative morphology in societies where surveillance, algorithmic control, and xenophobia are growing. In Feminism and the Politics of Commons (2011), Silvia Federici reflects on how a new commons is evolving from the informatization and cognitivization of production. Federici describes how networked technology can enable new collective political subjects; however, the capitalist spirit seems to suffocate it. 

#### Methods

The project uses many different methods:

* Using VR technology to create an immersive environment for exploring and interacting with digital materials and landscapes.   
* Using game development tools and processes to create interactive experiences that enable documentation, articulation, implication, and presentation in the same VR space  
* Using transdisciplinary collaboration involves artists, designers, and researchers from various fields to bring together different perspectives and expertise.   
* Using ‌a hands-on exploration of the virtual environment to develop a new language and widen the spectrum of art and what we see.   
* Using artistic exploration to examine the cultural boundaries of digital worlds and platforms by pushing content driven by norms and cliches to unconventional margins and unseen forms  
* Using intersections between queer theory and generative art and game development to explore the queer within the technical, algorithmic, and mathematical ontological object itself.  
* Using documentation of the process and results of the research in various forms, including the VR world, a book, installations, and a game wiki.

### Time plan and implementation

The research consists of five work packages to be ‌distributed over the research period. All packages could build on the previous packages to create a truly queer game experience. It takes half a year to complete one iteration at a 50% activity grade estimated from experience and ongoing studies. VR game development is a complex process, from research to implementation and testing. This estimate means the project delivers two iterations per year and four major packages or releases during the first two years. In the project the last year is dedicated to optimizing the final release and making the final publication about the project. Here is the negotiable outline of the content of the four first packages.

* 1\. Basic Elements. Implementing the foundational algorithms that are essential for creating generative art, such as Randomness and Fibonacci. It could also include more concrete algorithms such as Perlin noise, Voronoi diagrams, sine curves and resonance frequencies, Sound experiments, and Particle systems to create basic elements of the game environment or objects ​​and build a solid foundation for more complex algorithmic exploration.   
* 2\. Advanced Elements. Implementing more complex algorithms that enable the creation of more sophisticated forms, such as Fractal subdivision, Flow fields, Fourier transforms, L-systems, and Cellular automata. This package also explores the algorithmic topology of desire and the creation of queer meshes and textures for instance in soft body simulation deformable objects, such as cloth, liquids, rubber, and ​​other simulation algorithms.  
* 3\. Pattern and World Building. Implementing algorithms that enable the creation of unique and dynamic patterns and game environments, such as Reaction-diffusion systems, Graph Theory, Procedural generation, Neural networks, Shading algorithms, and Evolutionary algorithms. How do we navigate an auto-generated world?  
* 4\. Advanced Techniques. Implementing cutting-edge algorithms, such as Swarm intelligence and Chaos theory and further exploring algorithmic life and AI. In this package, we also deal with non-euclidean spaces.

Each package will consist of an exploration phase, a workshop-test phase, and a documentation-release phase. As project leader, I will use Agile methods to manage communication and collaboration, receive feedback, identify any issues, and remain adaptable to change throughout each work package.

##### 1\. Exploration phase

Building on the earlier documentation, feasibility study, and the pipeline from earlier VR works I enter the experimental prototyping phase. The method I use for my research begins with an exploration and implementation of the specific algorithms. I experiment with algorithmic systems, learning about their inner workings and pushing them to their limits to understand their capabilities and limitations. I utilize programming experiences and artistic methods I have developed over the years to uncover hidden paradoxes. I keep a coding diary where I document my process and articulate the grammar. Along with notes, I also save versions of my work at each stage and involve synthesizing existing literature on the topics. 

#### 2\. Workshop-test phases

After the exploration, collaboration, feedback, and co-creation with Design Academy Eindhoven and from KTH, the Royal Institute of Technology in Stockholm will start. Benoit Baudry professor at KTH will help guide the understanding of applying the work pack to computer vision. Baudry will also help create, test, and ensure a reliable complex pipeline between and openness of the software systems. Ian Biscoe professor at Design Academy Eindhoven will help to layer possibilities in the VR world and think about spatial configuration central to the decimation of complexity in the work. I host game testing workshops where the ground is discussed on the theoretical and technical challenges with each partner.

After package two, the research expanded the workshop group. Some renowned artists I have contacted and am happy to be involved, artists Rosa Menkman, Evan Roth, Anna Uddenberg, and Geraldine Juarez. The reference groups will help guide the aim of the research in exploring how the VR work can handle the tension between different dualities, such as order and disorder, function and dysfunction, accessibility and complexity, explicit and implicit qualities, and visible and invisible logic. 

#### 3\. Documentation-release phase

Data management is at the heart of the research project. GitHub works as an iterative publication tool (with version control to create, store, and update) to ensure transparency, openness, reproducibility, and long-term data management for each stage in each work iteration of the project. The props, algorithms, and labs of the game are documented in a fandom-like game wiki. Documentation is an integral part of the project. 

#### Public display and The final publication 

Years two and three also create exhibitions in galleries or institutions, nationally and internationally. To name two possible partners, I have been in discussions with Marina Schiptjenko (gallerist, Stockholm) and Acute Art (London). There will be at least four large exhibitions. Last year, I intensified the documentation and dissemination of the project. Finally, an extensive publication collects documentation of all the environments and props with deep explanations and critical reflections. The expected outcomes include the VR world, exhibited installations, a book, and a game wiki. The book documents the grammar and gives an overview of the complete project. The body of work becomes a new tool and resource to help researchers, artists, and students ‌understand and think with queer algorithms. This work will be a one-of-a-kind open-source resource to learn from, imagine with, and build on.
