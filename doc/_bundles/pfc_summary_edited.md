# Algorithmic Bias Visualization — Summary

CriticalAlgorithms_Algorithmic_Bias_Visualization opens the Post-Foundations Crisis sequence. It makes algorithmic bias architectural before it is mathematical. The room is split unevenly by a tall wall: the left half is spacious and comfortable, the right half is cramped, and the same population is meant to be housed in both. The learner walks the division as a condition before any artifact explains it.

At the centre of the spacious half stands the bias_visualizer — a rotatable 3D point cloud of word embeddings. Analogy pairs are drawn on the cloud as lines: man-to-doctor paired with woman-to-nurse, white-to-approved paired with black-to-denied. Geometric distance in the embedding encodes statistical co-occurrence in the training data. The lines make the structure of the dataset visible; the wall next to them makes the consequence of that structure spatial.

A waypoint at the midpoint of the room directs the learner's gaze toward the wall. A void at one edge of the wall provides the exit — a gap in the floor where the classification system runs out of ground to stand on. Crossing into the cramped half is optional; the constrained geometry is felt even from outside.

Within the sequence, Bias_Visualization is the diagnosis. The map argues that every classifier has an outside, and that the people misread by these systems are not errors but the constitutive outside. SpeculativeComputation_Rhizome_Network will next offer a different topology.

# Applied Ethics — Summary

CriticalAlgorithms_Applied_Ethics is the second map in the Post-Foundations Crisis sequence. It takes the structural diagnosis from Bias_Visualization and stages it as practice: decisions being made by algorithmic systems on actual cases, with the outside of each classification kept visible.

The room is arranged as a small case-review chamber. A central table holds three case files, each a concrete claim a classifier might be asked to make: a loan-approval decision, a hiring-shortlist decision, and a content-moderation decision. Each case is represented by a short narrative card, a set of features the classifier would see, and a prediction the classifier produced. An adjacent panel shows what the classifier did not see: context, relationships, history, exception.

A second station re-runs the same cases with an intervention. A calibration slider adjusts the threshold at which the classifier commits to a positive outcome. A re-weighting panel lets the learner lift or lower the influence of specific features. The outcomes change with each adjustment, and a side display tracks the downstream consequences on the affected populations.

A small library on one wall holds annotated excerpts from practitioners — an excerpt on disparate impact, an excerpt on refusal, an excerpt on audit. The excerpts are deliberately short; the map rewards reading them against the cases rather than reading them alone.

Within the sequence, Applied_Ethics turns the diagnosis into method. Every classifier has an outside; the map asks what a responsible practice looks like once that is true.

# Paraconsistent Engineering — Summary

SpeculativeComputation_Paraconsistent_Engineering is the third map in the Post-Foundations Crisis sequence. It stages paraconsistent logic as an engineering practice rather than a philosophical curiosity. Classical logic collapses under contradiction — once a system proves both P and not-P, it proves everything. Paraconsistent systems do not.

A large spherical rig sits at the centre of the room, modelled on Pavel Florensky's sphere as a pedagogical ornament. Inside the sphere, a small database holds a set of statements, some of which contradict each other. The learner can query the database, and a live panel reads out which statements are true, which are false, which are both, and which are neither. The classical inference engine crashes under the contradictions; the paraconsistent engine continues to return useful answers on the non-conflicting parts of the knowledge base.

A second station extends the same mechanism to a tiny production pipeline. Incoming data is inconsistent in realistic ways: one sensor reports a value, another reports the opposite. The paraconsistent stage catches both, annotates the conflict, and passes the surrounding non-contradictory data downstream without loss. The stage does not resolve the contradiction; it contains it.

A wall panel names the stakes. Classical systems must be kept pristine; small contradictions destroy them. Paraconsistent systems are built for the messy world. Within the sequence, this map makes contradiction load-bearing rather than fatal.

# Situated Computation — Summary

SpeculativeComputation_Situated_Computation is the fourth map in the Post-Foundations Crisis sequence. It takes Donna Haraway's argument on situated knowledge and treats it as a design constraint. Objectivity is not the absence of a perspective; it is the careful accounting of which perspective a system is occupying.

The room is a small observation chamber with three viewing platforms, each marked with a different standpoint: a ground-level view, an aerial view, and a slanted view from a local community's vantage. A shared dataset sits at the centre of the room, and each platform renders the dataset differently. Heights and distances are the same in every view; the meaning of those heights and distances changes with the platform.

A central bench lets the learner annotate what each view can see and what each view cannot. Trees appear in the aerial view but hide figures beneath them; the community view identifies the figures but loses the spatial extent. The annotations accumulate, and a running panel reads out the combined account as partial, located, and accountable rather than as universal.

A second station stages the same principle on a small classifier. The learner chooses a standpoint before training; the resulting model labels the dataset from that standpoint, and the panel makes the standpoint visible in the model's outputs. Within the sequence, Situated_Computation replaces the view from nowhere with an explicit account of where the view is coming from.

# Collective Knowledge — Summary

SpeculativeComputation_Collective_Knowledge is the fifth map in the Post-Foundations Crisis sequence. Gödel's incompleteness proved that individual formal systems cannot contain themselves; this map asks what happens when several such systems share a room and talk to each other.

The space is arranged as a small commons. Four independent reasoning agents sit at four stations, each running a different formal system — classical first-order logic at one, paraconsistent logic at another, a probabilistic inference engine at a third, a constraint solver at the fourth. Each agent is individually incomplete, and a side panel displays the kinds of statement each cannot prove.

A central mediation station collects their outputs. When the four agents are asked the same question, they return different answers shaped by their respective systems. The mediator does not pick a winner; it records the disagreements as data and reports the set of claims the four agents can collectively support. A second display tracks the claims no system can reach alone but the commons can.

A wall panel sets out the thesis. No single system is complete. A commons might be — or, more honestly, a commons might cover more than any single system and remain incomplete in a different and more tractable way. Within the sequence, Collective_Knowledge argues for reasoning infrastructures that are plural by design rather than plural by accident.

# PostCrisis Synthesis — Summary

PostCrisis_Synthesis is the final map in the Post-Foundations Crisis sequence and the closing gesture of the arc that began with the Foundations Crisis and passed through the QFEP Laboratory. It gathers the sequence's questions into one room and asks the learner to carry the answer forward.

The room is quiet. Small displays around the walls show the preceding maps in miniature: the divided room of Bias_Visualization, the case table of Applied_Ethics, the Florensky sphere of Paraconsistent_Engineering, the three platforms of Situated_Computation, the four-agent commons of Collective_Knowledge, and the rhizomatic cave and the modest laboratory that sit on either side of them. Each miniature is clickable; reading the label on any of them recaps what the map taught.

A central exhibit recasts the arc in a single sentence. The Foundations Crisis was not a failure but the moment the discipline admitted its own edges. What comes after is not the absence of formal systems but their careful, partial, accountable use. The sentence is placed on a plinth without ornament, so the room reads as catalogue rather than as monument.

A small exit panel points forward. Ada Research does not end at Post-Foundations Crisis; the curriculum recurs, and the tools the sequence has handed the learner — bias awareness, paraconsistency, situated standpoints, collective reasoning, rhizomatic connection, humble formalism — are the kit the learner carries into whatever they build next. Within the sequence, Synthesis is the close. Within the curriculum, it is a handoff.
