# Artifact: Noise Layers

> h(x,z) = Σᵢ amplitudeᵢ × noiseᵢ(frequencyᵢ × x, frequencyᵢ × z) — three independent FastNoiseLite layers summed into one terrain surface

## Context

**Category:** procedural | **Complexity:** advanced
**Tags:** animation, fractal, procedural | **Themes:** probability, procedural, randomness

Demonstrates octave layering (fractal Brownian motion) by stacking multiple noise frequencies. Shows how combining scales creates natural complexity.

*landscapes are not designed — they are superpositions of independent noise processes operating at different scales, and the earth's surface is an accidental sum*

## Design

- **Visual:** material: standard, animation: transform
- **Scale:** [220, 83, 200] (xlarge)
- **Interaction:** to stand on a landscape whose complexity you can feel was layered — mountains built from three different kinds of forgetting
- **Critical Parameter:** low_freq_amplitude — the macro landscape shape; dwarfs the other layers and defines whether the world feels like plains or alps
- **What Emerges:** the fractal nature of natural terrain becomes a walked-through sensation rather than an abstract formula — the learner finds that more octaves means more detail at every scale simultaneously
- **Triggers:** enabling erosion_simulation changes how slopes are smoothed — the terrain forgets its sharpest edges; switching between FRACTAL_FBM and FRACTAL_RIDGED on med_freq creates ridges vs rolling hills
