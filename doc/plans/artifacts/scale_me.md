# Artifact: scale_me

> world.scale *= scale_amount on pickup — the player's reference frame grows, making everything feel tiny

## Context

**Category:** unknown | **Complexity:** beginner
**Tags:** transformation, scale, embodiment, world_scale, perspective, interaction, algorithm | **Themes:** algorithms, misc

A grab sphere that rescales the world around the player. The geometry keeps its proportions, but your reference body changes, so scale becomes something felt rather than merely calculated. That shift makes the same room read as intimate, toy-like, or monumental without changing its form. It is scale as embodiment.

*scale is relational — 100× larger means nothing without a reference body to measure against*

## Design

- **Visual:** material: standard, animation: static
- **Scale:** [1.0, 1.0, 1.0] (compact)
- **Interaction:** learner experiences scale change as embodied perspective shift — not a number, a felt transformation
- **Critical Parameter:** scale_amount — at 100× the world becomes monumental; lower values are subtle
- **What Emerges:** the phenomenological difference between knowing a scale ratio and experiencing it as body-relative space
- **Triggers:** picking up the grab sphere — world scales up immediately; timer fires after scale_duration to revert
