# Artifact: Galton Board

> Binomial(n, 0.5) → N(n/2, n/4) as n → ∞ — the Central Limit Theorem

## Context

**Category:** randomness | **Complexity:** intermediate
**Tags:** galton, binomial, central_limit_theorem, gaussian, bell_curve, probability, physics, interactive | **Themes:** probability, randomness

Balls drop through a triangular peg array. Each peg deflects left or right with equal probability. After many rows, the bin counts form a binomial distribution converging to a Gaussian bell curve. VR buttons: DROP, AUTO, RESET, SPEED.

*The bell curve is not imposed — it is the inevitable shape of accumulated independence.*

## Design

- **Visual:** material: unlit, animation: physics
- **Interaction:** watch balls cascade through pegs and see the bell curve assemble itself from binary choices
- **Critical Parameter:** peg_rows — more rows means tighter Gaussian convergence; 8 rows gives 9 bins
- **What Emerges:** the bell curve overlay converges to match the histogram — theory becomes visible fact
- **Triggers:** auto_drop spawns balls at balls_per_second rate; peg collisions create the L/R branching
