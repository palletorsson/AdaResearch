# Artifact: Verlet Integration

> x(t+dt) = 2x(t) - x(t-dt) + a(t)*dt². No velocity. Positions remember.

## Context

**Category:** physics | **Complexity:** intermediate
**Tags:** verlet, integration, position_based, stable, time_reversible, molecular_dynamics, game_physics | **Themes:** numerical_methods, verlet, position_based, stability

Position-based Verlet integration — the workhorse of game physics and molecular dynamics. Instead of tracking velocity explicitly, it infers motion from the difference between current and previous positions. This makes it naturally stable, time-reversible, and beautifully simple: x_new = 2x_current - x_old + a·dt².

*The most stable integration method is the one that forgot to track the thing everyone assumed was essential.*

## Design

- **Visual:** material: unlit, animation: vertex
- **Interaction:** To show that forgetting velocity makes physics better. To make the learner SEE energy conservation.
- **Critical Parameter:** dt — at small dt, Euler and Verlet look the same. At large dt, Euler escapes, Verlet stays.
- **What Emerges:** Energy conservation from symplecticity. Stability from time-reversibility. Orbits from F=ma without velocity.
- **Triggers:** dt ramp → Euler spirals outward while Verlet precesses, ghost line → shows the position-differencing trick
