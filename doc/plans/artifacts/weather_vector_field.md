# Artifact: Weather Vector Field

> R = A + B; F_rain = gravity + wind. Vector addition drives both the field and the particles. Superposition made meteorological.

## Context

**Category:** vectors | **Complexity:** intermediate
**Tags:** vector_addition, weather, wind, force_superposition, vector_field, rain, pressure, interactive | **Themes:** linear_algebra, math, vectors

Applied vector addition as weather — two grabbable wind vectors combine in real-time. Drag arrow endpoints to feel how wind directions sum. Rain particles fall under gravity + wind, their diagonal paths demonstrating force superposition. Pressure zones add local variation to the field.

*Wind is a vector field. Rain is a particle advected through two fields at once. Weather is superposition you can stand in.*

## Design

- **Visual:** material: vertex_color, animation: particle_lifecycle
- **Interaction:** To let the learner drag two wind arrows and watch rain fall sideways — making force superposition a weather event you stand inside.
- **Critical Parameter:** gravity_strength — it competes with wind. High gravity = rain falls nearly vertical; low gravity = rain flies nearly horizontal. The ratio is the angle.
- **What Emerges:** Rain streaking diagonally as the decomposition display shows wind + gravity = trajectory. High/low pressure zones bending the uniform field into curves.
- **Triggers:** Drag wind handles → 36 grid arrows recolor and reorient, rain particles change trajectory, mode button → trade/cross/opposing/updraft presets, pressure slider → zones drift and distort the field
