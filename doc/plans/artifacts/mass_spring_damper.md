# Artifact: Mass-Spring-Damper

> F = -kx - cv. Spring restores, damper resists. Three regimes: underdamped (oscillates), critically damped (fastest return), overdamped (sluggish return).

## Context

**Category:** physics | **Complexity:** beginner
**Tags:** spring, damper, mass, hookes_law, oscillation, underdamped, critical_damping, overdamped, harmonic_oscillator | **Themes:** springs, damping, oscillation, hookes_law

The canonical second-order system: a mass on a spring with viscous damping. Hooke's law (F = -kx) provides the restoring force, damping (F = -cv) dissipates energy. Explore underdamped oscillation, critical damping, and overdamped decay by tweaking spring stiffness k and damping coefficient c. The foundation of every vibration analysis ever performed.

*Every vibrating system is a mass-spring-damper in disguise. The damping ratio is the system's temperament.*

## Design

- **Visual:** material: standard, animation: physics
- **Scale:** [6, 6, 3] (xlarge)
- **Interaction:** To pull three masses down in VR and release — watching one ring like a bell, one snap back efficiently, one creep home reluctantly. Damping ratio as personality.
- **Critical Parameter:** damping coefficient d (0.5, 12.0, 40.0). It alone determines the character: d < d_critical = ringing, d = d_critical = optimal, d > d_critical = sluggish.
- **What Emerges:** The underdamped mass overshooting and ringing. The critically damped mass returning fastest without overshoot. The overdamped mass returning slowest. Three personalities from one equation.
- **Triggers:** VR grab mass → pull down against spring, release → oscillation begins, each mass responds differently. R → reset all to rest. Spring lines update in real time.
