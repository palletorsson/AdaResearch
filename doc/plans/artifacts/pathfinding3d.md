# Artifact: pathfinding3d

> A* on a 3D voxel grid — f(n) = g(n) + h(n), with pluggable heuristics (Manhattan, Euclidean, Chebyshev) and movement models (ground, flying, swimming, climbing)

## Context

**Category:** unknown | **Complexity:** intermediate
**Tags:** graph_theory, pathfinding, a_star, voxel_grid, heuristics, navigation, algorithm | **Themes:** algorithms, misc

Search a voxel maze as if every cube were a graph vertex. The visualization steps A*, Dijkstra, JPS, or flow-field variants through open and closed sets, letting the heuristic shape the visible wavefront. Different heuristics bias the frontier differently, so the artifact makes prediction and optimality visible in space. It turns pathfinding into graph search you can inspect instead of a black box.

*pathfinding does not move through space — it moves through a graph that happens to be embedded in space, and the heuristic is the algorithm's guess about geometry*

## Design

- **Visual:** material: standard, animation: transform
- **Scale:** [10.0, 15.0, 10.0] (xlarge)
- **Interaction:** to carve a glowing path through a 3D maze of voxels — watching the open set expand like a wavefront and the path crystallize when goal is reached
- **Critical Parameter:** heuristic_weight — at 1.0 it is standard A*, above 1.0 it becomes greedy (faster but suboptimal), at 0.0 it degenerates to Dijkstra
- **What Emerges:** the search wavefront's shape reveals the heuristic's bias — Euclidean creates spherical expansion, Manhattan creates diamond-shaped expansion, Chebyshev creates cubic expansion
- **Triggers:** animated search mode steps through A* one node at a time, coloring open (yellow) and closed (blue) voxels; algorithm_type switches between A*, Dijkstra, JPS, and flow field
