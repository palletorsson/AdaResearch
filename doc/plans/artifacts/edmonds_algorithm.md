# Artifact: Edmonds' Algorithm

> greedy maximum matching — iterate edges in order; if both endpoints are unmatched, add the edge to the matching; matched vertices turn green, matching edges turn red

## Context

**Category:** unknown | **Complexity:** intermediate
**Tags:** graph_theory, matching, pairing, greedy_algorithm, edge_order, optimization, algorithm | **Themes:** algorithms, misc

Stage the pairing problem that maximum matching tries to solve. The current visualization greedily accepts or skips edges one by one, so you can see how a graph allows some partnerships and strands others. Because the result depends on edge order, the artifact also makes visible why matching needs richer augmenting-path logic than a naive pass. It closes the sequence by turning graph symmetry into the question of who can be paired with whom.

*matching is the mathematics of pairing — who connects with whom, who remains alone, and whether the graph's structure permits everyone to find a partner*

## Design

- **Visual:** material: standard, animation: transform
- **Scale:** [7.0, 6.0, 7.0] (xlarge)
- **Interaction:** to watch pairs form across a graph — each matched edge is a partnership, and the unmatched vertices that remain are the ones the graph's structure could not pair
- **Critical Parameter:** edge_density — sparse graphs leave many vertices unmatched; dense graphs approach perfect matching; the gap reveals structural constraints on pairing
- **What Emerges:** the greedy order matters — different edge orderings produce different matchings, and the greedy result is not always maximum; the gap between greedy and optimal reveals augmenting path opportunities
- **Triggers:** step-by-step animation highlights each edge in yellow before accepting (green) or skipping it; matching_size counter tracks progress; ui_accept toggles start/stop
