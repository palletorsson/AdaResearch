# One Artifact Per Artist

> A design set. Each entry is a buildable Node3D artifact whose MEANING IS ITS
> APPEARANCE — not an illustration of an idea but an object that can only be
> understood by looking at it and putting your hands on it. Each extends a family
> already in `algorithms/` so it thickens the curriculum instead of sitting beside it.
>
> Written 2026-07-26 out of the poetics-of-space thread: in a prefab world an object
> cannot hold your memory, so it must hold its **lineage, its limits and its
> antithesis** instead. Every artifact below is built on that.
>
> Format per entry: **the object** (what you see) · **your hands** (what you do) ·
> **the channel** (what encodes the finding — never decoration) · **truth** (the
> @identity truth line) · **extends** (existing repo family).

---

## 1 · `adjacency_shelf` — Haim Steinbach

**The object.** A cantilevered wedge shelf in two-tone laminate, the triangular
profile and hard formica edge exact. Three slots. Nothing on it is made by us; every
occupant is a stock artifact pulled from the registry. The shelf is immaculate and
slightly too good for what it holds.

**Your hands.** Grab any artifact in the map and set it in a slot. Rearrange freely.

**The channel.** The **shelf itself** answers. It reads its three occupants' distance
in the artifact atlas (`tools/artifact_embed.py`) and re-laminates: a tight semantic
cluster fuses the two tones into one continuous surface; an incoherent triptych
splits the laminate along the wedge and the seam runs visibly between them. Display
furniture editorialising on its contents — the shelf is never neutral.

**Truth.** *Arrangement is an argument. The shelf is not furniture holding objects; it
is the sentence the objects have been conscripted into.*

**Extends.** The interactables layer as placement grammar · `/atlas`.

## 2 · `stock_stratum` — Timur Si-Qin

**The object.** A geological core sample standing in a glass tube, lit from below.
The strata are not rock. Each horizon is a compressed bed of one of the project's own
most-placed prefabs — a 40 cm band of `request_note`, a thinner seam of
`fire_hose_box`, a hairline of some artifact placed twice. Corporate-render chrome
caps the tube. It looks like a mineral exhibit and it is a frequency table.

**Your hands.** Draw the core out of the floor. The strata separate along their
bedding planes; each slab can be lifted and read.

**The channel.** **Bed thickness = placement count**, straight from `map_data.json`.
The most-placed artifact is literally the bedrock everything else was deposited on.

**Truth.** *A world's defaults are its bedrock. What you place most becomes the ground
everything stands on, and no one ever decided it.*

**Extends.** The placement census behind `artifact_dna_agenda.json`.

## 3 · `default_body_bay` — Sondra Perry

**The object.** A changing bay walled in chroma-key blue — the flat, unbearable
digital blue that means *nothing has been decided here yet*. In the middle stands a
figure in the engine's own default state: untextured grey, T-pose, capsule collider
visible. A brass dial on the rail.

**Your hands.** Turn the dial. Each click adds one **default** — default skin tone,
default proportion set, default rig, default idle animation — and each arriving
default is stencilled with its provenance: which asset library, which convention,
which year.

**The channel.** The **blue recedes** as defaults accumulate. Neutrality is consumed
in front of you and revealed as a stack of unmarked decisions. Run the dial back and
the blue returns — the only honest surface in the room.

**Truth.** *There is no default body. There is a body someone chose, and then stopped
labelling.*

**Extends.** `algorithms/nature_system/` morphology · the grant's bias thesis.

## 4 · `inheritance_loom` — Jacolby Satterwhite

**The object.** A brass loom-frame, waist height, strung with nothing. Above it, empty
air waiting for a mark.

**Your hands.** Draw a line in the air with the controller. That drawn line is taken
as a **genome**, not a picture: it is parsed into an axiom and propagated into a
family of objects that bud, orbit and dance around the frame in a slow procession.

**The channel.** Your original stroke stays **visible as a bright thread running
through every descendant** — inside the vase, along the spine of the creature, around
the arch. Grab any descendant and it dims to show only the segment of your line it
metabolised. The ancestor is never hidden and never merely copied.

**Truth.** *Inheritance is not resemblance. The ancestor is not reproduced in the
child, it is metabolised — and it is still in there.*

**Extends.** `algorithms/lsystems/` · `player_trace` · VRBrush.

## 5 · `surrogate_lectern` — Ed Atkins

**The object.** A lectern with a head on it, rendered at the highest fidelity this
project can afford: pores, wet eyes, a swallow reflex. It watches you with real
attention.

**Your hands.** Nothing. You move; it mirrors you.

**The channel.** **Latency.** It mirrors you 350 ms late, always, and the delay never
varies or apologises. The higher the fidelity the worse the lateness reads. You will
try to catch it out, and it will keep almost being you.

**Truth.** *The surrogate is not insufficiently real. It is exactly as real as the
delay between you and it.*

**Extends.** The `time / physics` seam in `CONSERVATION_OF_THE_IRREDUCIBLE.md` —
and the one artifact in this set that can only be judged by a **temporal** probe,
never a still.

## 6 · `multiple_ring` — Katharina Fritsch

**The object.** Identical figures in a perfect ring, all facing inward, all one matte
ultramarine so even it kills the light. No highlights, no variation, no maker's hand.
The evenness is the horror.

**Your hands.** Take one out.

**The channel.** **The ring closes.** Instantly, smoothly, without drama, the
remaining members redistribute to a perfect circle. You cannot make it uneven; you
cannot leave a gap; the figure you removed is already not missed. Add one and it
absorbs. Past a threshold count the ring stops reading as *many objects* and becomes
*one object* — and you can feel the exact figure where that happens.

**Truth.** *A multiple is not many of one thing. Past a certain count it becomes one
thing, and the member cannot resign.*

**Extends.** QFEP **F** (order) · `swarmintelligence`.

## 7 · `sturtevant_bench` — Sturtevant

**The object.** Two identical plinths, side by side, lit identically. On the left, an
artifact from the registry, running its real code. On the right, **the same artifact
rebuilt from its own `@identity` description alone** — from the prose that claims what
it is, with no access to its implementation. They are recognisably the same object.
They are not the same object.

**Your hands.** Walk between them. Swap which plinth holds which — the labels stay put.

**The channel.** **Divergence made spatial.** Every place the documentation was
vaguer than the code, the right-hand twin is smoother, emptier, more confident. Every
place the code drifted from its claim, the right-hand twin is *more correct than the
original*. The gap between the two is the artifact.

**Truth.** *The copy is not the failure of the original. It is the test of whether the
original was ever fully written down.*

**Extends.** The `@identity` blocks on 817 artifacts — and today's finding that a
claim and its code routinely disagree.

## 8 · `poor_image_gate` — Hito Steyerl

**The object.** A doorway in a wall, with a distinct atmosphere on each side. On the
near side everything is gallery-grade: crisp, heavy, expensively lit, and framed. On
the far side the world is compressed to mush — banded gradients, blocky normals,
texture the size of a thumbnail.

**Your hands.** Walk through, either way.

**The channel.** **Resolution is coupled to mobility, inversely.** On the high-res
side you are slow, and the frame will not let you leave. On the poor side you are
fast, you can travel the whole map, and you can go through walls that the crisp world
enforces. The image degrades exactly as your freedom increases.

**Truth.** *Resolution is not quality. It is a decision about who is allowed to
travel.*

**Extends.** The `colour` and `the horizon` seams · the grant's §2 (critical
technology).

## 9 · `unfinishable_terrarium` — Ian Cheng

**The object.** A sealed vivarium on a steel stand, with a brass **hour meter** bolted
to the frame like a machine that has been running in a basement for years. Inside, an
ecology mid-episode. You have always arrived in the middle.

**Your hands.** You can intervene — feed, remove, block. There is no reset. There is
no button under the case. There never was one.

**The channel.** **Persistence, made visible as wear.** The simulation runs to disk
and continues between sessions whether or not anyone is in the map. The hour meter
counts real elapsed time and the glass accumulates an etched log of interventions with
dates. Damage you do is still there next week, under your name.

**Truth.** *A world you can restart is a demonstration. A world you cannot is a
responsibility.*

**Extends.** `algorithms/nature_system/` · `EcosystemManager` · `soft_stages.json`.

## 10 · `operational_eye` — Harun Farocki

**The object.** A bank of small monitors on a service rack, none of them showing the
world as you see it. Pathfinder cost fields. The culling frustum. The AABB the capture
camera frames by. Collision hulls. The LOD switch. Occlusion. All of it in the flat,
false-colour, unbeautiful register of machine vision.

**Your hands.** Nothing to press. But one screen, second from the left, shows **you** —
as the system holds you: a capsule, a velocity vector, a grounded flag.

**The channel.** Every image on the rack is a **real render of live engine state**,
never an illustration of one. They are operational images: made to run the world, not
to be looked at. Seeing them is trespass.

**Truth.** *Most images in this world were never made for you. They are how it runs,
and you were never the audience.*

**Extends.** `map_pathfinder.py` · `gaze_ride.py` · the capture rig's own AABB framing.

## Notes on the set

Nine of the ten put the finding in a channel the viewer reads **first** — thickness,
colour, count, latency, resolution, persistence — because today's audit found the
dominant failure mode in this corpus is the opposite: colour spent on an inert
variable while the finding hides somewhere nobody looks.

Three are **anti-artifacts** in the sense of the seams doc — they show the machine's
limit rather than its success: `poor_image_gate` (resolution), `surrogate_lectern`
(time), `stock_stratum` (the default as bedrock). Those are the ones closest to the
project's actual target.

`sturtevant_bench` is the one that could be built tomorrow and would immediately
generate work: it turns the gap between an artifact's claim and its code into an
object, and this repo has 817 such claims.

Deliberately excluded: Koons. Pop that elevates the commodity is not this. These
objects are dissected, and they keep their seams showing.
