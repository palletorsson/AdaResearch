## Sequence-specific notes

### forces (24 maps)

All maps are solid (no stubs). 21 blurbs written. Two clear sections: Vectors (maps 1-11) and Forces (maps 12-24). The sequence is long enough to be two chapters.

**Writing approach:** Vectors section teaches direction and magnitude, building to fields. Forces section applies vectors as physical dynamics. The critical arc could move from "what is direction?" (political framing of vectors as choice) to "what is force?" (who applies it, who receives it).

**Gaps:** Vectors_1, Vectors_5, Vectors_6 have generic names — check if their content is distinct enough to justify separate maps or if they should be consolidated.

### lsystems (7 maps)

**Stub:** LSystems_AnimatedTree has no meaningful artifacts. Needs an L-system artifact before text can be written properly.

**Flow:** Tree → AnimatedTree → ContextSensitive → ForestCompetition → CFG → CityGenerator → Hilbert3D. The progression from biological (trees) to formal (grammars) to spatial (cities, space-filling) is good. The animated tree is positioned as the second map but has no artifact — it should either get a proper animated L-system visualization or be removed/reordered.

**Suggestion:** AnimatedTree needs an `animated_lsystem_tree` artifact that shows step-by-step string rewriting with visual turtle interpretation. Without this, the map is a gap in the learning arc.

### foundationscrisis (7 maps)

All maps are solid with real artifacts. Zero text exists. This is a high-priority writing target because it contains the curriculum's philosophical climax: Godel, Russell, Brouwer, Florensky.

**Flow:** Euclid_Parallel → NonEuclidean → Russell_Paradox → Godel → Brouwer → Florensky → Synthesis. The arc moves from "geometry's hidden assumption" (parallel postulate) through "logic's self-destruction" (paradox, incompleteness) to "alternatives" (intuitionism, paraconsistency, synthesis).

**Critical arc suggestion:** Start from "what seems solid" (Euclidean geometry) and progressively dissolve certainty. Each map removes one floor the previous stood on. The synthesis map should articulate why incompleteness is not failure but the constitutive condition of life at the edge. Direct connection to QFEP: the formula cannot close because any formal system that tries will encounter its own Godel sentence.

### softbodies (9 maps)

8 maps are bare, 1 full (Reaction_Diffusion). The sequence moves from spring-mass deformation through cloth physics to affect theory and morphogenesis.

**Flow concern:** SoftBodies_Obsticals and SoftBodies_Obsticals_Part2 may overlap. Check if Part2 adds enough new content to justify a separate map, or if they should merge.

**Writing approach:** Technical arc: point masses → springs → constraints → cloth → deformable interaction → affect as material property → Turing patterns → morphogenesis. Critical arc: "what does softness mean?" — softness as vulnerability, as adaptability, as refusal of rigid form. Affect theory (Massumi, Ahmed) connects soft bodies to queer embodiment.

### qfeplaboratory (8 maps)

All 8 maps have blurbs. This is the climactic sequence where the QFEP formula itself becomes interactive. No other text files exist.

**Writing approach:** Each map should embody one term of QFE = F - λE(S) + φΔE(S,t). The technical.md files should include actual interactive manipulation of λ and φ. The critical.md files should explain why this formula matters — not as physics but as a way of thinking about identity, change, and the edge between order and chaos.

### postfoundationscrisis (3 maps)

Mostly bare. Only 1 critical.md exists. This sequence follows foundationscrisis and applies the limits of formalization to contemporary issues.

**Gap:** Only 3 maps feels thin for a synthesis phase. Consider whether this sequence needs additional maps covering: algorithmic bias as formal incompleteness, Deleuze's rhizome as post-foundational structure, or molecular design as material practice beyond formalism.
