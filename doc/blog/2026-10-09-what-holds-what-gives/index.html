Soft Bodies · 9 October 2026

What holds, what gives

Making the Soft Bodies chapter through attachments, moving supports, recorded shapes and a ghost student's new arrangement

Remove the frame and the cloth keeps hanging. Its upper edge stays where it was. Everything below can still move. The apparent support has disappeared; the attachment survives it.

This became one of the useful encounters in our Soft Bodies chapter. The visible frame and the rule holding the cloth had been made separately. Changing one exposed the other. A familiar question about an object became a question about its arrangement: when we call something soft, how much belongs to the material, and how much to the conditions under which we meet it?

We worked through nine halls inside the existing Endless Museum. The player, lighting, floor and means of holding things were already there. So were many of the artifacts. Our task was to find a useful teaching route through that collection while giving the surrounding encounters enough space to remain interesting on their own.

The opening lessons found a clear continuity. A cube yields around a selected point. An open sheet extends that selection to an edge. The carousel moves the attachment itself. The pressure room sends a periodic command into closed surfaces and waits for their response. Earlier chapters had taught us to turn or move an object. Here a commanded movement reaches another body through a relation.

Stopping becomes a more complicated action. Stop the carousel's hub and its hanging bodies can keep moving. Hold a pressure value and a wall can continue to settle. Restore the settings and the current folds remain. The reflective text found a sentence for this: the numbers return sooner than the body.

The familiar black point helped the tutorial stay continuous. Its class supplies a sphere, a collision shape and the behaviour needed to hold it. We could reuse it as a contact probe, a trigger for another trial or a control for keeping a surface. The next lesson could name what its action now did and spend its attention there. Continuity needed both a recognisable object and an exact account of its changing responsibility.

The rooms changed alongside the writing. A curtain initially extended beyond its doorway and hung below the crossbar. Correcting that fit led to a procession of five gates: a full upper edge, two corners, alternating single corners, uneven lengths and a central opening. Coral, rose, violet, blue and mint made the variations easy to find. What began as a misplaced strip became another way to compare boundaries by walking through them.

That expansion did not require five new tutorials. We kept the main lesson with one sheet and its selected points. The wider installation could offer repetition, pleasure and another bodily scale. The optional membrane stayed available too, with four anchors and a load that asks more of the middle. An artifact can matter without becoming another compulsory step.

The chapter's middle then changed what we kept from an encounter. In the falling-body comparison, two matching constructions receive different damping values. At a chosen time, the program retains a reading while the bodies continue moving. The next lesson keeps a whole surface: positions and triangle connections become a separate mesh beside the continuing simulation.

This was the strongest passage in the writing. A moment becomes something we can walk around. Its stillness gives us time to look, while also concealing the timing of the decision that selected it. Keeping the step number beside the shape makes that decision available again. The object has a history, but its appearance cannot supply every part of it.

There is another instrument nearby. It can colour a deforming surface according to a measurement. With feedback enabled, its reading also changes the pressure applied to the body. The measurement has become part of the mechanism. Here the volume reading comes from a box enclosing the vertices. A fold can change the body without changing that box very much. The experiment opens a precise question: what happens when we ask a body to preserve a number that only partly describes what we care about?

We left that question in the reflective track and the museum encounter. Sampling's tutorial still follows the smaller operation of copying a surface. This separation helps both texts. The tutorial must expose the construction and its dependencies. The reflection can stay with what an operation makes visible, what it leaves out and what we begin to expect from it.

In Affect, the saved geometry returns as a comparison. One copy stays neutral; another turns or changes colour. The surrounding specimens invite associations with shells, spines and vulnerable bodies. Bring your hand to one and it passes through the visible skin. The jelly had moved away from contact. These forms can suggest danger or delicacy without providing either physical relationship. Learning how they were made does not settle what it feels like to stand beside them.

The last two halls widen the inquiry again. Reaction–diffusion returns to the earlier cellular field and opens its local conversion, supply and removal. Morphogenesis begins with a different three-dimensional field and lets us separate the settings that a single control had joined. The distinction needed to be visible in the text. We carry a method of comparison between those halls; the concentrations do not silently turn into the next surface.

The external review helped name the larger connection: a form might persist through its physical relations, through a record we retain, through continuing exchanges, or through a rule we can run again. That gives the chapter a shared question while leaving its different mechanisms intact. The revised departures now follow that question through Affect, Reaction and Morphogenesis. Each tutorial also opens with three short lines: what carries forward, what the student writes here, and what is supplied. Cloth offers the tested three-attachment composition as an optional departure from its main exercise. These changes clarify the writing; the printed learner code remains the version already tested.

The imagined student made us check those promises. After revising the lesson guides and departures, we extracted the 28 printed programs and ran all nine lessons again in Godot. All 263 executable checks passed in fresh processes, without using cached results. They covered construction, particular interactions, the stated independent edits and deliberately faulty variants where provided. Five continuity checks confirmed that the supposedly inherited files really matched. No learner-code repair was needed. These were author-operated rehearsals: they establish specific behaviours, while an unfamiliar student’s understanding remains a question for another reader.

One correction concerned what it meant to carry a shape. Affect reconstructs the known step-40 specimen from the same recipe. It does not automatically retrieve the visitor's latest capture from Sampling. The revised text says so. Another correction made the membrane an optional return within Cloth, and the inherited files in Affect easier to set aside while reading the new operation. We kept the complete programs available.

The review also asked whether a student could formulate a material question of their own. We took that invitation into a new ghost-student exercise: give the same sheet a different set of attachments. One copy keeps its two upper corners. The other holds three places—upper left, lower left and near the middle of the right edge. The mesh and material settings remain the same. Its former upper-right attachment is now free.

Before running, we predicted that the three selected points would stay fixed while the released corner moved. The first native museum run confirmed both. After the editorial revision, we repeated the experiment: the corner moved about 0.805 metres over 120 awaited physics callbacks, compared with 0.813 metres before. The greatest attachment drift stayed below one millimetre. All 21 checks passed again, including the carried point’s grip, trigger and rebuild. The exact fold was left open. The exercise used a known construction to ask a new question.

The checker itself needed a correction. After the body entered the world, Godot exposed its mesh as an ArrayMesh. Our checker tried to read it through a PrimitiveMesh method and stopped early. The process still printed a passing subtotal. We rejected that run, preserved it, corrected the mesh query and required an explicit completion check. The attachment rule needed no repair. The images gave us a separate reason to change the caption's contrast: correct geometry still needs a readable setting.

The fresh run kept that stricter completion check. A complete set of expected assertions had to pass, and the logs had to be free of script errors. We also preserved the earlier receipts instead of replacing them with a new green result. The two observations can remain beside each other. What the lesson makes available is a way to choose and test an attachment, with room for the resulting shape to surprise us.

Working on this chapter has meant moving between a room, a small construction, a paragraph and a claim that can be tested. Each has corrected another. An image exposed an ill-fitting curtain. A short sentence revealed an assumed transfer that the program did not perform. An external reader identified where continuity needed a larger question. A new arrangement asked whether the earlier lesson had made an operation available for use.

The chapter now gives us several things to carry onward: a point that can intervene, a boundary we can choose, a record we can preserve, and a way to inspect what a control gathers into one gesture. Around that route, the museum still has room for a body to do something we have not yet found the right words for.
