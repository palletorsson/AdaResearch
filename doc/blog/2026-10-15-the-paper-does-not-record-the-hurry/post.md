“The paper does not record the hurry.”

That sentence arrived in a literary trial about five beads on five rods. The first four beads follow the same horizontal destinations at different speeds. Touch makes the visible virtual hand follow a bead; contact does not carry the body or viewpoint. Pale circles accumulate on the paper behind the rods. After the same number of completed segments, the horizontal patterns agree. The recording procedure samples positions within each segment, so changing the speed changes when the marks arrive without changing their places.

The sentence depends on that last condition. It also makes something available beyond a description of the mechanism: a trace can remain after the movement has ended, while something consequential about that movement is absent from the trace. This was the kind of scientific and poetic attention we were looking for on 15 October.

*A retained Godot preview, generated through scripted playback: four rates leave matching horizontal marks after equal segment counts; the fifth row adds vertical movement. This is an apparatus preview, not a record of a visitor's experience.*

Earlier in the day, the question had seemed to be who should speak. Direct address kept telling the reader what they could notice. We tried a first-person account, then thought without an identified speaker. Eventually Palle asked to try “we” and said that voice worked. The [versions remain available](/book-review/doc/book/iterations/2026-10-15-bachelard-opening/README.md).

“We touch” gives a sentence somewhere to begin. “We withdraw” changes what is available to observe. The pronoun allows a shared movement through the encounter without supplying a narrator's biography. It remains a working voice for this passage. It cannot establish that everyone feels the same thing, or turn a composed encounter into a report of an actual visit. A faster bead may invite attention to haste; the prose still has to leave room for boredom, confusion or another interest.

Bachelard helped complicate the ambition to be scientific and poetic. In *The Formation of the Scientific Mind*, he locates obstacles within the activity of knowing: what seems familiar can prevent a problem from being properly posed. In the introduction to *The Poetics of Space*, he distinguishes the demands of scientific thought from attention to the emergence of a poetic image. We were borrowing a tension from these works, rather than a recipe for blending their languages. [Scientific questioning](https://classiques.uqam.ca/collection_methodologie/bachelard_gaston/obstacles_epistemologiques/obstacles_epist_texte.html); [the poetic image](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bachelard_gaston/poetique_de_espace_3e_edition/poetique_de_espace_intro.html).

At the rods, a dark patch can make a question seem settled: perhaps the generator favours that part of the line. But the marks include travel between destinations. A place can be crossed often without being selected often. Staying with the surface makes the first explanation inadequate. The measured relation and the image now give each other work to do. This is our adaptation for Ada, not an example taken from Bachelard.

The [author-strategy collection](/book-review/doc/book/course/writing-patterns/AUTHOR_STRATEGIES.md) grew around such operations. Stay with one object until a detail resists the account. Let a rule frustrate an intention. Put a dated document beside a recurring sound. Allow a return to change meaning when it happens to another object. These are choices available to a passage. Assigning an author to every artifact would soon become another way of deciding what the encounter must mean before it happens.

The larger voice question became what could support a sentence. At Random Touch, the visible hand moves away from its tracked position while the viewpoint stays still. At the larger Out / Back sphere, holding can carry the body and viewpoint through the room. Calling both experiences “being moved” conceals the difference. The distinction changes the writing's position inside the scene.

The Geiger cabinet asks for another adjustment. Its irregular clicks are synthesized from ninety-six calculated event times. A display names the seed, 435: the number that established the generator's starting state. A dated receipt reports an earlier acquisition of that number from an atmospheric-noise service. The sound, the calculation and the receipt support different claims. We can write their proximity without making a repeated click authenticate the source named on the receipt.

This is where “we” needs limits. The voice can listen, read a display, inspect a record and learn something from the program. Those acts do not give it equal access to everything. It can also propose an experiment. Two different recorded gestures could reduce to the same seed; finding and keeping such a pair would give us something new to compare. Until then, the pair belongs to the work ahead.

We were also trying to give the chapter continuity, a problem explored in [What an Encounter Makes Necessary](/blog/2026-10-15-what-an-encounter-makes-necessary). On the falling RAND page, touch holds a group of five digits while the other rows continue. Their amber colour makes them easier to return to. Selection changes the time available to read a result. At the cabinet, another kept number supplies a beginning. The relation between the objects can create a reason to continue, while their different operations remain legible.

Our first [five complete drafts](/book-review/doc/book/iterations/2026-10-15-five-randomness-readings/index.html) explored observation, documentary arrangement, investigation, duration and a speculative constitution. They also exposed a constraint: every draft still visited the same objects in the same order. Much of their difference remained inside a walkthrough.

The [five short studies returned afterward](/book-review/doc/book/iterations/2026-10-15-returned-literary-forms/index.html) widened the experiment. A performance could follow the sphere's timed cycle. An investigation could follow one integer. A fictional office could offer a new seed to someone asking for a different rule. A whole piece could stay with a patch of ink. We therefore separated discovering a form from composing the complete room chapter. A short study can omit objects, change narrator or follow another order; the eventual chapter must reconcile its chosen sequence with the room.

Palle then brought back a sixth complete trial responding to the original five. It begins:

> The bead has come back to the middle of the rod. The paper behind it has not come back to white.

Its last sphere also returns, but a released person need not accompany it. Nothing marks the place where they parted. The ending lets that return stand beside the opening without explaining the relation again.

We kept those opening and closing paragraphs in the small revision. The held RAND figures now leave their production out of view before the cabinet introduces another kept number. We explained uniform selection and seed at first use, described bits as zeros or ones, and replaced unexplained hashing vocabulary with the operation it performs. We also corrected “holding begins an excursion”: Out / Back already cycles, and holding joins it. The revision grew from 1,109 to 1,181 words. Whether those additions make the middle easier to inhabit remains a reading question. [Original and revised, side by side](/book-review/doc/book/iterations/2026-10-15-sixth-randomness-trial/compare.html).

The edition also keeps a practical gap visible: MOVE / KEEP is implemented but currently removed from the saved room. Its passage describes the intended sequence, conditional on resolving that placement. A persuasive transition cannot put an absent instrument back into the room.

AI made it possible to generate these alternatives and check them against the apparatus quickly. It also made plausible agreement easy to produce. We are retaining exact drafts, their sources, specific responses and proposed corrections so that another fluent version does not erase what the previous one taught us. Useful ideas can survive a rejected chapter. A recommendation to develop a form is different from evidence that it held someone's attention.

The next reading should first meet the complete text, then its strategy notes. What detail remains? Where does the next encounter feel necessary? What becomes worth trying? Technical checking follows as a distinct reading. The tutorial can reconstruct the operation; the final can suggest changing it. Both can return to the same experiment.

At the ink wall, one possible reconstruction would keep two images: one of selected destinations, another of positions passed between them. The dark patch has made that comparison worth proposing. The writing can stay close enough to a surface to ask for an instrument we have not yet made.
