# Credits & Attribution

## The Nature of Code — structural inspiration

Ada Research's vectors-and-forces curriculum is structured in the spirit of, and indebted to,
**The Nature of Code** by **Daniel Shiffman** ([natureofcode.com](https://natureofcode.com)).
Shiffman's book is the canonical introduction to simulating natural systems through code, and
its pedagogical arc — vectors → forces → oscillation → particle systems → autonomous agents →
physics → cellular automata → fractals → evolution → neural networks — shaped the order in which
Ada teaches motion and simulation.

**What Ada takes:** the *concept progression* and *chapter structure* — ideas and ordering, which
are not copyrightable. Ada follows that arc and then extends past it.

**What Ada does not take:** none of The Nature of Code's prose, code, or figures are reproduced in
this project. Every tutorial line, artifact, scene, and image in Ada Research is an original work
written for Ada. Where Ada teaches the same concept (e.g. `W = F·d·cosθ`, `F = m·a`), it does so in
its own words, with its own VR artifacts, in its own voice.

## How Ada relates to The Nature of Code

Structurally, Ada Research is a **superset** of The Nature of Code: it covers most of NoC's chapters
as its own sequences (cellular automata, fractals, L-systems, swarm / autonomous agents, machine
learning), precedes them with a foundations layer NoC assumes (primitives, transformation, arrays,
color, calculus, isosurfaces, CSG), and closes with a synthesis arc NoC does not have (the
foundations crisis, the QFEP framework, the post-foundations practice).

See **[nature_of_code_coverage.md](nature_of_code_coverage.md)** for the chapter-by-chapter map.
