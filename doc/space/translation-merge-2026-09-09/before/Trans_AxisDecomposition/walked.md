## What this map holds (seed)

Translation deconstructed into individual axes. Movement along X, Y, and Z becomes explicit and constrained, revealing how three independent directions compose all position in space. Before movement, space is theoretical — coordinates without consequence. Translation tests the theory: can you get from here to there?

The `x_translation_cube` and `z_translation_cube` isolate single-axis displacement, ghosts and rails keeping the motion explicit. The `translation_cube_demo` turns the decomposition into a door-like puzzle — only the allowed axis passes. The `cube_scene` scaffold is translation frozen into architecture, a maze of placed positions with a `pick_up_cube` hidden inside. Overhead, the `toruscylinder` floats: a curved form built from nothing but translated cross-sections. The `pickup_gate` sequences the learning; the `dark_sphere` holds the still point.

## Why it was built (seed)

Concept: Translation decomposed into independent X, Y, and Z axes — three orthogonal directions that compose all position in space. The coordinate system revealed as the skeleton of displacement.
Sequence role: Third map. Where Trans_Translation used translation as infrastructure, this map anatomizes it. Completes the translation sub-sequence before the chapter turns to rotation.
Critical angle: Descartes' analytic geometry made literal — space as three independent number lines, each one a degree of freedom. Independence of axes is not obvious; it is a structural claim about the world.

## The walk

You start on a small platform with void on three sides, and the first thing you see is high and far: the `toruscylinder`, floating four units up across the gap — a curve, in a map about straight lines, hung there like a question the axes will have to answer. A transport cube takes you across on Z. One coordinate changes. You feel the platform's whole journey happen inside a single number.

On the far side the anatomy begins. The `z_translation_cube` runs its rail — approach and recession, depth made into an oscillation. Past the `dark_sphere`, quietly invariant as always, a second transport shuttles you sideways on X, and the `x_translation_cube` repeats the lesson on the perpendicular: an animated cube that moves only along X, ghosts and labels keeping the motion explicit, one coordinate changing while the others hold still. Z across, X sideways, and Y still to come — the map is walking you through the basis, vector by vector. A text line hangs over the route and states the thesis outright: *translation produces space as navigable extent.*

Then the map makes you do it with your body. The `translation_cube_demo` blocks the way like a door.

## The turn (critical)

The turn here is ontological, and the map's own blurb carries it: **translation produces space as navigable extent.** Not discovers, not enters — produces. Before a body moves, coordinates are theory; four units of height are abstract until the lift makes you wait through them. Extent is not intrinsic to space — it is made by traversal, the way Lefebvre said space is made by practice. The pickup at its coordinate means nothing until you have been there; then it becomes *there*, a place, carrying the trace of your passage.

Against this walked ontology stands the ruled anti-form: the 4×4 matrix, which holds every axis at once and produces nothing, because it goes nowhere. R-031's tension is the deep version of the chapter's question — what is a body, under decomposition? The coordinate system answers: three independent components, interchangeable in order, indifferent in kind. The body answers back: Y is not like Z. Vertical translation is lift and fall, effort and hierarchy — the critical file notes what every city knows, that height is status and access to the vertical is never universal. Depth is approach; lateral is passing. The operation is identical; the phenomenology never is. The axes' independence is real mathematics and, simultaneously, a flattening — the matrix cannot represent the difference between rising and arriving.

And the trans reading, held with care: to be decomposed is to be described by a system that claims your components are separable and your order is irrelevant. The map honors the other truth. You become your translations — not a position but a trajectory, not a point but a path; where you have been becomes part of who you are. The scaffold remembers every placement. So does the body. Identity, this map says, is not the coordinates; it is the walking that made them into somewhere.
