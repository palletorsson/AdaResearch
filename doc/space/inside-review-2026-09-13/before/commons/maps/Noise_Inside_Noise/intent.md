Concept: Domain warping — noise distorts the coordinate system of another noise function, producing organic swirling patterns impossible through simple layering. When the input to noise is itself noisy, space folds and the result remembers turbulence the way marble remembers pressure.
Sequence role: Sixth map in the Noise sequence; the advanced composition technique that transcends additive octaves. After Noise_One layered noise by amplitude and Noise_6_Wall moved computation to the GPU, this map introduces multiplicative composition — noise as coordinate transformation. The output of f(x) becomes the input to g(f(x)), and the resulting patterns are qualitatively different from anything achievable through summation alone. This is the technique behind clouds, lava, alien skin, and every organic procedural texture that looks genuinely alive; leads to Noise_Space_10.
Technical angle: Domain warping as noise(position + noise(position)) — the first noise evaluation offsets the sampling coordinates of the second. Recursive application (warping the warp) increases organic complexity. The noisesphere artifact wraps domain-warped noise onto a sphere, showing how coordinate distortion interacts with surface curvature. Chaining multiple warp layers: p' = p + noise(p), p'' = p' + noise(p'), each iteration deepening the turbulence.
Critical angle: Domain warping is noise reflecting on itself — recursion without base case, distortion without origin. The resulting patterns are undecidable in a specific sense: given the output, you cannot recover the input coordinates. Information is not lost (the function is deterministic) but it is scrambled beyond practical recovery. This is irreversible entropy in the computational sense — structured disorder that remembers its process but conceals its starting point. The organic quality of domain-warped textures comes precisely from this: nature is the accumulation of transformations whose origins are no longer legible.
Key artifacts: noisesphere wraps domain-warped noise onto a spherical surface, demonstrating how coordinate distortion compounds with curvature to produce marble-like, turbulent patterns; dark_sphere sits alongside as the un-warped reference — same topology, no transformation, making the effect of domain warping visible by contrast.
Gap: A step-by-step visualization showing the coordinate grid before warping, after one warp pass, and after two passes would make the recursive distortion legible — seeing the grid fold reveals what the final texture conceals.
