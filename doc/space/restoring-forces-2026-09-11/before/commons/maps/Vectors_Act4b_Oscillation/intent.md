Concept: The oscillation-and-balance half of Act IV of the Vector & Forces field — the forces that *return what they are given*, met at body scale. A 30×30 hall holding the restoring and conserving forces: spring (F = −kx, period 2π√(m/k)), pendulum (T = 2π√(L/g)), momentum/collision (p = mv handed on through a still middle), restitution/bounce (h' = e·h), lever/balance (τ = F·d, balanced at F₁d₁ = F₂d₂), and wind (F_drag ∝ v²). Act IV was split a/b because the forces are many; this half holds the periodic and equilibrium ones, while Act IV-a holds the kinetic, path-bending ones.

Sequence role: Pairs with Act IV-a (kinetic forces) and follows it before Act V (force as place) in the six-map loop. Where IV-a's forces *bend a path*, IV-b's forces *restore* or *conserve* — they are the clocks and balances of the curriculum, the place where the learner meets why springs and pendulums keep time and why momentum cannot be destroyed, only handed on. It is the most "beautiful object" heavy act: the Calder mobile is staged here as the showpiece of the lever law, drifting with every arm answered by a real weight.

Technical angle: A 30×30 hall, max height 2. Interactables: spring_tower (stiffen the coil and it gives less and ticks faster — F = −kx), pendulum_hall (lengthen the string and the swing slows — T = 2π√(L/g), restoring force mg sin θ), momentum_cradle (lift and release; momentum passes through the still middle, conserved), bounce_well (set the bounciness e; each bounce a fraction of the last, h' = e·h), lever_balance (slide a load along a beam — far out, a small weight outweighs a big one near in), calder_mobile (the lever law drifting, every arm balanced by real weights), and weather_vane (a force seen only in the bend of what it pushes; drag ∝ v²). Subtitle truth-beats narrate the walk, one per station plus a reflection, in the same pattern as the other acts.

Critical angle: The act's argument is honesty — a spring "returns exactly what it's given, which is why it keeps time," and a lever "trades force for distance and never gets something for nothing." These forces are the curriculum's argument that conservation and proportionality are not constraints imposed on physics but the reason physics is legible at all. The Calder mobile carries the aesthetic weight: it is the balance law as art, not illustration. The risk is that seven stations — several of them genuinely beautiful objects — invite contemplation over comprehension; the per-station truth-beats keep each as a lesson, and the mobile's placement as showpiece rather than first station keeps it a reward rather than a distraction.

Key artifacts: spring_tower keeps time because the pull back is proportional to the pull; pendulum_hall is the oldest clock, slowed by length; momentum_cradle hands motion on through stillness, conserved; bounce_well taxes every fall by e; lever_balance trades force for distance with nothing free; calder_mobile is that same balance law set drifting; weather_vane shows a force only in the bend of what it pushes.

Gap: The act mixes restoring forces (spring, pendulum), conserving ones (momentum, restitution), and equilibrium ones (lever, mobile) under one "returns what it's given" banner — a strong frame, but the three families could be spatially grouped to make the kinship visible rather than asserted. The Calder mobile and the spring tower are showpieces that reward VR captures; this map is a strong candidate for the "beauty anchors" the field's design notes call out, so the open work is a fresh capture pass and a VR walk to confirm the mobile reads as the act's centrepiece.
