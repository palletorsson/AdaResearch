What kinds of passage can a repeating rule make? Two walls face each other across eight metres, each a sum of three sines, and the crests come slowly toward the entrance while you stand still, or pass you at walking speed when you freeze them and walk. Position and phase both move a crest; the passage looks the same under either.

The `sine_wall_corridor` puts one number between the walls, their phase offset, on a slider at the mouth. At zero the walls agree and the gap breathes from about 1.45 to 2.55 metres. At half a turn every sine flips sign, one wall copies the other, and the passage becomes a snake of constant width. AMP brings both walls in together, to a pinch you can walk into and through: the wall is drawn, not built, and the deck is what holds you. The `sine_space` floor in the basin below keeps the same rule under your feet instead of beside them.
