# WaveFunctions_Sine_Space — Summary

The hall opens with a four-row antechamber and a corridor across it: two facing walls of red plush, eight metres long, two metres apart, four metres tall, each a sum of three sines along the length. The visitor comes in from the north door, turns west along the first row and enters the corridor at its west mouth; the east mouth opens at the head of the bridge that crosses the basin to the south door. The direct museum route beside the corridor is untouched, so the passage is a detour a body chooses.

A panel at the west mouth holds two sliders and two buttons. AMP is the shared amplitude of both walls, from nothing to 0.45 metres. PHASE is the offset between the walls, from zero to half a turn. FREEZE stops the phase from advancing and RESET returns the placement's declared values, handles included. A readout prints the amplitude, the offset, the number of crests along the length, whether the walls are running, and the narrowest and widest gap they make.

Two exact readings anchor the comparison. At zero offset the walls agree and bulge inward together: the gap breathes between about 1.45 and 2.55 metres. At half a turn every sine changes sign, the right wall copies the left and the gap is exactly two metres everywhere: a snake instead of a pinch. While the walls run, the crests travel toward the entrance at about a crest every eighteen seconds; frozen, they pass a walking body at walking speed.

The walls are visible surfaces only. They carry no collision, and the final says so; the museum's deck is what supports the visitor, who can walk through a crest to check. The basin below the bridge keeps the older floor of spheres, a product of two sines, with its colour balls and the dark sphere; the two display cases in the antechamber's corners show each rule at hand scale.

Third active hall of the sequence. The Pendulum laid a swing out in time; this hall lays the same rise and fall along a passage. Effect Sound, next, puts one oscillation inside another and makes the offset audible.
