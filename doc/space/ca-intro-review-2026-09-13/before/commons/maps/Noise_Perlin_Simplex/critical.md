# The conditions that let a difference appear

A comparison begins by deciding what may differ. The pair keeps sample positions, seed, octave count, coordinate scale, height mapping and material parameters aligned. That preparation gives the generating rule a clearer place to become observable. It also closes other avenues of inquiry. A visitor who wants the colour to do something else has a legitimate question the matched comparison has temporarily set aside.

The shared sample makes this decision explicit. One coordinate goes to two functions. Their answers differ without either becoming a failed version of the other. A seed gives each generator a repeatable configuration; it does not supply corresponding ridges across algorithms. Difference at a selected point is a measurement. Its usefulness depends on what the learner is trying to make.

VIEW changes that use. The values persist while their height contribution disappears from the display. What looked like a terrain becomes a flat arrangement of coloured cubes. Neither form reveals a pristine original behind the other. Each admits a relation the learner can work with. The visible square grid is another construction, common to both displays, and must not be mistaken for a direct view of either generator's internal lattice.

The central plate is implicated too. Previously it claimed that the basis was the only difference even after a control had changed another condition. Its sentence described the intended experiment rather than the current one. The revised instrument reads the instantiated generators and lets its claim change. A museum label can be accountable to the work it names.

The small terrain complicates the paired lesson without spoiling it. It has another sample spacing, seed, basis, mesh and clock. Its inherited Perlin name does not describe the basis selected by its helper. Keeping the work lets the visitor inspect that distinction. It can be a useful slice through a 3D field without being a valid third member of this controlled pair.

The queer possibility here is methodological: different uses remain available after we have learned a rule precisely. A hollow might obstruct a crossing, shelter a body, hold a pool or offer an image someone wants to wear. This hall does not test all those uses. It gives us a way to avoid confusing the current test with the field's only possible purpose.

The orb continues while MATCH restores the pair. We make a small region repeatable inside a world whose other processes continue. Holding a condition still is work performed for an inquiry; it is never the recovery of a world without conditions.
