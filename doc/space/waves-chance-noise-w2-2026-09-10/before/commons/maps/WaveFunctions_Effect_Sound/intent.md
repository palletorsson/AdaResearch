Concept: Sound as oscillation made audible — waveforms become bleeps and bloops, FFT decomposes signal into frequencies, and the gap between physical vibration and perceived sound reveals quantization noise and latency.
Sequence role: Sixth map in Wavefunctions; the pivot from visual/spatial waves to auditory waves. Previous maps showed oscillation you see and walk; this map makes oscillation you hear. The rich artifact set (synthesizers, sound controllers, audio monitors) creates a working audio laboratory. Connects forward to Bernini (sound-as-form), John Cage (silence), and Synthesis Lab (Fourier); follows WaveFunctions_3D_Wave_Propagation; leads to Wavefunctions_Bernini.
Technical angle: Audio bus amplitude analysis, FFT spectrum decomposition into frequency bands (bass, mids, highs), quantization noise from digital sampling, latency as temporal displacement between event and perception. The synthesizer racks (303 Acid, DX7 Piano, Moog Bass, Sine Basic) provide hands-on sound generation; VRAudioMonitor and chord_tension_spring visualize the results.
Critical angle: Sound is where oscillation becomes experiential in the most direct way — you don't observe it, you feel it. Latency reveals that auditory perception reacts to the past, not the present. Quantization noise reveals that digital audio is an approximation — the continuous waveform sampled and reconstructed with gaps the ear usually ignores. The chiptune aesthetic (8-bit square waves) makes the approximation audible.
