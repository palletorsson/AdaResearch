# A surface, a measurement, a route

Put a hand into a crest. The visible surface keeps moving and the hand passes through. `sine_wall_corridor.tscn` leaves wall collision off. The flat museum deck carries the visitor; the waving sides arrange what the visitor sees. Whether the body follows that arrangement or crosses it is an experiment the room permits.

A surface, a boundary and a route are distinct things in this implementation. The renderer draws a wall from sampled positions. A collision shape would give it another power. The museum's planning system would also interpret that new collider when it allocates walkable space. A seemingly small change to one artifact can therefore alter several systems' accounts of the room. Keeping collision off here is a stated spatial decision, not evidence that all algorithmic walls are permeable.

Turn PHASE through half a turn. Every sine in the right wall changes sign, and the two sides retain a separation of two metres along local X. Their agreement is relational: neither wall needs to become straight. But read the instrument's name, `x-gap`. It compares points at the same position along the passage. It does not measure the shortest distance across a bend or decide which body fits. Constant width depends on what is held constant and how width is measured.

Run the walls, then freeze them and walk. A crest can arrive through a changing phase or a changing viewpoint. With three spatial frequencies sharing one running phase, those are different transformations: the layers drift at different speeds, while walking the frozen surface carries one unchanged arrangement past the eye. The resemblance invites a comparison; it does not erase the difference in the code.

The wall's skin is another decision. Two hundred columns sample the length; triangles connect those columns. The available fabric variants can withhold parts of that skin while keeping the displacement rule. Smoothness is something this mesh constructs. What its resolution costs, and what a headset can sustain, require measurement in the running scene; a denser mesh is not automatically a richer encounter.

The corridor does not record its visitors. The previous room kept a finite list of a body's past; this room recomputes surfaces from parameters and a phase. Both are available to a visitor, and they make different things available. The next hall puts oscillation into sound, where another sampling system will decide which differences can be heard.
