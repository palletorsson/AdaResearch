# The set before the choice

What does a removal rule choose before chance chooses an object?

<!-- @remove_random -->

Start at the board of sixty-four cubes. Press RANGE and look for the amber square inside the grey border. Before removing anything, count its rows and columns. Four by four gives sixteen possible first choices. The other forty-eight cubes remain present, but the current rule cannot choose them.

Press REMOVE ONE. One amber cube turns red briefly and disappears. Find its empty slot, then check the remaining count: fifteen. The display names the last removed column and row, so you can connect a counted event to a particular place. Press again. Does the new gap touch the first one? Either answer is possible; keep watching the eligible set rather than expecting the gaps to spread like a growing stain.

Now press ROW. All sixty-four cubes return, and only row three becomes amber. There are eight candidates. Before your next press, point to a cube you know cannot disappear. Try COLUMN, which also starts again with eight candidates, arranged in the other direction. The equal counts conceal a spatial difference: a rule can offer the same number of choices while making different places available. ALL removes that boundary within the board and offers every cube.

These buttons expose two decisions that are easily confused. Selecting a mode defines the candidate set. REMOVE ONE then draws one of its remaining members with equal probability. A grey cube has no chance of being selected in that run, however unpredictable the amber choices look. Chance does not choose the boundary or justify why it was drawn there.

Return to RANGE and continue until all sixteen amber cubes have gone. Predict the final shape before the last few presses. Although you do not know the removal order, you can know the final empty square. Each removed cube leaves the candidate set, so it cannot be selected again. With fifteen remaining, each has a one-in-fifteen chance on the next draw; with one remaining, the next result is certain. Pressing REMOVE ONE on the exhausted set leaves the grey border intact.

RESET restores the whole board and starts the current mode again. Try emptying the same range twice while remembering the first two gaps. Both runs must end in the same square, even when their orders differ. The permanent slot plates let you locate absence, but the final arrangement cannot tell you which cube went first. The display retains only the latest removal. To compare entire histories, someone would have to record the steps.
