# Subtraction is authorship — randomness as erasure and the politics of selective absence

A fully populated grid is maximum order. Every cell is occupied; nothing is missing. The random_remove rig turns the grid's order into a substrate for entropy-by-subtraction: each activation selects cells — by column, by row, by range, or uniformly — and deletes them. The grid after removal is not a damaged version of the original. It is a different grid, one whose structure is defined by what the removal process chose to take.

Wolfgang Iser's reception theory argues that the work of interpretation depends on the gaps a text leaves. What a text does not say is as determinative as what it does say, because the reader's sense-making runs through the unsaid as much as through the said. Random_Remove installs Iser's reading at spatial scale. The grid with holes is more legible than the grid without. The holes carry the signature of the removal process, and reading the signature is what the map rewards.

The arena is a 12×12 outer grid with an 8×8 interior region where cubes are stacked in a tidy population. The remove_random rig exposes four deletion modes. Column mode deletes everything at a matching x-coordinate. Row mode deletes everything at a matching z-coordinate. Range mode deletes everything within a distance threshold of a chosen centre. Uniform mode distributes deletions across the whole grid without reference to position. Each mode leaves a different kind of hole, and the hole's shape is the mode's signature.

A second rig demonstrates the consequence of removal as gameplay. Random gaps in the floor become a navigable risk field; the learner must cross the grid while more cells are removed in real time. A side panel logs each deletion with its mode, its selected index, and the time at which it occurred. The log converts the experience of crossing into a dataset about the removal, and the dataset is what Iser's reading relies on.

The politics of stochastic erasure are in the irreversibility. Nothing in the map can restore a cell once it has been emptied. The grid moves in one direction only: from populated toward sparse, from order toward entropy, from structure toward the record of structure's dismantling. The map's honesty is in not hiding the arrow. Entropy in the physical sense is irreversible, and the map treats stochastic removal as the same operation at a smaller scale.

Within the sequence, Random_Remove is the chapter on entropy as erasure. The previous map, Random_Definition, established what randomness is. This map shows what randomness does to existing structure. The remove_random artifact reappears in Random_Noise_Types and further maps in the sequence, so the deletion tool becomes a continuing presence rather than a one-off demonstration. The sequence's later maps will restore construction alongside the destruction, but this map keeps the destruction in focus and names it as a legitimate computational practice.

The authorship Iser points to is in what the removal process decided to take. Uniform removal produces a texture that reads as noise; column removal produces stripes that read as architectural damage; range removal produces a cleared circle that reads as a blast. The reader of the grid — the learner — constructs a narrative about what happened from the shape of what was taken. The narrative is not in the grid; it is in the relation between the grid and the reader, and the relation is the map's actual subject.
