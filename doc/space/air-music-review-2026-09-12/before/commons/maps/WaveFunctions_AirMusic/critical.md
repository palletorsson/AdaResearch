# What continues after the hand has left — resonance, the chosen cut, and knowing by ear

Steven Feld writes about acoustemology: sound as a way of knowing, and knowing-through-sound as inseparable from the place the sound is made in. This hall is a small place of that kind. It is built round a void, and the thing you come to know in it is the difference between an event and its continuation.

The event is a strike. The stick meets the bar and is lifted away, and the contact is over in a fraction of a second. What continues is not the contact but a response with its own duration: a sine at the bar's frequency under an exponential decay, three seconds to fall to a third of itself, seven and a half seconds before the room lets it go. The plate above the row names that response as a voice with an age, counting up from the strike, and the glow of the bar follows the same curve as the sound. Learning to hear the response as something the strike began, rather than as the strike itself, is the hall's first lesson, and it is one the ear does faster than the eye.

The second lesson is overlap. A second bar struck while the first still rings does not stop the first; each bar has its own player, and the plate says two voices with two ages. The ribbon above draws their sum, and the sum is not a third bar. A strike on a bar that is already ringing starts it again and adds nothing. These are properties of how the instrument was built, and the room says so: it synthesises a resonance, it does not solve the vibration of a metal bar. What it keeps of the physics is the relation between length and pitch, so the row can be read as a scale before anything is struck; what it leaves out is everything a real bar would add, the partials, the stick's material, the table underneath. To know this instrument by ear is to know a decision as well as a sound.

The third lesson is the cut. The eight bars are a pentatonic collection, C4 to E5 without the fourth and the seventh. That is a choice about which pairs of notes are available to a visitor, and it is not a claim that those pairs are the harmonious ones and the missing ones mistakes. Other cuts exist in the same instrument, seventeen bars to the equal temperament, ten to just intonation, twelve to the overtone series, and each would offer other pairs. The room offers this one, and says that it is offering it.

Around the ring the rest of the hall keeps an ambient layer going: the looper's FM piano over the pillar, the drone rack, the pipes where pitch is a length. None of it is the lesson; it is the place the lesson is learned in, in Feld's sense, and a visitor who has learned to separate a strike from its response will hear the room's other sounds differently on the way out.

Within the sequence, AirMusic follows Effect Sound, where one oscillation changed another's phase; here a contact sets an oscillation going and two of them overlap. The Synthesis Lab adds such sinusoids together on purpose. The habit to carry there is the one the plate teaches: give a sound time to change before deciding what sort of body has answered.
