# WaveFunctions AirMusic — Summary

What continues after the hand has left? This hall puts a struck instrument at the end of a walk round a void, so that a brief contact and its long response can be told apart, and two responses heard together.

The room is a ring of floor round a basin of nothing, with a pillar standing in the void. Along the north side are glass racks and lab tables; over the pillar an FM-piano looper plays its patterns; a drone rack and a run of pipes, where pitch is a length, stand on the way round. On the south side, at a table, is the metallophone: eight metal bars cut to a pentatonic scale, the longest at the left, a striker lying across a cradle on the front edge.

Take the stick and strike a bar. Lift it away: the bar glows and rings for seconds, and the plate above the row names the voice, its hertz, its level and its age. Strike a second bar while the first still fades and the plate says two voices; the ribbon above it draws their sum. Strike the same bar again and it starts again, adding nothing. A slow strike lands softer than a fast one. Put the stick down anywhere else and it finds its way back to the cradle.

Each note is a sine wave under an exponential decay, three seconds to fall to a third of itself, seven and a half seconds long, made once by the bar and played from its own speaker — a synthesised resonance, not a solved metal bar, with the length-to-pitch relation of a free bar kept so the scale can be read before it is heard.

Within the sequence, AirMusic follows Effect Sound on the active route: one oscillation changed another's phase there; here a strike sets a response going and two responses overlap. The Synthesis Lab adds such sinusoids on purpose.
