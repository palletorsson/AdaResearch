# ISO_FlatTerrain — walked

> ghost-drafted from the working map (turn slot, isosurfaces thread); Palle rules the voice.

## The cast

mc_flat_landscape · mc_terrain_demo · mc_overhang_landscape

## The walk

Three terrains, arranged as an argument. The mc_flat_landscape marches the gentlest ground the algorithm can speak — one value per column, the land as the graph of a function, what every heightmap game has always done. The mc_terrain_demo opens the workbench: density thresholds, noise octaves, resolution, dials on a landscape, the ground revealed as an opinion held by a field. And then the mc_overhang_landscape does the thing that ends the heightmap era: the cliff leans out over your head.

## The turn

The overhang is the turn. A heightfield can only answer *how high is the ground here* — one number per place, no ceilings, no shelter. A full 3D field can answer *is this point inside* anywhere in space, so above can be solid while below is air. Standing under the overhang you feel the data structure change around your body: the third dimension of the field made visible as the first roof. Terrain stops being a floor and becomes a *volume* — which is the door the cave rooms walk through next.
