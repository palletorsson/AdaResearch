A board of sixty-four cubes on a pedestal, numbered along two edges. Sixteen are amber; the rest are grey. RANGE, ROW, COLUMN and ALL decide which cubes can be chosen; REMOVE ONE draws one of them and takes it, leaving its slot plate; RESET puts all sixty-four back and replays the same seed's order; NEW SEED starts another.

What matters: the choice is random only inside a set a rule decided first. A grey cube has no chance at all, however unpredictable the amber ones look. The bench keeps the three steps apart — the filter that admits, the draw that picks, the deletion that scales a cube to nothing — and lets you run the same draws over two different sets. A hall shaped by absence is a hall shaped by who was eligible to be absent.
