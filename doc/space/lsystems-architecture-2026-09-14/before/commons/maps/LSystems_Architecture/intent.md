Concept: Replace 'branch' with 'corridor' and a tree becomes a dungeon. L-Systems escape biology — the same production-rule mechanism that grows plants can generate cities, floor plans, and navigable spaces. Grammar is architecture when the turtle draws walls instead of stems.
Sequence role: Fourth map in the L-Systems sequence (11th spine sequence, lambda_edge phase). The pivot map where L-Systems leave the domain of natural forms and enter designed environments. Intermediate difficulty — the learner has internalized the grammar-to-shape pipeline and now sees it applied to spatial design, a conceptual leap that reframes everything learned so far. Connects forward to LSystems_Competition where multiple grammars coexist, and laterally to procedural generation sequences where dungeon and city generation appear through different algorithmic families; follows LSystems_Grammars_And_Curves.
Technical angle: L-Systems beyond biology — production rules generate not branching trees but branching corridors, rooms, and urban blocks. The lsystem_dungeon artifact interprets turtle commands as wall placement, door insertion, and room expansion. CityGenerator scales the same principle to urban grids where streets branch and terminate according to grammar rules. The key technical insight is that the turtle-graphics interpreter is a pluggable layer: swap the renderer and the same grammar produces different spatial artifacts.
Critical angle: Architecture has always been generative — building codes, zoning laws, and stylistic grammars constrain what can be built, and within those constraints, form proliferates. L-Systems make this literal. The dungeon is the tree is the sentence — all are products of iterated rewriting. This map tests whether the learner can abstract past the biological metaphor to see the grammar itself as the fundamental object.
Key artifacts: CityGenerator demonstrates urban-scale grammar application with street networks and block subdivision; lsystem_dungeon generates navigable dungeon layouts from production rules with room and corridor semantics; dark_sphere anchors the research space.
Gap: Needs an interactive grammar-to-architecture converter where the learner writes production rules and immediately walks through the resulting 3D space, closing the loop between symbolic rule-writing and spatial experience.
