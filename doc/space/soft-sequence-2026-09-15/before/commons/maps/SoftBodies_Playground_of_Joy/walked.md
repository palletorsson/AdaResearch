## The cast

jelly cubes · elastic spheres · capsules · branching_growth_algorithm · grid agents

## The walk

No instructions. No objectives. Just a field of soft things waiting to be touched. Rounded bodies scatter across an open grid — jelly cubes, elastic spheres, capsules that deform under pressure and remember their shape. Push them, stack them, watch them bounce and settle and ripple. A branching growth algorithm seeds structures that sprout between the toys, organic geometry threading through elastic matter, and grid agents wander the space copying what they find. After five maps of lessons, the scaffolding is simply gone, and you bring your accumulated intuition into a sandbox that asks nothing and answers everything.

## The turn (critical)

The empty-handed design is a **pedagogical stance**, and a pointed one: that haptic intuition precedes formal understanding, that the best way to know compliance is to play with it until your hands have a theory your mouth doesn't yet. By removing objectives the map forces you to *generate your own questions* — what if I push here, what if two of these collide — which is exactly the posture Primitives_Ignorance argued for and this map trusts you enough to just hand over. But the branching growth algorithm is the radical thing in the room, and it deserves the spotlight: it merges soft-body dynamics with procedural generation, quietly proposing that **growth and deformation are the same process at different timescales** — a body bending under a one-second push and a structure branching over many seconds are the same negotiation between material and force, only the clock differs. That single artifact is the hinge that turns the whole sequence from physics toward biology, one map before the radiolarian makes it explicit.
