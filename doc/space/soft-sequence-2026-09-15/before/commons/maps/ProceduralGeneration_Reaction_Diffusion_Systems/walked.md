## The cast

Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion · activator · inhibitor · feed / kill parameters

## The walk

Two chemicals. One miracle. Start with uniform gray — nothing, everywhere the same. Add two substances that activate and inhibit each other, let them diffuse at different speeds, and wait. Spots emerge. Stripes appear. Labyrinths form. The Gray-Scott model gives you exactly two knobs — feed rate, kill rate — and behind those two numbers hides the entire zoo of animal markings. You turn a dial and the surface tips from spots into stripes into maze, and the map lands its one unforgettable line in your hands: *the leopard didn't inherit its spots. It computed them.*

## The turn (critical)

This is **Turing's final gift** — the reaction-diffusion paper he published in 1952, two years before his death, a mathematician proving that biology's patterns are computational, that pattern needs no blueprint. The mechanism is almost insultingly simple and utterly general: local positive feedback (the activator amplifies itself) plus long-range negative feedback (the inhibitor spreads faster and suppresses) together break symmetry and manufacture structure out of uniformity. It is the deepest statement the whole integration phase can make — **form is not designed, it is computed by matter itself** — and it is the sequence's spine meeting the book's spine, because the same Turing whose halting problem waits at foundationscrisis to name the limit of computation is here, at the end of his life, showing computation writing a leopard. The book has quietly arranged for you to meet Turing twice: once as the man who found what machines cannot do, and once as the man who found what chemistry can. This map is the second meeting, and it is the tender one.
