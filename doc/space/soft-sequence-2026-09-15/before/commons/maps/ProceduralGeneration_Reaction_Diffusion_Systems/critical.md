# Reaction-Diffusion — Critical

## Turing's Ghost

Alan Turing published "The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis" in 1952. Two years later, he was dead — prosecuted for homosexuality, chemically castrated, driven to suicide by the state.

The man who showed us that pattern needs no designer, that form emerges from process, that the beautiful arises without intention — this man was destroyed for existing outside the pattern that society demanded.

When we simulate reaction-diffusion, we invoke his ghost. The spots and stripes carry his fingerprints.

## The Morphogen Problem

Turing proposed that biological patterns arise from chemical gradients. But for decades, biologists couldn't find the morphogens. The theory was mathematically elegant but empirically orphaned.

Now we know: Turing was right, but the reality is messier. Morphogens exist. They interact. But biology adds layers of complexity that the equations cannot capture.

The model is not the territory. The map is beautiful but incomplete.

## Emergence Without Essence

Reaction-diffusion teaches a radical lesson: **form without former**.

The leopard's spots are not designed. They are not inherited as a pattern, only as parameters. The spots emerge from chemistry, from the mathematics of diffusion. There is no blueprint.

This undermines the designer. It undermines the essence. The pattern is process crystallized, not intention manifested.

For queer theory, this is liberating. If form emerges from conditions rather than essence, then categories are contingent. The boundary between "natural" and "unnatural" dissolves.

## The Politics of Parameters

Feed rate. Kill rate. Diffusion coefficients. These numbers determine whether you get spots or stripes, coral or chaos.

Who sets the parameters? In simulation, we do. In biology, evolution. In society, power.

The pattern that emerges depends on the conditions that precede it. Change the conditions, change the pattern. This is not determinism — it's ecology.

## Homogeneity as Starting Point

Reaction-diffusion begins with uniformity. The initial state is gray, undifferentiated, same everywhere. Pattern emerges as a *breaking* of this symmetry.

This is the **Turing instability**: the homogeneous state becomes unstable, and differences amplify into structure.

The uniform cannot sustain itself. Sameness is unstable. Difference is inevitable.

## The Edge, Again

Reaction-diffusion systems exhibit phase transitions. Below critical thresholds: uniform gray. Above: stable patterns. At the threshold: chaos, fluctuation, sensitivity.

This IS the edge of chaos. This IS λ ≈ 0.4.

The QFEP lambda slider is not metaphor when applied to reaction-diffusion. It is the actual parameter space. Move the slider, cross the threshold, watch the system reorganize.

## What Cannot Emerge

Not all patterns are reachable. The equations constrain what forms can exist. Certain structures — sharp corners, perfect grids, text — cannot emerge from diffusion.

The medium has affordances and resistances. Reaction-diffusion can make organic curves but not industrial edges. The mathematics has preferences.

Every generative system excludes as it includes.

## Life as Computation

Turing showed that morphogenesis is computation. The developing embryo is running an algorithm. The zebra is a cellular automaton. The shell is a GPU shader before GPUs existed.

If life computes, is computation alive?

This question haunts us still.
