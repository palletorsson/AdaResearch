## The cast

grip-deformable cube · flexing long stick · compound obstacles · soft meshes

## The walk

Twelve more tests; twelve more ways matter refuses to behave. Pick up a cube and it deforms under grip pressure, bulging where your fingers press and recovering when you let go. Grab the long stick and it flexes, oscillates, and settles into a shape that remembers the force that bent it. Every object is a spring-mass system negotiating between what it was and what contact made it. Where Part 1 taught the meeting, Part 2 is a *gallery* — obstacle after obstacle, compound geometries and edge cases, each one a separate small experiment you run with your hands.

## The turn (critical)

The gallery format is itself a **methodology**, and naming it is the map's real content: twelve scenarios is an admission that soft-body behavior cannot be deduced from first principles alone — it must be *observed across conditions*. This is a genuinely different epistemology from the earlier sequences, which mostly proved things (the theorem holds, the rule iterates, the point is placed). Here each obstacle is a question posed to the material, and each deformation is the material's answer, and the only way to know the answer is to watch. It quietly rhymes with Primitives_Ignorance — knowledge as contact, not code; understanding that comes from probing rather than from viewing from above — but where Ignorance argued it, this map simply *is* it: an empirical investigation you conduct one grip at a time, because the matter will not tell you in advance.
