# Affect Theory Gallery — Summary

The seventh Soft Bodies map shifts from physics simulation to biological contemplation. A single radiolaria artifact generates a procedural specimen in an intimate gallery (7x9 grid, spatial temperature 0.4). No interactive tools, no sliders — the learner walks around and observes.

The radiolaria artifact constructs biological forms through CSG boolean operations: spheres modified by union and subtraction with cylinders, tori, and smaller spheres. Six form types (radiolaria, spiky forms, lattice spheres, ringed forms, pollen, hybrids) are gated by random selection and feature probability. Spike placement uses golden-ratio Fibonacci lattice distribution on a sphere — the same mathematical principle governing phyllotaxis in plants and spine distribution in real radiolarians. The `spikiness_probability` parameter sweeps from smooth spheres to fully bristled specimens.

The radiolarian skeleton is the terminal case of the soft body sequence: a form that was soft during formation (protoplasm shaping silica through surface tension and osmotic pressure) and rigid in its final state. The artifact models only the endpoint — the frozen skeleton — leaving the growth process implicit. The gap between the instantaneous procedural generation and the biological reality of lifetime formation invites the learner to infer process from product.

Through Ahmed, the radiolarian demonstrates affect as capacity: a single cell with no intention produces geometry that generates aesthetic response in any viewer. The beauty is in the mathematics (golden-ratio optimization, structural efficiency), and the mathematics is in the material. Through Merleau-Ponty, the skeleton is flesh become stone — a body that was both soft and rigid at different moments, whose final form is an impression of the forces that shaped it. After five maps of watching deformation in real time, this map presents a form where the dynamics are frozen into the geometry, and the learner must read the result as a record of withdrawn forces.

**Artifacts:** radiolaria (CSG procedural biological forms).
