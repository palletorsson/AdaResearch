A single radiolarian floats in a display case. One cell, no brain, no intention — yet its silica skeleton is geometry so precise that Ernst Haeckel drew it as art. Spikes radiate at computed angles. Lattices tessellate with mathematical inevitability. The organism didn't design this. The material did.

Softness is not weakness. It is the capacity to be affected — to register force, to deform, to hold the imprint of what touched you. A soft body remembers. A rigid body breaks. The radiolarian builds its impossible architecture not through hardness but through yielding to the physics of surface tension, crystal growth, the logic of silica finding its lowest energy state.

Rotate the specimen. Watch the spikes catch light. This is what happens when matter is allowed to think for itself.
