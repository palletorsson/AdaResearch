# SoftBodies_Carusell — walked

> R-021/R-028: considered critical tutorial, ghost-drafted from the working map;
> Palle rules the voice. The walk (tutorial) woven with the turn (critical).

## The cast

spinning carousel · soft mushroom bodies · hanging cloth · obstacles

## The walk

Cloth hangs from a spinning carousel; soft mushroom bodies dangle from its arms and swing outward as the ride turns, then slam into obstacles and recover — or not. Walk into the straps and feel them part around you. This is physics as play: centripetal force pulls the soft bodies taut, collisions compress them, and elastic restoration fights back, springs inside a mesh trying to remember a shape that keeps getting interrupted. You do not read the force field here; you *watch the material read it for you*, each hanging form bending exactly as much as its radius and momentum demand.

## The turn (critical)

The carousel makes an invisible force **legible through softness**, and that is a sharper idea than "things swing outward." A rigid pendulum swings too — but it swings as one stiff fact, telling you nothing about the gradient of the force across its length. Cloth and soft mushrooms reveal what the rigid body hides: each point on the surface sits at a different radius, feels a different acceleration, and deforms by a different amount, so the material becomes a *distributed instrument* that measures the entire force field it hangs in. This is the sequence quietly generalizing its thesis: a soft body is not just matter that yields, it is matter that *records* — an analog sensor whose shape at any instant is a reading of the forces authoring it. Rigidity gives you a number; softness gives you a field.
