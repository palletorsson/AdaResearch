Concept: Stochastic automata and the interplay between determinism and randomness — rules that fire probabilistically rather than certainly. Rule 30 and Rule 110 are revisited under gravity, showing how adding noise or external forces to deterministic rules alters their computational character.
Sequence role: Seventh map in the Cellular Automata sequence; the introduction of randomness after six maps of purely deterministic rules. Stochastic CA bridge toward physical systems where thermal noise, quantum uncertainty, or measurement error prevent perfect determinism. The gravity modifier on Rules 30 and 110 adds a directional bias — connecting to the Forces sequence's gravitational themes and forward to CA_8's continuous state spaces where noise is inherent; follows CA_6.
Technical angle: Stochastic CA: transition function returns a probability distribution over next states rather than a single state. A rule might fire with 80% probability and hold with 20% — the same initial condition produces different trajectories on each run. Lattice gas automata (a CA model of fluid dynamics) use probabilistic collision rules to recover Navier-Stokes at macroscopic scale. The rule_30_110_gravity artifact applies a gravitational bias to elementary CA, pulling active cells downward and creating asymmetric evolution from symmetric rules.
Critical angle: Stochasticity reveals which features of a CA are robust (structural) and which are fragile (contingent). If a pattern survives noise, it reflects a deep attractor in the rule's dynamics; if it dissolves, it was an artifact of perfect synchrony. This connects to statistical mechanics: macroscopic laws (thermodynamics) are robust summaries of stochastic microscopic dynamics. The entropy phase finds its literal expression here — randomness as the source of thermodynamic irreversibility.
Key artifacts: rule_30_110_gravity demonstrates Rules 30 and 110 under gravitational bias, showing how external forces and stochasticity alter deterministic CA behavior.
Gap: Needs a side-by-side deterministic-vs-stochastic comparator — the same rule and initial condition run simultaneously with and without noise, so the learner can see exactly which structures are robust attractors and which are noise-sensitive.
