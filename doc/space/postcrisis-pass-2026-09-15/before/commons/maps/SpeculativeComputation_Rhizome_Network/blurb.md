Deleuze and Guattari proposed a structure with no root, no trunk, no privileged path. The rhizome: any point connects to any other. Not a tree. Not a hierarchy. A cave system that grows the way thought should.

Four chambers linked by narrow passages. No center — or every point is center. Marching cubes extract solid geometry from a density field shaped by growth patterns, branching without plan, thickening where paths cross. The cave generates itself the way mycelia colonize soil: local rules, no architect.

After the foundations crisis broke the promise of complete formal systems, this is what remains. Structures that don't need a root to hold together. Networks that route around absence. Connection without permission. The rhizome doesn't overcome hierarchy — it never needed one.
