*No one system is complete. A commons might be.*

Gödel's result was about single formal systems. He said nothing about what happens when you put several of them in a room and let them talk. Every system is incomplete on its own; each system's outside can be another system's inside.

This is not a proof that the commons is complete — nothing is. It is a practice. Wikipedia is not a formal system; it is a ledger of disagreements, continuously rewritten. Peer review is not truth-detection; it is a distributed-cognition protocol with known failure modes. The scientific record is a rhizome, not a pyramid.

What these have in common: they do not try to be consistent. They try to be *corrigible*. A commons can hold a bad entry because another node will challenge it. Gödel's shadow lives in the margin between any two entries in a wiki.

This map is many small reading stations. No single one carries the thesis. Walking between them carries it.
