One cube you can lift, and then the same cube counted. Three of them in a row along X, three running away along Z, sixteen laid flat across a floor, sixty-four stacked into a block you walk around rather than through. The hall has no door — it opens straight onto its own axis, the Y mast and the origin standing at the entrance, so the first thing you meet is not a lesson but an address. Each pool after that is the last pool with one more index on it. In the final basin the arrays stop being furniture and start working: bars comparing their neighbours, four sorts racing the same sixteen values. Somewhere has to be nameable before anything can be stored there.
