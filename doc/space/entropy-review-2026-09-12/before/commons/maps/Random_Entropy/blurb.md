# Random: Entropy

This room existed before it was built. The census found it hiding inside Random_Definition — a clean pocket of artifacts that all pointed the same way: the jar, the meter, the decaying hardware, the dark sphere. Seven things about one idea, homeless in a survey room. Now they have a door of their own.

The idea: entropy is not messiness. It is **how much you don't know yet**, counted in bits. The Shannon gauge hangs over a desk at the corridor's side, and on the desk its two hundred draws stand as coloured tiles in the order they came. Press SORT and the tiles regroup into blocks while the bars and the number stay exactly where they were; press CONTRAST and a sample of the same two hundred from a lopsided source drops the number to about two bits; press DISCLOSE and the gauge shows more of its own measurement, rung by rung, the reading never changing. Around it, the company: a jar where order decays without anyone touching it; hardware whose randomness is harvested from physical noise, standing next to randomness grown from a seed — true and pseudo, side by side, and the gauge reads outputs, never sources.

At the dark end of the room, the things that will not compress: a sphere you cannot predict, butterflies that will not repeat. You leave past them — through the part of the world that stays surprising.
