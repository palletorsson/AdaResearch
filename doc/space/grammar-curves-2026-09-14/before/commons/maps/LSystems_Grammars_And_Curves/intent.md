Concept: Production rules formalized as context-free grammars, and the extraordinary consequence: space-filling curves. One continuous path can visit every point in a region. The bridge from biological branching to mathematical rigor — the same rewriting mechanism that grows trees can also tile space.
Sequence role: Third map in the L-Systems sequence (11th spine sequence, lambda_edge phase). Formalizes the intuitive rule-writing of the first two maps into proper grammar notation, then demonstrates the power of that formalism through space-filling curves. Intermediate difficulty — the learner transitions from experimental rule-tweaking to understanding L-Systems as a subset of formal language theory. Connects forward to LSystems_Architecture where the grammar generates built environments, and laterally to the computational geometry sequences where space-partitioning appears from a different angle; follows LSystems_Growth.
Technical angle: Context-free grammars (CFG) as the formal framework — production rules map single non-terminal symbols to strings of terminals and non-terminals, with no dependence on surrounding context. Space-filling curves (Hilbert, Peano, Moore) are L-Systems whose turtle-graphics interpretation traces a continuous path through every cell of a grid as generation count increases. The Hilbert3D artifact extends the Hilbert curve into volumetric space, demonstrating that the same grammar mechanism scales dimensionally.
Critical angle: Space-filling curves are among the most counter-intuitive objects in mathematics — a one-dimensional line that completely fills a two-dimensional (or three-dimensional) region. They dissolve the intuitive boundary between dimensions. That such objects emerge from simple grammar rules reinforces the lambda_edge thesis: grammar is generative beyond what its author imagined. Formal language theory provides the vocabulary to discuss what L-Systems can and cannot express.
Key artifacts: Hilbert3D extends the space-filling curve into three dimensions, making the dimensional-transcendence tangible; space_filling_curve_gallery provides side-by-side comparison of Hilbert, Peano, and Moore curves at multiple generation depths.
Gap: Needs a grammar-type comparison artifact that places the same biological form (a branching tree) under context-free, context-sensitive, and stochastic grammar treatments side by side, showing how expressive power changes with grammar class.
