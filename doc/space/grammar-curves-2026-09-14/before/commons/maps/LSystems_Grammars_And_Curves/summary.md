# LSystems_Grammars_And_Curves — Summary

Production rules formalized as context-free grammars, and the extraordinary consequence: space-filling curves. The same rewriting mechanism that grew trees in the previous two maps can trace a single continuous path through every point in a region — a one-dimensional line that saturates two dimensions, then three.

The `Hilbert3D` artifact floats on a raised platform, rendering a volumetric space-filling curve generated by twelve non-terminals and a 3D turtle with six rotational commands. Below, the `space_filling_curve_gallery` lays three curves on the floor — Hilbert, Peano, Moore — each at the same generation depth, each tracing a different grammar through the same grid. The visual comparison is immediate: Hilbert serpentines in recursive quadrants, Peano zigzags in nines, Moore forms a closed loop.

The map formalizes what the first two maps demonstrated intuitively. Context-free grammars — single non-terminal rewrites with no neighbor dependence — sit at Type 2 in the Chomsky hierarchy. The Growth map's context-sensitive rules sit at Type 1. The distinction is not merely taxonomic: it determines what the grammar can express. Context-free produces self-similarity. Context-sensitive produces differentiation.

The 8x10 layout is mostly flat floor with a single raised pedestal for the Hilbert3D, giving clear sightlines. The grammar-to-geometry pipeline is unchanged from Grammar Lab — derive the string, interpret with a turtle — but the objects it produces have leapt from biology to mathematics. A grammar that fits in a sentence fills a space without bound. The teleporter leads to LSystems_Architecture, where the same mechanism generates cities and dungeons.
