# Colour joins the primitives

Reframed with Palle, 17 September 2026. This is the first hall of Color, following the primitive and transformation vocabulary. Its purpose is to add colour to familiar static constructions before the later halls investigate illumination, wearing, sampling, writing, transparency and reception.

The hall is 23 by 43 cells, with a clear central aisle. Seventeen artifact instances make twenty-three columns. The studies are: identical geometry under different colour rules; different primitive alphabets under one rule; aligned, turned, narrowed and combined static poses; richer stacks with varied proportions, offsets and composed paint; translated copies whose colours are kept or calculated from displacement.

Primary artifacts, in book order: `chroma_stack`, then `chroma_translation`. No animation, colour mixing, cellular automata or pattern-machine lesson is required to enter this hall. The visitor's movement changes the view of fixed objects.

The previous rear collection, placements and complete prose were archived in `doc/space/color-column-hall-2026-09-17/before/`. Artifact implementations remain available. The accepted filter, drum and Life passages are retained as deferred research material outside this opening chapter; their previous placement brief is superseded.

Question: what does colour add to a body we already know how to construct? A colour assignment can group pieces, distinguish kinds, mark height or count repeated positions. Those choices begin the investigation; this first hall need not explain all of colour.
