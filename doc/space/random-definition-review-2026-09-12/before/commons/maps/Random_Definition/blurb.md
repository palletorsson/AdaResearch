Two grids of sixty-four coloured cubes, side by side, that look as if nobody could have planned them — and they match. Press REPLAY and they match again. Press RANDOM and both change together into another arrangement that comes back just as exactly. The panel says what a grid costs: 192 draws, three per cell, row by row, from a generator put into the state the seed names.

Then press +1 DRAW. The right grid takes one value from the generator and throws it away before it starts colouring. Same seed, same cells, and nothing lines up. Reproducibility belongs to the whole procedure: the seed, the generator and the order in which its values are used.

Across the room a crank turns the same kind of generator one state at a time, and a bench compares a source with a seed to a source without one. The catalyst here is labelled for randomness, not chaos. Nothing in this room is chaotic.
