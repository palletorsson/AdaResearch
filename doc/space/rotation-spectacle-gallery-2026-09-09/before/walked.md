## What this map holds (seed)

Two corridors and a destination. The map is processional architecture — the long nave before the altar — and what waits at the end is the `carousel_cake`: layers rotating at independent speeds, some clockwise, some counter, a wedding cake of rotations that never repeats a configuration. Everything before it is approach. The `boolean_tunnel` opens the walk with rotation's other face — not display but subtraction, passage carved from solid by a turning cutter. The `righttriangle` stands raised at the entry as the geometric anchor, the right angle that will not bend no matter what turns around it. `dark_sphere` floats on a lone plinth in the void between the corridors, the still point of the whole procession. A remote `pick_up_cube` waits out over the southern void, near where the carousel turns on its own island — past the exit, beyond reach, built to be watched.

## Why it was built (seed)

Concept: Rotation as procession and spectacle — layered angular velocities, long corridors converging on a carousel, boolean-carved passage — orientation and time shaping spatial experience at architectural scale.
Sequence role: Fifth map. Extends Trans_Rotation from discrete grid reasoning to continuous, layered, large-scale rotation. Where the previous map taught that order matters, this one teaches that speed and direction compound over time. Completes the rotation sub-sequence; leads to Trans_Scale.
Critical angle: The carousel as cosmological metaphor — nested layers at independent rates, apparent complexity from simple rules. Rotation as temporal experience: unlike translation, rotation unfolds. Procession frames rotation as something approached, not simply performed.

## The walk

You spawn at the north-west corner and the map turns you — an annotation swings your attention ninety degrees, and the first thing framed is the `boolean_tunnel`, sunk into the floor ahead, a passage that exists because something rotated through solid matter and took it away.

Above the entry wall the `righttriangle` stands at half-again scale — the directional anchor, the one shape carrying an angle that rotation cannot negotiate. You pass under it and the map splits: two corridors, left and right, walls two high on the outside, a channel of open void between them where `dark_sphere` hangs on a single plinth, dark, unturning, equidistant from both paths. You choose. It does not matter, and choosing is still yours: the walls take the horizon, the corridor takes your facing, and for forty-odd cells the map gives you nothing but forward. This is the dolly-in. The floor feeds under you, the void keeps pace on your inside shoulder, the far platform grows by increments too small to notice until suddenly it is architecture. The corridors converge. The teleporter stands here — *before* the finale, an exit offered in advance. And past it, over open void, on the last island the map owns: the `carousel_cake`, turning. A `pick_up_cube` sits its own island nearby, out where you cannot walk. You stop at the edge. You watch.

## The turn (critical)

The claim under this map is that **spectacle stages rotation as performance, and the deepest rotation is not a performance at all**. The carousel is pure Debord: something to watch, watching as the whole activity, the crowd's attention as the event. And the map plays it straight — builds the pilgrimage nave, earns the reveal, makes the watching genuinely good — while planting its two dissents. The hero is the axis, the perpendicular, the one line in the spectacle that does not perform. The anti is the form the map would not even place: the torus, whose rotation is what it *is* rather than what it *does*.

Read as the trans chapter's page, and read with care: this is the difference between being watched turning and being a turning. The carousel's rotation exists in the economy of display — it must run every frame, hold the gaze, justify the corridor walked to see it. The torus owes nothing to that economy. Its revolution is constitutive; no audience completes it; it cannot be caught not-rotating because the rotation is not behavior but identity. The map's quiet argument is that the second kind is real in a way the first is not — and that the difference is dignity. You are observed observing here; the infrastructure logs the gaze even when the carousel doesn't. Against all that, the exit stands *before* the finale: the one power spectacle cannot absorb is the power to stop watching. The form that is its own rotation never needed you to look.
