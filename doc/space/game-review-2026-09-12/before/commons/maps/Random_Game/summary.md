# Random Game — Summary

Random_Game is the thirteenth and final map in the Randomness sequence. It converts every concept the sequence has introduced — distributions, independent samples, stochastic selection, entropy as erasure — into a playable arena where survival depends on inhabiting the distributions rather than predicting the instances.

The space is an 8×8 field inside a larger 12×12 map. Cubes fall from above on independent random timers, each with a sink-and-rise cycle that ignores the other cubes. The floor is therefore probabilistic rather than solid: any tile can be open or occupied at any moment, and the learner must cross the grid as the pattern churns. A cube_projectile_spawner controls the distribution and exposes mode toggles — uniform, clustered, wave-based — through a control at the edge of the arena.

Five origami enemies inhabit the field, each weaponising a different stochastic behaviour. Kaleidocycles tumble forward through four attack faces — fire, ice, spike, shield — cycling at random intervals. Kresling spires twist from flat discs into sniper towers and collapse again to relocate. Miura crawlers lie nearly flat on the floor, then unfold from the noise. Scissor stalkers extend telescoping legs to three times their compact length. Armadillo egglings roll in at randomised speeds.

A small controller at one corner manages game state and scoring. Within the sequence, Random_Game is the playable capstone. It does not test knowledge of randomness; it tests whether the learner can act inside it. The arena is the sequence's final exam, and passing it means having internalised the distributions well enough to move inside them as material rather than as obstacle.

## The crossing (2026-09-12)

The room's west corner holds a five-by-three hole in the floor — `0` cells, which the museum lays no floor on at all. `r_c#stand:chasm` stages it as a crossing: the artifact lines the pit, lays a bed one metre down, cuts a ramp back out of it, and stands three of its own cycling tiles in a row across it as stepping stones, a step proud of the floor, gaps of twelve centimetres between them.

Each stone keeps the tile's fixed four-state order — it stands, it leaves, it is gone, it returns — and draws only the waits, from its own seed, with a shorter band for being gone than for standing. A stele on the near lip carries that order cut in stone; a tablet beside it carries the live draws, one line per stone: what this turn gave it and how much of that is left. The figure can be counted down because the draw is written down once, the moment it is made, rather than re-thrown each frame.

The middle stone wears a crown ring for the second before it goes, lit while it still stands: an advance cue, against the tile's shipped signal, which is a lamp lit only once the block is already moving. CUE at the lip swaps between them for all three stones without touching the machine. REPLAY deals the same rhythm again from the crossing's five-digit seed, cut into the idol on the far lip; NEW SEED names another.

The fall is a metre onto the artifact's own bed, and the ramp is the way back. The bypass is the hall's own corridor east of the wall stub, through a doorway at (6,2): the far lip is reachable without a single stone, so crossing is a choice rather than a toll. The arena and its origami bestiary come after the crossing on the route, not on top of it.
