Concept: The second act of the Vector & Forces field — vector arithmetic taught as six operations, each shown at two scales. A 20×38 gallery pairs an intimate artifact (a toy you hold) with a walk-in one (an idea you stand inside) for the operations that have both, so the learner first does the move at hand-size and then occupies it at room-size. The six operations: addition, subtraction, scaling, dot product, projection/reflection, and cross product/torque.

Sequence role: Comes after Act I (what a vector is) and before Act III (fields and motion) in the six-map walkable loop. Act I gave the learner the object; Act II is where vectors first *act on each other*. It rehearses the scale rhythm the whole field is built on — open intimate, close walk-in — most fully, because addition, dot, projection, and cross each have a `_walk`/`_xl` twin. The closing teleporter leads to Act III. The reflection beat ("To add is to walk; to dot is to agree; to cross is to turn") compresses the act into three verbs.

Technical angle: A wide hall, 20 wide by 38 deep, max height 2, laid out so each operation's small and large versions sit together. Interactables run in script order: vector_add + vector_addition_walk (head-to-tail resultant, hand-scale and walk-in), vector_sub (flip to −b and add — the arrow of the gap), example_2_3_gravity_scaled_by_mass_vr (a mass dial that grows the force arrow proportionally — scalar × vector), dot_aligner + vector_dot_product_xl (close the angle to charge a lock; a·b = |a||b|cos θ), projection_shadow + vector_projection_reflection_xl (the shadow's length on a rail is the projection, the perpendicular drop the rejection), and torque_crank + vector_cross_product_xl (push across an arm, not along it; r×F points up the axle). Eight subtitle truth-beats narrate the walk — intro, one per operation, and a closing reflection.

Critical angle: The act's claim is that arithmetic is *what vectors do to each other*, not what you compute about them — addition is walking, the dot is agreeing, the cross is turning. Showing each at two scales is the pedagogical wager: the toy proves the rule, the room makes it bodily. The risk is breadth — six operations and ten artifacts in one hall is the densest act, and a learner could treat it as a museum to skim rather than a sequence to walk. The intimate→walk-in pairing is the mitigation: each concept is meant to be *done twice* before moving on, which is why the gallery is long rather than wide.

Key artifacts: vector_add / vector_addition_walk make the resultant a place you arrive at by walking a then b; vector_sub draws the difference as the direction of the gap; example_2_3_gravity_scaled_by_mass_vr turns scaling into a volume knob for a direction; dot_aligner / vector_dot_product_xl make agreement (cos θ) into a charge that locks; projection_shadow / vector_projection_reflection_xl cast one vector onto another's line; torque_crank / vector_cross_product_xl produce the third direction born when two refuse to align.

Gap: With ten artifacts the room risks reading as a catalogue. A clear floor path enforcing the intimate→walk-in order for each operation (rather than letting the learner wander between scales) would protect the "do it twice" pedagogy. The dot-product station converts a foe FOE→FRIEND at full agreement, a strong beat that no on-floor sign currently names — a label tying "agreement is mercy" to the lock charging would land the dot product's stakes. A summary wall at the exit listing the three verbs (walk / agree / turn) would consolidate before Act III.
