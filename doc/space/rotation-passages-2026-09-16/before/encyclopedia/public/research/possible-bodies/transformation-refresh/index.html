Turn aside → remove cells → shrink apart

Slats turn near your body. A lattice loses the cubes reached by your passage. Nearby blocks shrink to make room and regain their size when you leave.

Clarified: proximity supplies the input. Rotation and scaling are reversible here; deleting cells changes occupancy and leaves a record.

The carving operation has its own tolerance: the body is stopped by collision, so the removal test reaches a small distance beyond that boundary.

For the book: distinguish a returning boundary from one that keeps what happened. Deletion is more than moving an existing cube to another position.

The next iteration has a concrete starting point

Walk the sequence. Read beside the encounter.

Each hall now has a question, a small set of operations and a consequence to observe. Use “Read the text” to comment on the current draft, or “See the map” to compare its encounter order with the floor.

Does the next action read?

Notice where a visitor hesitates: boarding a carrier, finding the next landing, taking a handle, locating the door.

Does the comparison hold?

Keep the bank, pivot or reference frame in sight. Check whether the intended difference is available to someone encountering it for the first time.

Does the prose earn its claim?

Keep the concrete observation. Revise a sentence when the artifact does something else, or when it explains a difference the visitor has not yet encountered.

What has been checked

This is an implementation review, with actual Godot images and recorded runtime checks. The opening halls check contact collection, floor alignment and crossing geometry. Scale checks pickup, retained body scale, thirteen physical landings, the exit walk and clearance from museum furniture. Rotation has source-grid movement checks and an actual museum build confirming the merged hall and its eight placements. Translation checks both grabbable constraints; Body checks the repaired exit floor; Pit checks configured pusher paths and grounded growth.

Headset reach, timing, comfort and the experience of walking all seven halls remain pending. Static crossing poses and automated body movement do not establish those things.
