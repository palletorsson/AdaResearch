# Noise_Columns — field notes

## 2026-09-12 — N2: can you tell how a body changes just by looking at its strange shape (Fable, the second hall of Astra's noise arc, after Random_Noise_Types; Astra's card: "keep a recognisable baseline column, one periodic deformation and one deliberately implemented coherent-noise-driven deformation, same mesh, material, displacement range and viewing frame, and reveal the driver after observation; allow local freeze and separate whole-column rotation from deformation; inspect marble shading independently; use bounded resolution and rebuild frequency; preserve a stable architectural margin and do not present these display columns as simulated load-bearing stone; correct the erosion/entropy claims in supporting docs")

**The ruling.** The hall's description promised 3D Perlin erosion and reversible entropy. The shipped melt was `sin(time * melt_speed + i)`, there was no field anywhere in the file, and nine columns all moving at once made comparison impossible — nothing in the room could have shown a visitor what a field looks like, because the room contained none. So the staging is a controlled comparison: three columns in one frame, identical in mesh, resolution, material, mapped displacement range and viewing distance, differing in one thing — what proposes the melt phase. A fixed baseline, the shipped sine, and a real `FastNoiseLite` field seeded by the room. The drivers are dealt to the three positions from the same seed, and nothing says which is which until REVEAL, because a label before the looking answers the question the room asks.

**Two faults in the placed scene, found before any of that could work.** `MeltingBerniniScene.tscn`'s root was a scriptless `Node3D` with the script on a `BerniniColumns` child, so the grid's `apply_grid_config` reached nothing and no token could ever have configured this artifact (the probe's first run: `script: -` on the body). And the scene carried a `Camera3D` with `current = true`, which takes the view in every hall that places it — twelve maps do. The script moved to the root with the child's exports carried over verbatim (the child sat at identity, so the twelve placements build exactly what they built) and the camera is gone.

**Every deformation amplitude in that file is in metres.** They were tuned for an eight-to-ten-metre column. On the trio's 2.55 m columns the first build photographed as stacked flying shards, the second as corkscrews. They scale with the height now, and three of them — the `wave1/wave2/wave3` wobbles inside `generate_spiral_column_mesh` — were hardcoded, so they take a guarded `_amp_scale` that is 1.0 by default and leaves the shipped mesh identical to the vertex. Astra asked for a recognisable baseline column; that is what the scaling is for.

**The drivers share one function and one range.** Both hand a phase in [0,1] to the same mesh call, so the mapped drop is 0.00–0.70 m for either of them and the comparison is of drivers and nothing else. The probe checks this at four matched times: both displacements inside the one range, and the two phases differing at three of the four (they are genuinely different drivers, not one dressed twice).

**What the instrument proves that a shape cannot.** FREEZE stops the clock: nothing drifts, no mesh is rebuilt, and rebuilding a column at a named time twice gives the same mesh to the vertex while another time gives a different one — the geometry is a function of the driver and the time and of nothing else. SPIN turns the columns without touching a vertex (the checksum is unchanged), so turning cannot be mistaken for melting. MARBLE takes the veined shader off and leaves the same geometry, so the material's noise cannot be mistaken for the geometric driver. All three were Astra's items and all three are checked.

**The return is the only tell.** Over forty-eight seconds of the artifact's own driver function the periodic column's peaks are 0.05 s apart in spacing and the field column's vary by 1.9 s. That is the room's actual answer to its question: the shape at any one moment carries no mark of what produced it, and only the history separates them.

**Bounded and measured.** Twelve rebuilds a second at 40 × 16 segments, about 3.5 ms a mesh, two moving columns; the baseline is never rebuilt at all. The shipped ring rebuilt nine full meshes every frame. No column and no plinth carries a collider, and the probe checks that separately from the instrument's own buttons, which do carry bodies and are not a load-bearing claim.

**The hall is severed by the museum's passage, and was before this.** `[em-walk] Noise_Columns: SEVERED and nothing reopens it`. Measured both ways: 193 free cells with the trio staged, 213 with the staging switched off, and the same verdict. The museum's own tile for the hall is open end to end (rows 1–14 all floor, the doors at x5–7 north and south); the passage rows it adds afterwards read `44444411444444 / 44444411111444 / 44444444411444` — they bend east and end at x9–x10 while the door lane is x5–x7, so the walk arrives at the wrong x. The probe checks the hall's own tile door to door, which passes, and records the museum's verdict as a measurement. Reported as its own forum thread (260912-fca2z) because a seam is not this room's to fix.

**Exactness decisions.** The trio stands at (9,7), east of the hall's centre: any collider in the staging seals the body's whole footprint, and a 4.4 m row across the middle sat in the narrow x7 lane. Three columns and not five, because five will not fit between the hall's raised border and its lane. The melt range is 0.70 m on a 2.55 m column — a quarter of the height, enough to read as melting and not enough to lose the column. The token's `180` turns the instrument toward the north door, where a visitor arrives; the rig presses from 0.45 m, which is the lesson N1 paid for.

**Rejected.** Leaving all nine columns and colouring three of them differently: the comparison needs identical bodies, and nine moving meshes is the cost Astra asked to bound. Rejected too: labelling the columns from the start, which would have made the room a diagram of an answer instead of a question.
