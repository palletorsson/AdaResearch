## The walk

One scene, played as elegy. The MeltingBerniniScene takes Bernini's columns — history's most deliberate architecture, every curve an argument — and lets noise-driven vertex displacement have them. The baroque dissolves, but not into rubble: the displacement is *correlated*, so the form flows rather than shatters, sagging like wax that remembers being marble.

## What it teaches

This is the chapter's primitive shown at its most tender: noise does not destroy form, it loosens form's grip while preserving its neighborhood — each vertex drifts, but drifts *with* its neighbors, which is the entire definition under demonstration. Against white noise the columns would be static; under Perlin they melt. Memory between samples is the difference between corruption and transformation — and the room lets you grieve and admire the same event.
