Concept: Trees grow toward scattered attractors. Branching emerges not from grammar rules but from proximity — each branch tip reaches for the nearest unclaimed point in space. The space colonization algorithm produces organic branching without any L-System, demonstrating that structure can arise from spatial hunger alone.
Sequence role: Second map in the Procedural Generation sequence (12th spine sequence, lambda_edge phase). Bridges from the evolutionary approach of Genetic_Evolution to spatial growth algorithms. Advanced difficulty — the learner contrasts this attractor-driven growth with the grammar-driven growth of the L-Systems sequence, seeing that branching is not owned by any single algorithm but emerges from multiple generative paradigms. Connects forward to PG_Percolation_Network where connectivity itself becomes the subject, and laterally to the L-Systems sequence where branching was grammar-based; follows PG_Genetic_Evolution.
Technical angle: The space colonization algorithm distributes attractor points in a volume, then iteratively grows branch segments toward the nearest attractors. Each attractor influences only the closest branch tip within a radius of influence; once a branch tip comes within a kill distance, the attractor is consumed. This produces realistic venation patterns (leaf veins, arterial trees, root systems) without production rules. The algorithm's parameters — attraction distance, kill distance, segment length — control density, tortuosity, and branching angle.
Critical angle: Space colonization inverts the L-Systems model: instead of grammar generating form, space itself generates form through the distribution of attractors. The "program" is the point cloud, not the rule table. This is procedural generation's deeper claim — that the distinction between algorithm and data dissolves when the environment is the program. Rules and randomness (the attractor distribution) are enough to produce convincingly organic structure.
Key artifacts: space_colonization_algorithm implements the full attractor-driven growth pipeline with configurable attraction distance, kill distance, and segment length parameters.
Gap: Needs a comparative artifact placing L-Systems branching and space-colonization branching side by side from identical initial conditions, making the algorithmic-family difference visually and structurally explicit.
