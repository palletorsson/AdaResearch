Concept: The third act of the Vector & Forces field — fields and motion, the point where a vector stops being a single arrow and becomes either a vector at *every* point (a field) or a vector the world *keeps editing* over time (motion). A 18×30 map split into two halves: a field half (a flow console plus a walk-in storm chamber) and a motion half (a launch arc, a bubble gun with visible output velocity, and the decomposition of velocity into carry and fight-gravity components).

Sequence role: Follows Act II (arithmetic) and precedes Act IV (forces) in the six-map loop. It is the hinge of the spine: Acts I–II treated vectors as static objects and operations; Act III is where vectors gain *extent* (a field spread over space) and *history* (motion spread over time), setting up Act IV where named forces edit that motion. The closing teleporter leads onward to Act IV. The reflection beat ("A field is force spread over space; motion is force spread over time") states the act's symmetry directly.

Technical angle: A 18×30 hall, max height 2, organised as field-then-motion. Interactables: weather_vector_field (the walk-in storm chamber — a vector waits at every point and pushes the walker along streamlines), VectorFieldFlow (the flow console framing the field as a function from place to push), launch_arc (set angle and power, fire; the straight launch bends to a parabola as vy fights gravity), VectorMotion (velocity decomposed into vx that carries and vy that fights), and bubble_blaster (a gun whose output velocity is made visible in the bubbles it throws). Five subtitle truth-beats narrate — intro, truth_field, truth_motion, and the reflection.

Critical angle: The act's argument is a symmetry — a field is force distributed across space, motion is force distributed across time, and both are vectors that refuse to hold still. Walking the storm chamber is the key embodied move: the learner *feels* a field as push at their own position rather than reading arrows on a chart, which is the difference between a vector diagram and a vector field. The risk is that two distinct ideas (field, motion) in one room blur together; the hard split into two halves and the parallel-construction reflection beat are what keep them legible as a matched pair rather than a jumble.

Key artifacts: weather_vector_field is the walk-in field — force decided to be everywhere at once, felt as drift; VectorFieldFlow frames the field as a function from place to push; launch_arc shows motion as a straight velocity the world bends into a parabola; VectorMotion pulls velocity apart into carry (vx) and fight-gravity (vy); bubble_blaster makes launch velocity visible in flight.

Gap: The two halves teach genuinely different ideas but share a room with no clear threshold between them — a doorway, lighting change, or floor sign marking "field | motion" would stop the concepts bleeding together. The tutorial flags both weather_vector_field and launch_arc as "[needs scale]" single-size artifacts; a walk-in motion piece (a body-scale launch) would let the motion half close at the same scale the field half reaches, completing the act's intimate→walk-in rhythm that Acts I–II already have.
