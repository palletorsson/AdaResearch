# LSystems_Competition — Summary

Multiple L-systems share a single world. Each tree follows its own axiom and production rules, but all compete for finite light and soil. The `ForestCompetition` artifact manages the ecology: a shared light map darkens where canopies cast shadow, a shared soil map depletes where roots consume nutrients, and collision penalties damage trees whose branches occupy the same space. Health drops below a threshold and growth stops — the grammar falls silent, not because its rules changed but because the environment denied it resources.

Tropism bends branches toward available light. Niche partitioning emerges as trees space themselves to avoid mutual shading and root competition. The competitive dynamics produce forest structure that no single grammar would generate alone — dominant canopy trees, stunted understory growers, and open gaps where competition was too intense for any strategy to survive.

The `branching_coral` demonstrates the same principle underwater. Multiple shoots grow from a shared base, each following independent rules (`F -> FF[+F][-F][>F][<F]`), competing for space and current flow. Different kingdom, same formal structure: branching grammars in a shared substrate producing emergent colony morphology.

The map's open field layout with void clearings as ecological niches gives the simulation room to demonstrate its dynamics. The `dark_sphere` at the south previews the branching catalyst — the tool that in LSystems_Living will let the learner seed their own authored trees into the competitive world. The sequence arc comes into view: Grammar Lab showed one grammar producing one tree; Competition shows many grammars producing an ecology. The teleporter leads to the finale.
