Concept: The kinetic half of Act IV of the Vector & Forces field — the named forces that act through motion, met at body scale. A 30×30 forces park where each machine embodies one force editing a path: work (only the component along the ground counts), drag (resistance taxing speed), projectile launch (a given velocity that gravity finishes), centripetal (constant pull toward an unreachable centre), and gravity/orbit (two bodies falling around a shared point). Act IV was split a/b because the forces are many; this half holds the kinetic, path-bending ones, leaving oscillation and balance to Act IV-b.

Sequence role: Follows Act III (fields and motion) and pairs with Act IV-b (oscillation & balance) before Act V (force as place) in the six-map loop. Where Act III introduced motion as an editable vector, Act IV-a names the editors — the actual forces a physics learner must know — and stages them at walk-in scale so each is a place rather than an equation. The reflection beat ("Work, drag, launch, turn, fall — every one is a force bending a path") frames the whole half as a single claim: forces are what edit motion.

Technical angle: A 30×30 open park, max height 2, sized to let each force be a station you walk between. Interactables: force_mower (push by an angled handle; W = F d cos θ — raise the handle and the work falls as the useful component shrinks), drag_corridor (lanes of rising resistance where F = −b·v decays velocity exponentially — the snapshots bunch up), force_pad (step on and launch into an arc; range = v² sin 2θ / g), centrifuge_ring (a ring hauling you inward; a = v²/r, so double the speed quadruples the pull), and orbit_walk (walk an orbit around a barycenter that belongs to neither body; F = G m₁m₂/r²). Seven subtitle truth-beats narrate — intro, one per force (work/drag/projectile/centripetal/gravity), and a closing reflection.

Critical angle: The act's argument is that a force is not a label on an arrow but something you can be *inside of* — the mower's wasted effort, the corridor's rent on speed, the ring's inward haul are all felt rather than computed. Staging the forces at body scale is the project's central bet against the diagram. The risk is that five distinct machines in one open park become a playground without an order — without a walk path, the "work → drag → launch → turn → fall" progression the reflection names could be experienced in any sequence, weakening the argument that these are rungs on one ladder.

Key artifacts: force_mower makes work the part of a push that goes the world's way; drag_corridor charges rent on speed as velocity decays e^(−bt); force_pad gives a launch velocity and lets gravity write the rest; centrifuge_ring turns going-in-a-circle into constant acceleration toward an unreachable centre; orbit_walk is two bodies falling toward each other and missing, forever, around a point that is neither's.

Gap: The five forces have spoken truth-beats but no enforced walk order, so the kinetic progression reads as optional. A floor path or numbered stations would make "work → drag → launch → turn → fall" a sequence rather than a scatter. The tutorial marks force_mower, drag_lane, circle_train, and orbit_pair as "[needs scale]" — the walk-in versions placed here are exactly the scale variety the field's gap list asks for, so the remaining work is verification in VR that body-scale reads as intended rather than as oversized props.
