# Material logic grammar — working rules

The tabletop is a stage, not a length that must be filled. Use the smallest construction that makes a physical question legible, leaving room for hands, removed pieces and the next experiment.

## The shared sentence

**Prepare → release → approach X → consequence → approach Y → consequence → arrival or loss.**

- **Ball:** one continuous, visible carrier. Keep its size and finish consistent between studies. A result comes from contact, not from a Boolean flag or a timed animation.
- **Slope:** stored height becomes motion. Expose the supports and the change in height.
- **Input:** a manipulable piece changes a physical relation. Keep X and Y near their actual decision points.
- **Route:** timber carries motion; a visible opening threatens interruption. Low edges guide without concealing contact.
- **Output:** the same pale cone, balanced on its tip, provides a vulnerable, readable end. Arrival makes an actual impact.
- **Control:** pink means something the hand can operate; black supports controls; timber and concrete carry loads. Administrative SAVE/RECOVER controls should not dominate the experiment.

## Timing and feeling

Anticipation needs a visible cause and enough travel time to form a prediction. Do not maximize total travel or enforce equally spaced events. Let the sequence have a beginning, an uncertain middle and a short decisive ending. Gravity supplies the acceleration; no scripted waiting, easing, magnetic snapping or guaranteed success. A blocked or broken route must remain visible long enough to be understood. Reset is an explicit new attempt.

Use a compact route on the two standard sheets. Spare board is working space, not filler. Keep the input pieces in the player's reach and leave a clear foreground view of the ball and cone. Any sound cue should come from a measured contact, rather than announcing an outcome in advance.

## Reference observations

- [Matter Gates](https://claude.ai/artifact/8xNFmto9VYmEUqeF3rdum6): the displayed AND uses two gaps and suspended crates. X and Y supply missing support; both must exist for the ball to reach Z. The iron ball is always released. The displayed controls separate input selection, release and observation.
- [Tyre, Plank, Wedge](https://claude.ai/artifact/8wcwsQkwvjq7GS6bfG4Ba9): the displayed setup stages distinct ramp, plank, wedge, boxes and rest events. Its description commits to one physical take after release and variation between attempts.

These are shared material roles, not permission to imitate a cinematic camera, substitute a canned result or change the user's hand-built arrangements. The active floor-12 pallet AND now uses `logic_grammar: supports`: X and Y are the two removable gap bridges. Earlier saved sketches without this field retain their legacy blocker convention. A compact 2.64 m route leaves the rest of the two-sheet stage available to hands and later assemblies. No original pallet piece is resized.


## Series and parallel

AND repeats a decision along one route: both gaps need support. OR places the
same physical decision on two routes leading to one output. Release a ball into
each route, so either supported route can produce the result without a hidden
selector. Keep the ball, removable support, slope, vulnerable cone and control
roles consistent. Lane starts may differ slightly in position to separate the
arrivals physically. Neither study needs its route to fill the tabletop.
