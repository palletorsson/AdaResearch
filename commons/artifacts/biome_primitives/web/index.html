THE GODOT COMPANION
The ground at full size.
A twelve-by-twelve metre collidable ground with no table, handles, control panels or buttons. The active desktop or XR walker supplies positions. The hall supplies the stage. The first four stages share one recorded gesture and viewpoint. The two form studies share a second, collision-checked route and a forward-looking viewpoint, also at 1.65 metres.
The black mark follows the projected position. The straight reference, sampled trail and cell boundary become visible as their capabilities arrive. Five equal forms then introduce surface and depth. Before scaling, the gaps are 0.35, 0.65, 1.25 and 1.65 metres; the standalone body is 0.5 metres wide. This is separate from the museum's invisible-point artifact.


AN ARTISTIC THREAD / NOW AND LATER
The interval belongs to the work.
The five volumes are a study prompted by Donald Judd: an object gives shape to the space around it. Here, equal objects leave unequal openings. Walk alongside them. Try a gap. Follow your own detour in the trace.
The question is already bodily: which version of a body did the passage expect? Queer inquiry begins here, in the rules of the encounter, and can accompany every later form.
This is our spatial experiment. Judd’s attention to space is a reference; this is not a reconstruction of a particular work. Judd Foundation.

The artist references can recur across halls. “More organic” does not automatically mean more alive or more queer. Each new capability needs an encounter that lets us test what changed.
