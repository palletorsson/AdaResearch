# Curation — Vectors_Act1_WhatAVectorIs

> *"Curation is an argument made with placement."* (curation_station @identity)

## The argument the wall makes

The map's whole lesson is one sentence, spoken in its closing truth-beat:

> **"Four facts, one arrow: a where, a which-way, a how-far — and a how-far refused, leaving pure direction."**

The **mechanical baseline** stated those four facts at their *thinnest* tier — four small artifacts on four plinths in a flat row, one wordless panel, no centre, no ladder. A shelf, not an argument.

This wall keeps the four-fact spine as the **reading order along +X** (left → right *is* the processional walk the map describes), but turns it into a real **small → medium → large → applied ladder** by deepening the *heart* of the act — the **which-way** fact — and giving the other three facts a small + applied beat. Crucially, the deepening is not invented: the candidate artifacts were *built as a ladder*. Their `@identity` blocks say so in their own words — `vector_bench` is "the **bench-scale opening** of the Vector ladder," `free_vector` is "the **intimate companion** to VectorBasics," `route_vector` is "the **applied face** of Vector basics," `coordinate_locator` is "the **applied face** of the Coordinate system concept." And all of them share the act's central truth verbatim: *"a vector is a journey with no starting address."* The wall simply assembles a ladder the artifacts were already asking to stand in.

So the wall's claim is: **a vector is not four separate things — it is one object (an arrow) examined from four sides, and the object is most fully itself in the which-way fact, where the same arrow slid anywhere stays the same vector.** That fact gets the centerpiece; the others orbit it as its where, its how-far, and its how-far-refused.

## The focal point

**`free_vector` at x=17, on a 3×3 plinth raised to 1.0 m, crowned by the "journey with no starting address" panel.** It sits at the dead centre of the wall's run, at the climax of the only complete four-rung ladder, and it is the one artifact that *demonstrates* the act's thesis rather than restating it — the ghost copies make "same arrow, slid anywhere, same vector" a thing you see, not a thing you're told. Everything left of it builds the vocabulary (where); everything right of it spends the object (how-far, refused).

## Why these props (meaning, not just size)

- **`station_stage` (4×4) for CoordinateSystem3M** — the *only* walk-on deck on the wall. The frame is the one artifact whose desire is to be *entered* ("feel orientation as embodied direction by standing inside"); a stage's soul is "raise it a little and admit you are presenting it" while staying "low enough to step onto." Right tool for a world.
- **`station_plinth`, sized per meaning** — the plinth soul: "size IS part of the argument — what you raise high and narrow, you call precious; what you set low and broad, you call a world." So the centerpiece and the lantern go high-and-narrow (precious), the applied beats go low-and-broad (workaday), and the small openers get modest 1×1 podiums.
- **`station_pillar` as dividers** — its soul: "one upright, repeated, makes a room out of an open floor." Three pillars turn one long shelf into four *bays*, so the four facts read as four rooms in a procession.
- **`station_panel` as labels** — its soul: "a place that presents must also explain… saying it in plain pinned words." The map *already wrote* the words (its `subtitles`); the panels surface them on the wall.

## Sieve pass

- **Q1 — does it thicken the cognitive water?** Yes. The baseline gave four flat handles; this gives a *ladder* (you can see the same idea at four magnifications) and a *centerpiece* (one place the eye and the argument converge), plus the four facts framed as four bays you walk through in order. New handles to *think* "what a vector is," not just objects to look at.
- **Q2 — what is foreclosed?** The wall now strongly *asserts* the four-fact reading; a learner who wanted to discover their own grouping is gently overruled by the bays and labels. Acceptable for an Act I opener whose whole job is to install vocabulary — but it is a real foreclosure, not a free lunch.
- **Q3 — what lives in the dark spot?** The ghost-copy demo of `free_vector` and the drone's hunt are *embodied*, not captioned — the wall trusts the artifacts to carry meaning the panels don't spell out. That's generative habitat (room to play), not a sterilising seal.
