**9. The Circle of Fifths as Graph**
Harmonic Space is a Network

Summary: The Circle of Fifths is usually presented as a diagram, but mathematically, it is a specific type of Graph: a Cyclic Graph (C12) isomorphic to the modular group Z12. By treating pitch classes as Nodes and intervals as Edges, we can model modulation and harmony as a pathfinding problem.

---

## 1. The Nodes: Pitch Classes

The 12 notes of the chromatic scale are the nodes of our graph.
`{ C, C#, D, D#, E, F, F#, G, G#, A, A#, B }`

In linear chromatic space, C is adjacent to C# (distance 1).
But in **Harmonic Space**, C is adjacent to G (distance 7, a perfect fifth).

---

## 2. The Edges: The Generator

The graph is generated by the rule:
`Node(i) connects to Node((i + 7) % 12)`

This creates a single connected component—a ring.
*   **C** -> **G** -> **D** -> **A** -> **E** -> **B** -> **F#** -> **C#** -> **G#** -> **D#** -> **A#** -> **F** -> **C**

Traversing this edge adds one sharp to the key signature.
Traversing backwards (distance -7 or +5) adds one flat.

---

*   C connects to E (Major 3rd)
*   C connects to G (Fifth)
