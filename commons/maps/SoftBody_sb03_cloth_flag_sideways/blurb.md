A flag pinned along its left edge. Wind blows sideways. The cloth catches the wind, sags under gravity, and settles not into a straight banner but into a slow drooping wave — a shape that holds the argument between three forces: pinning on one side, gravity below, wind across.

Each vertex is a mass; each neighbor is a spring. Wind pushes each face by an amount proportional to how square it points into the breeze. Vertices closest to the pinned edge barely move. Vertices at the free edge swing the farthest. Between them the springs propagate the disturbance back, and the cloth finds a draped, asymmetric resting shape.

This is the third pin pattern in the cloth study. Two corners gave a catenary. A top row gave a flat curtain. A side edge with wind gives a wave. The springs and gravity have not changed. The wind is new — and the boundary has rotated by ninety degrees. The cloth responds in kind.
