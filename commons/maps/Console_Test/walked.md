## The walk

Before this chapter throws anything, it hands you instruments. The control_console anchors the room; around it stand five borrowed workbenches — Cantor's diagonal, Riemann's sums, Shannon's channel, the tangent's slope, the triangle's curvature. None of them is a force. All of them are ways of *measuring*, and that is the primitive this room installs: direction and magnitude are measurements before they are pushes. The tangent bench especially leans forward into the chapter — a slope is a rate, a rate is a velocity waiting for mass.

## What it teaches — and what it confesses

This room is honest about its inheritance: the chapter of forces opens with instruments carried in from other chapters, because F = ma is a sentence written entirely in borrowed quantities — m from measure, a from change. Whether these five benches are the right delegation or placeholder furniture is a live question the room itself poses; the walk works either way, but the confession belongs in the text.
