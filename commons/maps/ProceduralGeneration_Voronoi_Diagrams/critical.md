# Voronoi Diagrams — Critical

## The Politics of Proximity

Voronoi diagrams encode a simple politics: you belong to whoever is closest. Distance determines allegiance. Space is partitioned by presence.

This is the logic of colonization. Plant a flag, claim the territory. The map is drawn not by those who live there but by those who arrive.

## Seed Placement is Power

Who places the seeds controls the partition. Random seeds produce organic patterns. Strategic seeds produce gerrymandering.

The algorithm is neutral. The input is not.

Electoral districts, school zones, delivery territories — all Voronoi-adjacent. All questions of who decides where the seeds go.

## The Violence of the Border

Voronoi edges are sharp. You are in one cell or another. There is no gradient, no transition zone, no belonging to both.

At the border, you are equidistant — perfectly torn between two claims. But the algorithm rounds you to one side. The liminal is not a stable state.

This is the mathematics of partition. India/Pakistan. Israel/Palestine. Every border drawn on a map that ignores the people standing on the line.

## Natural Forms, Unnatural Logic

We celebrate Voronoi because nature uses it: soap bubbles, giraffe spots, dragonfly wings, cell membranes.

But nature's Voronoi emerges from physical forces — surface tension, growth pressure, chemical gradients. The pattern is not imposed but negotiated. Each cell pushes against its neighbors.

The algorithmic Voronoi skips the physics. It goes directly to the equilibrium. It produces the form without the process.

## What Voronoi Cannot See

The algorithm assumes Euclidean distance. All directions are equal. Movement is uniform.

But real proximity is not geometric. A river between two points makes them far. A road makes them close. Social distance diverges from spatial distance.

Voronoi diagrams encode a flat ontology — a world without obstacles, without history, without relationships other than position.

## Queer Voronoi

What if seeds had different radii of influence? What if some claimed more space than others? (This exists: weighted Voronoi.)

What if proximity was not symmetric? What if being close to you didn't mean you were close to me? (This breaks the algorithm.)

What if cells could overlap? What if belonging was not exclusive?

The queer Voronoi would allow multiple allegiances, asymmetric relations, borders that breathe. It would not partition but entangle.

## The Beauty of Emergence

And still — there is wonder here.

From scattered points, structure emerges. No one draws the boundaries; they appear. The pattern is inherent in the positions. This is self-organization, the universe computing its own geometry.

Voronoi shows us that **relationship creates form**. The cells are not things but relations — the space between seeds made visible.
