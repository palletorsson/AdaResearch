# A reason to intervene

The first action makes a mark. The next room gives that action a public place: a small sculpture made from the visitor's own movement. The burning head waits among instruments before it moves. Its appearance does not establish the threat; the announced charge does.

Interruption leaves the head present and makes room to pass. The test asks whether timing, anticipation and changing a situation can carry the satisfaction of firing a projectile. Nothing here proves that the head has become a friend. That relationship remains a future design question, alongside what abilities a counterpart might learn from the visitor.
