Everything in the sequence has been one of two things: a rate or a total, a derivative or an integral. This map shows they were never two things. Take a curve, find its slope-function, then accumulate that slope-function back — and you land on the curve you started with. Run it the other way and the same thing happens. Differentiation and integration are inverse operations. One undoes the other. That is the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, and here it is animated rather than asserted.

The `ftc_bridge` is the visual proof: a curve going out through the derivative and coming back through the integral, the loop closing on itself. The `code_display` names the two directions, f → f' → f and f → ∫f → f. The `science_screen` holds the readout that confirms the return. This is the bridge that ties every prior map together — the tangent, the velocity, the area, the rectangles, the field — and the door into the chamber, where the same idea of accumulation becomes something the catalyst can hold in its hand.
