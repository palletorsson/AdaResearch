Concept: A pressurized soft-body sphere is dropped inside a tapered mould — narrow at the top (radius ~0.25), wide at the bottom (radius ~0.7), with radius varying smoothly along the vertical axis. Internal pressure pushes vertices outward; contact constraints against the tapered wall stop them at whatever radius the cavity defines at their current height. The body settles into a classical amphora silhouette — narrow neck, bulging body. The map is a gallery study; the player walks around the single soft-body marker.

Sequence role: Second glass-meets-mould study (gm02) in the imported soft-body gallery cluster within the Soft Bodies sequence (13th spine, integration phase). Pair-contrast with gm01: gm01's cavity has constant radius (cylindrical) → uniform bottle; gm02's cavity has varying radius (tapered) → amphora. Together they document the simplest distinction in cavity-driven form: constant vs varying cross-section. The cluster continues with gm03 (rectangular cross-section) and gm04 (external obstruction). Also a cross-family contrast with gl02: gl02 made a long bottle by pinning the sphere at a narrow neck-ring and letting gravity stretch the lower mass; gm02 makes a similar silhouette by letting the body inflate freely against a cavity that has a narrow neck and a wide belly. Two paths to a comparable form — the gl path uses pin geometry, the gm path uses cavity geometry.

Technical angle: Spherical mesh of mass points with structural and bending springs. Pre-inflation pushes the sphere out to ~70% of average mould radius. Mould collision: at each vertex, sample the mould's radius at the vertex's current height (linear interpolation or function evaluation between the rim radius and base radius). If the vertex's distance from the central axis exceeds the local cavity radius, project it back to the boundary and zero the outward velocity component. Internal pressure applied per-face proportional to area; gravity applied to every mass. Verlet or PBD integration for ~200 steps. Because the cavity radius increases from top to bottom, vertices in the upper third of the body reach the wall at a small radius (forming the neck), vertices in the middle reach the wall at the intermediate radius (forming the shoulder), and vertices in the lower third reach the wall at the large radius (forming the belly). The amphora's characteristic profile is a direct readout of the mould's radius function.

Critical angle: The gm-family insists that the cavity geometry is itself a form-encoding. A taper rate is a single number. A taper function is a one-dimensional curve. Either way, the cavity's geometry encodes the vessel's silhouette before any body enters. The simulation's job is just to drive the body into contact everywhere; the body's job is just to inflate. The vessel that results is the cavity's silhouette of revolution made physical. This is why industrial glassblowing scales — the human craft is moved into mould-design, which is performed once per vessel type and reused indefinitely. The simulation makes the same move legible: design the cavity, get the vessel, regardless of which exact body started inside it.

Key artifacts: gallery_marker_soft-body holds the gm02_vase_in_tapered_mould configuration. The amphora pressed against the tapered cavity is the artifact and the demonstration of cavity-as-silhouette.

Gap: A taper-rate slider on the gallery marker would let learners watch the silhouette transform across the cylinder ↔ taper continuum, passing through the bottle of gm01 on its way to the deeper amphora. Same physics; one parameter on the cavity; visibly different vessel.
