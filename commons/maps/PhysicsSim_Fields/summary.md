# Physics Simulation: Fields & Emergent Dynamics - Map Summary

## Overview

This map teaches the physics of invisible influence. Where Springs connected bodies directly, Fields introduces action-at-a-distance — every point in space carries a force vector, and particles moving through these fields create emergent behavior that no individual particle intended. The sequence culminates in the three-body problem, which proves that deterministic rules can produce effectively unpredictable outcomes.

## Key Elements

### Interactables

| Artifact | Position | Purpose |
|----------|----------|---------|
| `force_fields` | First station | Assign a force vector to every point in space. Radial (gravity wells, repulsors), uniform (wind, gravity), vortex (whirlpools). The player sees invisible architecture made visible. |
| `vector_fields` | Second station | Visualization of fields as arrow grids, streamlines, or color maps. The act of *seeing* a field — translating the invisible to the visible. Curl, divergence, flow. |
| `particle_systems` | Third station | Many particles responding to fields simultaneously. Emitters, lifetimes, size/color curves. The first many-body system — fire, smoke, rain, sparks. |
| `nbody_simulation` | Fourth station | Gravitational democracy: every body attracts every other body. O(n²) force calculation. Stable orbits, unstable orbits, gravitational slingshots. The computational cost of mutual interaction. |
| `three_body_problem` | Fifth station | The climax: three bodies under mutual gravitation. Deterministic equations, chaotic trajectories. Tiny changes in initial conditions → wildly different outcomes. The limit of predictability. |

## Atmosphere

- **Feel**: An observatory or planetarium. Dark background with luminous particles tracing paths through invisible fields. The aesthetic shifts from Springs' organic warmth to Fields' cosmic scale.
- **Visual emphasis**: Trails, trajectories, vector arrows. Everything is about *path* — where things go when invisible forces guide them.
- **Scale shift**: From the intimate (single force field) to the cosmic (n-body gravitational simulation). The room should feel like it expands as you progress.

## Learning Sequence

1. **Player enters** from PhysicsSim_Springs, transitioning from elastic connections to action-at-a-distance.
2. **Force fields** — learns that forces can exist everywhere simultaneously. No connection needed — just position determines force. Radial, uniform, and vortex fields.
3. **Vector fields** — sees the field itself. Arrows at every point, streamlines showing flow. The invisible made visible. Learns curl and divergence intuitively.
4. **Particle systems** — many particles in the field at once. Emergence: fire isn't one particle, it's thousands responding to the same upward field with turbulence. The first truly many-body system.
5. **N-body simulation** — the fields are generated by the bodies themselves. Every particle is both source and subject. Gravitational interactions, orbital mechanics, the beauty and cost of O(n²).
6. **Three-body problem** — the capstone. Only three bodies, but their mutual interaction produces chaos. The player adjusts initial conditions and watches trajectories diverge. Determinism ≠ predictability.
7. **Exits** to PhysicsSim_Continuum — from discrete particles to continuous matter.

## Design Intent

The five-station sequence tells the story of increasing interaction complexity: external field → visualization → many particles in a field → particles making their own fields → chaos from simplicity. The three-body problem at the end is philosophically crucial — it establishes that simulation doesn't guarantee prediction, a theme that resonates through all computational science.

## Connection to Sequence

**Position**: Map 4 of 5 in the Physics Simulation sequence.
**Prerequisite**: PhysicsSim_Springs (elastic forces, multi-body systems) — fields generalize the idea of forces between bodies.
**Prepares for**: PhysicsSim_Continuum (fluids, FEM, soft bodies) — continuous media are the limit case of infinitely many particles with infinitely short-range interactions.
**Key handoff**: The player leaves understanding that fields assign forces to space, many-body interactions are computationally expensive, and even simple deterministic systems can be chaotic. Continuum mechanics will trade discrete particles for continuous fields of matter.
