A pendulum hangs beside a line that does not move. Take the bob, let it go, and watch it pass through the ring at the bottom of that line twice: once going one way, once coming back. The label above the pivot reads the angle and the angular velocity with its sign, and counts the crossings. The same place, two states.

Nothing here tells the bob where to be. Each physics step, gravity's pull — proportional to the sine of the angle — changes the velocity, the velocity changes the angle, and the return comes out of that. Your release becomes the first velocity. A wide swing takes longer than the length-only rule promises, and the drawn prediction behind the swing shows the standard release it was made for, not yours.

Around the pendulum, cubes that bob to a sine of the clock, a cube that only spins, a cube driven by the pendulum's own signal, and a trace that records motion in time. Take hold of the bob and the swing stops; the sine-driven cube would not notice you.
