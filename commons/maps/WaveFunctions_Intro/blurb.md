# Oscillation Control Room

A small room lined with oscilloscopes. Green traces sweep left to right — sine, square, sawtooth, triangle. Each one a different signature of the same principle: periodic motion between extremes.

Four cubes teach the grammar. The first sits still — a baseline, pure rest. The second rides a vertical rail: y = A·sin(ωt), position mapped from time through sine. The third spins: θ += ω·dt, constant angular velocity, no return. The fourth takes input from a pendulum — one swing drives position, rotation, and scale simultaneously. One signal, three outputs. Oscillation as control language.

Sliders let you feel what the parameters do. Amplitude sets the range. Frequency sets the speed. Phase shifts the starting point. Springs stretch and snap back under Hooke's Law. Everything here moves — but nothing leaves. That's the definition. Oscillation is motion that refuses arrival.
