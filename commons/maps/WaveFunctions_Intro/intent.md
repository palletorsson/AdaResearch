Concept: The first wave room. A pendulum the visitor releases by hand, measured against a still reference — a plumb line and a ring at the rest point — so that two visits to the same place can be told apart by their direction. Around it, cubes whose motion is prescribed by sin(t), a spinning cube, a cube driven by the pendulum's own signal, and a time trace: the integrated swing set beside motions that were only told where to be.
Sequence role: Opens the Wavefunctions sequence, after Formfinding on the current spine. Formfinding left a shape at rest; this room shows that a body can persist through returning motion as well. Leads to WaveFunctions_Pendulum, which gives the swing somewhere to leave its history.
Technical angle: A pendulum integrated per physics step — angular acceleration -(g/L)·sin(θ), velocity and angle updated from it, a per-regime damping multiplier — against cubes that evaluate A·sin(ωt+φ) from a clock. The release converts the hand's last positions into an initial angular velocity along the swing. The label reads θ and signed ω; the ring lights on each centre crossing; under evidence=trace the predicted θ(t) for the standard release is drawn behind the swing with the same update rule.
Critical angle: Position is not state. The same place is visited with opposite velocities, so a still picture of "the bob at the centre" misses half of what is there. A prescribed sine has no memory and cannot be interrupted by a hand; an integrated pendulum can. The length-only period rule is a small-angle claim, and the room lets a large release show where it stops holding.
Key artifacts: control_pendulum#reference:plumb#evidence:trace is the primary encounter; oscillation_controlled_cube is driven by its signal (one signal, three transformations); pickup_cube_static, transformation_cube and y_oscillation_cube are the prescribed-motion comparisons; rotating_cube and rotating_cube_demo show rotation without return; mario_cube_time_trace records a motion in time and hands off to the next room; dark_sphere anchors.
