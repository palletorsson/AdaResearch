Concept: Slime mold builds networks — no brain, no neurons, yet Physarum polycephalum solves mazes, recreates the Tokyo rail network, and finds shortest paths through sheer material computation. The organism is a single cell that grows tubular networks by reinforcing high-flow paths and pruning low-flow ones. Stigmergy in its purest biological form.
Sequence role: First map in Swarm Intelligence (14th spine sequence, integration phase), intermediate difficulty. Opens the sequence with the most striking example of leaderless coordination: a unicellular organism that computes. Establishes the sequence thesis — no leader, yet coordinated; the environment is the message. Connects forward to SwarmIntelligence_FlowFeilds where the flow-field abstraction generalizes what Physarum does biologically.
Technical angle: Agent-based Physarum simulation where virtual agents deposit chemical trail on a 2D grid, sense trail concentration at forward-offset sensors, and turn toward higher concentration. Trail diffuses and decays over time. The emergent network topology approximates Steiner trees — minimum-cost networks connecting multiple points. Parameters: sensor angle, sensor offset, turn angle, deposit rate, decay rate, diffusion kernel.
Critical angle: Physarum collapses the distinction between computation and material process. When slime mold solves a maze, there is no algorithm running on a substrate — the substrate is the algorithm. This is the radical claim of the integration phase: intelligence is not a property of minds but of systems. Physarum's network optimization preceded human network theory by millions of years. The environment is both medium and message.
Key artifacts: PhysarumColony simulates the growth, network formation, and path optimization behavior of Physarum polycephalum using agent-based trail deposition and reinforcement.
Gap: Needs a multi-food-source configuration tool — an artifact that lets the learner place food sources and watch the network self-organize to connect them, with a cost metric comparing Physarum's solution to the mathematically optimal Steiner tree.
