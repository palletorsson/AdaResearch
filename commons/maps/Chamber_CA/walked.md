## The cast

## The walk

The catalyst chamber that closes the chapter. The becoming_catalyst waits; the targets stand ready to take a mode; the science_screen flattens the room's state into a pane; the proximity_spawner answers your approach with population. The chamber's floor plan is the chapter's grammar one last time — a neighborhood, a rule for what happens when things meet, and consequences that propagate.

## The living floor (authored 2026-07-21)

Twelve dormant cells, and for the first time the floor carries an *argument*
rather than a decoration — because this chapter's subject is exactly the
question the biome's two fungus substrates disagree about.

**The computed.** Cells at the practice target's feet are `fungus:ca` — a
cellular automaton — and they wake only to `catalyst.cellular`, the mode the
pedestal hands you. The chapter's own algorithm, grown by the chapter's own
tool. A chromatic shot does nothing here: the room wants *its* rule.

**The grown.** Everything the converted creatures touch is `fungus:mycelium` —
filaments that branch because local food ran out, not because a rule said to.
Any friend blooms the greeter by the pedestal; the two muted cells flanking the
vent open only under a `friend.replicator` walking back across its own
battlefield; and the south trail seeds and claims its way toward the door as the
flock escorts you out.

Same kingdom, two ontologies, and you earn them differently: the automaton by
computing it, the web by converting something. The room does not say which is
truer — it puts them on the same floor and lets the walk decide. Verified in
walk order (8/8, including the wrong-mode no-op); boots dormant, 0 invalid.

## The seed

What this chamber wants to hand over is the chapter's verb: *to seed*. Everywhere else you watched initial conditions unfold; here the initial condition should be yours to place — a touch that starts a cascade, a cell lit by hand. The catalyst's exact grant in the CA register is still an open design decision, but its form is fixed by the sequence: the player leaves not knowing more about automata, but *being* one more rule the world now runs with.
