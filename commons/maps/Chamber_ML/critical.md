# The learner is the training set — the gradient hunter and the politics of being optimised against

Chamber_ML stages an encounter in which the creature improves by studying the learner's movements. The gradient_hunter samples the learner's position each frame and updates its prediction of where the learner will go next. The learner is not only the participant in the encounter; they are also the training signal, and the creature's policy refines only to the extent that the learner is legible.

Shoshana Zuboff's analysis of surveillance capitalism argues that the most valuable commodity in contemporary data economies is behavioural surplus — the data produced by ordinary activity, beyond what the activity itself requires. Platforms harvest this surplus and train models on it, and the models' accuracy depends on users behaving predictably enough to be modelled. Zuboff's diagnosis applies directly to Chamber_ML. The learner is the data source, and the gradient_hunter is the model.

The chamber is small and bare. A single hunter paces the floor. Every time the learner moves, the hunter samples the move as a training example and updates its prediction of the learner's next position. The hunter's loss function is the distance between its prediction and the learner's actual next position, and the loss drops over time as the hunter learns the learner's patterns.

The science screen on the wall plots the hunter's loss over time. Early in the encounter, the hunter's predictions are random and the loss is high. As the encounter continues, the loss drops. The screen's plot is a live graph of the hunter's success in modelling the learner, and the learner can watch themselves being learned.

Zuboff's argument about behavioural surplus lands on the learner's counter-practice. The learner's only defence against the hunter is to move unpredictably. Regular movement gives the hunter gradient to follow; noise withholds gradient. The learner can escape being modelled by moving in ways that do not fit the hunter's assumptions, but the escape is exhausting: unpredictable movement is harder to sustain than predictable movement, and the hunter is patient.

The chamber's catalyst slot is empty. There is no projection to emit. The encounter is between the hunter's optimiser and the learner's behavioural output, and the learner's only contributions are their movements. The emptiness of the catalyst is deliberate: the chamber's argument is about optimisation under surveillance, and adding a projection tool would change the subject from optimisation to combat.

The politics of being optimised against are in the asymmetry. The hunter has an optimiser; the learner does not. The hunter's predictions get better over time; the learner's movements do not automatically get less predictable. The asymmetry is Zuboff's political structure at a small scale: the surveilling system has computational resources and time that the surveilled subject does not, and the result is a steady drift toward legibility.

The gradient_hunter's learning is visible rather than hidden, which is one of the chamber's departures from the surveillance-capitalism model. Zuboff's critique is partly about the invisibility of the modelling: the surveilled subject does not know they are being modelled, and so cannot adapt their behaviour. The chamber shows the modelling on the science screen, and the showing gives the learner a chance to react. The visibility is a small corrective, and the corrective is what distinguishes the chamber as a pedagogical encounter rather than as a simulation of surveillance.

Within the sequence, Chamber_ML reframes catalyst practice as mutual learning. The creature is the optimiser; the learner is the distribution. The chamber hands the learner back to the Lab after the sequence's last demonstration of what it means to learn from another body's behaviour. The Machine Learning sequence has spent eight maps developing optimisation as a skill; this chamber insists that the skill has a political dimension, and the dimension is who gets to optimise whom.
