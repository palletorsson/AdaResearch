# Random Definition — working map brief

From random values to repeatable spatial experiences

**Can the same random values produce different spatial experiences—and what must we keep to recreate them?**

This is an editorial proposal for testing the room, not a claim that its research questions are settled. The writing wall’s Map brief tab shows these subjects and links them to the current numbered artifacts. Tutorial/Final tags select writing contributions independently of these subject roles.

## Core instruments

- Seed Study — 48 Values / Five Spatial Assignments
- Seed Replay Demo
- PRNG Crank Machine
- Seed source console

The 48-value seed bench remains the first pilot. Complete baseline, one-condition change, observation, preservation, replay and independent reconstruction; use the result to propose a further change. A confusing difference or failed comparison is useful evidence.

## 1. Assignment and range

**Where is randomness allowed to act?**

- **Seed Study — 48 Values / Five Spatial Assignments**: First pilot: compare colour, height, size and rotation using the same 48 stored values.
- **Seed Replay Demo**: Compare channel assignment or output range against the unchanged colour baseline.
- **Out / Back — the carried point**: Experience sampled travel inside an authored rule that brings the sphere back.

Try: Keep the values fixed. Change one assignment or its range. Describe what becomes noticeable.

## 2. Seed, state and procedure

**What makes an outcome reproducible?**

- **PRNG Crank Machine**: Step and reset the state transition; distinguish the starting seed from the current state and output.
- **A number has a previous state**: Optional close view of the procedure beside the crank.
- **Seed Replay Demo**: Change draw order separately from assignment; a seed alone is not the complete picture recipe.

Try: Keep seed, draw order, mapping and version. Replay the result, then reconstruct the procedure independently.

## 3. Source and retained record

**What arrived, what was kept, and what is generated again?**

- **Seed source console**: Follow a chosen input through its record or conversion into the seed used by a generator.
- **Seed movement sphere — MOVE / KEEP**: Make a gesture, keep its record, and inspect what survives in the derived seed.
- **Random Number Book Page 1955**: Ask how a stored outcome remains addressable; contrast that requirement with the installation’s falling digits.
- **TRNG vs PRNG**: Support the distinction between physical sources and deterministic generation by checking provenance, not appearance.

Try: Record provenance and conversion into a seed. Compare a fresh capture with reuse of its saved result.

## 4. Observation and measurement

**What changes when the apparatus observes differently?**

- **Time / frame study**: Contrast a measured interval with a counter or generated value. Irregular appearance alone does not establish a source of entropy.
- **Lava Wall — What Can You Predict?**: Extension: change the sensor mask on a repeatable simulated lamp process.
- **Geiger Counter — Counting the Gaps**: Extension: change clock resolution on one simulated event trace and reconstruct the resulting interval-comparison bits.

Try: Hold a trace or simulated process fixed. Change only the display range, sensor mask or clock. Keep an observation that motivates the next revision.

## Scope and handoff

The virtual lava and Geiger studies are extensions of the observation question. Their simulated records are not evidence of fresh physical entropy. Keep the distinction between replaying a retained result and acquiring a new outcome explicit. This room investigates usable procedures and their limits rather than settling whether the universe is fundamentally random.

**Slot Machine**: Exit into counting: what do outcome frequencies preserve or lose about an individual encounter?

## What this changes in the texts

The tutorial should first let a learner reconstruct one result, then introduce machinery and source distinctions when the experiment needs them. The final text should follow what was noticed and which choices made that experience possible. Either text can propose a new experiment. The four topics are questions for investigation rather than four required textbook sections.

The paired RGB grid tutorial remains its own reconstruction: 48 displayed scalar draws in a 4 × 4 RGB grid are not the same experiment as the bench’s five assignments of 48 stored values. Existing tutorial.md and final.md remain intact while this brief is tested.
