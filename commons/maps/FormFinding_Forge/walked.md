## The cast

## The walk

Now shape your own — but not the way a sculptor does. Feed raw matter into the `self_shaping_forge` and it does not take the form you press onto it; it *descends*, sliding down its own energy landscape toward its liveliest configuration, and emerges as whatever that turned out to be. The `gradient_descent_optimizer` beside it is the bare mechanism you can watch step by step — a soft body reading the slope beneath it and stepping downhill, an energy readout ticking lower with each move until it flattens. Then the `balance_puzzle` hands you the proof: stack pieces, each drop translating and reorienting a block, until the whole system *stops moving*. You are not building a target shape and checking it against a picture. You are letting a configuration fall to its rest, and the moment it goes still, you have solved it — the resting form is the answer, and your hands found it the way the marble found the bottom of the bowl in the first map.

## The turn (critical)

The forge is the chapter's closing argument, and its turn is a reversal of what "making" means. Every other way the curriculum has let you build — snap the tetrahedron, compose the transform, paint the domain — was *imposition*: you had a target and you pressed the world toward it. The forge refuses that. It does not let you press a shape in from outside; it lets matter *fall* to its shape, and your role is not to dictate the form but to set the landscape and release. That is form-finding as a stance rather than a technique, and it rhymes all the way back to the chapter's first marble: you do not carve the form, you *find* it, by arranging the conditions and letting descent do the rest. And the `balance_puzzle`'s proof-by-stillness is the honest test of the whole idea — mastery here is not making the system do what you want but making it *stop*, come to rest at the minimum it was always going to find, with your hand only guiding it toward the basin. It closes the arc that forces opened two chapters back: forces set things in motion; form-finding is the discipline of the resting place, the shape that motion settles into when you stop pushing and let equilibrium answer. The forge's last quiet lesson, carried out of the chapter: the best forms are not the ones you impose, but the ones you make it possible for matter to fall into.
