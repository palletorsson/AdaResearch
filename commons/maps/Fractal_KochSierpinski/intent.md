Concept: The Koch curve — infinite length contained in finite area. Every edge becomes a spike, every spike spawns sub-spikes, and the boundary grows without bound while the enclosed region remains bounded. D=1.262 captures the dimension between line and plane.
Sequence role: Fourth map in the Fractals sequence (10th spine, lambda_edge phase). After Cantor's deletion (Fractals_3), Koch introduces addition — the curve grows by replacing, not removing. The Sierpinski pyramid artifact previews Fractals_5's triangle in 3D, creating a forward thread. The cube_cabin applies fractal logic to architectural form, grounding abstraction. Koch and Cantor together establish the two fundamental fractal operations: remove and replace.
Technical angle: Koch construction by replacing each line segment's middle third with an equilateral spike; after n iterations, length = (4/3)^n, approaching infinity. D=log(4)/log(3)=1.262. The koch_curve_3d extrudes the 2D Koch snowflake into 3D, demonstrating how fractal dimension transforms under extrusion (D_3d = D_2d + 1). The sierpinski_pyramid implements the Sierpinski tetrahedron as a preview of the next map's triangle.
Critical angle: Koch curve is the boundary that refuses to be measured — infinite length at every scale, yet enclosing finite area. This paradox was historically dismissed as pathological, but the lambda_edge reading inverts the judgment: the pathological is the natural. Coastlines, mountain ridges, and cloud boundaries are Koch-like, not Euclidean. The smooth curve is the exception; the fractal boundary is the rule.
Key artifacts: fractal_koch_curve implements the 2D Koch snowflake construction; koch_curve_3d extends Koch into three dimensions; sierpinski_pyramid previews Fractals_5 with the 3D Sierpinski analog; cube_cabin applies recursive geometry to architectural space; dark_sphere anchors.
