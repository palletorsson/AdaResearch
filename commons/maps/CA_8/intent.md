Concept: Continuous cellular automata — replacing discrete states with floating-point values and discrete grids with curved surfaces. SmoothLife generalizes Conway's Game of Life to continuous space and state, and wrapping the automaton onto a sphere eliminates edge boundaries entirely.
Sequence role: Eighth map in the Cellular Automata sequence; the transition from discrete to continuous, paralleling the sequence's arc from simple to general. After stochastic rules (CA_7) blurred deterministic transitions, continuous CA blur the state space itself. The sphere topology eliminates the artificial boundary conditions that plague flat grids — every cell is interior, every direction equivalent. Connects forward to CA_9's 3D automata (another topological generalization) and backward to CA_5's multi-state gradients (discrete approximation of continuity).
Technical angle: SmoothLife (Stephan Rafler, 2011): replaces discrete cells with continuous fields, and discrete neighborhoods with smooth kernel functions (inner disk for cell state, outer ring for neighbor integral). The transition function is a smooth sigmoid rather than a step function. On a sphere, the 2D surface is periodic in all directions — no edges, no corners, uniform neighbor geometry everywhere. The ca_sphere artifact renders this continuous automaton on a spherical surface.
Critical angle: Continuous CA challenge the definition of "cellular" — if cells are infinitesimal and states are real-valued, the automaton becomes a partial differential equation discretized on a mesh. SmoothLife sits at the boundary between automata theory and continuum physics, suggesting that the discrete/continuous distinction is a modeling choice, not an ontological fact. The sphere topology raises a deeper question: are flat-grid CA artifacts of Cartesian thinking, and does curvature change what can be computed?
Key artifacts: ca_sphere renders continuous cellular automata on a spherical surface, eliminating boundary artifacts and demonstrating SmoothLife dynamics on curved topology.
