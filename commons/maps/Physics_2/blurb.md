# Physics 2

One dimension is a corridor. Two dimensions are a world. The grid extends along X and Y — columns and rows, width and depth. Every cell has an address. Every address has two coordinates. Navigation becomes a problem of knowing where you are twice.

Dark spheres pulse at grid intersections, purple light breathing against the lattice. Move through the array and the structure reveals itself — not a list but a plane, not a sequence but a territory. Falling boxes test whether you can think in two axes simultaneously.

A 1D array is memory. A 2D array is space. The jump from one index to two is the jump from reading to mapping. Flatland's entire politics reduced to a data structure.
