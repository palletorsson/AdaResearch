Arrays become architecture. A conveyor belt carries raw shapes — cubes, pyramids — toward ghost projections that demand specific combinations. Place the right form in the right slot. The production line doesn't stop.

This is the 2D grid made consequential. X marks column, Y marks row, and every coordinate is a decision: which shape goes where, which slot remains empty, which assembly completes the house. The grid is no longer abstract. It builds things.

Workstations compound the problem. Equipment assembled in one stage becomes material for the next — microscopes, scales, diagnostic rigs snapping into designated positions. Composition from composition. Arrays indexing arrays.

A factory is just a grid that moves. The coordinates stay fixed; the objects flowing through them do not. Structure permits passage.
