Concept: A jelly-grid soft body is built from a regular 3D lattice of mass points connected by structural and bending springs, dropped under gravity with light damping, and allowed to settle to equilibrium. After equilibrium, a cellular-noise (Worley/Voronoi) field is sampled at each settled vertex position; the field's value drives a displacement that pushes vertices radially. The result is a faceted, crystalline surface whose facets are the cells of the cellular-noise diagram. The map is a gallery study; the player walks around the single soft-body marker.

Sequence role: First and only noise→soft-body bridge (sb_noise01) in the imported soft-body gallery cluster within the Soft Bodies sequence (13th spine, integration phase). Closes the rule-as-loom set: rd writes the stiffness field (sb_rd01/sb_rd02), ls writes the branching skeleton (sb_ls01/sb_ls02), ca writes the spring topology (sb_ca01/sb_ca02), and noise01 writes the surface displacement. The four rule families form a matrix: rule type × body layer. Each family writes onto a different layer of the same physical body.

Technical angle: Standard jelly-grid construction — N×N×N mass points (default ~6×6×6), structural springs along grid edges, bending springs across diagonals to preserve volume, top layer pinned. Verlet or PBD integration for ~200 timesteps under gravity. Post-settle, a 3D Worley noise function is sampled at each vertex's world position. The noise value (typically distance-to-nearest-feature-point or the F2-F1 difference) is used as a displacement magnitude along the vertex normal. The resulting mesh has cell-boundary creases wherever two adjacent vertices fell on opposite sides of a Voronoi cell boundary, which is the visual signature of crystalline fracture.

Critical angle: The post-process matters because it stages a separation that the simulation otherwise hides — the difference between what the physics computes and what the surface looks like. The springs compute a stable equilibrium. The noise paints a fracture pattern on top. The fracture has no physical meaning — there is no stress field, no failure mode, no actual crystallography. But the eye reads it as crystalline structure because the Voronoi pattern matches how real crystals fracture (along grain boundaries). The map is honest about this: the body is jelly underneath, the surface is a noise function on top, and the gemstone appearance is the perceptual sum. This is the integration-phase claim made small: form is what a sequence of rules collectively leaves behind, not what any single rule computed.

Key artifacts: gallery_marker_soft-body holds the sb_noise01_jelly_crystal configuration. The faceted crystalline surface is the artifact and the demonstration of noise-as-skin.

Gap: A noise-frequency slider on the gallery marker would let learners watch the facets coarsen and refine — high frequency makes a finely-cracked surface, low frequency makes a few large planes. The same body, the same physics, different noise scale, completely different vessel.
