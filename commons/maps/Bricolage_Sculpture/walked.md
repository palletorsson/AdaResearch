## The walk

The same constraint as the chair — balance — but here it stops being a requirement and becomes a medium. The counterweight_demo shows the lever law with the arithmetic actually holding, the big mass close to the fulcrum, the small one far out. The tension_demo holds a load two ways, a thin tie pulling where a thick strut pushes. The sculpture_builder catches the negotiation in progress. And at the back, two finished works — balanced_sculpture_1 counterweighted, balanced_sculpture_2 three members leaning into a shared cap — both upright, sharing no strategy at all.

## The world

The world room widens the chapter from making things that work to making things that mean. The chair had one job and one solution; a sculpture has neither. Its only requirement is that it stand, and the map's title is exact — *meaning through relation*: the balance is not in any part but in how the parts answer each other. What makes this the sequence's most generous room is the two works that disagree. Both satisfy the identical constraint, by opposite means, and both are right — which is the proof that a constraint, fully met, does not close a form but opens it. Gravity fixes one number and leaves everything else to you. The engineer minimises to a single optimum; the bricoleur discovers that the world of the balanced is plural, and lives there.
