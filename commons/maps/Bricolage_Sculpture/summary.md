# Bricolage_Sculpture - Summary

## Overview

No function except standing. No blueprint except balance. This map explores sculpture as bricolage at its purest—constraint satisfaction without utility. Where the chair had "sit-ability," the sculpture has only "stand-ability." Meaning emerges from visual relations, not from use.

## Spatial Layout

A 13×13 gallery space with sculpture demonstrations at the perimeter and an interactive builder at center. The square layout creates a gallery-like contemplation environment.

## Atmosphere

Gallery lighting—cooler than workshop, more dramatic directional light. Background color deeper (0.2, 0.2, 0.25) to focus attention on sculptural forms. The atmosphere shifts from making to contemplating.

## Learning Sequence

1. Enter gallery space—notice the shift from workshop atmosphere
2. Examine balanced_sculpture_1: observe how it stands without function
3. Examine balanced_sculpture_2: compare different approaches to balance
4. Pass through dark_sphere
5. Use sculpture_builder: assemble abstract forms that satisfy only standing
6. Examine counterweight_demo: understand how cantilevers balance
7. Examine tension_demo: understand how tension creates stability
8. Recognize: sculpture is negotiation with gravity, nothing more
9. Exit to Dome—structure scaled up

## Design Intent

The contrast with the chair is deliberate: the chair serves a function (sitting), the sculpture serves none (except existing). This reveals that bricolage doesn't require utility—constraint satisfaction alone produces form.

The technique demonstrations (counterweight, tension) show that sculptors and bricoleurs share methods: both work outward from constraints, not inward from vision.

## Theoretical Framework

### Tatlin's Constructivism
The blurb references Tatlin: "Tatlin was a bricoleur." Vladimir Tatlin's constructivist sculptures emerged from material properties, not from representational goals. The material "wanted" to take certain forms; the sculptor discovered rather than imposed.

### Balance as Constraint
Sculpture reduces bricolage to its essence:
- **Gravity**: Must reach ground or be supported
- **Balance**: Center of mass over base (or counterweighted)
- **Connectivity**: Parts must hold together (or be in tension)

No ergonomic constraints, no functional requirements—just standing.

### Visual Mass vs. Physical Mass
Sculpture introduces a new consideration: visual mass—how heavy something *looks*. A small dense form can visually balance a large light form. This is design beyond physics, where perception matters alongside constraints.
