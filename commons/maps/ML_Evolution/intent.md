Concept: Evolutionary algorithms as the precursor to gradient-based learning — before calculus-driven optimization, nature discovered search through variation, selection, and reproduction. A population of candidate solutions evolves toward fitness without any single agent knowing the landscape.
Sequence role: First map in the Machine Learning sequence (15th spine sequence, integration phase). Opens the ML arc with biology rather than calculus, grounding optimization in intuition before formalizing it. Connects forward to ML_Gradient_Landscape where evolution's random search is replaced by directed descent.
Technical angle: Genetic algorithms with crossover, mutation, and selection operators. Fitness function as the only feedback signal. Population-based search vs. single-point search — the trade-off between exploration (mutation rate) and exploitation (selection pressure). Tournament selection, roulette wheel selection, elitism strategies.
Critical angle: Evolution is optimization without a gradient — proof that intelligence can emerge from blind variation and selective retention. The absence of a global view is the point: no individual in the population understands the fitness landscape, yet the population as a whole navigates it. This is the integration phase thesis: learning is distributed, emergent, and fundamentally about landscapes you cannot fully see.
Key artifacts: evolutionary_algorithms demonstrates population-based search with configurable mutation, crossover, and selection parameters.
Gap: Needs a fitness landscape visualizer that shows the population distribution evolving across generations, making visible how diversity collapses as the population converges.
