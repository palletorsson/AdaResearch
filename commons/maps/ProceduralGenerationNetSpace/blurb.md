# PG: Tesseract Net Space

A tesseract has eight cubic cells. Unfold them into three dimensions and you get a net — a cross-shaped arrangement of cubes laid flat in space you can walk through. Dalí painted one. Mathematicians enumerate 261 distinct versions. This is one of them, made navigable.

Each cube is a room. Each shared face is a doorway. The net preserves adjacency — which cells touch which — but sacrifices the closure that makes a tesseract a tesseract. To flatten is to cut. The fourth dimension becomes a series of turns through three-dimensional corridors.

Stand inside the unfolded hypercube. The structure is legible now, all eight cells accounted for. But legibility cost a dimension. Every map of a higher world is a wound — topology opened so that lower beings can read it.
