# The criterion is a small law

Evolutionary computation is often narrated as nature’s creativity translated into code. That story hides the evaluator. Forms do not survive because they are naturally better; they survive because a procedure assigns them a score and spends future computation on the ones that score well.

The workbench makes that allocation tangible. Selection is a micro-political order: it distributes time, reproduction, attention, and disappearance. Mutation can open a margin, but only the criterion decides whether the margin is allowed to persist.

This is where the queer question enters without becoming a decorative synonym for difference. Change the criterion and the possible body changes. Refuse the demand for one optimum, and the population can remain plural. The algorithm becomes speculative when it lets us test which worlds a rule can host, and which bodies its measure quietly excludes.
