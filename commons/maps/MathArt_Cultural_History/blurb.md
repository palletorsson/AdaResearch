# Mathematics and Art: A Cultural History

> "Mathematicians and artists have long been on a quest to understand the physical world they see before them and the abstract objects they know by thought alone."
> — Lynn Gamwell

## The Gallery

Begin with **construction**: assemble a shelf from simple rectangles. Donald Judd showed that furniture is geometry made useful—vertical supports, horizontal planes, the grammar of space.

Move to **abstraction**: Biederman's reliefs project colored bars into space, each depth a decision about light and shadow. Mondrian's grids reduce nature to essence—horizontal, vertical, primary. Albers nests squares to reveal how colors change each other.

At the center: **mathematical pattern**. Penrose tilings achieve the impossible—perfect order without repetition, infinite variety from finite rules.

## The Question

What connects a carpenter's shelf to a mathematician's proof?

Both are arrangements. Both follow rules. Both reveal structure through constraint.

Self-reflection is inherent to both practices. Gödel asked questions about mathematics *in the language of mathematics*. Johns asked "What is art?" *in the vocabulary of art*.

Here, you ask: What is form? And you answer with your hands.
