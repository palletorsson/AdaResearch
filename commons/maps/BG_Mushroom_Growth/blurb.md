# BG Mushroom Growth

Mycelium grows underground. No eyes, no map, no plan — just branching hyphae extending through soil, forking at obstacles, fusing when they meet themselves. The network that emerges moves nutrients across meters of forest floor. A mushroom is just the fruiting body. The organism is the web beneath.

Colonization without coordination. Each hyphal tip follows moisture gradients and chemical signals, growing toward food, away from waste. Where paths overlap, they merge and thicken. Fairy rings form — not by design but by depletion. The mycelium eats outward from its origin, leaving dead center behind. A circle drawn by hunger.

No optimization. No shortest path. Mycelium doesn't solve — it persists. Growth as its own argument. The network has no architect, yet it feeds forests.
