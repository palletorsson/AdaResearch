Concept: A pressurized soft-body sphere pinned at a wide top ring settles under gravity into a shallow bowl. The pin-ring radius is the key parameter that distinguishes this vessel from the gl01 bulb and gl02 bottle. The map is a gallery study; the player walks around the single soft-body marker.

Sequence role: Third glass-vessel study (gl03) in the imported soft-body gallery cluster within the Soft Bodies sequence (13th spine, integration phase). Continues the gl-family parameter sweep — gl01 small-ring pin → bulb, gl02 narrow neck → long bottle, gl03 wide ring → wide bowl, gl04 off-center gravity → lopsided vase, gl05 over-cooked low-stiffness collapse. The cluster stages glass-blowing as a parametric family rather than a craft skill.

Technical angle: Spherical mesh of mass points connected by structural and bending springs to maintain surface integrity. A user-specified ring of vertices near the top of the sphere is pinned to fixed world positions. Internal pressure force applied to every face proportional to face area and inflation parameter; gravity applied to every mass. Verlet or PBD integration over ~200 timesteps lets the unpinned portion of the sphere settle into the bowl shape under the pressure-vs-gravity balance, with the pinned wide ring fixing the bowl's rim. A wider pin ring means less unpinned surface to deform, which means a shorter rim-to-base distance and a wider-than-tall final geometry.

Critical angle: The gl-family is the integration phase's most explicit demonstration of "form as parameter, not design." Glass-blowing in physical reality requires craft skill — breath control, gravity timing, cooling rate — none of which the simulation has access to. What the simulation has is a small parameter set: pin-ring radius, pressure, gravity, steps. Sweeping any one parameter gives you a family of vessels indistinguishable in genre from what a glassblower would call studio output. The point is not that the simulation makes glass; the point is that the same dimensional family of parameters operates in both the digital sim and the wet-glass studio. The vessel is what those parameters together leave behind.

Key artifacts: gallery_marker_soft-body holds the gl03_wide_bowl configuration. The shallow-walled bowl is the artifact — the simulation's record of one specific (pin-radius, pressure, gravity, timesteps) tuple.

Gap: A pin-radius slider on the gallery marker would let learners sweep through the bulb-bottle-bowl continuum live, watching the rim shape redistribute as the pinned region grows or shrinks. Same physics; one parameter; visibly different vessels.
