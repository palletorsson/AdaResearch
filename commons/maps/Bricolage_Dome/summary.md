## Learning Sequence

1. Spawn elevated—see the geodesic dome from above
2. Examine icosahedron_base: the starting platonic solid (20 faces, 12 vertices)
3. Examine subdivision_demo: how faces subdivide (frequency)
4. Approach geodesic_dome: observe how triangulation creates curved appearance
5. Recognize: local flatness yields global curvature
6. Pass through dark_sphere
7. Examine strut_inventory: all struts are nearly identical (bricoleur's inventory)
8. Use dome_builder: construct dome from struts
9. Understand: Fuller discovered what triangulated struts want to become
10. Exit—sequence complete

## Design Intent

The dome is the payoff: everything in the sequence—inventory, affordances, arrays, constraints—comes together. The dome isn't designed; it emerges from:
- **Inventory**: Struts (cylinders)
- **Affordances**: Struts span, connect, brace
- **Arrays**: Radial array on spherical surface
- **Constraints**: Triangulation prevents racking; geodesic distribution minimizes material

The geodesic dome is bricolage at architectural scale.

## Theoretical Framework

### Fuller's Discovery
Buckminster Fuller claimed to discover, not invent, the geodesic dome. The geometry was latent in triangulation and sphere. His contribution was recognizing what already existed in mathematical possibility space.

This is bricolage epistemology: structures are found, not made.

### The Icosahedron Path
The geodesic dome starts from the icosahedron:
1. **Icosahedron**: 20 equilateral triangular faces, 12 vertices
2. **Projection**: Project vertices onto circumscribed sphere
3. **Subdivision**: Divide each face into smaller triangles (frequency)
4. **Project again**: Push new vertices onto sphere
5. **Repeat**: Higher frequency = smoother sphere

Each step uses local rules (triangulate, project) that produce global curvature.

### Local → Global
The dome embodies the sequence's core insight:
- **Local rules**: Triangulate, connect, maintain angles
- **Global structure**: Spherical topology, maximum strength-to-weight

No one designs the dome's global properties—they emerge from local constraints. This is bricolage's deepest lesson: global structure emerges from local constraint satisfaction.
