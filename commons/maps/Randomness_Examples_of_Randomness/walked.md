## The walk

The world room shows what civilization has done with dice. The monte_carlo_dartboard computes π by throwing: random darts, count the hits, and integration — the workhorse of physics, finance, and rendering — emerges from aim-lessness. The pollock_painting_in_3d hangs the art world's answer, drip and flick as sampled gesture, chance elevated to signature. pipe_dream resurrects the Windows Pipes screensaver, the desktop's oldest proof that a few dice and a growth rule can furnish a world while everyone is at lunch. And extreme_randomness runs the source raw — undamped, undistributed, entropy as weather.

## The world

Monte Carlo is the world-lesson: when a problem is too hard to solve, *sample it*. The twentieth century quietly rebuilt itself on this move — bombs, markets, movies, all estimated by organized dart-throwing. The room's span from dartboard to Pollock to screensaver says the same thing three registers apart: randomness is not the absence of method; it *is* a method, the one you reach for when the space is too vast to map and too free to control. Which is this chapter's definition of entropy, working for a living.
