## The cast

push_relabel_algorithm · karger_algorithm

## The walk

The graph fills with traffic. The push_relabel_algorithm works the max-flow problem like a hydraulic engineer — heights assigned, excess shoved downhill, capacity saturating pipe by pipe until the network carries all it can. The karger_algorithm attacks from the destructive side: contract random edges, collapse the graph again and again, and watch the minimum cut — the network's cheapest place to break — emerge from repeated demolition.

## The world

The two are secretly one exhibit: max-flow *equals* min-cut, the throughput of a network and the width of its narrowest bottleneck are the same number read from opposite banks. That duality runs the world — supply chains, power grids, borders, bandwidth. Whoever holds the cut sets the flow; every blockade is applied graph theory. The room's world-lesson is to look at any system carrying anything and ask the twin questions together: how much can move, and where does it break?
