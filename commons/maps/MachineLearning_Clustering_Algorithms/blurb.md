A 12×12 grid. An 8×8 arena inside it. Algorithms compete for space using nothing but dice rolls and distribution curves.

Random walks trace drunk paths across the surface — raising cubes where they step, leaving topology as footprint. Gaussian distributions cluster height around a center, bell curves made solid. Columns disappear by lottery. Profiles generate from noise. Each algorithm reshapes the same grid differently: same substrate, different logic, different landscape. The walker wanders. The distribution concentrates. The remover erases. Watch long enough and the arena becomes a fossil record of process — every ridge a decision that wasn't decided, every gap an absence that wasn't chosen.

Randomness isn't the opposite of structure. It's structure before intention arrives.
