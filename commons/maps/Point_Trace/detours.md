# Return visits in Point_Trace

These experiments remain in the room as secondary encounters. The book follows the drawing dots, stick and whiteboard; a return can ask a different question of the same hall.

## Give the pause another record

At the paired telemetry monitors, look at the footer and move the corresponding controller. Check that the displayed coordinates follow the movement before interpreting the rows as your hand's record. When the controller supplies the readings, hold it still and watch for another timed row.

Compare that with pausing the drawing dot. Its movement gate can leave the point count unchanged. The telemetry instead appends time and position when its timer reaches the sampling interval. The two instruments make different accounts of waiting.

DEMO FEED labels the synthetic fallback. The current TRACKING label means the script found a controller node; its lookup does not verify an active pose or choose the currently driven rig. A staging controller can therefore make that label too confident. Checking the active source is the next implementation improvement before relying on this comparison as evidence of a visitor's pause.

## A mark that seems to know you

The automatic-writing desk offers wordless handwriting. When it finds a headset camera, changes of that camera's position vary the amplitude of the writing. Otherwise, a demonstration can keep it moving.

Compare its response to movement and rest when the input is actually yours. What makes a mark feel as if it knows you? The appearance can invite a reading of character or confession while the implementation supplies a much narrower relation between motion and amplitude.

This inquiry can develop further without becoming a prerequisite for learning to keep a list of positions.

## The finite list

The drawing instrument's capacity is 4096 positions. Keeping a new point beyond that limit removes the oldest. Current list indices change when the front is removed; the table's last ten rows are another, smaller window onto that list.

Lowering the capacity would make this easier to observe quickly, but the room does not currently offer that control. The idea is retained as a future experiment, not an instruction the visitor can perform now.
