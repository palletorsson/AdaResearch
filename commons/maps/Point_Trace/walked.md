## What this map holds (seed)

Now: duration. The trace records what the line forgets — your hand moved through space, hesitated, curved, returned. Time accumulates as visible residue. The line will compress this to two points. The trace resists.

Pick up `draw_dot` and move. Each frame your position is stored; each dot marks a moment that has already passed. The trace is not a line but what the line erases — Derrida's residue made geometric. Gesture, hesitation, error, return: all preserved at the frame rate, none of it symmetric with what a line can hold.

## Why it was built (seed)

Concept: The trace introduces duration and embodied residue — geometry as lived process. Movement accumulates as record; gesture, hesitation, error, and return become geometric data.
Sequence role: Third map. Breaks Point_Lines' static network by adding time. Lines were connections; traces are histories. The hand enters.
Critical angle: Derrida's trace — presence constituted by the residue of what has passed. Geometry shifts from ideation to inscription.

## The walk

Two maps ago you made a point: one decision, no duration. Here the map hands you `draw_dot` and asks you to **keep moving**, and the moment you do, something new appears that the point could not hold — *time*. Every frame your position is caught and left behind as a dot, so the path you draw is not a shape you place but a **history you deposit**. Move fast and the dots space out; hesitate and they pool; double back and the trace crosses itself, keeping the return your hand made. You are not drawing a line. You are leaving evidence.

The `mystic_writing_pad` sits in the room as the map's quiet thesis object — the child's toy of a waxed slab under a lifted sheet, that shows a mark and then, lifted, seems to forget it, while the wax beneath keeps every impression forever. Write on it and you watch the map's whole argument in miniature: the surface presents a clean *now* and hoards an unerasable *was*. The `science_screen` turns the same gesture into data — your trail as a `Vector3` array, the body's motion made legible as a path you could replay — and `floating_sphere_field` holds, again, the undecided haze the trace commits against.

The map works because it makes you feel the asymmetry: what your hand can *do* in a second is richer than what a line can *say* about it. You leave having produced more geometry than any symbol will ever carry back.

## The turn (critical)

Point_One asked where a mark comes from; Point_Trace asks what a mark *keeps*. And its answer is the one idea the whole rest of the curriculum will lean on: **presence is made of residue.** Derrida's trace is not a faint copy of a present thing — it is the condition for there being a present thing at all, the *was* that every *now* is stitched from. The map does not illustrate this; it *is* it, because it literally constitutes the drawn present out of stored past frames. And it is no accident that the room hands you the `mystic_writing_pad` to prove it — that toy is the exact object Derrida read in "Freud and the Scene of Writing," the model of a psyche that shows a surface and archives a depth. Ada stages the philosopher's chosen example as something you hold in your hand.

This is why the trace is the sequence's first genuinely **queer** artifact, and the book should say so plainly. The point and the line are ideal — clean, timeless, symmetric, the kind of object that pretends to have no body and no history. The trace refuses all four: it carries duration, it carries error, it carries the weight and hesitation of a particular hand, and it is *asymmetric* — you cannot run it backward and recover the intention, only the record. Geometry here stops being ideation and becomes **inscription** — writing, with everything writing drags in: the body, the archive, the impossibility of a clean erasure. The line will arrive next and try to tidy this into two endpoints. The trace is what it has to throw away to do so, and the book's sympathies are with what gets thrown away.
