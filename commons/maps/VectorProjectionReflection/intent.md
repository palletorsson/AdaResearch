Concept: Projection finds the shadow of one vector along another; reflection flips the perpendicular component — together they handle collisions, mirrors, and decomposition along arbitrary axes.
Sequence role: Fourth map in Forces; completes the core vector operations toolkit (add, subtract, cross, project/reflect). After this, the learner has every operation needed for physics simulation. The symmetric mirror room makes the geometry literal — reflection is what walls do to moving objects; follows VectorCrossProduct; leads to Vectors_1.
Technical angle: proj_b(a) = (a·b / b·b) × b for projection, refl = a - 2×proj_n(a) for reflection across a normal. The dot product appears here as the engine of projection (measuring alignment). The mirror room physically demonstrates reflection — stand in the room and see symmetry. Connects forward to collision response (VectorForces) and bounce mechanics (VectorThrowing).
Critical angle: Projection is dimensional reduction — collapsing a vector onto a single direction, losing information deliberately. Reflection preserves magnitude while inverting one component — the first reversible transformation in the vector toolkit. The mirror room raises the question: what is preserved under reflection? Length yes, handedness no. This connects to deeper symmetry discussions in later sequences.
Key artifacts: basis_vectors_rig provides the coordinate frame for understanding arbitrary-axis projection; dark_sphere continues. The symmetric room layout is the primary teaching artifact — the architecture itself demonstrates the concept.
Gap: An interactive projection_reflection_tool where the learner adjusts an incoming vector and a mirror normal, seeing projection (shadow on the normal) and reflection (bounce off the surface) simultaneously.
