Concept: Subtraction as the operation that encodes the relationship between two vectors — displacement, relative position, the arrow from here to there.
Sequence role: Second map in Forces; the first binary vector operation. VectorBasics defined single vectors; this map introduces the idea that two vectors together produce a third. Displacement is the seed of velocity (position difference over time), which seeds force; leads to VectorCrossProduct.
Technical angle: v₂ - v₁ = displacement vector, geometric interpretation as tail-to-head arrow, negation as direction reversal. The vector_subtraction_demo artifact shows the construction visually — two position vectors and the difference arrow connecting their tips. Connects directly to the distance calculations introduced in VectorBasics (displacement.length() for distance checks).
Critical angle: Subtraction reveals that vectors are relational, not absolute. A displacement doesn't care about the origin — only about the gap between two positions. This is the first hint that physics is about differences (forces arise from gradients, motion from velocity differences). The compact gallery format forces attention on the essential geometry.
Key artifacts: vector_subtraction_demo provides the core visual — two vectors and their difference; VectorSubtraction anchors the map; dark_sphere continues as the ambient constant across the sequence.
Gap: Could use an interactive artifact where the learner drags two points and watches the displacement vector update in real time, with magnitude readout showing how distance emerges from subtraction.
