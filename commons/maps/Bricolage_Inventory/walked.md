## The walk

The chapter opens in a workshop that has not started working. Eight primitives stand on eight plinths — point, line, triangle, plane, cube, sphere, cylinder, torus — each lifted to eye height, each named on a small plaque, none of them doing anything. The inventory_workbench gathers the whole set on one bench, the collection made visible as a collection. The clipboard states the discipline the room is built on: this is not a gallery of shapes but a *stock* of parts, and the difference is the whole chapter.

## What it opens

The threshold performs Lévi-Strauss's distinction with furniture. The engineer starts from the problem and designs the part it needs; the bricoleur starts from the parts at hand and discovers what they can become. This room is the bricoleur's opening move — before any structure, an *inventory*: the primitives taken off the shelf of pure geometry and set aside as material. A part is a primitive that has agreed to wait. Nothing is built here because bricolage begins earlier than building, in the act of looking at a cube and seeing not a cube but a candidate. The seven rooms after this one are what the waiting was for.
