## The cast

pattern_tile_4x4 · pattern_tile_cube · tile_meander_floor

## The walk

One painted cell, and everything the wall will ever be is already decided in it. The `pattern_tile_4x4` gives you a small grid to fill — sixteen squares, a few colours — and that little square is the pattern's whole genome: whatever you put here, the machinery of the next five maps will repeat, mirror, and turn into a surface without end. Grab the `pattern_tile_cube` and place colour by hand; you are not decorating, you are *specifying* — writing the seed a rule will grow. Then look down. The `tile_meander_floor` is a Greek key built from exactly three tile types — field, straight, corner — a border that ran around Pompeii's rooms two thousand years ago, laid by people who had no word for "algorithm" and were performing one anyway. The domain is the atom of this chapter. Everything else is what the atom does when a rule gets hold of it.

## The turn (critical)

The whole sequence turns on one claim, and this map plants it before any symmetry operation arrives: **a pattern is a rule, not a picture.** The meander floor is the proof, and it is a historical one — the makers who laid it were not drawing a decoration, they were executing a procedure (place field, place straight, turn the corner, repeat) whose output happens to be beautiful. Name them as the ledger asks, since the floor outlived them and the credit did not: a Pompeian mosaic workshop, tile-setters cutting and placing tesserae by a rule they carried in the hand, the kind of labor that signs nothing and is catalogued as "Roman decorative floor." The rule ran, in stone, in a Roman room, laid by workers whose names the eruption kept better than the record did, centuries before Lindenmayer or group theory existed to name what they were already doing. That is the deep provocation the sequence inherits from transformation next door: transformation taught you to move one object; here the object is a *move* itself, stored in a cell and waiting. And it forward-rhymes with the loom in arrays — the addressable grid was always a place where rules are written, whether the rule weaves cloth or lays a border. Hold the tension the map opens: the tile is small and mute, and it contains the pattern the way a seed contains the tree. Nothing here repeats yet. But everything here is already the whole thing, folded down to its rule.
