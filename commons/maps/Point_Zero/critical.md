# Point Zero - Critical Reflection

## Thrownness and Inheritance

You arrive late. Before you see anything, a lineage has already survived: physics engines, rendering pipelines, coordinate systems, and the long history that makes "now" possible.

Heidegger called this **thrownness** (Geworfenheit) - we begin inside conditions we did not choose. The VR headset is already tracking. The GPU is already rendering. The frame loop is already executing. You cannot begin "from scratch" because scratch is itself an inherited position.

## The Origin as Infrastructure

Vector3(0,0,0) is not a point. It is the **fact of beginning inside an inheritance**.

The origin is:
- Not an object, but a **prerequisite**
- Not a visible marker, but an **implicit agreement**
- Not foundational, but **infrastructural**
- Not a ground, but an **effect of system stability**

Before any geometry can appear, invisible scaffolds must already be in place: memory allocation, transform matrices, spatial partitioning, temporal update loops. The origin is what remains stable while these systems execute.

## What the Origin Cannot Hold

The origin as Vector3.ZERO excludes:
- **Uncertainty** - It cannot be approximately (0, 0, 0)
- **Movement** - It cannot drift or vibrate
- **Multiplicity** - There can be only one origin per coordinate system
- **Absence** - It cannot not exist once declared
- **Embodiment** - It has no thickness, temperature, or texture

The origin is **pure abstraction** maintained by continuous computational work.

## The Politics of Zero

To declare (0, 0, 0) as origin is to assert:
1. **Cartesian space** is the natural framework (not polar, not spherical, not topological)
2. **Euclidean geometry** is universal (not hyperbolic, not projective)
3. **Discrete coordinates** can represent continuous space (not differential, not smooth)
4. **Three dimensions** are sufficient (not 2D, not 4D, not infinite-dimensional)

These choices are not neutral. They privilege certain kinds of thinking (analytical, discrete, measurable) over others (embodied, continuous, felt).

## Thrownness as Privilege

The tutorial acknowledges: "I am thrown too - into a body, a position, a history that lets me build this."

To create this VR world requires:
- **Access** to development tools (Godot, XR hardware)
- **Time** to learn complex systems
- **Infrastructure** (electricity, internet, manufacturing supply chains)
- **Cultural capital** (literacy in code, 3D math, game engines)

The ability to declare an origin is **unevenly distributed**. Not everyone can write `Vector3.ZERO` and have a world appear.

## Desire and the Origin

The text says: "The future is queer, is desiring-machinery."

What does a queer origin look like? Perhaps:
- **Multiple origins** that refuse to resolve into one
- **Drifting origins** that move with bodies
- **Contested origins** where different systems disagree
- **Embodied origins** that carry temperature, texture, history
- **Provisional origins** that can be unmade and remade

The origin need not be stable, singular, or certain. It could be a **site of desire** rather than a foundation.

## The Renderer's Gaze

The frame counter reveals: This world is being **continuously re-created** 60-90 times per second.

The origin does not persist - it is **re-instantiated** every frame. What appears stable is actually:
- Rapid destruction and reconstruction
- Memory being written, read, discarded
- Calculations repeated identically
- An illusion of continuity produced by repetition

The origin is not eternal. It is **contingent on execution**.

## Return and Repeatability

The origin's power is that you can **return** to it.

In code:
```gdscript
position = Vector3.ZERO  # Return to origin
```

This return is:
- **Guaranteed** by the system
- **Identical** every time
- **Instant** (no duration of travel)
- **Absolute** (no approximation)

But return is only possible because the origin is:
- **Empty** (nothing to disturb)
- **Abstract** (no material resistance)
- **Maintained** (computational work keeps it stable)

A truly embodied origin would **change** when you return to it. It would remember your passage. It would be warm where you touched it.

## Conclusion: The Already-There

The origin is not where we begin. It is what was **already there** when we arrived.

It is:
- The **inheritance** we cannot choose
- The **infrastructure** we cannot see
- The **convention** we cannot escape
- The **privilege** we cannot evade

To acknowledge this is not to reject the origin, but to recognize it as **constructed, contingent, and political** rather than natural, necessary, or neutral.

The question is not whether to use Vector3.ZERO, but whether we can remain aware that zero is not given - it is **declared, maintained, and continuously re-instantiated** by systems of power that precede our arrival.
