# What the flight has already assumed

The visitor occupies the middle of a full-scale representation. Looking between two lanes makes attention part of the comparison: equal horizontal range does not imply equal elapsed time or one world landing address. The paired paths are separated for visibility, and the text states that difference.

The marker is analytically positioned under constant downward acceleration. No drag, collision response, mass or measured applied force enters that calculation. Landing freezes an incoming impact state. A physical simulation would have to answer further questions. The projection of flight into a known formula is useful precisely because its assumptions make repeatable comparisons possible.

The local playback clock can slow while the museum clock continues. Predicted paths use 64 segments; live markers use render-time evaluations. These are distinct encodings of a shared mathematical curve. Their finite resolutions are inspectable without claiming that higher resolution is always the better representation.

A body could use a high route to clear one obstruction and a low route to pass below another. Those capabilities depend on conditions not currently simulated by this primary. Keeping the absent conditions explicit allows the next room to introduce dynamics rather than passing a diagram off as a complete world.
