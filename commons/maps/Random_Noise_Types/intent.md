Concept: Establishes that randomness is not monolithic — white noise and blue noise are both random but structurally opposite, and the full noise color spectrum (pink, brown, violet) maps frequency weighting to perceptual character.
Sequence role: First map in the Noise sequence (8th spine, phase E_entropy); the entry point where randomness acquires spectral identity. Bridges from the Randomness sequence's coin-flip independence into the structured disorder that defines everything ahead — coherent noise, domain warping, voxel worlds. What follows requires understanding that different noise distributions serve different purposes; leads to Noise_Columns.
Technical angle: White noise as independent uniform samples with flat power spectrum; blue noise via Poisson disk sampling enforcing minimum distance; pink noise (1/f) through octave-weighted summation. NoiseColors3D extends the spectrum into 3D color-space visualization, WhiteNoiseGallery isolates the pure static baseline, and randompoint/randompoints demonstrate the spatial consequences of unconstrained versus constrained placement.
Critical angle: The paradox at the core: blue noise is mathematically less random than white noise yet appears more random to human perception. Adding constraints removes accidental clustering, producing the appearance of pure disorder through deliberate structure. This is entropy's first lesson in the sequence — the E term in QFEP is not a scalar but a texture, and the politics of noise (who filters, who amplifies, who suppresses) begins with the choice of distribution.
Key artifacts: NoiseColors3D maps the full noise spectrum into 3D color space, revealing frequency weighting as spatial character; WhiteNoiseGallery is the null condition — pure independence, maximum entropy per sample; randompoint and randompoints show single versus collective placement, making the birthday paradox of clumping visible; dark_sphere persists as the constant surface whose noise response anchors every map in the sequence.
Gap: A real-time spectral analyzer showing the frequency content of each noise type — visualizing why they are named after colors of light — would make the analogy between noise spectrum and light spectrum experiential rather than metaphorical.
