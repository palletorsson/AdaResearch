White noise screams chaos — every frequency, every sample independent, pure static. Blue noise whispers structure — random but refusing to clump, maintaining distance. Between them: the spectrum of coherent randomness.

The `WhiteNoiseGallery` isolates the null condition — pure independence, maximum entropy per sample, visible static. `NoiseColors3D` maps the full noise spectrum into 3D color space; pink, brown, violet all appear as distinct spatial characters. `randompoint` and `randompoints` show single versus collective placement, making the birthday-paradox clustering of unconstrained uniform sampling visible. The paradox at the core: blue noise is mathematically less random than white but appears more random to human perception. Adding the minimum-distance constraint removes accidental clumps. The appearance of pure disorder requires deliberate structure. Entropy's E-term is not a scalar. It is a texture, and the politics of noise begins with which texture you choose.
