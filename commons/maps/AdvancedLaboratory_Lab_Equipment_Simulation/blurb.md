# Lab Equipment Simulation

A laboratory. Benches at regulation height. Surfaces clean. Tools organized. The molecular designer sits at the center — a formal system made tangible, atoms snapping to valence rules, bonds obeying electron logic. Build a molecule. Watch it fold. The simulation enforces constraints that are not arbitrary but physical. Chemistry as proof theory. Each valid structure a theorem.

Then the void pit. The floor opens beneath the benches and something moves underneath — the substrate the clean room tries to forget. Every formal system rests on something it cannot formalize. Gödel in the foundation. The lab equipment works. The lab equipment is incomplete.

After the crisis, build anyway. Molecular design is post-reductionist practice: knowing the limits of formalization and choosing construction over paralysis. The molecule holds. The floor doesn't.
