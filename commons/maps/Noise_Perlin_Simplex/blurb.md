Two fields of small cubes, one each side of an aisle, and above each a line of text: the basis, the seed, the octaves, the frequency, the gain, and the field's value at two coordinates. Read them side by side. Every word matches but the first. Press REGEN and a field changes; press REPLAY and it comes back. Move one dial and watch the two values at the same coordinate drift apart.

The display named Perlin drew simplex noise for a year, because the code's default was simplex and nobody passed the word. Now it is told its basis, and the readout reports the basis from the generator rather than from the name. The same seed in two bases is not the same field twice; what you can compare is the character of the variation, once everything else has been held still.

A small terrain behind the fields turns the same sampling into ground. The door marked optional is a side room; the main route continues to the cells.
