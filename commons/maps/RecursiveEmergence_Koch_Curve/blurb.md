The corridor zigzags. Walls indent and indent again, each notch sprouting smaller notches of its own. The boundary between walkable floor and void grows longer with every iteration, but the space it encloses never changes.

The Koch curve has infinite length and zero area. At each step, every straight segment sprouts a triangular bump — the perimeter increases by a third, forever. This is the coastline paradox made spatial: measure with a shorter ruler and you always find more coast. Finite territory, infinite boundary. Dimension is not always a whole number.
