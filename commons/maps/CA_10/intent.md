Concept: Langton's ant — a single agent on a grid that flips cells as it walks, following two rules (turn right on white, turn left on black), yet producing a highway of ordered structure after thousands of chaotic steps. The shift from field-based CA to agent-based CA.
Sequence role: Tenth map in the Cellular Automata sequence; the pivot from global parallelism to individual agency. Every previous map updated all cells simultaneously; Langton's ant updates one cell at a time via a moving agent. Beginner difficulty reflects the rule's extreme simplicity (two states, two turns). The sierpinski_pyramid artifact connects to the fractal self-similarity that emerges from simple recursive processes — Sierpinski from subdivision, ant highway from iteration. Connects forward to CA_12's edge-of-chaos classification and backward to CA_9's 3D volumetric exploration.
Technical angle: Langton's ant: 2D grid, binary cell states, one agent with orientation (N/S/E/W). On white cell: flip to black, turn 90 degrees right, move forward. On black cell: flip to white, turn 90 degrees left, move forward. After approximately 10,000 steps of apparent chaos, the ant constructs a diagonal highway — a periodic structure that extends indefinitely. The highway emergence is proven for the standard ant but the proof of universality for generalized ants remains open. The sierpinski_pyramid artifact demonstrates recursive self-similarity in 3D.
Critical angle: Langton's ant is the minimal example of emergent agency — the ant has no memory, no plan, no goal, yet it builds a highway. The chaotic transient followed by ordered construction mirrors phase transitions in statistical mechanics: the system explores microstates before settling into a macroscopic attractor. The ant also connects to the Turing machine (a head moving on a tape, reading and writing), reinforcing the sequence truth that simple rules compute complex outcomes. Agency, here, is an illusion produced by interaction between agent and substrate.
Key artifacts: sierpinski_pyramid demonstrates fractal self-similarity through recursive structure, paralleling the ant's emergence of order from simple iterative rules.
Gap: Needs a Langton's ant simulator artifact — an interactive grid where the learner can watch the chaotic phase, witness the highway emergence, and experiment with generalized ant rules (multiple colors, different turn sequences) to explore the boundary between chaos and construction.
