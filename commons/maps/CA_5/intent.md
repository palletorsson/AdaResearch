Concept: Multi-state cellular automata — cells are no longer binary but carry a gradient of intensities, decaying, brightening, and cycling through states. The petri dish and mold network show how continuous-like behavior emerges from discrete multi-valued rules.
Sequence role: Fifth map in the Cellular Automata sequence; the leap from two states to many. After CA_4 generalized the rule structure (totalistic), CA_5 generalizes the state space. Cells now carry history in their intensity values — decay implies memory, cycling implies oscillation. This connects backward to the Wavefunctions sequence (oscillation as periodic state change) and forward to CA_6's extended neighborhoods. The mold network artifact introduces biological growth patterns that will recur in CA_9's 3D automata.
Technical angle: k-state automata where k > 2. State transitions can model excitable media (resting, excited, refractory — the Greenberg-Hastings model), cyclic automata (states rotate through a fixed cycle), and decay automata (active states fade through intermediate values). The game_of_life_petri demonstrates multi-state Life variants where birth and survival thresholds shift with state intensity. The mold_network shows branching growth reminiscent of Physarum polycephalum.
Critical angle: Multi-state CA blur the boundary between discrete and continuous models. A cell with 256 possible intensity values approximates a continuous field while remaining computationally discrete. The decay mechanic introduces irreversibility — a cell's past matters through its current intensity, embedding a shadow of thermodynamic entropy into the automaton. The mold network is a reminder that biological intelligence (slime mold solving mazes) operates on CA-like principles.
Key artifacts: game_of_life_petri extends Life to multi-state dynamics in a laboratory setting; mold_network demonstrates branching biological growth on a CA substrate; dark_sphere anchors the research space.
Gap: Needs a cyclic-automata artifact showing the rotating state spiral (Cyclic CA) to contrast with decay — cycling is reversible locally, decay is not, and the visual difference is striking.
