## The walk

The machine opens its eyes. The computer_vision_vr slides a convolution filter across an image and puts you inside the sliding — the same small question (is there an edge here?) swept across every position until a picture becomes a field of answers. The convolutional_neural_networks_cnns_vr stacks that move into a hierarchy you can walk up: edges feeding corners feeding textures feeding parts feeding, at the top, a face — each layer built from the one below, kernels you can grab and inspect.

## The turn

The chapter turns from learning-in-general to *perception*, and the turn matters because perception is where machine learning stopped being a lab curiosity and became something that looks back at you. A CNN sees by a built-in humility — the same detector must work in every position, translation invariance wired into the weights — and out of that constraint grows the uncanny competence that reads faces, x-rays, and license plates. The room holds the achievement and its shadow together: a machine that perceives is a machine that can be pointed at people, and the same hierarchy that finds a cat in a photo finds a face in a crowd. Sight, once mechanized, does not stay neutral about what it is aimed at.
