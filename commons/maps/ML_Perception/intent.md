Concept: The perceptron as the atomic unit of neural computation — a single neuron with inputs, weights, a threshold, and a binary output. The simplest possible learning machine, and the place where the biological metaphor meets linear algebra.
Sequence role: Fifth map in the Machine Learning sequence (15th spine sequence, integration phase). A deliberate zoom-in after ML_Neural_Networks showed the whole architecture. By isolating one neuron, the map makes the weight-sum-threshold mechanism fully transparent before the learner re-encounters it multiplied by thousands. Connects back to ML_Neural_Networks (the single unit within the layers) and forward to ML_Sequence_Memory (adding state to the neuron).
Technical angle: Perceptron learning rule: adjust weights by the error times the input. Convergence theorem: if the data is linearly separable, the perceptron converges in finite steps. XOR problem as the proof that single perceptrons cannot learn nonlinear functions — the historical motivation for multi-layer networks. Geometric interpretation: the weight vector defines a hyperplane, and learning rotates it until separation is achieved.
Critical angle: The perceptron is where the myth of the artificial neuron begins and should be questioned. Biological neurons are vastly more complex — dendritic computation, temporal coding, neuromodulation — yet the stripped-down abstraction works. Rosenblatt's perceptron and Minsky's critique of it shaped decades of AI research, proving that a single mathematical result can redirect an entire field. The simplest model teaches the most about the politics of simplification.
Key artifacts: perceptron_demo provides an interactive single-neuron simulation with adjustable weights, threshold, and input space visualization showing the decision boundary rotating during learning.
Gap: Needs an XOR failure demonstration — an artifact that shows the perceptron trying and failing to learn XOR, making the linearity limitation visceral rather than theoretical.
