Concept: The foundational grammar of cellular automata — a grid of cells, each knowing only its immediate neighbors, applying the same rule simultaneously. The persian rug pattern reveals how local symmetry propagates into global ornament; columns and bridges show structural emergence from repetition.
Sequence role: First map in the Cellular Automata sequence (9th spine sequence, E_entropy phase). Opens the entropy arc by establishing the substrate: discrete cells, discrete time, local rules. The intermediate difficulty signals that the learner already has vector, force, and wave intuitions — now computation itself becomes the medium. Connects forward to CA_2 where the rules gain biological metaphor (birth, survival, death).
Technical angle: 2D grid with synchronous update, neighborhood definition (von Neumann or Moore), state transition as lookup table. The persian_rug artifact demonstrates how mirror symmetry in initial conditions is preserved by symmetric rules — the automaton as a pattern amplifier. The line_network_ca shows the connectivity graph underlying the grid, making the neighbor relation explicit.
Critical angle: Cellular automata invert the usual approach to complexity: instead of writing complex programs, you write the simplest possible rule and let parallelism do the work. The persian rug is the first hint that entropy and order are not opposites — deterministic local rules produce patterns that look designed but have no designer. This is the E_entropy phase thesis: structure emerges at the edge of information.
Key artifacts: line_network_ca exposes the neighbor graph; persian_rug demonstrates symmetry preservation under local rules; dark_sphere anchors the research space.
Gap: Needs a step-by-step rule application artifact — a slow-motion single-step visualizer that highlights which neighbors are read and how the new state is computed, making the update rule fully transparent before the learner encounters more complex automata.
