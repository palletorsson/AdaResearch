A grid is a claim about space. Seven columns, fourteen rows — every cell addressable by two numbers. X marks the column, Y marks the row. Together they pin a point. This is the minimum structure needed to say *where*.

Pedestals rise from the grid in ordered families. Cube, pyramid, octahedron — the Platonic regulars, faces identical. Then dodecahedron, prism, icosahedron — more faces, more axes of symmetry. Then the mutations: L-shapes, bipyramids, halocubes. Rough rocks and truncated tetrahedra. Each row a deviation from the last. The gallery moves from pure symmetry toward something stranger — forms that resist easy classification.

A dark sphere pulses at the center. Not a shape to name but a presence to navigate around.

Two indices locate anything. But what they locate keeps changing.
