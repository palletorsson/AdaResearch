A flat cloth that is also a reaction-diffusion field. Two chemicals on the surface compete and spread — activator and inhibitor, the same Turing pair that paints leopards and zebras. Where activator wins, the cloth stiffens. Where inhibitor wins, it stays loose.

The pattern emerges first as a chemical map. Then the cloth drapes — but not uniformly. The stiff regions hold ridges. The loose regions sag into folds. What you see is two simulations bleeding into each other: the pattern of a coral, drawn in spring stiffness, draped under gravity.

Turing wrote the reaction-diffusion equations in his last paper, asking how organisms get patterned. He did not get to ask what happens when the pattern is also the material. This map answers it: the pattern bends what carries it. Form and decoration stop being separate.
