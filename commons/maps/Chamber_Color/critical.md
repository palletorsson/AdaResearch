# What receives the change?

A colour value has no intrinsic authority to turn a cube, make a creature friendly or open a route. The receiving code supplies those possibilities. In this chamber a single contact tints the target, paints nearby grid instances and changes their visual transforms. Calling the result chromatic does not erase those separate decisions.

The mismatch between rendered shape and collision gives the visitor something specific to question. The image develops gaps while the original floor continues to support the body. A transformed appearance is a real change, but it does not entail every other change we might desire. Keeping that distinction makes the queer question more precise: which permissions would have to change for another body or another way of moving to become possible here?

The 1.5-metre radius includes cube centres rather than every touched surface. The launch chooses hue randomly, the flight glow cycles, and the recipient uses the original stored colour. Each stage makes a selection. There is no single unmediated colour whose journey the exhibit simply reveals.

The target's return resets its hit count while the grid keeps its current edits. This gives different parts of one encounter different histories. The bench neither grants a portable wrist capability nor saves the altered world. Those absences delimit this experiment; they are useful places to design the next one.

Nothing in this demonstration measures an entropy threshold or establishes a transition from foe to friend. Such claims need an actual receiving system and their own evidence. Here the research question lands on rendering, collision, message dispatch and the local duration of a change.
