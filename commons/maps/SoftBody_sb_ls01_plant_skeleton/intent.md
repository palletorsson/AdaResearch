Concept: An L-system production rule (F→F[+F]F[-F]F, the canonical branching plant) is expanded a fixed number of derivation steps and instantiated as a spring-mass skeleton. Segments map to springs, branches map to child spring chains, base nodes are pinned. High stiffness plus 60 simulation steps produces a near-rigid plant that still settles slightly under gravity. Direct DNA bridge to lsystem-gallery entry ls03.

Sequence role: First L-system DNA-bridge study (sb_ls01) within Soft Bodies (13th spine, integration phase). Sister to sb_ls02_plant_wind_collapse (same skeleton, lower stiffness, wind force enabled, demonstrates failure under load). Establishes the DNA-bridge motif: the same generative rule rendered through different substrates produces recognizably related artifacts. Connects forward to ls-gallery (line drawings) and to other DNA bridges (sb_ca, sb_rd, sb_noise).

Technical angle: L-system derivation: start axiom, apply production rule N times, walk the resulting string with turtle state (position, heading, stack) to emit a tree of segment endpoints. Convert segments into springs with high k (so the structure stands), branches into child-parent attachment springs, base endpoints into world-pinned constraints. Verlet integration for 60 steps under gravity yields a near-stable posture; remaining wobble reveals which substructures are most cantilevered. Spring stiffness is the single parameter dividing "plant" from "tower" from "string."

Critical angle: The DNA bridge is the Ada-specific contribution: a generative rule is not married to a single rendering. The plant grammar from lsystems is the same grammar here — only the realization differs. This collapses the distinction between "geometry" and "structure": a tree drawn on paper, a tree simulated as physics, and a tree built from biology are three projections of one grammar. The map is a worked example of the integration-phase thesis that substrate is interchangeable when the rule is preserved.

Key artifacts: gallery_marker_soft-body presents the sb_ls01_plant_skeleton configuration — derived L-system expanded into a spring-mass plant pinned at the base, integrated under gravity.

Gap: A side-by-side rendering of the same L-system string as line drawing and as spring skeleton in one room would make the substrate-independence of the grammar directly visible rather than implied across the lsystems and softbodies sequences.
