Columns rise six units high on both sides of a narrow nave. The ceiling is far above. Your footsteps sound small. The proportions are designed to make you feel the weight of vertical space pressing upward, pulling your gaze with it.

Height is an emotion. Gothic builders knew that a tall, narrow room creates awe more reliably than any ornament. The cathedral trades horizontal freedom for vertical drama — the walls close in so the ceiling can soar. Scale is not size. It is the ratio between your body and the void above it.
