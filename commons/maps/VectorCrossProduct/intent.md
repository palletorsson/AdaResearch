Concept: The cross product generates a vector perpendicular to two inputs — escaping the plane they define, with magnitude encoding the area of their parallelogram.
Sequence role: Third map in Forces; the first operation that produces a vector outside the plane of its operands. After addition/subtraction (staying in-plane), the cross product breaks into the third dimension. This is the gateway to surface normals, torque, and angular momentum — concepts essential for Forces_6 through VectorTorque; follows VectorSubtraction; leads to VectorProjectionReflection.
Technical angle: a × b = perpendicular vector, right-hand rule for orientation, |a × b| = |a||b|sin θ as parallelogram area. The perpendicular wall segments in the room layout physically embody the cross product's geometry — two walls define a plane, the normal escapes upward. Basis_vectors_rig shows the three axes; the cross product explains why î × ĵ = k̂.
Critical angle: The cross product is where vectors become genuinely three-dimensional. Addition and subtraction work in any dimension; the cross product is specific to 3D (and 7D, but that's not the point). It produces something new — a direction that neither input contained. This generative quality mirrors the QFEP dynamics term: new structure emerging from interaction.
