# What can you take with you?

Look down before choosing another appearance. Your museum garment grows as you enter new sequences. Returning to one does not add another layer. If you chose a nail colour in the salon, your virtual hands have already carried one decision here. A dress can carry part of the walk too.

The studio supplies its own small source. We have brought ways of making and reading a mark, rather than automatically carrying every earlier edit. The shader studio samples its pattern onto an existing garment. Beside it, the cutting table offers another route: bring the record you saved at the loom and decide what it will become around a body.

Shift the reading, then change how many repeats fit. The same counts occupy different physical widths on the wall and skirt. Walk around the preview. Where does a mark stretch, end or begin again? A surface address can carry an image without supplying a tailor's cutting pattern or a matching seam.

Try CUT OUT and look through an opening. Then attempt to cross the sample wall. The view opens; the collision still refuses you. There are two accounts of the wall: one receives your gaze, another your attempt to move. Their disagreement gives us somewhere precise to work.

Other controls change the finish or displace the rendered surface. Those remain appearances produced by this material system. A moving edge alone does not establish cloth that can bend, carry weight or respond to your hand.

At the cutting table, fronts and backs lie beside sleeves, skirts or trouser pieces. Load the cloth you kept. Change a short dress to a long one, then open its outline into pleats. The weave remains; the cut changes. Follow a band to a cut edge. The repeat continues in the source, but this piece ends here.

The arm lifts each piece and carries it to the fitting. A flat arrangement becomes a silhouette. We prescribed that shape too: knowing the crossings did not tell the machine whose shoulders or legs it should fit. Wear the result and look down. Does the pattern feel different now that your movement carries it?

Choose a silhouette. SAVE the fabric recipe and WEAR SAVED, then walk to the mirror. The saved pattern goes onto the skirt; the bodice, crown and sleeves keep the look's own materials. The silhouette is a separate choice from the stored fabric.

Turn slightly. Find the altered cell again. A decision made at the small editor has become part of how you meet yourself. What do you notice now that you did not notice on the wall?

*Mamma Mönster* records a room filled with a mother's fabrics, mirrors, strong colours and reflections. Their appeal was already bound to ways of appearing and becoming. The hope introduced in Foundry—that different clothes might change people—returns here as something to try on. No choice at the console can guarantee what another person will make of it.

Return to the studio and edit the preview. Your worn skirt keeps the saved version. You can change your mind without immediately changing what you are wearing. The record and its present use have become separate decisions.

The walking garment can also grow while keeping its repeat count. More surface becomes available to the same source. Which part would you call the same dress?

Send the saved fabric to the runway and join the four performers. Find your mark approaching, turning and moving away. Shared fabric does not make their silhouettes or appearances identical. One record can take part in several encounters.

These walkers follow a programmed route. What they offer is a procession to enter and compare. Stand among them, then watch from the side. Has joining changed what you see?

We began by choosing what a floor repeated. We learned to author a source, alter its reading and decide where its reach should stop. At the loom, a pass joined earlier work. Here a saved mark becomes part of an appearance you can wear.

What did you hope the pattern would change? An image, a room, the way you move, the way you are seen? The source keeps its values. Those desires still need you.

In Change Intro, a curve waits. Read its height at two places and join them. How far should we look before saying which way something is changing?
