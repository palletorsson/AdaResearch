A particle moves. As it goes, its position over time draws a curve behind it — height is where it was, width is when. The arrow growing out of the particle is its velocity, and velocity is the derivative you met in the last map, now made physical. The slope of the position curve is the length of the arrow. They are the same fact wearing two bodies.

The `velocity_arrow` is the moving particle and the vector it carries. The `player_trace` returns here from Primitives — the path you leave becomes a function you can read, the move where walking turns into graphing. The `derivative_pair` again stacks position over velocity, and the `code_display` names them: x(t) above, x'(t) below. Position is where you are. Velocity is how fast the where is changing. Calculus is the grammar that lets you say both at once.
