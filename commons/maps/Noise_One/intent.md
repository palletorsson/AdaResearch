Concept: Noise octaves as fractal summation — layering multiple frequencies of coherent noise with decreasing amplitude produces complexity that no single frequency can achieve alone. The torus makes the layering tangible, wrapping noise across a surface where seams reveal continuity.
Sequence role: Third map in the Noise sequence; the pivot from single noise fields to composite noise. After Noise_Columns showed what one Perlin field does to geometry, this map introduces the technique that makes noise useful for natural textures: octave stacking. Frequency doubles, amplitude halves, and the sum converges toward organic complexity. The interactive parameters anticipate Noise_Space_10's full parameter exploration by introducing the core controls — frequency, amplitude, octave count — in a focused context; leads to Noise_Voxel.
Technical angle: Fractal Brownian Motion (fBM) as the summation: value += amplitude * noise(position * frequency) with frequency *= lacunarity, amplitude *= persistence per octave. The noiselayers artifact visualizes each octave independently before combining, making the contribution of each frequency band visible. The noisetorus wraps the noise field onto a closed surface, demonstrating tileability and exposing how noise behaves across topology changes.
Critical angle: Octave layering is the computational analog of natural scale invariance — coastlines, clouds, mountains all exhibit detail at every magnification. The mathematical structure (each octave at half amplitude, double frequency) is a choice that mimics 1/f distributions found throughout nature. The torus surface raises a topological question: can noise be truly seamless, or do we always impose boundaries and then hide them? The learner adjusts parameters and discovers that "natural-looking" is a tunable property, not a given.
Key artifacts: noiselayers displays each octave as a separate visual layer before summation, making fractal decomposition spatial rather than abstract; noisetorus wraps the composite noise onto a torus, testing continuity across a closed manifold; dark_sphere persists as the sequence anchor.
Gap: An audio component playing each octave as a tone, then their sum, would connect visual fBM to auditory synthesis — the same principle (harmonics with decreasing amplitude) governs both texture and timbre.
