Before Perlin, noise was static — random values with no memory, no structure. Then came coherent noise: smooth, continuous, organic. Sample adjacent points and get adjacent values. The noise function remembers its neighbors. Chaos with correlation. Randomness that flows.

The `noiselayers` artifact makes fractal Brownian motion visible layer by layer: each octave at half amplitude and double frequency, the sum converging toward organic complexity no single frequency achieves alone. `noisetorus` wraps the composite noise onto a closed surface, testing tileability across topology. Octave layering is the computational analog of natural scale invariance — coastlines, clouds, mountains all exhibit detail at every magnification. The mathematical choice (half-amplitude per octave) mimics the 1/f distributions found throughout nature. Natural-looking is not a given. It is a tunable property.
