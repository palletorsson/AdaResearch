## The walk

The grammar leaves botany. The ContextFreeGrammars station shows the machinery in its plainest clothes — production rules deriving strings, the same formalism that parses programming languages and this sentence. Then the space_filling_curve_gallery makes the strange demonstration: grammars whose derivations, read as turtle paths, *fill space* — Peano and Hilbert curves folding a one-dimensional line toward a solid. The Hilbert3D piece completes the argument in the third dimension, a single unbroken thread visiting every region of a cube.

## What it fixes

The walk fixes the chapter's scope: grammars do not just grow trees, they *measure space*. A Hilbert curve is a sentence whose meaning is an addressing scheme — the reason databases and image formats still walk Hilbert's path is that nearby points on the curve stay nearby in space. Dimension itself bends here (a line of dimension two, then three), which shakes hands with the fractal chapter next door — but the L-systems version insists on the stranger half: the bending was *written*, rule by rule, in a language with an alphabet you can count on one hand.
