Concept: Springs and Hooke's law — F=-kx as the paradigm of restoring force, where displacement from equilibrium produces proportional opposition, generating oscillation when disturbed.
Sequence role: Fourteenth map in Forces; introduces the first force that depends on position rather than being constant (like gravity). Springs create oscillation — the defining behavior of the sequence's phase. This is where the QFEP oscillation term becomes literal: a physical system that oscillates around equilibrium; follows Forces_2; leads to Forces_4.
Technical angle: F=-kx (Hooke's law), spring constant k as stiffness parameter, equilibrium position as the zero-force point. Damped oscillation when friction is added. The connection between springs and simple harmonic motion — sinusoidal position over time emerging from a linear restoring force.
Critical angle: The spring is the simplest system that produces temporal pattern from spatial displacement. Push it, and it pushes back. Overshoot, and it reverses. The oscillation is not programmed — it emerges from the restoring force applied every frame. This is the oscillation phase's thesis: periodicity arises from opposition, not from design.
