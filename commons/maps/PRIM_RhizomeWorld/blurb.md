This is a test, and the thing being tested is scale.

It is the same body as the one in the isosurfaces chapter — eight chambers, ten passages, three independent cycles, one signed-distance field hollowed by its own absolute value. Here it is four times the size. A hundred and forty-three metres across the diagonal. Chambers you could put a museum hall inside. Passages ten metres wide.

It costs exactly what the small one costs. Six hundred and twenty thousand triangles, the same mesh, scaled rather than rebuilt — because the bill for marching a field is surface area, and four times the size at the same resolution would be nine million triangles and about six minutes of waiting. Scale the finished thing instead and the cell simply becomes a one-metre facet, which at this size reads as construction rather than as error.

What does not scale is you. Every clearance in the body was derived from a capsule three tenths of a metre wide and one point eight tall, and multiplying the walls away from that can only ever be more generous. A doorway that fitted at one metre fits at four. The arithmetic only runs one way, which is why this direction is safe and the other would need doing again.

You arrive on the approach line with the mouth already in front of you and a hundred metres of body behind it. The plain is empty on purpose. At this size the thing is the landscape, and anything else standing on the floor would only be arguing with it.
