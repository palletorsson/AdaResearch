# Bricolage_Chair - Critical

## Philosophical Framing

The chair embodies Heidegger's "ready-to-hand" (Zuhandenheit): equipment that disappears into use. You don't see the chair—you sit. The chair reveals itself only when it breaks (too low, unstable, uncomfortable). This is equipment ontology: being-as-useful rather than being-as-present.

The bricolage framing adds: the chair is not designed for use but discovered through constraint satisfaction. Chair-ness is latent in the inventory; assembly reveals it.

## What This Concept Cannot Hold

The map presents the chair as emerging from neutral constraints. But:

- **Ergonomics is political**: "Standard" chair dimensions assume a particular body (male, Western, able-bodied)
- **Sitting is cultural**: Floor sitting, kneeling, squatting—many cultures don't use chairs
- **Chair history is colonial**: The spread of chairs tracked colonial expansion; indigenous seating was displaced
- **Chair labor**: Who makes chairs? Who can afford chairs? Who sits while others stand?

The "universal chair" is a myth. Every chair encodes assumptions about bodies, power, and culture.

## The Function Problem

The map treats "sit-ability" as obvious. But:
- Is a stool a chair? (No back)
- Is a bench a chair? (Multiple seats)
- Is a throne a chair? (Symbol, not just seat)
- Is a wheelchair a chair? (Movement, not stasis)

"Chair" is a family resemblance category (Wittgenstein), not a set of necessary and sufficient conditions. The map's constraint satisfaction approach works for prototypical chairs but fails at boundaries.

## Queer Reading: Sitting Wrong

Ahmed's "queer use" asks: what if we sit wrong?
- Cross-legged on the seat
- Backwards, arms on the back
- Lying across multiple chairs
- Standing on the seat

The chair "affords" sitting, but bodies can refuse the affordance. Queer use reveals that affordances are offers, not commands.

Additionally: chairs enforce particular body configurations (straight back, feet on floor, thighs horizontal). This is disciplinary furniture. The "good posture" chair is a technology of normalization.

## The Vernacular Romance

The map frames the chair as "vernacular bricolage"—locally evolved, unpretentious, authentic. But:
- Vernacular doesn't mean equitable (vernacular chairs reflect local hierarchies)
- "Evolved forms" naturalizes design choices that were political
- Romanticizing vernacular erases the labor and conflict behind traditional crafts

The chair has a history: woodworkers, upholsterers, factories, unions. "Bricolage" can obscure this.

## Joint Logic vs. Form Logic: A Critique

The map privileges joints (how parts connect) over form (what chair looks like). But:
- Form carries meaning (throne vs. folding chair)
- Aesthetics are not superficial (a comfortable chair feels different if it's ugly)
- Craft traditions have formal logics that aren't reducible to constraints

Pure constraint satisfaction might produce functional chairs but miss the craft dimension—the shaping, finishing, and proportion that vernacular chair-makers spent lifetimes refining.

## Questions That Remain Open

1. If the chair is "discovered" rather than "designed," who gets credit? The bricoleur, the constraints, the materials?

2. The map uses cylinders for legs. But many chairs use flat boards, bent wood, molded plastic. Does the inventory determine what can be discovered?

3. "Sit-ability" assumes sitting is good. But prolonged sitting is associated with health problems. Is the chair a good invention?

4. The map moves from chair (functional) to sculpture (non-functional) to dome (structural). What's the relationship between function and bricolage?

5. Children's chairs, bar stools, dentist chairs, electric chairs—do these share anything with the map's prototypical chair except the name?

## QFEP Connection

In the Queer Free Energy Principle, the chair represents a local free energy minimum—a stable configuration that satisfies constraints. But:
- **Multiple minima**: Chair, stool, bench, throne are all local minima in the sit-ability landscape
- **Depth of minimum**: How stable? A folding chair is a shallow minimum (collapses easily); a throne is deep (persists)
- **Attractors**: The chair is an attractor in furniture configuration space—many starting conditions evolve toward it

The λ parameter appears in chair stability: a wobbly chair (high λ) is at the edge of its minimum; a solid chair (low λ) is deep in the basin.

The φΔE(S,t) term (adaptive capacity) is interesting: the chair that can be adjusted, folded, reconfigured has higher adaptive capacity than the fixed chair. Modular furniture—bricolage chairs—maintain entropy potential even after assembly.
