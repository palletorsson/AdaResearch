A neural network sees a cat. It's correct. But why? The weights say nothing. The layers say nothing. Millions of parameters converge on "cat" and no single one can explain itself. The black box works. The black box is mute.

Explainable AI cracks the box open. Saliency maps light up the pixels that mattered. Attention scores reveal where the model looked. Convolutional filters show what patterns the network learned to detect — edges, textures, shapes — building from the trivially obvious to the genuinely alien. Some filters learn features no human would name.

Step inside the convolution. Watch the kernel slide across an image, compressing raw vision into abstract representation. Each layer forgets more of what the image looks like and remembers more of what it means.

Legibility is not transparency. A system that explains itself in terms you understand may be lying in terms you can't detect.
