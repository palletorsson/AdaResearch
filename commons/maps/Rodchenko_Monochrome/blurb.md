# Rodchenko Monochrome

In 1921, Rodchenko hung three canvases at the 5×5=25 exhibition. Pure Red. Pure Yellow. Pure Blue. No composition, no figure, no gesture. He declared painting finished. Three monochrome panels — the last possible pictures.

Strip color of reference. Red means nothing. Yellow depicts nothing. Blue represents nothing. Each canvas is a flat fact, a pigment without metaphor. Constructivism's wager: autonomous elements in autonomous arrangement. The artist as engineer, assembling meaning-free units. Except meaning won't stay evacuated. Red reads as revolution. Blue reads as sky. The sign refuses its own emptiness.

Magritte showed the gap between sign and signified. Rodchenko tried to close it — make the sign identical to itself, pure surface, zero depth. But a canvas that means nothing still means "nothing." Ceci n'est pas une couleur. The monochrome proves what it tried to deny: representation has no off switch.
