# Lines, Networks, Measure — in the Void

*Point Lines, Floor 03. Tower reading, 18 October 2026. Adapted from the museum final text; the original remains available.*

You arrive on a landing below Coordinates. Unfinished walls screen the floor beyond. A doorway leads into a small black enclosure. Begin with two points.

In the black room, a pale line hangs between two small points. Take one end and move it. The line grows longer, turns, shortens. Release it. The end stays where your hand left it, still joined to the other.

A number on the wall follows the distance between them. Try changing the direction without changing that number. The dark room leaves very little else to look at: two positions, and what happens between them.

Can you find a span of one metre? Keep one end still and move the other around it. The segment turns while its length stays steady. The number can remain the same while the line points somewhere else. What has the measurement kept, and what has it left for your eye and hand to follow?

Give the segment an order — from here to there — and something new appears. It points. `b - a` is a displacement: it has a size and a way round. `.length()` throws the way round away and keeps a number. `.normalized()` throws the number away and keeps the way round. Nothing is left of the segment in either result, and neither result can be turned back into it alone.

Your hand may also keep moving while the rounded number on the wall appears still. A display has its own smallest difference.

Through the side opening, the route passes around the shaft into the west wing. The pair opens into a field.

Twelve small points wait at hand height. Bring one near another and release it. A line appears between them. Move either end away: the line follows. Other points remain available. A pair can become a chain, a branching arrangement, a shape whose next connection has not yet been chosen.

Two distinct positions determine a straight line; the visible segment keeps only the part between them.[^point-lines-euclid] Here proximity initiates the connection. It does not require the endpoints to remain close. The hand has made a relation that outlasts the gesture.

No barrier falls when the points join. Making a segment and opening a passage would require two different instructions. For now we can attend to what this one instruction makes available: a connection whose length and direction can change.

Back on the open slab, two blue wooden police barricades turn the direct route aside. **POLICE LINE DO NOT CROSS** is painted white across the boards. The endpoints have become A-frame supports; the connecting plank has a collision boundary underneath it. The destination remains visible, but ordinary walking must take another route.

Their turns do not measure our journey. They prescribe it. A line we could lengthen by moving one point has become a fixture that lengthens our own movement.

A red line marks another division. In speech, a red line can name the limit beyond which we refuse to go. Here the phrase has a colour and a place in the room. Before touching it, you may already have supplied the warning.

The line gathers these associations as we move: a connection, a border, a promise to go no further. The words beside it, the bodies it separates and what happens when we cross give its thin shape different force. What, here, gives it the right to stop you?

The red marking lies beside an open shaft. It does not catch a foot or take health. The missing floor gives the warning its consequence.

Across the concrete, a black stripe: **WALK THIS LINE.** There is room to follow it or step aside.

Try following it. Then walk alongside it. Cross it at an angle. The stripe stays straight through all three journeys.

Did a sideways step feel like a mistake? The marking does not measure how closely you follow it. Its script draws the stripe and lettering. Yet you may already have corrected a step in answer to the words.[^point-lines-ahmed-paths] You might also find yourself dancing around the same steady line.

Choose which end to start from, and the line gives you a direction to follow. It cannot settle what following will mean to a body.

Turn and look at the route your movement has left behind. Compare it with the stripe. Where do the two lines part company?[^point-lines-ahmed-deviation] One was waiting for you; the other has taken shape while you moved.

The recorder saves positions and joins them with straight segments. The X and Z come from your headset; the line is drawn near floor level. A lean can change the drawing even when your feet stay put.

Choose a start and an end. Walk between them with a detour. Imagine walking directly between those same positions instead. The span between the endpoints is the same for both journeys. It contains no record of the turn you took.

The trace has kept more positions. How many did it need? What happened between the ones it kept?

## A body for the line

The point needed a sphere before we could see it. Here a line takes the form of a stick, a plank, a luminous rod, a measure, a beam. The mathematical line has no width. Each of these has enough width to appear, and a shape the hand can find.

We might call this a *conceptual skin*: the body we give an idea so that we can encounter it. Look at what comes with that gift. The plank has a broad face and a narrow edge. The ruler divides its length into intervals. The light can seem to have hardly any substance, although this one is a mesh with a pickup body. Their common direction does not tell us what it will be like to hold them.[^point-lines-ingold]

The barrier already gave this distinction consequences. Its visible plank and its invisible collision shape occupied different amounts of space. Here the thin rods have slightly wider pickup shapes so a hand can find them. What we can see and what we can touch are being decided together, but they need not coincide.

Two black survey lines lie parallel on the slab. Walk between them. Their distance apart stays the same, although the gap seems to narrow ahead. A pair of lines can suggest depth in a flat drawing. Here you can walk into the depth they describe.

Across the shaft, timber provides a different continuation. Four planks lie side by side between the floor edges. Their surfaces and collision boundaries carry the player. Uprights and rails border the crossing. These fixed pieces are part of the route; the loose specimens wait on the bench beyond.

## Two lines, another relation

What can two lines become together? Two sticks make a plus. Further along, two of the same length make an X. From in front, each reads as one familiar sign. Step sideways. The sticks sit in slightly different depth planes; the crossing belongs to your view. Take one in your hand and bring it forward. How far can the sign come apart before you stop seeing it? Two lines that cross agree on precisely one point and disagree about every other point in the universe, and we call that agreement an intersection, as though it were a place. It is a coincidence with a name.

Nearby, two planks lie parallel. Walk between them. From one end the gap narrows into the distance; moving your head changes that appearance without moving either plank. Then move a plank. Now you have changed the relation itself. Parallel is the stronger claim: not *they have not met yet* but *they will not, ever, however far you follow them*. It is a promise about infinity made by two short segments in a small room, and everything built square is built on it. A graduated measure on the bench offers another way to compare a span.

Seven planks lean at the same angle. Their lengths increase. The angle stays; the silhouette changes. At the open edge, similar members form a rail and crossed braces. The arrangement now meets the moving body. Its usefulness depends on where we put the pieces and which boundaries we give them.

On the bench, take the stick, plank, luminous rod or measure. Two beams stand upright nearby. Turn one in your hand and keep it there. The room does not correct it when you let go.

A short luminous line hangs below the slab above. From one position, it can seem to continue a distant ceiling edge. Move sideways. The agreement comes apart. The objects stayed still; your movement changed the relation you could see.

The surveying laser offers another reading. Its number ends at the body the beam meets. In this installation it measures without burning. Moving the same instrument changes which surface becomes the limit of its measurement.

## Follow this line

On the floor: **FOLLOW THIS LINE.**

The mark climbs a white crate, crosses its top and continues into the air above the shaft. Your eye follows it. Your next step needs something the line does not provide.

The timber crossing nearby offers what this mark lacks: width, thickness and a collision boundary that can carry your weight. What must we add to a line before we can trust it with a body?

An instruction has reached the limit of the body to which it was addressed. Do you stop, turn, look up? The mark remains available to another kind of following.

## What the crossing does

Beyond the workbench, two more crossed lines: **X** and **+**. You can go around this pair. Enter the X and health falls. Leave it and the loss stops. The plus restores some health, breaking into a scatter of light; after a short interval it returns.

Two crossed lines now address a variable in the moving body. The difference is not contained in their resemblance to familiar signs: the room has assigned each a different operation. The wooden X at the edge supports a different claim. Crossing its shape does not make it a hazard.

If health runs out, or a fall is fatal, the tower returns you to the last safely reached floor. It restores health and returns the movable line specimens to their starting arrangements. A recovery mark cannot supply the floor missing beneath you.

Look back at the route you have left among these arrangements. The question taking us towards the next encounter has become precise: **what must we save if we want to keep something of the journey?**

Two endpoints were enough for the segment. To remember the detour, we will need to add something.

Bring the detour.

[^point-lines-euclid]: See Euclid, [*Elements*, Book I, Postulate 1](https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/elements/bookI/post1.html), on drawing a straight line between two points. The construction concerns the finite segment; extending it and treating it as an unbounded line are further steps. The postulate is a historical reference, not an account of the renderer or the barrier’s programmed response.

[^point-lines-ingold]: Tim Ingold, [*Lines: A Brief History*](https://www.routledge.com/Lines-A-Brief-History/Ingold/p/book/9781138640399) (Routledge Classics edition, 2016), especially “Traces, Threads and Surfaces” and “Up, Across and Along.” Walking, drawing, weaving and connecting enter his inquiry as different practices of making lines. This helps us take the room’s proliferation seriously: a connector, a trace and a boundary need not perform the same work merely because we call each a line.

[^point-lines-ahmed-paths]: Sara Ahmed, [*Queer Phenomenology*](https://www.dukeupress.edu/queer-phenomenology) (2006), 15–20, especially 16: repeated journeys make paths, while established paths help direct later journeys. Ada takes up this relation as an experiment. Its stripe is authored rather than worn into the ground, and its instruction does not score compliance. Being oriented by a line, evaluated against one and physically blocked by one remain different operations to investigate.

[^point-lines-ahmed-deviation]: Ahmed, [*Queer Phenomenology*](https://www.dukeupress.edu/queer-phenomenology), 20, considers how deviations can leave marks and generate other lines. This sampled drawing offers one specific record, not the whole history of the body’s deviation.
