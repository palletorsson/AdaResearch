Concept: Facade_Venetian_Gothic is the *hybridized actualization* of the facade assembly principle. Where Facade_Classical applies the canonical six moves and Facade_Baroque dramatizes them, this map *substitutes* one of the moves — the column-order axis — with an entirely different ornamental vocabulary (Gothic), while leaving the other five axes Italian-Renaissance. The Ca' d'Oro and the Palazzo Ducale are the canonical references: a building speaking two grammars simultaneously, where the Gothic vocabulary (pointed arches, lacy quatrefoil tracery, foliated capitals) sits inside the Italian rhythm structure (regular bay spacing, three-zone vertical hierarchy, framing cornice). Neither grammar dissolves.

Actualizes: Facade_Assembly_Principle, in the *hybridized* mode. The position in the virtual field is where the column-order axis is *Gothic-vocabulary* (pointed-arch arcade rather than Doric/Ionic/Corinthian columns) and all other axes (rhythm, hierarchy, fenestration, rustication, cornice/base) remain Italian. The map is a counterexample to the assumption that the assembly grammar is locked to a single tradition; it accommodates substitution along any axis as long as the other axes carry the composition.

Sequence role: Fourth map of the facade_assembly branch (after Principle → Classical → Baroque). The principle's second deviation, this time at a different axis than Baroque touched. Where Baroque dialled the *intensity* up, Venetian Gothic replaces the *vocabulary*. Together the two prepare the player for further deviations (Rustication at the surface axis, Tenement at the ornament-stripped extreme, Critique at the rule-refusal extreme). Leads forward to Facade_Rustication.

Technical angle: A three-storey facade where the ground floor is an arcade of seven pointed arches (Gothic), the second storey (piano nobile, still hierarchically privileged in the Italian way) is a row of paired-light pointed-arch windows separated by lacy stone tracery and quatrefoil openings, the third storey is a lighter band of smaller paired windows with crenellation above. The whole composition is symmetrical (Italian); the bay rhythm is regular (Italian); the cornice frames at top (Italian); but every vertical element is a Gothic ornamental motif. Pass-2 build: the room walks today as 10x6x3 with spawn and teleporter; the Gothic-vocabulary artifacts (pointed_arch_arcade, quatrefoil_tracery_row, foliated_capital, crenellated_band) are queued for pass-3 artifact authoring.

Critical angle: Hybridization is what *makes architecture into a vernacular*. Pure styles are exceptional; living building traditions are usually hybrids. Venice's gothic was never pure — it ran the Italian commercial-republic spatial grammar through Byzantine, Arab, and Northern Gothic ornamental vocabularies simultaneously, producing a city whose facades read *as Venetian* precisely because the hybridization is consistent. The lesson the map carries is that the principle accommodates substitution along any axis without losing legibility, *because* the other axes carry the composition. The deeper political note: the assumption that styles must be pure is itself a Renaissance-and-Neoclassical Western invention; the world's other architectural traditions are mostly hybrids by default. Insisting on stylistic purity is its own ideological choice, and this map shows the alternative.
