Concept: Three-dimensional randomness is not one roll but three — independent random rotations around X, Y, and Z axes compose into orientations that are unintuitive and irreducible to any single axis. Randomness in 3D is qualitatively different from randomness in 1D.
Sequence role: Fifth map in Randomness sequence. Follows Random_Cubes (randomness shaping form) and extends to randomness controlling transformation. Precedes Random_Walk, where random transformation becomes sequential and temporal. The hardware_entropy_decay artifact introduces physical entropy sources, connecting algorithmic randomness back to thermodynamic processes. The random_decay_multimesh scales individual random rotation to population-level visualization.
Technical angle: Euler angle randomization — independent randf_range(0, TAU) for each axis, rotation composition order (XYZ vs ZYX) and gimbal lock implications. Quaternion vs Euler representation for uniform random orientation. The Random_Rotate_Random_XYZ artifact demonstrates per-axis independence; random_decay_multimesh applies random rotation to hundreds of instances via MultiMesh, showing population-level orientation distributions. GDScript basis.rotated() and Transform3D composition.
Critical angle: The map reveals a deep subtlety: uniformly random Euler angles do not produce uniformly random orientations. The three-axis decomposition is a coordinate artifact, not a geometric truth. This echoes the VectorBasics insight — the coordinate system is imposed, not discovered — now applied to randomness itself. The hardware_entropy_decay artifact materializes entropy as physical process: radioactive decay, thermal noise, the universe's own random number generator, contrasting with the algorithmic PRNG from map 1.
Key artifacts: Random_Rotate_Random_XYZ is the core demonstration — three independent random rotations composed into one orientation; hardware_entropy_decay bridges from PRNG to physical entropy sources; random_decay_multimesh scales random rotation to hundreds of instances, making distribution patterns visible; dark_sphere serves as the rotation target.
Gap: No artifact demonstrates the non-uniformity of naive Euler angle randomization — a sphere plot showing orientation clustering at poles would make the mathematical subtlety visible.
