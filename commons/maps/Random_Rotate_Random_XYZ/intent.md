Concept: Which part of an arrangement can change while its pieces remain? Here only orientation changes. Every piece keeps its place, size and colour, and accumulates small in-plane turns driven by a per-piece hidden number that grows and is kicked at random; the angle has no bound. Restoring the arrangement is not replaying its future.
Sequence role: Sixth hall of Randomness, after Random_Cubes (records that keep or forget outcomes; a heap that loses pieces at random) and before Random_Walk (a trail of one accumulated quantity, position). This hall shows an accumulation without a trail: each piece shows only the sum of its turns, and RESET wipes the sum.
Technical angle: random_decay_multimesh#study:rotation sets both stacks to the cubes' rate (0.3), switches off drift, sinking and colour, and lays the prisms on their regular twelve-column grid; the random stream is unchanged (drift draws are still taken, scaled by zero). Kicks per stack scale with piece count, so every piece expects the same share. Angles accumulate per frame, so the pace depends on the machine. The seed is applied only at start and RESET does not reseed. hardware_entropy_decay pushes the same wear values to a box, a sphere and a cylinder: rust rises by itself and faster with hand speed, grip and head turning; scratches follow the hands live; no RNG. RotateGridCubes loads SlopeGradientCubes, a 7 x 30 field tilted by position along z, still and deterministic; only its first rows lie inside the hall.
Critical angle: A difference between two stacks is not a difference of shape until the rates are matched, and even then colour, count and spacing still differ. The DECAY label names the model's presentation, not thermodynamic entropy. The rig shows wear that looks accidental but records the visitor's movement.
Key artifacts: random_decay_multimesh (primary, under #study:rotation); hardware_entropy_decay (along the right-hand wall); RotateGridCubes (a still tilt by place).
