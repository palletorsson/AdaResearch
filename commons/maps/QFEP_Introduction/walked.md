## The walk

One line of mathematics, rendered large enough to walk around: **QFE = F − λE(S) + φΔE(S,t)**. The `qfep_formula_3d` hangs the equation in navigable space, and the tutorial move is disarmingly literal — the terms are spheres, and you pick them up. Hold `grab_sphere_F`: free energy, the drive to predict and order, the pull toward pattern that every crystal and every proof obeyed. Hold `grab_sphere_E`: entropy, the size of the possibility space, everything the randomness chapters ran on. Hold λ: the balance between them. Then take the first slider and move it, gently, and watch the balance workbench respond. Nothing in this room is new — that is its design. Primitives and transformation were F; randomness and noise were E(S); forces and waves were the dynamics, ΔE over time. Twenty sequences each built one capacity, and the introduction's whole work is the moment of retrospective coherence: *oh — that is what all of it was about.*

## The turn (critical)

The formula is not learned here; it is **recognized** — and that distinction is the map's honest account of its own pedagogy. A formula introduced cold is an authority: memorize, apply, defer. A formula recognized is an *inventory*: every term redeems experience you already own, and you can audit it — you know what F feels like because you snapped grids together in the second sequence; you know E because you stood in the static. The grabbable spheres press the point further than convenience: the book has insisted since Point_One that its atoms are held, not viewed, and now the claim reaches the theory itself — abstraction, too, must survive being picked up. But keep the criticality the book has practiced elsewhere: a formula that unifies everything is exactly the kind of object the foundations crisis taught you to frisk at the door. QFE = F − λE(S) + φΔE(S,t) arrives *after* Gödel in this curriculum, on purpose — it presents itself not as the completed science of life but as a working lens, one line that holds order, freedom and becoming in a single grammar so they can be argued about. The laboratory ahead will take the terms apart one room at a time. The right posture walking in is the lab's own: hold the formula the way you are holding the sphere — firmly, and ready to put it down.
