Concept: Vector fields assign a vector to every point in space — a spatial function mapping position to direction and magnitude, visualized as flow patterns and streamlines.
Sequence role: Sixth map in Forces; the pivot from discrete vector operations to continuous spatial structures. Previous maps worked with individual vectors; this map tiles the entire space with arrows. Fields are the bridge to forces (every force field is a vector field) and the conceptual prerequisite for VectorForces and the Forces sub-sequence; follows Vectors_1.
Technical angle: field(position) → vector, streamline tracing by following local arrows, divergence and curl as field properties (intuitive, not formulaic). The directional flow corridor layout channels the learner through the field, making flow tangible — you walk along streamlines. The VectorFieldFlow artifact generates the field; dark_sphere sits within it as a test particle.
Critical angle: Fields shift the ontology from objects to space itself. A force doesn't require contact — it exists as a field that permeates space, waiting for objects to enter. This is the conceptual leap from mechanics to field theory, from particle to continuum. In QFEP terms, the field is the environment E that acts on system states S.
Key artifacts: VectorFieldFlow generates the spatial vector assignment; dark_sphere acts as the particle within the field, showing how an object responds to the local vector at its position.
Gap: An interactive streamline_tracer where the learner drops particles at different positions and watches them follow diverging paths through the field, revealing how initial position determines trajectory.
