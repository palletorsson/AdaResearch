Concept: The Sierpinski triangle — the fractal that emerges from deletion of centers at every scale. D=log(3)/log(2)=1.585, exactly between line and plane, a structure that is simultaneously too sparse to fill area and too dense to be a curve.
Sequence role: Fifth map in the Fractals sequence (10th spine, lambda_edge phase). Continues the intermediate tier by presenting the most iconic 2D fractal after Koch. Where Koch (Fractals_4) added to boundaries, Sierpinski removes from interiors — the Cantor principle (Fractals_3) elevated to 2D. The cube_bookshelf maintains the thread of fractal architecture. Connects forward to Fractals_6 where Menger sponge extends Sierpinski's logic to 3D.
Technical angle: Sierpinski construction by iterative removal of the central triangle from each remaining triangle; three self-similar copies at scale factor 2, giving D=log(3)/log(2)=1.585. The triangle can also be generated by chaos game (random vertex selection with midpoint rule) or by Pascal's triangle mod 2 — three independent algorithms converging on the same attractor. The cube_bookshelf artifact applies recursive subdivision to furniture, maintaining the object-recursion thread.
Critical angle: Sierpinski at D=1.585 occupies the precise midpoint of the lambda_edge — too much structure for a line, too little for a plane, existing in a dimension that Euclidean geometry cannot name. The triangle is also a universal attractor: the chaos game produces it from randomness, Pascal's triangle produces it from arithmetic, and iterated function systems produce it from contraction mappings. Three roads converge on the same form, suggesting that the Sierpinski triangle is not constructed but discovered — a fixed point in the space of self-similar structures.
Key artifacts: sierpinski_triangle implements the iterative center-removal construction and serves as the canonical D=1.585 reference; cube_bookshelf applies fractal subdivision to functional furniture design; dark_sphere anchors.
