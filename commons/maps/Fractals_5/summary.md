# Fractals_5 - Koch Curve

## Overview
Fractals_5 presents the Koch curve—a fractal defined by edge replacement rather than deletion. Each straight segment becomes four segments with an outward spike. After infinite iterations, the result is a curve of infinite length that never crosses itself, contained within a finite area. The Koch snowflake (three Koch curves joined) encloses finite area with infinite perimeter.

## Learning Sequence
1. Player enters and observes the Koch curve
2. Traces the edge—notes the self-similar spikes
3. Examines the snowflake—sees the curve tripled into closed form
4. Contemplates: infinite perimeter enclosing finite area
5. Compares to Cantor: addition vs. deletion
6. Understands the coastline paradox
7. Exits to explore recursive trees

## Design Intent
The map introduces **edge-replacement fractals**—a different generative method from the deletion-based Cantor set. The Koch curve adds complexity through addition, yet achieves a similar result: a structure with fractional dimension (≈1.262) that exists between line and plane.
