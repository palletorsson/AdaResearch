# Primitives 1 - Map Summary

## Overview
Primitives_1 serves as a synthesis map, bringing together all previously encountered primitives (point, line, triangle) into 3D volumetric forms. The map introduces the **trihedron** (three triangular faces meeting at a vertex) and the **tetrahedron** (simplest Platonic solid) - the first true 3D primitives that enclose volume rather than just defining boundaries.

## Spatial Layout
- **Dimensions**: 7×8 grid (compact workshop)
- **Architecture**: Elevated pedestals at (2,2) and (4,2) height 2, creating display platforms
- **Central void**: Recessed area at rows 4 (absent tiles at x=2-4) creating focal pit
- **Southern exit bay**: Absent tiles at (0-3, 7) for teleporter access
- **Entry**: Standard spawn

## Learning Sequence
1. Player spawns into workshop with elevated displays
2. Encounters trihedron - the first 3D junction (three faces, one vertex)
3. Observes how triangles can meet in 3D space, not just on plane
4. Engages with snap_tetrahedron_puzzle in sunken area
5. Constructs tetrahedron from four triangular faces
6. Recognizes: This is the first **closed 3D solid** from primitives
7. Understands progression: Point → Line → Triangle → Tetrahedron
8. Sees how 2D primitives (triangles) combine into 3D volumes
9. Exits having encountered dimensional ascension

## Design Intent

This map is the **dimensional threshold** - where 2D geometry becomes 3D volume.

**Progression of Enclosure**:
- Point: Singular position (0D)
- Line: Two points, open connection (1D)
- Triangle: Three points, closed boundary on plane (2D)
- Tetrahedron: Four points, closed boundary enclosing volume (3D)

The **trihedron** (three faces at corner) demonstrates the **junction** - how flat triangular faces can meet in 3D space at angles to each other. It's the "corner piece" that shows volume beginning to form.

The **tetrahedron** (four triangular faces) is the **simplest Platonic solid** - minimum vertices, edges, and faces needed to fully enclose 3D space.

The **elevated pedestals** display these forms at eye level, emphasizing their status as **fundamental units** - not decorative shapes, but geometric atoms of 3D space.

The **sunken puzzle area** creates a "pit of construction" where you build the tetrahedron from component triangles, revealing that 3D solids are **assemblies** of 2D primitives.

## Platonic Perfection

The tetrahedron is the first of five **Platonic solids** - perfectly regular convex polyhedra where:
- All faces are identical regular polygons
- Same number of faces meet at every vertex
- All edges are equal
- Form is fully symmetric

The five Platonic solids (tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron, icosahedron) represent **geometry without remainder** - discrete, finite, exact forms.

From primitives_axioms: "Only five convex polyhedra satisfy the conditions of Platonic perfection."

This map introduces the **fantasy of perfect discreteness** - the idea that complex 3D space can be built from perfectly regular, countable primitives.

## Synthesis Function

Primitives_1 synthesizes the sequence so far:
- Uses **points** as vertices
- Uses **lines** as edges
- Uses **triangles** as faces
- Assembles them into **volumetric solids**

It demonstrates that all previous primitives are **components** of 3D geometry. The tetrahedron is:
- 4 vertices (points)
- 6 edges (lines)
- 4 faces (triangles)
- 1 volume (enclosed space)
