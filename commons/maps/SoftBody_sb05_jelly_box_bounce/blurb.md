A cube made of eight corner masses and twenty-eight springs. Drop it. It hits the floor and wobbles — not bouncing like a rigid body, not splatting like a fluid, but doing the strange thing that only soft things do: shaping itself in mid-fall.

The wires are visible. Twelve edges along the cube, twelve face diagonals, four body diagonals. Each spring stores energy when stretched, releases it when compressed. The cube oscillates because the springs cannot agree all at once on what shape they want.

This is the minimum topology for a 3D soft body. Take away any one spring and the cube collapses into a sheet or a string. Add too many and it goes rigid. The twenty-eight are the smallest set that holds together while still admitting deformation. Engineering by minimal sufficiency.
