Concept: Mosaic_Majolica_Tiles examines the painted ceramic tiles of the Santa Chiara cloister in Naples — hand-painted landscapes, vines, and figures laid out on a strict square grid. Unlike the cut-stone tesserae of Pompeii or the angled gold of Byzantine mosaics, majolica tiles are mass-produced ceramic squares where the pattern is painted onto the surface rather than built from discrete colored pieces. This map sits at the tension between narrative imagery (unique painted scenes) and geometric repetition (the square grid that organizes them), letting the learner map baroque color palettes into procedural pattern generation.

Critical angle: Majolica tiles are the first industrial tessellation — mass-produced in workshops where molds standardized the shape and apprentices hand-painted the designs. The noise shifts from hand-cutting (geometric irregularity in Pompeii) to hand-painting (chromatic and figural irregularity). Different entropy source, same grid. This transition from craft-cut to craft-painted parallels the computational transition from discrete geometric tiling to continuous color-space mapping.
