## The walk

The chapter closes with the graph as matchmaker. The edmonds_algorithm works the pairing problem — match as many vertices as possible, no one used twice — and does it on graphs where odd cycles make greedy pairing fail. Its famous move is the blossom: when an odd loop blocks progress, shrink the whole loop into a single vertex, match through it as if it were simple, then unfold the flower and read off the pairing that was hiding inside.

## The close

Matching is the chapter's gentlest ending: after rooms of reachability, hierarchy, and bottlenecks, here the graph is asked to make *partnerships* — organs to patients, students to schools, work to hands. The blossom is the closing image the thread has earned: the tangle that stops the algorithm is not deleted but folded, carried, and re-opened as structure. Squint hard enough and everything is a graph; look closer and the graph's hardest knots are where its flowers were.
