A 12×12 grid. An 8×8 arena at its center. Inside, algorithms that run on dice rolls — random walks that wander without destination, Gaussian distributions that cluster around invisible means, height maps that rise and fall on noise alone. Each process reshapes the same cubic terrain differently. Same grid, same cells, different law of selection. The walker steps north, south, east, west — equal probability, no memory. Over 128 steps it leaves a topography. The height randomizer sculpts peaks and valleys with configurable variance. The Gaussian concentrates change near the center, sparse at the edges — a bell curve made architectural. Remove functions erase at random, carving absence into structure. Randomness is not the opposite of pattern. It is pattern's substrate — the field from which form condenses. Order is randomness that hasn't been watched long enough.
