## The walk

The walk widens the definition of "cell" until it almost breaks. The game_of_life_petri holds the canonical dish — birth, survival, death on a square grid. Then the hexagon room asks the quiet subversive question: who said four neighbors? Six changes what can live. The mold_network drops the grid entirely — a slime mold reinforcing its own good paths, efficiency without an engineer — and the ca_growth_network lets the automaton build the very network it then runs on. The science_screen flattens whichever world you stand near into a readable pane.

## What it loosens

Binary was a convenience, the square grid a habit. The walk's lesson is that the *form* of the neighborhood is an authored choice, and every choice authors different possible worlds — the chapter's first genuinely political sentence, delivered entirely by furniture.
