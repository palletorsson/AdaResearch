Concept: ForcesFoundations grounds Newton's three laws in physical experience across three islands. One island hosts Newton's laws as interactive demonstrations — F=ma with adjustable parameters, action-reaction pairs, inertia resistance. Another presents projectile motion as parabolic trajectories that the learner studies before becoming the projectile themselves via a jump pad. The third introduces friction and drag as forces that resist motion, with a ramp demonstrating how surface properties transform sliding behavior. The jump pad between islands is not a convenience — it is the lesson: the learner's own arc through the void follows the same parabolic math they just observed.

Sequence role: Map 5 of 9 in Vectors & Forces: From Direction to Dynamics (position in the combined sequence; map 1 of the forces sub-arc). The learner arrives from the vector workbench carrying magnitude, direction, dot product, cross product, field flow, and throwing experience. This map pivots from vector geometry to physical dynamics — the same vectors now represent forces that cause acceleration. It feeds into ForcesComposition (superposition and energy) and then ForcesSystems (attractors and springs). In QFEP terms, this is where the temporal evolution operator enters: F = ma means forces drive the change of state over time. The learner moves from "vectors describe" to "forces change."

Technical angle: newtons_laws demonstrates F = ma interactively — the learner applies force vectors and watches acceleration respond proportionally, with mass as a scaling factor. friction_ramp implements surface friction as a force opposing motion along an inclined plane, decomposing gravity into normal and parallel components (connecting back to the normal_force_demo from VectorOperations). bouncing_ball demonstrates elastic collision with restitution coefficient controlling energy retention per bounce. example_2_5_fluid_resistance_vr implements drag force proportional to velocity squared (F_drag = -c * |v| * v), showing how air and fluid resistance create terminal velocity — a force that grows with speed until it balances gravity. example_2_3_gravity_scaled_by_mass_vr demonstrates that gravitational acceleration is independent of mass (all objects fall at 9.8 m/s^2) but gravitational force scales with mass (F = mg), resolving the Galileo paradox through interaction. basis_vectors_rig and VectorBasics anchor the force vectors in the spatial frame established in earlier maps.

Critical angle: Newton's second law is not a definition — it is the claim that acceleration is proportional to force and inversely proportional to mass, and this claim is empirically true of the physical world. The sequence truth states that "acceleration is the only thing you feel" — you never feel position or velocity, only the force that changes them. ForcesFoundations makes this visceral: the learner watches objects accelerate under applied forces, feels the jump pad launch them along a parabolic arc, and observes friction decelerating motion on a ramp. The QFEP connection runs through the distinction between state (S = position + velocity) and evolution (dS/dt driven by F/m). The fluid resistance demo reveals something deeper: drag force depends on velocity, which means the force itself depends on the current state. This is a self-referential loop — the state determines the force that determines the next state — and it is the simplest instance of the feedback dynamics that QFEP places at the center of all evolving systems. Terminal velocity, where drag exactly cancels gravity, is a fixed point of this feedback loop: a state that perpetuates itself. The learner encounters equilibrium as a dynamic phenomenon, not a static one.

Key artifacts: newtons_laws demonstrates F = ma with interactive force application and mass scaling. friction_ramp shows surface friction opposing motion on an inclined plane with adjustable surface properties. bouncing_ball demonstrates elastic collision with tunable restitution (energy retention per bounce). example_2_5_fluid_resistance_vr implements quadratic drag (F = -c|v|v) creating terminal velocity — the force that grows with speed until equilibrium. example_2_3_gravity_scaled_by_mass_vr shows gravitational acceleration independent of mass but gravitational force proportional to it. VectorBasics and basis_vectors_rig anchor forces in the spatial frame. catalyst_target provides practice targets for projectile application. proximity_spawner introduces combat pressure via kresling_spire and miura_crawler enemies. dark_sphere serves as ambient marker.

Gap: The map lacks a dedicated projectile motion artifact that visualizes the parabolic trajectory with decomposed horizontal (constant velocity) and vertical (accelerating) components — currently the jump pad enacts projectile physics but does not annotate it. A spring force demo (F = -kx) would preview Hooke's law before ForcesSystems introduces springs formally. The friction_ramp would benefit from a coefficient-of-friction slider letting the learner compare ice, wood, and rubber surfaces on the same incline.
