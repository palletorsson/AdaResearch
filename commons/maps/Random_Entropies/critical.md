# Which differences are admitted?

A pink chair can be a colour outcome, a seat in a marked site, an exception to a pattern, or a partially retained identity. Each instrument makes one of those relations available to calculation. None exhausts the chair or the possible encounter with it.

The useful QFEP question is therefore concrete: which change can the present rule register, and where might another rule become necessary? A shuffle that leaves Shannon entropy unchanged is productive evidence. The difference did not disappear; it lay outside the variable being measured. A repeated irregular motif similarly exposes the gap between appearance and description. Splitting one category into two can increase average uncertainty without changing the best guess. Rearranging the all-pink chairs changes a composition without changing that colour prediction.

The room does not use entropy as an all-purpose name for freedom, disorder or emergence. Its measures depend on distributions, macrostates, channels, dynamical rules or thermal boundaries. Changing those contracts is an intervention worthy of preservation and comparison. There is also an exclusion to notice: the finite chair model treats same-colour chairs as interchangeable, the glass channel reduces perception to a small code, and the chair-ID sampler ignores the effort of reaching or lifting. Those simplifications permit exact tests while leaving bodily differences to a later experiment.

The next research step is to find where one of these simplifications becomes the subject. A person's failed prediction, a form made illegible by its sign, or a different way of selecting a chair can justify a revision. No participant response or recognition threshold is inferred from a passing software test.
