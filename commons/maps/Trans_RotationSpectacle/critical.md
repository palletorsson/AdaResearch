# The time needed to see a rule

The previous room asks where a body can pass. This gallery gives the visitor room to pause. The floor supports both an unhurried look and a return to something already passed.

The tunnel has its orientations when it is built. The visitor needs time to move through them. The carousel's layers keep turning while the visitor decides what to follow. A rule can be available in code without being available to attention all at once.

The five profiles keep angular speeds and layer heights constant. Their shapes alter what stands out: the faster layers can diminish into a point, widen into a brim, or occupy the same radius as their neighbours. This is a particular comparison between silhouette and motion. It cannot establish that perception is caused by shape alone; viewing position, stripes, lighting and the visitor's movement also participate.

The hole and the turn should retain their distinct operations. Subtraction makes a hollow in each tunnel segment. Rotation arranges those hollows. Naming the result a passage does not remove the need to inspect its physical clearance for a body.

There is no required single gaze that sees all eight layers equally. The layers continue while attention follows one stripe. That remainder connects this room to the book's earlier counter: beginning an investigation always leaves other processes running.
