## 1. Twenty kept, seven removed

At Stage 0, the Menger cube stands sealed inside its cyan `FlightBox` frame (`5.30 m` on a side as scaled into Floor 118, inheriting the nine-metre standalone study): no doorway, no inside. At the `MengerLesson` desk beside it, each press of **NEXT** divides every surviving cube into twenty-seven (`3 × 3 × 3`) sub-cubes and deletes the seven-cell cross running through its centre and six face-centres:

- **Stage 0:** `1` solid cube (full initial volume).
- **Stage 1:** `20` retained cubes (`20/27` of the volume) — three orthogonal corridors pierce clean through the solid, each a third of the cube's side (`1.77 m` on Floor 118).
- **Stage 2:** `400` retained cubes (`400/729` of the volume) — every surviving block now carries smaller copies of the exact same square opening (`0.59 m` on Floor 118). More separate objects exist to hold less matter.

Toggle **GHOST** and the seven large removed volumes plus the hundred and forty smaller ones reappear as pale non-colliding shells — drawing the architecture out of what was discarded.

## 2. Same geometry, different body

In pure mathematics, the Menger sponge is self-similar at every depth and converges to a universal curve (`D = log 20 / log 3 ≈ 2.727`) with infinite surface and zero volume. Inside the `FlightBox` enclosure on Floor 118, that scale-invariance breaks the instant a real body approaches:

- The **Level-1 opening** (`1.77 m`, `L / 3`) admits Model B standing upright (`1.72 m`) and flying straight across the heart of the structure.
- The **Level-2 opening** (`0.59 m`, `L / 9`) repeats the exact same square ratio, lets light pass freely across the room, and **stops a standing body and sixty-four-centimetre walker cold**.

*I find the same turn at another scale.* — Step the Menger sponge from 1 to 20 to 400 cubes, then compare the large opening against a smaller copy: the shape repeats, yet your body fits only one.

There was a connection in the geometry. There was not yet a route for every body.

## 3. Finite collision beside an infinite theorem

A third cut would demand eight thousand cubes and shrink the corridors by another factor of three. The museum stops at Stage 2 on purpose: not because the theorem ends, but because real collision, real frame budgets, and a real sixty-four-centimetre body live in finite space. Meanwhile, across the hall, `diffusion_limited_aggregation` builds structure by the opposite gesture — wandering particles sticking one by one rather than carving an interior out — beside `film_menger_twenty` (*Twenty of twenty-seven*) and the catalyst installations.

## 4. What carries downward

Hold the mismatch between mathematical self-similarity and bodily affordance as you descend. Repeating a rule across scales changes who can enter, who can turn around, and who stays outside looking through the light.
