Concept: Multiple trees, one world. Shared light, shared soil — the forest is an ecology of competing grammars. When several L-Systems grow in the same space, resource competition shapes their morphology. The grammar is no longer isolated; it negotiates.
Sequence role: Fifth map in the L-Systems sequence (11th spine sequence, lambda_edge phase). The move from single-grammar demonstrations to multi-grammar ecologies. Intermediate difficulty — the learner must hold multiple rule sets in mind simultaneously and observe how interaction produces forms that no single grammar would generate alone. Connects backward to Growth's environmental sensitivity, forward to LSystems_Living where grammar becomes terrain, and laterally to swarm intelligence sequences where multi-agent dynamics produce emergent structure; follows LSystems_Architecture.
Technical angle: Competing rule systems operating in shared spatial substrate — each L-System has its own axiom and production rules, but all share the same light field and soil resources. Branching decisions are influenced by proximity to other organisms: crowding triggers tropism adjustments, light occlusion favors vertical growth, nutrient depletion limits expansion. The ForestCompetition artifact implements this multi-grammar simulation. The branching_coral artifact demonstrates that the same competitive dynamics apply underwater, where marine branching organisms compete for current flow rather than sunlight.
Critical angle: Competition reveals that form is relational, not intrinsic. A tree growing alone looks nothing like the same tree growing in a forest. Ecological niches emerge not from the grammar but from the interaction between grammars and shared constraints. This is the social dimension of generative systems — individual rules produce collective morphology. The lambda_edge truth deepens: a sentence can become a forest, but a forest is more than the sum of its sentences.
Key artifacts: ForestCompetition simulates multiple L-Systems competing for light and soil in shared space; branching_coral demonstrates marine branching competition under flow dynamics; dark_sphere anchors the research environment.
Gap: Needs a resource-visualization overlay that renders light availability, soil nutrients, and competition pressure as heatmaps layered over the growing forest, making the invisible ecological forces visible.
