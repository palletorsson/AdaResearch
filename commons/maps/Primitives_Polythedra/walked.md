## What this map holds (seed)

A trihedron is a geometric configuration where three triangular faces meet at a single vertex, forming a corner of space. It is not a closed solid by itself, but a spatial junction — an elementary expression of volume beginning to form.

## The walk

Until now every form has lain flat — points, lines, triangles, all obedient to a plane. Here the map lifts one edge off the floor. Take `grab_trihedron` and you are holding the first genuinely *three-dimensional* thing in the sequence: three triangular faces folded up to meet at a single vertex, a **corner of space**. It is not a solid — you can see it is open, unclosed, a junction rather than a container — and that openness is the point. Volume does not arrive here as a box. It arrives as a *meeting*, three surfaces agreeing on one vertex.

Then the map lets you close it: `snap_tetrahedron_puzzle` asks you to bring trihedra together into the first fully enclosed solid, and `pyramid_edit` lets you modify the volume you've made. The felt arc is small and complete — from a corner you can see through, to a solid you cannot. And the room holds a stranger: `becoming_catalyst`, the artifact that does not sit still to be measured but *changes you* — the first hint, at the threshold of volume, that the primitives are about to stop being neutral building blocks and start being things that act back.

## The turn (critical)

The quiet radical claim of this map is that **volume begins as junction, not as enclosure.** The intuitive story of a solid is the box — an inside sealed off from an outside. The trihedron refuses that story: it says the elementary fact of three dimensions is not the container but the *corner*, the place where surfaces meet, and that enclosure is a later, optional consequence of enough corners agreeing. This is topology's intuition (vertices, edges, faces, and the relation Euler will formalize) offered before its formula — volume as a fact about *meetings* rather than a fact about *interiors*. It reframes the whole idea of a solid: not a thing with an inside, but a sufficiently negotiated set of junctions.

And `becoming_catalyst` in this room is the map making its most important move early. Every primitive so far has been inert — a point you place, a line you draw, a solid you assemble, all of it holding still to be known. The catalyst is the first primitive that *acts on the knower* — a form that changes you rather than waiting to be changed. Its placement here, exactly at the emergence of volume, is the book's thesis breaking surface: the "building blocks" were never neutral, and the moment they gain a third dimension they gain the capacity to turn and address you. The queer turn on the whole vocabulary of "primitives" starts at the corner where volume first stands up.
