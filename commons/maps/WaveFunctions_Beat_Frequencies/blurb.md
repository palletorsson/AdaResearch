Two frequencies, nearly identical. 440 Hz and 442 Hz — close enough to seem the same, different enough to fight. Their sum produces a third thing: a slow pulse, a rhythmic swelling and fading. The beat frequency equals the difference. Two hertz apart, two throbs per second. The closer the frequencies, the slower the pulse. Perfect unison produces no beat at all.

Piano tuners listen for this. They narrow the gap until the beating stops — until difference dissolves into identity. Binaural beats go further: send one frequency to each ear and the brainstem constructs the interference internally. The beat exists nowhere in the air. It is built inside the listener.

Dissonance is proximity. Rhythm emerges from the failure to match. Perfection is silence.
