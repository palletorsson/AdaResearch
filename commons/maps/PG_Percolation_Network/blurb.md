# PG Percolation Network

Fill a grid with random cells. At low density, isolated clusters. Islands in void. Increase the probability — 10%, 20%, 30% — and clusters grow, touch, merge. At 59.27% (the critical threshold), something happens: a connected path spans the entire grid. One more percentage point and the system goes from fragmented to traversable.

Percolation theory studies this phase transition. Not gradual change but sudden connectivity. Below threshold, information stays local. Above it, signals cross the whole network. The transition is sharp, universal, and unavoidable — the same mathematics governs coffee filters, forest fires, epidemic spread.

From the raised walkway, watch clusters pulse and merge in the void below. The dark sphere marks where the threshold lives. Connectivity is not a property of the parts. It emerges at a density no individual cell controls.
