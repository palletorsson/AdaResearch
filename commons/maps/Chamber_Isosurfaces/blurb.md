The chamber's verb is *to threshold*.

A field runs beneath your feet. Where you place a block, the field crosses its cutoff — air becoming stone, the surface finding itself around your hand. Below the line is nothing. Above it, world.

Inside and outside are not properties of space but a number's relation to a cutoff, and here you hold the cutoff. Move your intent and the skin redraws, the same field yielding a different world. The queer cave two rooms back argued that the cutoff is a politics; this chamber hands you that politics as a tool. You do not carve the surface. You name the line, and the line does the rest.
