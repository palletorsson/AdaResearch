Concept: Forces as vectors that act on bodies — gravity, wind, friction summed into a net force that determines acceleration via F=ma.
Sequence role: Seventh map in Forces; the moment vectors become physical. Previous maps were geometric (operations on arrows); this map connects arrows to Newton's second law. The elevated workbench provides a laboratory frame for force experiments. This is the conceptual hinge of the entire sequence — from math to physics; follows VectorFieldFlow; leads to VectorMotion.
Technical angle: F=ma as the core equation, net force as vector sum, force_field_visualizer showing spatial force distributions. Free body diagrams as the analytical tool: isolate an object, draw all forces as arrows from its center, sum them. The workbench elevation gives a clear view of force vectors acting on objects below.
Critical angle: Force is the bridge between state and change. Position and velocity are states (the S in QFEP); force is what drives temporal evolution (the φ·ΔE(S,t) term). Without force, states persist unchanged (Newton's first law). Force is the reason things happen. This map marks the transition from the sequence's vector-math phase to its dynamics phase.
Key artifacts: VectorForces provides the core force demonstration; force_field_visualizer maps force vectors across space; dark_sphere receives forces as a test body.
Gap: A net_force_calculator where the learner adds/removes individual force arrows (gravity, wind, spring) and watches the resultant arrow and resulting acceleration update in real time.
