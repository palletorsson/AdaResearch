## The walk

The strictest operator, alone in its room. The csg_intersection_demo overlaps two solids and keeps only what they share — A∩B — discarding everything either one held alone. Two generous forms can yield a thin sliver, an almond, or nothing at all; the survivor is only the space both agreed to occupy.

## What it teaches

The primitive is coincidence, not contact. Where two things *meet* is not where their surfaces touch but where their interiors genuinely overlap, and intersection is the chapter's instrument for isolating that shared inside. It teaches a discipline the union room could not: that composition can *reduce*, that combining two solids can leave less than either, that agreement between forms is scarce and worth finding. Every well-fitted joint in the chapters ahead — every place two parts truly mate rather than merely abut — is this operator quietly at work, keeping the common volume and throwing the rest away.
