## The seed

The chamber's verb is **to sculpt**. The noise chapter taught that structured disorder — randomness that remembers its neighbors — is what makes a world livable rather than static, and here you hold the sculptor's end of that lesson. Every block you place is a sample dropped into the field, and because the noise is correlated, your single edit ripples into terrain, one deposit swelling a whole slope. The room is deliberately weaponless because its verb is generative, not defensive: the chapter that began at the white-noise wall — pure amnesia, unlivable — ends with you standing inside a landscape you grew from remembered chance. It plants the gentlest seed in the book, and maybe the truest to the project's want: that making a world is not authoring every stone but seeding a field with the right memory and letting the ground rise to meet you.
