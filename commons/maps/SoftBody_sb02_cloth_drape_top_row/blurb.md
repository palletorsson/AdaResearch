A rectangle of cloth held all along the top — every vertex in the first row pinned to the ceiling. Gravity does the rest. The middle does not sag into a catenary now; the row of pins refuses any single curve. What hangs is a flat sheet with the faintest side curl where the grid ends and the constraint runs out.

Each unpinned vertex still negotiates with its neighbors through springs. But the top is rigid: there is no high-point pulling sideways, only the downward pull and the cloth's own width holding itself open. The shape that settles is the answer to a different boundary condition — not "two points" but "a line."

Switch the pin pattern and the same physics gives a different vessel. Sb01 hung two corners and got a catenary. Sb02 pins the whole top edge and gets a curtain. Same equations, same gravity, same springs. The difference lives entirely in the boundary.
