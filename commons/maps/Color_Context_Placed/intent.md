Concept: A survey gallery for the whole color sequence — it holds every color artifact the other seven maps don't, so the range of color phenomena can be seen in one walk rather than one lesson at a time.
Sequence role: Eighth and final map in Color; the overflow exhibition. After the focused single-idea rooms (nails, grid palette, rainbow, pillar, paint, walls, flashlight), this is where the leftovers become a collection. Position is curatorial, not pedagogical — it assumes the earlier vocabulary and lets the player browse.
Technical angle: A 15×35 gallery whose interactables are mostly delegate_to artifacts — DNA color stacks, modern-art homages (Albers, Mondrian, Kandinsky, Rothko), loom/mill pattern variants, and primitive assemblers — each a parameterized instance of a shared builder rather than a bespoke scene. The map demonstrates the /dna-promoted pattern at gallery scale: dozens of distinct exhibits, almost no new scene files.
Critical angle: Putting every color artifact in one hall makes the sequence's core claim spatial — color is relational. The simultaneous-contrast stacks and the complementary pairs only do their work because a second color is in view. The gallery is an argument that perception is contextual: nothing here means anything by itself.
Key artifacts: the dna_modern_art_* homages anchor the walls (color theory as art history); the dna_color_stacks_* exhibits stage the contrast demonstrations; the loom/mill pattern variants carry palette into surface and weave.
Gap: With this many delegate exhibits in one room, the map leans on placement quality — worth a VR walk to confirm the simultaneous-contrast pieces sit where their neighbors actually read against them.
