# A ceiling is also a rule

Floorplans often make horizontal territory visible while treating height as an infinite, neutral resource. The neural-network hall disproves that convenience. Its eleven-metre body cannot inhabit a system whose ceiling is silently fixed at five metres, however generous the plan appears from above.

The smaller objects expose the same politics at another scale. A plinth can lend authority, but it can also misdescribe a complete working cabinet as a specimen. A wall can liberate floor space, but it also determines the side from which information is legible. A loom's output is not empty circulation; it is space claimed by ongoing production.

These contracts do not remove curatorial judgment. They make its assumptions inspectable: what receives elevation, what receives a wall, which movements stay possible, and whether the museum reserves room for a process or only for its photographed body.
