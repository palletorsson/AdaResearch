## The walk

## The turn

The turn is the chapter's argument arriving where you live: buildings are subtractions. A doorway is a difference, a room is a void bounded by solid, and the whole mass of a wall exists in order to be selectively removed — the cavity is the real subject, the wall merely its frame. This inverts how architecture is usually seen; the chapter insists the space is composed and the material is only what holds the space's shape. Every inhabited interior is a lawful hollow, and the operator that makes it is the same A−B you met two rooms ago, now at the scale of a life. The turn leaves you standing inside the demonstration: the room you occupy is a Boolean result, and you are living in the answer to a composition.
