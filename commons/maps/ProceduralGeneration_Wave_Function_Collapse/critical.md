# Wave Function Collapse — Critical

## The Quantum Metaphor

WFC borrows from quantum mechanics: superposition, observation, collapse. But this borrowing is not innocent.

The algorithm suggests that **observation creates reality** — that before we look, all possibilities exist simultaneously. This is a seductive metaphor, but it encodes a particular epistemology: reality is discrete, enumerable, rule-bound.

What cannot be a tile cannot exist.

## The Violence of the Grid

WFC operates on grids. Every cell is identical in size, in potential, in the violence of discretization. The continuous becomes the discrete. The flowing becomes the snapped.

What organic forms does the grid exclude? What curves cannot be captured by adjacency rules? The algorithm generates "organic" dungeons, but the organicism is constrained by orthogonal thinking.

## Whose Rules?

Adjacency rules are the DNA of WFC. They determine what can exist next to what. But who writes these rules?

The designer. The architect. The one who decides that walls cannot be next to nothing, that floors must connect, that certain combinations are "invalid."

These rules encode normativity. They define the possible by excluding the impossible. Every adjacency matrix is a statement about what belongs together and what must be kept apart.

## Contradiction as Failure

When WFC reaches a contradiction — a cell with no valid states — the algorithm considers this a failure. It backtracks, erases, tries again.

But what if contradiction is generative? What if the impossible combination is exactly what needed to exist? WFC cannot produce the paradox, the glitch, the thing that shouldn't be.

The Escher staircase cannot emerge from WFC. It violates local consistency.

## The Queer Reading

Queer existence often violates adjacency rules. It places things next to things that "shouldn't" go together. It finds the contradiction and lives there.

WFC generates coherent worlds. But coherence is not the only value. Sometimes we need the incoherent, the contradictory, the impossible.

**What would a WFC look like that embraced contradiction instead of backtracking from it?**

## Entropy as Control

The algorithm collapses low-entropy cells first — the ones with few choices. It starts with the most constrained and works outward.

This is a politics of certainty. Decide the easy things first. Let the difficult emerge from accumulated decisions.

But what if we collapsed high-entropy cells first? What if we started with maximum uncertainty and let order emerge from chaos?

The algorithm makes a choice. That choice is not neutral.
