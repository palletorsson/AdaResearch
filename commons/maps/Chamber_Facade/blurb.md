Six moves walked. Now the room is small and open, and you compose.

Pick a column order. Choose how many bays. Set the hierarchy: heavy base, speaking middle, crowning top. Decide what the windows say. Pick a surface — smooth or rusticated, marble or diamond. Frame the whole with cornice and base, or refuse the frame entirely.

The grammar reads every choice. Coherent compositions feel right; dissonant ones feel like errors until you realise they are deliberate, which is when refusal becomes composition. A facade is a position in the space the rules carve out. The chamber is where the player finds out what they think the grammar is for.

Return to lab when the facade you made teaches you what the next one should be.
