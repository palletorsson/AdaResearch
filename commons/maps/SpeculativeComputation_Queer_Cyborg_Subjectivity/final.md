# Who is the body that looks back?

The mirror is not a neutral surface. It returns the body the system knows how to render, and leaves the rest to the imagination.

<!-- @super_drag_mirror -->

Change one layer of the avatar at a time: skin, garment, posture, voice, or interface. Watch the reflection update and notice which changes feel like decoration and which change how the body occupies the room. The code treats these as parameters; your movement gives them consequence.

The cyborg is not half human and half machine. It is a relation assembled from sensors, defaults, textures, physics, and expectation. The mirror makes that assembly inspectable. It can also make a norm feel natural: a body preset arrives before you have asked who selected it.

Try a combination that the menu did not name. Save it, then return to the default. The difference is not only visual. It is a small test of which bodies the interface lets you compose without explanation.

The next room moves from one assembled body to systems that can change their own morphology. Keep asking what a rule permits, and who gets to alter it.
