# The mirror is an interface with a politics

The cyborg is often presented as liberation by addition: give the body a tool and call it more capable. The mirror asks who wrote the tool, who is recognised by its categories, and who has to improvise around them.

A preset can feel intimate while remaining administrative. It names a body before the player does. Queer assembly is the practice of making a body that the menu did not anticipate, then noticing which defaults still return when the scene reloads.

The mirror does not reveal an authentic self underneath the code. It shows a negotiated surface: mesh, shader, gesture, camera, and social reading. That incompleteness is useful. It keeps embodiment open to revision without pretending that every revision is equally available.
