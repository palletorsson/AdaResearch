One index finds a point on a line. Two indices find a point on a plane. The 2D array is a grid — rows and columns, X and Y, a coordinate system that turns flat space into addressable territory. Every cell has an address. Every address has two parts.

Navigate a 7×9 grid. Dark spheres pulse at intersections, their purple glow marking positions you can name with numbers. Move along X — horizontal. Move along Y — vertical. Together they sweep the entire surface. No cell is unreachable. No position is ambiguous.

A single axis is a path. Two axes are a world. The moment you add a second dimension, space stops being sequential and becomes *spatial* — and the question shifts from "what comes next" to "what's adjacent."
