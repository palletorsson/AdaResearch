# The room and its volume

<!-- @facade_room_cast -->

A door, a window, a cornice. The facade gives you enough to imagine a room before you have entered it. The opening on the left reaches the ground. Walk through.

The wall has depth. Its pale reveal stays beside you for a step, then the room opens. One side keeps a straight wall, with a low skirting edge and a rail near the ceiling. On the other side, the enclosure curves away. The outside did not tell you everything about the space it held.

![The facade and the corresponding positive form.](/book-images/iterations/2026-09-24-facade-room-and-cast/pair.png)

*Two results from the same construction. The left admits a body. The right makes the interior present as matter.*

Turn towards the window. Its recess widens on the room side. A view passes through it, but the low wall beneath it remains in your way. Door and window are both cuts. Their dimensions let them receive different parts of your presence.

## The room outside

Come back through the door. Beside the building stands a pale body with a familiar arrangement on its face. A tall projection reaches the ground. A smaller one sits higher, to its right. They occupy the places where the door and window let something through.

This form comes from the same interior. We kept the volume removed from the block and placed its positive beside the room. The cut that made an entrance now supplies a surface. Approach the tall projection: it stops you.

![The positive retains the doorway and stepped window profile.](/book-images/iterations/2026-09-24-facade-room-and-cast/positive.png)

*The positive is bounded by the original block. Extensions of the cutting shapes outside that block are not included.*

Follow its side. Near the top, a groove runs along the face. Inside the room, the corresponding rail stood out from the wall. Here it withdraws into the surface. The same detail changes which way it meets you.

At the back, the curved alcove becomes a rounded body. You can walk around a space you have just occupied. A moment ago, it held your view from within. Now it interrupts your view of what stands beyond it.

## A trace of an interior

Rachel Whiteread's *Ghost* and *House* give this exchange another history. Their casts retained surfaces from particular domestic spaces. A fireplace, a window or a moulding could remain legible when its former use was no longer available.[^1]

Our room was made for this encounter. It has no former household whose history we can claim. What you carry between these two forms is the memory of having entered: the doorway's depth, the window's position, the turn towards the alcove. That small history is enough to begin recognising what the positive preserves and what it cannot offer back.

The computer can keep both results. Subtract the interior from the block and it makes room; keep their intersection and the removed volume becomes an object. This reversibility belongs to the construction. It does not restore a place that has been lost.

![A view back towards the real doorway and window from inside.](/book-images/iterations/2026-09-24-facade-room-and-cast/inside-return.png)

*The openings remain available in the room while their positive counterparts stand beside it.*

The facade parts return from the existing architectural works: a window frame, a door surround, the repeated small blocks beneath a cornice. This time they belong to an interior you can verify by entering. Their regular arrangement still leaves something undisclosed. You have to go behind the face to find the curve.

[^1]: Rachel Whiteread, *Ghost* (1990), [National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/131285-ghost); *House* (1993), [Artangel's construction account](https://www.artangel.org.uk/project/house/construction/). The plaster sections of *Ghost* and the concrete construction of *House* are material casting processes. This digital positive is a related spatial investigation, not a reconstruction of either work or its making.
