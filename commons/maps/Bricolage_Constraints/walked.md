## The walk

The room is a hall of paired experiments — fail on the left, pass on the right, three constraints down its length. Gravity: an unsupported span hangs off its single post, its load on the floor; beside it, two posts carry the same span level. Balance: a stack creeps outward until its mass leaves the base and leans; beside it, the same courses centred and upright. Triangulation: four members racked into a parallelogram; beside them, the same four plus one diagonal, rigid. The verdict is in the colour — red where it fails, cyan where it holds. The clipboard names the room: what pushes back.

## The turn

This is where the chapter turns, from freedom to resistance. The first three rooms handed you an open field — parts with no fixed meaning, arrangements with no wrong answer. Here the world answers back. A constraint is not a rule someone imposed; it is what happens when you build wrong, and you cannot argue with the fallen beam. The pairs teach by comparison because that is how the bricoleur actually learns — not told the law but shown the gap the missing member leaves. And the deepest of the three, triangulation, is the one the last rooms are made of: a quad hinges, a triangle cannot, and that single fact is what the chair, the sculpture, and the dome all have to satisfy. To recombine until the constraints are satisfied — the chapter's own definition — starts here, at the moment the constraints become visible.
