Concept: Force composition as gameplay — four rotor thrust vectors balanced against gravity, where tilt translates thrust into lateral motion and the learner's control inputs are literally force vectors being summed.
Sequence role: Twenty-second map in Forces; the first game map. After 21 maps of concept and demonstration, the drone makes force composition playable. Every control input is a force vector; every wobble is a net-force imbalance. The game framing (health display, targets, hit tracking) transforms physics understanding into skill. Connects back to VectorForces (map 7) for force summation and Forces_1 for F=ma; follows VectorTorque; leads to Forces_Destruct.
Technical angle: Four thrust vectors (one per rotor) summed with gravity to produce net force; net force / mass = acceleration; Euler integration for position update. Hover requires thrust = mg exactly. Tilt redirects thrust laterally, trading lift for horizontal acceleration. The gravity_gun_test_scene adds a force application tool; targets (queer_cylinder_target) provide precision goals.
Critical angle: The drone is the complete test of force literacy. To hover, you must balance forces. To move, you must create controlled imbalance. To stop, you must anticipate inertia. Every failure mode — wobble, overshoot, crash — maps directly to a force concept from earlier in the sequence. The game makes physics consequential: understanding isn't optional, it's the difference between flight and crash.
Key artifacts: vector_drone is the playable force-composition vehicle; gravity_gun_test_scene adds interactive force application; health_display and hits_reset_display provide game feedback; queer_cylinder_target provides precision goals that demand fine force control.
Gap: A force_overlay_mode that shows the four thrust vectors, gravity, and net force as visible arrows during gameplay would close the loop between playing the game and understanding the physics.
