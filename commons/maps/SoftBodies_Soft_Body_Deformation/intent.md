Concept: A cube that refuses rigidity — the jelly cube is a spring-mass system where every vertex is connected to its neighbors by springs with stiffness and damping, so the shape deforms under gravity, collision, and impulse rather than shattering or holding. Form is not imposed; it emerges from the material's own dynamics, and the bouncy room with elevated platforms is the proving ground.
Sequence role: First map in Soft Bodies (13th spine sequence, integration phase), intermediate difficulty. Opens the sequence by establishing the fundamental substrate: mass points connected by springs. Connects forward to SoftBodies_Carusell where soft bodies gain rotational context, and to SoftBodies_Cloth_Physics where the spring topology flattens into fabric.
Technical angle: Spring-mass system with Hooke's law (F = -kx) governing each edge, Verlet or semi-implicit Euler integration for stability, damping coefficients to prevent perpetual oscillation. Collision detection against rigid platforms using signed distance fields or raycasting. The softmill artifact likely demonstrates continuous deformation under mechanical constraint — a mill that kneads the soft body.
Critical angle: The jelly cube is a philosophical object: it has no fixed shape, only a rest configuration it perpetually negotiates with external forces. This is the integration phase thesis — that form is a dynamic equilibrium, not a static property. The spring-mass model is also the simplest refutation of the solid/fluid binary: soft bodies are neither, occupying the continuum between.
Key artifacts: jelly_cube demonstrates spring-mass deformation under gravity and collision; softmill applies continuous mechanical force to a soft body, revealing deformation under sustained load.
Gap: Needs a spring-constant comparator — an artifact that renders the same cube with k values from very stiff to very soft side by side, making the stiffness-to-behavior relationship visible as a continuous spectrum rather than a single tuned parameter.
