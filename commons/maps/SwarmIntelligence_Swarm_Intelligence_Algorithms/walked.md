## The walk

The world room lets the agents eat each other. The ecosystem_simulation runs predator and prey with no referee — populations booming until the food runs low, crashing, and circling each other in slow oscillation, the Lotka-Volterra waltz danced by creatures who never heard the music. Coordination here is not a flock's grace but a food web's balance, held by nothing but the consequences agents impose on one another.

## The world

The world register widens the chapter to its largest claim: this is how the living world runs. No ecosystem has a manager; every stable one is a swarm of local appetites whose global regulation is emergent, provisional, and real. The oscillations are the point — balance in nature is never a resting state but a chase that never quite ends, predator lagging prey around a loop. Standing in it you see the thread's whole argument at biosphere scale: local rules produce global intelligence, and the intelligence looks less like a plan than like a *persistence* — the systems that coordinate are simply the ones still here. Which is the chapter's quiet answer to who is in charge: nobody, and it works, and it has always worked.
