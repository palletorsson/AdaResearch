A second CA rule, taken from the same family but with one change: cells that come alive never die. Run it for twelve generations on a small grid. What hangs from this rule is not a tattered lattice but a filled mosaic — every cell that ever ignited is still there, woven into a dense fabric pinned at two corners with a light wind blowing across it.

Where Conway's rule (sb_ca01) gave a sparse skeleton that barely cohered, Life-without-Death writes a fully populated surface. Every birth is permanent. The grid fills, the spring graph saturates, the cloth that emerges is dense and woven. Hang it from two corners and the wind moves it like a curtain. Hang Conway's product from the same corners and it tears.

Two cellular rules, identical in everything except how cells die, produce two qualitatively different fabrics. The rule is the loom. The death rule is what decides whether the loom weaves a tapestry or a net.
