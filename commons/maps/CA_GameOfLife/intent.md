Concept: Conway's Game of Life as the canonical cellular automaton — birth with exactly three neighbors, survival with two or three, death otherwise. Six phenomena (avalanche, crack, dendrite, disease, ecosystem, percolation) demonstrate how the same rule set produces radically different dynamics depending on initial conditions.
Sequence role: Second map in the Cellular Automata sequence; the moment the abstract grid from CA_1 gains biological language. Birth, survival, and death reframe state transitions as a life cycle. The six named phenomena show that one rule generates an entire taxonomy of behaviors — connecting forward to CA_3's systematic classification of elementary rules. The mirrored_cellular_automata artifact bridges back to CA_1's symmetry theme.
Technical angle: Conway's Life rules: B3/S23 in birth/survival notation. The map demonstrates sensitivity to initial conditions — small perturbations in starting patterns produce avalanches (cascading state changes), dendritic growth (branching expansion), and percolation thresholds (connectivity phase transitions). The ca_bridge and ca_columns artifacts show stable and oscillating structures (still lifes, oscillators) that persist within the chaos.
Critical angle: Life is the proof-of-concept for artificial life — a universe where complex self-replicating structures arise from four lines of rules. The disease and ecosystem framings are not metaphors but isomorphisms: epidemic spread on a lattice and predator-prey dynamics both reduce to neighbor-counting. The philosophical shock is that "alive" and "dead" are computational states, not biological essences.
