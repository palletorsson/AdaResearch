# A hole changes its obligations

The same stored one becomes a desk square, a support tile and a coloured part of a screen. This is a small, inspectable instance of the project's question about shared computational material. The bodies are related by a shared record. Their ability to carry, obstruct or attract a visitor depends on further decisions.

A gap can make the screen desirable. It interrupts a regular surface and offers a view through it. Put that gap under a foot and the visitor has another reason to judge it. Neither judgement is contained in the bit. The floor adds collision, scale, gravity and a particular player body. The lattice places the cell among a frame, sightlines and colours. We can point to the added procedures instead of treating these identities as essences.

The desire for an organic or queer surface does not need to be disowned. It can become an experiment. Keep the pattern and change what we ask it to do. Does the desired body still welcome us? Which body could use a route that ours cannot? What changes when the appearance is retained but its support is removed? The present hall implements the first comparison; further collision toggles or alternative player bodies remain work to do.

The spatial record also carries an ambiguity. Adjacent rows look contemporaneous because they stand together. They were produced at different updates. The screen turns each row upright, making another plausible picture from the same order. A viewer can read a fabric where the machine stored a past. The gold witness lets that interpretation be checked against an address and generation.

The controls expose a further limit: a new beginning replaces this record. It does not revise every possible past. There are fifteen sites, a periodic boundary and a twenty-four-row budget. The museum clock continues while the experiment is held. We choose enough of the process to investigate, and a remainder stays beyond this instrument.

Beauty here is usable research material. A visitor can keep a pleasing beginning, repeat its construction, and discover how its application alters their judgement. The work leaves that judgement open while making its computational conditions specific.
