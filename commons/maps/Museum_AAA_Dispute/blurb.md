# The Dispute

Two rooms down the corridor, a slider recommends a value for lambda and marks three schools
who disagree about it. It draws the disagreement as a plate divided between them, and the
code that draws it says the three stand at equal strength.

They do not. The plate splits at the midpoints between the marks and lets the outer two run
to the ends, so the shares come out 35, 10 and 55 — and the smallest belongs to the value the
instrument ships with.

You are standing in that plate. The floor is the scale, twenty metres for twenty cells, and
the walls fall where the arithmetic puts them. Seven paces, then two, then eleven.

Eight boards hang along it. Each one stands where its own claim begins, and each shows the
same two things: the shape of what is recommended, and what the instrument will show you of
the measurements underneath. Four of the boards show nothing underneath. That is not an
omission — it is the two sliders' complete behaviour, drawn accurately.

There are two ways through the middle. Take both.
