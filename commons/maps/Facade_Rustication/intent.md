Concept: Facade_Rustication is the *surfaced actualization* of the facade assembly principle. Where the other expression maps push or substitute other axes (Baroque pushes the rhythm/ornament axes, Venetian Gothic substitutes the column-order axis, Tenement strips most axes to minimum, Critique refuses the rule entirely), this map *concentrates the entire compositional voice into one axis*: the rustication. Naples-style diamond bossing — every visible stone block on the ground storey carved into a four-sided pyramid that projects outward — turns the wall surface from neutral substrate into the facade's loudest statement. The other axes (rhythm, hierarchy, fenestration, cornice/base) are at canonical settings; rustication carries the argument alone.

Actualizes: Facade_Assembly_Principle, in the *surfaced* mode. The position in the virtual field is where the rustication axis is held at "Naples diamond bossing — maximum" and the other five axes are held at "canonical / Italian." The map is the demonstration that the principle's six axes are *independently dial-able* — pushing one to maximum while leaving the others canonical still produces a legible facade. Diamond bossing is the architectural defence-and-decoration double — historically used on palazzi to project (literally) the wealth and security of the owner against the street. The texture is the political statement.

Sequence role: Fifth map of the facade_assembly branch (after Principle → Classical → Baroque → Venetian Gothic). Demonstrates the third axis-deviation: rhythm and column-order already covered, surface is the third independent move. Together the three deviations (Baroque's intensity, Venetian Gothic's vocabulary substitution, Rustication's surface concentration) span enough of the design space that the player can read the canonical Classical facade as one position among many. Leads forward to Facade_NYC_Tenement (the principle modularized — the most reductive expression).

Technical angle: A four-storey facade where the ground storey is faced entirely in Naples-style diamond-bossed rustication — projecting pyramidal stone blocks in a regular grid, each block roughly 40cm × 40cm, projecting ~15cm from the wall plane. The pyramids are oriented with one vertex outward; their four facets catch raking light dramatically. Upper storeys are smooth ashlar (the contrast is the move). Windows on the rusticated ground are framed in smooth surrounds, creating a deliberate textural conflict at every opening. The cornices between storeys are pronounced. Pass-3 build: room walks today as 10x6x3 with spawn and teleporter; the rustication-vocabulary artifacts (diamond_bossing_grid, smooth_ashlar_band, framed_window_in_rustication, projecting_cornice_horizontal) are queued for artifact authoring.

Critical angle: Rustication is the facade's *political surface*. Diamond bossing emerged in Italian Renaissance defensive-decorative architecture — Pitti Palace, Naples palazzi — at the moment when wealthy families needed buildings to be simultaneously *secure* (literal defence) and *legible-as-rich* (decorative excess). The pyramidal projection is both: it scatters projectiles and it advertises the cost of carving thousands of identical stone pyramids by hand. The map's deeper lesson is that the assembly grammar's axes are not aesthetically neutral. Each axis encodes specific ideological histories; pushing one axis to maximum amplifies what that axis has historically said. Hierarchy says "the second storey deserves more attention." Rustication says "this wall is defensible and the defence is on display." Reading a facade is reading what its grammar settles to argue.
