# Judd Minimalism

Specific objects. Not painting, not sculpture — something else. Donald Judd stripped art to industrial materials and serial repetition. Aluminum boxes, equal intervals, factory-fabricated. No composition, no hierarchy, no metaphor. What you see is what you see.

Except it isn't. Place ten identical units on a wall at equal spacing and something happens that no single unit contains. Rhythm emerges. Presence accumulates. The room changes. Judd evacuated meaning from each element and meaning rushed back through the gaps between them — through relation, sequence, the body moving past.

This is the minimalism paradox. Subtract expression, subtract symbolism, subtract the artist's hand. What remains still signifies. Signs stripped to nothing still point somewhere. The dark room where meaning was killed and showed up anyway. Ceci n'est pas une pipe — but you're already reading it.
