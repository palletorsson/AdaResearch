## The walk

Two ways to carve a walkable void, set side by side. The caverandomwalk digs by drift — a wanderer eating rock as it staggers, the tunnel it leaves the negative of its own chance path. The maze_generation carves by discipline — recursive backtracking, drilling forward until it hits a dead end, then reversing just far enough to try again, until every cell is visited exactly once and a perfect maze stands with a single path between any two points.

## The turn (critical)

The critical room stages the chapter's central tension as two corridors. The cave is pure randomness given a chisel — organic, lumpy, forgiving, full of loops and dead pockets; the maze is pure rule — crisp, exhaustive, unforgiving, exactly one solution. Neither is the world; both are *chosen*, and the choice is a politics of space. A cave says wander, get lost, there are many ways; a maze says there is one correct path and the rest is failure. Every generated level, every dungeon, every open world sits somewhere on the line between them, and where a designer places it decides whether the player is invited to explore or made to solve. Authorless space is still not neutral — the rule that grows it has an attitude toward the body that will walk it.
