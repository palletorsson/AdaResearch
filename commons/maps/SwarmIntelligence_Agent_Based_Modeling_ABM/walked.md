The turn is a single, patient machine: the ant_colony_optimization colony, ants laying pheromone on the routes they travel and evaporation quietly erasing the routes they abandon. ## The turn

The chapter turns here from *watching* emergence to *modeling* with it. Agent-based modeling is the method's coming-of-age: stop writing equations about populations and instead write the rules of one agent, run a thousand copies, and let the aggregate behavior be the finding rather than the assumption. The ant colony is the exemplar because its intelligence is auditable — the pheromone field is right there, memory made visible, the environment doing the remembering. This is where swarm stops being a spectacle and becomes an epistemology: a way to *know* systems too tangled for the top-down view, by growing them from the bottom and asking what they do. Half of modern social science, epidemiology, and traffic planning walked through this door.
