## What this map holds (seed)

Before the point, infrastructure. The origin is not a point but a prerequisite — coordinate systems, render loops, the void made addressable. Point_One is the first mark: position without extension, existence without duration. You arrive late. The system was running.

The `interactive_point_origin` lets you enact the placement. The `CoordinateSystem3M` makes the coordinate triad visible — three axes, one cell of space, a reference frame pretending to be a pin. The `frame_counter_display` reveals what the point itself cannot report: that the loop was already iterating before your first mark. A point is a decision made inside someone else's clock.

## Why it was built (seed)

Concept: The first instantiated point — position without extension, existence before duration — and the infrastructure (coordinate systems, render loops, void) that must precede it. A point is not geometric but computational: a Vector3 bound to a frame that was already running when the learner arrived.
Sequence role: Opens the Primitives sequence and the whole curriculum. No predecessor. Establishes the zero-to-one act.
Critical angle: Individuation from infrastructure — the point arrives late into a pre-existing field. Heidegger's thrownness; the mark as minimal ontological commitment.

## The walk

You do not begin. You **arrive** — and the `frame_counter_display` is already counting, a number that was climbing before you had eyes to read it. This is the first thing the map teaches before it teaches anything you could call geometry: the render loop did not wait. `you_are_here` fixes you in a room (`lab_room`) that was built and lit and running; the world's clock has a head start you can never close. You are late, and the map wants you to feel it.

Then the reference frame resolves. `CoordinateSystem3M` draws the triad — three axes crossing at a nothing, one cell of addressable space. It looks like the point. It is not: it is the *convention* that will let a point mean anything at all. Read the axes and you have already accepted an entire prior agreement — an origin, a handedness, a scale — none of which you authored. The pin is a promise the room made before you got there.

Now you act. `interactive_point_origin_force` puts the placement in your hand: you commit a mark, a Vector3 bound to that borrowed frame. This is the zero-to-one — the curriculum's whole arc compressed into one gesture, *there was a field, and now there is a this*. And the map refuses to let it be clean: `fontana_puncture` stages the mark as a **cut** — the point not as a dot politely added to a plane but as a wound in it, Fontana's slashed canvas, the violence a first individuation does to the continuum it comes from. Around it, `floating_sphere_field` holds the alternative the point rejected: extension without decision, a haze of position that never resolved into a *here*. `folding_past` keeps the memory that the room remembers being marked — the point, once made, is already history the moment after.

The map works because the beats land in the body in that order: *arrive late → inherit the frame → make the cut → see what you refused.* You leave having done the smallest possible thing, and having been shown it was never small.

## The turn (critical)

The lie every tutorial tells is that you start at the beginning. Point_One tells the truth instead: **there is no first act.** The point is a *second* act — it can only happen after the axes, the loop, and the addressing convention are already in place, which means individuation is always downstream of an infrastructure you did not choose and cannot see from inside. This is thrownness rendered as software: Heidegger's *Geworfenheit* is not a metaphor here, it is the `frame_counter` that was running before your first frame.

And this is where the map earns its place at the head of a *queer* curriculum. The point is the field's first act of self-division — the moment a continuous everything agrees to become a discrete *this and not-that*. Fontana's cut names the cost: every individuation is a wound in the plane it separates from, and the clean geometric dot is a cover story for that violence. The book's whole argument starts here, in miniature — that the primitives are not innocent building blocks but *decisions*, made inside someone else's clock, that foreclose as much as they create. QFEP: the field (E) was full of possibility; the point spends some of it (ΔE) to buy a single determinate mark; and the question the map leaves open is whether that trade was yours to make or the infrastructure's.

The vector, in Ada, comes with its Turing: the point is not just a Vector3, it is the minimal ontological commitment, and it arrives already political — late, borrowed, and cut from something that was whole.
