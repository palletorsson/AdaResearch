A cube has 6 faces. A tesseract has 8 cubic cells. One of them is the cube you think you're standing in.

The tesseract is the 4D analog of a cube — built by extruding a cube along a fourth axis that doesn't exist in our space. To see it, project. The same way a 3D cube casts a 2D shadow, a 4D hypercube casts a 3D shadow. Walk through that shadow. The tunnel is a projected tesseract rotating through the fourth dimension — cells nesting inside cells, edges crossing through geometries that feel wrong because they are. Perspective distortion in 3D is parallax. In 4D, it is architecture.

Eight cubes share every face. The structure is complete, closed, rigid — pure order extended one dimension past visibility. Plato's cave, but the forms are rotating.
