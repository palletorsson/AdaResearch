Walls rise on either side. The only open direction is forward. Your body already knows what to do — walk. The corridor does not ask; it pulls. Parallel surfaces compress peripheral vision and aim your gaze at whatever waits at the end.

Directed movement is the oldest spatial trick. Temples, train stations, hospital hallways — all of them use the same geometry. Two walls and a destination. The corridor is not a room. It is a verb.
