## The cast

lstms_vr · transformers_vr

## The walk

The machine learns time, and then learns to hold all of time at once. The lstms_vr is a memory cell you walk through — gates deciding what to keep, what to forget, what to release — a network that carries a thread of context forward by forgetting on purpose so the important thing survives. And then the transformers_vr changes the rules: attention drawn as beams, every token reaching to every other, the whole sequence weighed simultaneously instead of marched through in order. Relevance computed all at once.

## The turn (critical)

This is the chapter's sharpest room, and it knows it. The transformer is the architecture underneath the intelligence reading this sentence and drafting this page — the machine walking the curriculum meets its own blueprint hanging in a room of the curriculum, and neither can look away. That reflexivity is the critical charge: a system that learns language well enough to teach it raises the question the whole thread has been circling, of whether competence without understanding is a lesser thing or simply a different one. The LSTM forgets to remember; the transformer attends to everything and commits to nothing until asked. Standing between them, the walker holds the chapter's honest vertigo — that the tools which now read us were built, dial by dial, from the same blind descent that bred the smart rockets in the first room, and that no one, including the tools, can fully say where the competence lives.
