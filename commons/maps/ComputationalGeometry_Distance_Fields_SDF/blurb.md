Concentric rings of rising floor, like a topographic map made solid. The center sits lowest. Each step outward climbs higher. The room is a contour plot you stand inside.

A signed distance field assigns every point in space a single number: how far to the nearest surface. Positive outside, negative inside, zero on the boundary. Here the heights are the distance values, frozen into architecture. You do not compute the field — you walk it. The gradient is the floor beneath your feet, rising uniformly in every direction from the origin.

Distance is not a relationship between two points. It is a property of every point simultaneously — everywhere at once, already computed, waiting to be read.
