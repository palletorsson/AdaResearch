The previous map ended with a claim: shape without geometry, form without vertices, the object described rather than constructed. This one asks what happens when you describe a building that way, and then walk into it.

Take the union of eight chambers and ten passages — one field, smooth-minimum everywhere, so nothing meets anything, things *become* each other. Then subtract the field from its own absolute value at a wall thickness. Where the solid runs deeper than the wall, you are in a cavity. The equation that made a lump makes a hollow, and it takes one line.

What that buys is not efficiency. It is that nobody drew the plan. There is no schedule of rooms and no corridor list — wherever two limbs blended, a way through exists, because a way through is just the place where two negatives overlapped. The graph has three independent cycles in it, so there is no root, no single route, and no point from which the whole is ordered.

The body is sealed by construction: an outer skin, an inner skin, and nothing between them. One chamber is bored. Finding that mouth is the first thing the map asks of you, and it is the only concession to a visitor in the whole structure.

Inside, the walls carry their own index. Every surface point knows which limb it belongs to and how close the second-nearest limb came; where the two are tied, the field fused, and those ties are lit. Colour tells you which limb you are standing in. Light tells you where it joined another. That is the entire wayfinding system, and it was not designed — it was already in the arithmetic, waiting to be read out.
