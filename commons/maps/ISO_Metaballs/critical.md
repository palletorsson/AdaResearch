# ISO_Metaballs — Critical Reflection

Each metaball is a point radiating influence. A distance field — strongest at center, fading outward. One sphere alone is trivial. But place two near each other and their fields sum. Where combined density crosses the threshold, a surface appears. Not two objects touching — one object, born from proximity. Move the sources closer: the surface bulges, stretches, merges. Pull them apart: it pinches, thins, splits. The geometry is a consequence of relationship.

Simondon's theory of individuation finds its most literal computational expression in metaballs. For Simondon, individuation occurs when a metastable system resolves into a new structure — the crystal forming from the supersaturated solution, the organism differentiating from the preindividual field. The metaball surface is exactly this: a structure that resolves from a field of potentials. The threshold is the moment of individuation. Below it, the fields coexist without producing form. Above it, form precipitates. And the form is not a property of any single source — it is a property of their collective influence. The individual emerges from the transindividual.

Merleau-Ponty's phenomenology of the body offers a reading of the merging behavior. For Merleau-Ponty, bodies are not bounded objects in a void. They are fields of motility and sensation that overlap, interfere, and co-constitute. When two people stand close enough, their personal spaces merge — not in the metaball's geometric sense, but in the phenomenological sense of shared atmosphere, mutual awareness, the felt presence of another body. The metaball literalizes this. Two fields overlapping do not collide. They become one body, then separate again, unchanged. Merging is not loss. It is the native state.

Three artifacts in this map enact the same phenomenon through different computational strategies. The metaballs artifact uses CPU-driven sphere deformation — sixty blobs approximating organic form through geometry that chases the field. The raymarched_metaballs artifact uses GPU SDF raymarching, rendering the implicit surface directly per pixel, never extracting a mesh. The nakama_metaballs artifact follows Kouhei Nakama's approach. One method builds triangles to describe the surface. The other evaluates the field per pixel and stops when it hits the edge. Both find the same thing: the contour where inside becomes outside. The surface was always there. The question is whether you extract it or just look until you see it.

What does this map normalize? That identity is not bounded. That two entities can merge without either losing itself. That the boundary between inside and outside is a threshold in a continuous field, not a wall between discrete regions. This is a deeply relational ontology — objects do not have surfaces; surfaces are what happens when influence fields overlap enough. The metaball world is one where isolation is the exception and merging is the rule.

What does it exclude? Resistance. Friction. The refusal to merge. In the metaball model, proximity always produces combination. There is no repulsive field, no boundary that pushes back, no surface that says "not here, not with you." The merging is frictionless and reversible — pull apart and you are what you were before. But embodied merging is not like this. When bodies come together, they change. When they separate, they carry traces. The metaball model of relationship is one without history — merging leaves no mark, separation leaves no scar. It is an idealized topology of encounter that has never met an actual encounter.

Simondon's concept of the associated milieu raises a further question. The metaball sources float in an open arena, their fields summing in empty space. There is no medium. There is no resistance from the environment, no gravity pulling the fields downward, no viscosity slowing the merging. The associated milieu is null. This means the metaballs can only demonstrate the mathematics of field summation, not the physics of material behavior. Real organic forms — the blobby shapes the metaballs resemble — arise from surface tension, viscosity, pressure differentials, and chemical gradients. The metaball achieves the appearance without the causation.

Merleau-Ponty wrote that the body is "not a thing among things, but a way of being among things." The metaball is a thing among fields. It does not perceive, it does not act, it does not care about its neighbors. Its merging is mathematical, not ethical. But the visual experience of watching two blobs stretch toward each other, contact, and become one — that experience is felt by the viewer's body, not computed by the algorithm. The merging that matters happens in the phenomenological space between screen and skin.

Form without skeleton. Identity without boundary. Is the surface where the field crosses the threshold the most honest representation of how selves are constituted — not from the inside out, but from the outside in?
