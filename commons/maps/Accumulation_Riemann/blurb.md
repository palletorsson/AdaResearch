The smooth area from the last map was a lie of convenience — or rather, a limit. Here you see what it is made of. Rectangles stand under the curve, crude and stepped, their tops missing the curve by visible gaps. Then you pump up the count. More rectangles, thinner, the gaps closing, the staircase melting toward the smooth fill. The integral is what the sum becomes when the steps go to zero width.

The `riemann_pump` is the control that adds rectangles; the `integral_area` returns from the previous map so the discrete sum can be laid against the continuous answer. The `riemann_pi` is borrowed from the QFEP lab — a finite, famous case where summing rectangles converges on π, a number you can watch arrive. Discrete becomes continuous not by decree but by limit. This is the engine room of accumulation: addition, repeated, refined, until the seams disappear.
