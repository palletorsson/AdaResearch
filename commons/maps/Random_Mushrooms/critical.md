# Random_Mushrooms - Critical Documentation

## What the room shows, and what it declines to claim

The bed looks alive and is not. Nothing in `mushrooms.gd` feeds, spreads, competes or dies; the meadow is an arrangement made in one pass — six templates, eighty candidates, a threshold, a facing and a size per copy, a ring and two clusters — and made again on request. The earlier texts of this room spoke of spores, substrate, moisture and a mycelial network under the floor. None of that is in the scene, and the specimen table is built so that a visitor can find that out rather than be told: the plate counts what was requested, accepted and refused; the highlight says which rule put each body where it stands; REGROW proves the whole population is one number's consequence.

That is the room's critical move. A field of recognisable bodies invites the vocabulary of ecology, and the vocabulary carries claims (growth, competition, self-organisation) that the picture does not support. The fairy ring is the sharpest case: the emblem of order arising from below is, here, a centre, a radius and `int(radius * 8)` members at equal angles. The ring did not organise; it was specified. The scattered mushrooms did not find their places; eighty positions were drawn and a noise field refused some. To describe the bed without the words natural or artificial, and to say instead which draw made what, is to refuse a resemblance in favour of a construction.

## Which parts were allowed to vary

The variation is real and it is bounded, rule by rule. Which of six (a draw modulo six, the glowing one thinned by a chance); which way (a yaw); how big (a range, 0.7–1.3, 0.8–1.2 or 0.5–1.2 by kind); where (a uniform square, gated); whether at all (the threshold, the bed's edge). What never varies: the six forms, the threshold's value, the ring's arithmetic, the counts of rings and clusters (set by the bed's size, `int(size / 5)` and `int(size / 3)`, not drawn). A visitor who turns SIZE off under the same seed sees exactly one of these decisions removed and everything else hold; that is a controlled comparison the shipped meadow could not offer, because its draws came from the global stream and could not be repeated.

The seed matters politically as well as technically. A population that can be regrown is a population whose variety is a consequence of a number, and the plate names the number. Nothing is hidden underground; what was decided is on the table.

## The RAND digits and the bubbles

The 1955 tables cascading on the east wall are the room's reminder that randomness has been an infrastructure before it was a texture: extracted by an electronic roulette wheel, tested, printed and sold, put to work in Monte Carlo runs and sampling designs. The bubbles rise on a looser rule. Neither is the lesson; both are company for it. The reaction-diffusion field at the south wall, the book's hero line for this pearl, makes pattern from a random start by a different route (two rates, a narrow window, spots or a dead grey field), and a visitor who has just learned to ask which draw made what can ask it there too.

## Questions that remain open

1. What is lost when a population is made replayable? The shipped meadow's charm included that it could not be seen twice. The bed keeps NEW SEED for that, but the room's argument prefers the seed.
2. Is a rule that never varies (six forms, one threshold) a limit on variation or its condition? The room says both and does not resolve it.
3. What else could the six permitted shapes become? The rules say nothing about mushrooms. The final ends on this and hands the next question, a draw that decides, to Random_Game.

## For QFEP

The bed is F without φΔE(S,t): a landscape fixed by a seed, with no entropy injected after the build. The room treats that honestly rather than dressing the arrangement as a living system. The contrast the earlier texts wanted — archive against organism — is still available in the hall, but it runs between the RAND tables and the reaction-diffusion field, not between the tables and the mushrooms.
