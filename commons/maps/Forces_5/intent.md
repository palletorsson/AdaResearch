Concept: Air resistance and fluid drag — forces that grow with velocity, creating terminal velocity where acceleration ceases and objects reach their speed limit.
Sequence role: Sixteenth map in Forces; extends friction into velocity-dependent resistance. Where friction (Forces_4) is constant during motion, drag increases with speed — a qualitatively different dissipation mechanism. The force_field_visualizer and vector_fields artifacts mark the return of field concepts from VectorFieldFlow, now applied to physical drag; leads to Forces_6.
Technical angle: Drag force F_d = -½ρv²C_dA (proportional to velocity squared), terminal velocity where drag equals gravitational force and net acceleration is zero. The transition from accelerating fall to constant-speed descent. Reynolds number as the regime indicator (laminar vs turbulent).
Critical angle: Terminal velocity is the first equilibrium in the sequence — not a designed stopping point but an emergent balance between gravity and resistance. The faster you fall, the harder air pushes back, until the two forces exactly cancel. This is dynamic equilibrium: not static (nothing moves) but steady-state (everything moves at constant rate). The QFEP system reaches a stable attractor.
Key artifacts: force_field_visualizer maps drag force across the space; force_fields and vector_fields provide the continuous field representation of fluid resistance.
