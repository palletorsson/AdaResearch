A transformation is a way of moving a thing without losing what it is. This map takes the transformation concept map — the 2D diagram of how the operations relate — and lays it down as a floor you can cross, so that the relationships between the operations become distances between places.

You spawn at `Three rigid motions`, the family that preserves length and angle, and walk out through `Translation`, `Rotation`, `Scale`, `Shear` — each loosening a different invariant — into `Composition`, where operations stack, and `Matrix / homogeneous`, where they become arithmetic. Past `Invariants` (what survives), `Tiling / pattern` (transformation repeated), and `Field / flow` (transformation made continuous), the path ends at the teleporter on `Curved space (non-Euclidean)`, where the flat assumptions finally break.

One representative artifact per concept. The order is the lesson: from the motions that change nothing essential to the geometry where "nothing essential" no longer holds.
