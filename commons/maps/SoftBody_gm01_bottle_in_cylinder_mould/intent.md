Concept: A pressurized soft-body sphere (pre-inflated to ~70% of mould radius) is dropped inside a cylindrical mould of constant radius r=0.55. Internal pressure pushes vertices outward; contact constraints against the mould wall stop them when they reach the cavity boundary. The body settles pressed against the cylindrical wall along its full height, producing a uniform-radius bottle. The map is a gallery study; the player walks around the single soft-body marker.

Sequence role: First glass-meets-mould study (gm01) in the imported soft-body gallery cluster within the Soft Bodies sequence (13th spine, integration phase). Opens the gm-family, which shifts the source of form from pin-and-gravity (gl-family) to mould cavity (gm-family). The cluster will cover four mould variants: gm01 cylindrical (constant radius), gm02 tapered (varying radius), gm03 box (rectangular faces), gm04 external sphere (obstruction rather than cavity). Pair-contrast with gm02: gm01's cavity has constant radius along its length; gm02's cavity tapers — narrow top to wide bottom. Same body, same pressure, same physics; different cavity → different vessel proportions.

Technical angle: Spherical mesh of mass points with structural and bending springs. Pre-inflation: at t=0 each face is pushed outward along its normal by a small amount so the sphere starts already inflated to ~70% of the mould radius. Mould collision: each vertex tests against the mould geometry (here a cylinder centered on the world up-axis); if outside the cavity, vertex position is projected back onto the cavity boundary and velocity component along the outward normal is zeroed (or reflected with damping). Internal pressure applied per-face proportional to area and current inflation. Gravity applied to every mass. Verlet or PBD integration for ~200 steps. Because the cylinder radius is constant from rim to base, the body reaches the wall at the same radial distance everywhere, producing a uniform-radius bottle shape regardless of how the body was pinned or where it started.

Critical angle: The gm-family makes a stronger version of the gl-family's claim. In gl, form is what force-balance leaves behind; in gm, form is what the cavity leaves room for. The body's own springs and pressure are still computing equilibrium, but the equilibrium is constrained by an external geometry that the body did not author. This is exactly the industrial glass-blowing process — the mould pre-decides the outer shape; the breath and cooling only have to drive the glass into contact with the cavity wall. The simulation reproduces the structure of that process without reproducing any of the materials: there is no glass, no thermal conduction, no surface tension, no wall release. There is only contact projection and internal pressure. The fact that what emerges resembles industrially-formed glass bottles is the integration-phase claim about parameter spaces overlapping across substrates.

Key artifacts: gallery_marker_soft-body holds the gm01_bottle_in_cylinder_mould configuration. The uniform-radius bottle pressed against the cylindrical mould is the artifact and the demonstration of cavity-as-form.

Gap: A mould-visibility toggle on the gallery marker would let learners turn the cavity wireframe on and off, alternating between "see the vessel" and "see the cavity that made the vessel." The lesson is that one is sufficient to recover the other; the relationship is bidirectional.
