A 5×5×5 cube of mass points — one hundred twenty-five vertices, hundreds of springs holding them in a lattice. Low stiffness. Drop it. The grid does not bounce so much as collapse: every layer settles onto the one below, every spring along the way gives a little, and the cube ends as a squashed lattice on the ground.

Where sb05's eight-corner cube was the minimum topology for a soft 3D solid, sb06 is the next step up. More mass means more inertia. More springs means more places for energy to disperse and damp. The simulation is heavier; the visible behavior is gentler. Stiffness has been turned down. The grid no longer remembers it was a cube.

This is what gel does. What a sponge does. What a denser-than-water body of any compliant material does when the springs holding it together cannot quite resist their own weight. The lattice goes from architecture to puddle. Settling, not bouncing, is the signature of low stiffness.
