Walls breathe. Flags dance. A trampoline catches what falls. Soft bodies don't just exist in space — they negotiate it. Every surface here pushes back, deforms, recovers, or doesn't.

The breathing room pulses — corridors whose walls expand and contract on their own rhythm, forcing passage through timing rather than force. Cloth flags ripple under wind they cannot see, pinned at one edge, free at every other. Balls drop into pits and press against each other, each one yielding to its neighbors' weight. Nothing here is rigid. Nothing holds its shape without effort.

This is where elastic force meets obstacle — where structure must continuously earn its form against pressure, gravity, and the geometry of what surrounds it. Matter that remembers what it was, but keeps becoming something else.
