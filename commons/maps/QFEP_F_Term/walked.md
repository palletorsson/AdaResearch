## The walk

The first term in isolation. F is free energy — prediction error, surprise — and every adaptive system works to shrink it: find the pattern, predict better, be surprised less. The map lets you *feel* the drive before naming it. The snap puzzles hand you scattered pieces and a shape that wants to exist; each correct placement clicks home with a small, unreasonable satisfaction — that click is F falling, structural error going to zero in your hands, and noticing how good it feels is the tutorial's first data point. The `crystal_cluster` runs the same descent without you: disorder settling into lattice, F minimizing as geometry. The `shannon_workbench` gives the drive its measure — surprise quantified in bits, the entropy arithmetic from the randomness chapter now working for the other side. And then the map plays its counterpoint: the dark room. Follow F-minimization to its logical end and you arrive somewhere perfectly unsurprising — dark, silent, eventless — the one place a prediction never fails.

## The turn (critical)

The dark room problem is the sharpest thought experiment in predictive-processing theory, and the map stages it rather than citing it: if organisms minimize surprise, why don't we all crawl into dark rooms and stay there? Prediction error there is zero; by the theory's plain arithmetic, that is *optimal* — and it is also death. The trap is worth dwelling in, because F is not a villain; F is Euclid, F is the snap-click you just enjoyed, F is learning and homeostasis and every pattern this curriculum taught, and the puzzles were designed to make you complicit — the drive toward order is *pleasant*, which is exactly why its failure mode is seductive. The crystal completes; then it is done; nothing further will ever happen to it. The critical claim is structural, and it rhymes across the whole book: pure order is not the perfection of life but its terminus, and the foundations crisis already proved the formal version — Gödel showing F can never actually reach zero, the complete-and-consistent crystal impossible in principle. So the term stands here with a minus sign waiting for it: necessary, insufficient, one half of the negotiation the rest of the lab assembles. The map's honest summary is the room you declined to stay in — beautiful, finished, and dark.
