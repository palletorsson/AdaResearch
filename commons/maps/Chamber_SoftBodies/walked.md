## The walk

The hopper bounces softly. Spring physics, deformable matter in deformable space — everything gives when you push it. This is the catalyst chamber that closes the sequence, and its rule is that **contact is the interaction**: the `spring_hopper` has internal constraints that absorb force instead of transmitting it as velocity, so when you push it a travelling wave of displacement runs across its mass-spring mesh and it rebounds, redistributing the energy rather than bouncing off you as a hard event. There is no catalyst mode here, no special verb — pushing *is* the verb, because both you and the thing you push are soft.

## The turn (critical)

The chamber's closing move is to name **rigidity as a design choice rather than a fact.** Every earlier sequence quietly assumed hard boundaries — the point that holds still, the solid that keeps its corners, the collision that resolves in a frame — and this chamber, by making both sides of every contact soft, reveals that assumption as an assumption. When force is a shared wave in a shared medium rather than an impact between separate facts, the very distinction between actor and receiver *softens*: who pushed whom becomes a question the elastic surface refuses to answer cleanly, because the energy is now in the medium, belonging to neither party. That is the sequence's last and largest claim, and it reaches past physics into ethics: the player-creature boundary, the self-other boundary, the whole architecture of one-thing-acting-on-another that rigid physics made feel natural — all of it was a simplifying story, and softness is what the world looks like once you stop telling it. The sequence that started by squeezing a cube ends by dissolving the line between the squeezer and the squeezed.

## The living floor (authored 2026-07-24)

This room has no pedestal and no vent — no mode is handed to you, no creature is
converted. So for the first time the floor answers the one force that *is*
present: **your body**.

The map already said it — *"Everything gives when you push it."* Now the floor
does. `fungus:softbody` cells wake to **touch** where you step, in the poses that
read as an instant give: pressed flat, shoved sideways. Others wake to **dwell**,
where you stand still long enough for weight to tell: drooping, collapsing. A
touch cell will not answer dwelling and a dwell cell will not answer a footstep —
a give is not a settle.

And the last cell on the way out claims its neighbours, so the deformation
travels rather than staying where it started, the way force does through a soft
body.

There is an honest tension here worth keeping: the chapter teaches *simulated*
deformation, and `fungus:softbody` is a mushroom frozen mid-give — no physics, a
pose that remembers a force it never computed. The floor is not a soft-body
solver. It is a record of having been pushed.
