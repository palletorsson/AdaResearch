Cube. Prism. Wedge. L-shape. Pyramid. Plane. Six shapes — the entire vocabulary of built space.

Every structure ever assembled reduces to a finite set of parts. These are the parts. A cube is a room. A prism is a roof. An L-shape turns a corner. A plane is a floor, a wall, a boundary — depending on rotation. The pyramid points upward for no structural reason, only gesture. Triangular blocks fill the gaps that right angles leave behind.

Construction is combination under constraint. Snap a wedge to a cube, repeat, and a staircase appears. Mirror an L-shape and a corridor bends. The primitives don't change — only their arrangement does. Identity is fixed. Relation is everything. What a block *is* matters less than where it sits and what it touches.
