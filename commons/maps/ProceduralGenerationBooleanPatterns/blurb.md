AND, OR, NOT. Three operations that build worlds from yes and no. Constructive Solid Geometry treats shape as logic — union adds, intersection keeps only overlap, difference carves. Every complex form is a sentence written in boolean.

Layer a grid of circles over a grid of squares. Intersect: only the overlap survives. Subtract: holes appear where solids met. Union: everything merges into one mass. The operations are simple. The compositions are not. Stack enough boolean layers and organic complexity emerges from binary decisions.

Shape as proposition. Geometry as argument. Two volumes walk into an intersection — what remains is neither original, only what they had in common. Identity through overlap. Form through exclusion. The simplest logic produces the most surprising structure.
