Concept: The opening act of the Vector & Forces field — what a vector *is*, taught as four embodied stations rather than a definition. A 14×22 processional hall walks the learner through the four facts that make a vector: a coordinate frame (where), a free displacement arrow (which-way + how-far), magnitude (how far it would carry you), and normalisation (which-way with how-far thrown away). The room is deliberately pre-arithmetic — nothing is added or multiplied here; the learner only meets the object before anyone does anything to it.

Sequence role: First of six walkable Act maps that assemble the vectors→forces tutorial into a loop (Act I → … → Act V → back to Act I). It sits at the very front of the conceptual spine, before VectorOperations and the VFM field maps, and establishes the vocabulary every later act spends: direction, magnitude, unit vector. Its closing teleporter leads to Act II — vector arithmetic. The reflection beat ("Four facts, one arrow: a where, a which-way, a how-far — and a how-far refused, leaving pure direction") names the whole act so the learner carries a single sentence forward.

Technical angle: A four-station linear hall, 14 wide by 22 deep, max height 2, with the diagram demoted to a wall label so the artifact carries the lesson (the project's standard embodied-pedagogy move). The four interactables are placed in script order: CoordinateSystem3M (read a point's address off three axes), VectorBasics (grab the tip and slide the arrow — direction and length are the only facts), length_lantern (stretches and brightens with the Pythagorean magnitude √(x²+y²+z²)), and vector_normalize_demo (divide by length, snap to the unit sphere). Six subtitle truth-beats drive the narration — an intro, one truth per station (truth_coord/basics/length/normalize), and a closing reflection — surfaced as `sub:` cells along the walk.

Critical angle: The act's argument is that a vector is a *journey with no starting address* — sliding the same arrow around the room without changing it is the lesson, not a side effect. By refusing arithmetic, the room makes the case that the hardest idea is the first one: that direction-and-magnitude is a single thing independent of where you draw it. The risk is that four near-identical stations read as repetitive; the rising stakes (a point → an arrow → its length → its pure direction) and the per-station truth-beats are what keep it a progression rather than a list.

Key artifacts: CoordinateSystem3M is the frame you stand inside, turning "position" into three readable numbers; VectorBasics is the free arrow whose only facts are direction and length; length_lantern makes magnitude felt by growing with √(x²+y²+z²); vector_normalize_demo performs the final move — keep the direction, refuse the distance — landing every direction on the unit sphere.

Gap: The walk has rich spoken truth-beats but no on-floor sign naming the act's spine ("four facts: where, which-way, how-far, and how-far refused") at the entrance, so a learner who misses the intro subtitle has no anchor. A faint floor line connecting the four stations would make the processional order legible from the start, and a single back-reference at the exit ("you now have the object; next, what vectors do to each other") would tie the teleporter to Act II more explicitly than the current handoff.
