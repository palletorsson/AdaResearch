## The walk

Where forces cancel, form holds still. The `calder_mobile` hangs in a stillness that is not rest but perfect opposition — every arm balanced by the lever law, τ = w·d, the heavy disc riding close to the pivot and the light one far out, each arm itself hung from the arm above, a cascade of balances that lets the whole thing turn on a breath without ever falling. The `fractal_truss_tower` is the same principle turned structural: a lattice whose struts branch into thinner struts, spanning the widest gap with the least material — the Eiffel trick, strength as *geometry* rather than mass. And the `tensegrity_triangle` is the strangest of the three: three rigid struts that touch nothing, held apart and rigid by cables alone, standing firm under a load that would collapse the same parts thrown in a loose pile. Push it and it springs back. Nothing is stacked; everything is *tensioned*.

## The turn (critical)

The tensegrity is the map's turn, and it overturns the intuition the whole body arrives with: that structure is *stuff stacked up* — bricks on bricks, weight bearing weight down to the ground. The triangle's struts bear on nothing; they float in a web of tension, and the form holds because the forces balance in a *field*, not because the mass piles in a column. **Structure is a field of forces, not a stack of stuff** — and once you have felt a rigid thing held up by invisible tension, the ordinary building looks different too: it was always forces in balance, the stacking was just the most obvious way to balance them. This is where form-finding becomes an *art*, and the map names its masters. Gaudí found the arches of the Sagrada Família by hanging chains weighted to scale, photographing the catenaries they settled into, and turning the photograph *upside down* — the shape a chain finds in pure tension is, inverted, the shape a stone arch needs in pure compression, so gravity itself designed his cathedral. Frei Otto dipped wire frames in soap to find his tent roofs. The turn worth holding: the engineer stops *imposing* a form and starts *finding* one — hanging the chain, dipping the frame, letting equilibrium solve the shape and then reading it off. The most efficient structures in the world were not drawn. They were discovered, by matter, and copied down.
