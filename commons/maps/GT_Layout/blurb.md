# Force-Directed Layout

Scatter nodes. Connect them with edges. Now let physics do the thinking. Repulsion pushes every node apart — Coulomb's law borrowed from electrostatics. Springs pull connected nodes together — Hooke's law borrowed from mechanics. The system oscillates, overshoots, dampens. Iterates. Eventually settles into something readable. No one designed the layout. It emerged.

The room is the algorithm. Platforms at irregular heights, no symmetry, no grid — a graph trying to find its own shape. Nodes drift through asymmetric space until forces balance. Equilibrium is not stillness. It's the point where every push meets an equal pull, where structure and tension negotiate a truce.

Force-directed layout treats topology as a physics problem. What emerges isn't optimal — it's legible. The graph doesn't know what it looks like. It only knows what pulls.
