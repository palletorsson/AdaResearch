Concept: Particle systems — many bodies under forces, where individual rules produce collective behavior and statistical patterns emerge from simple per-particle physics.
Sequence role: Eighteenth map in Forces; shifts from single-body dynamics to many-body systems. Every previous Forces map applied forces to one or a few objects; this map applies them to hundreds or thousands. The firework_launcher is the canonical example: each particle obeys F=ma independently, but together they produce recognizable forms (arcs, bursts, cascades); follows Forces_6; leads to Forces_8.
Technical angle: Particle emission (spawn rate, lifetime), per-particle state (position, velocity, age), force accumulation per particle per frame, particle death and recycling. The firework_launcher demonstrates burst emission; force_fields apply spatial forces to all particles simultaneously. Particle systems as the bridge between physics simulation and visual effects.
Critical angle: Emergence — the defining theme. No particle knows about the firework shape. Each one simply follows F=ma with its initial velocity and the forces acting on it. The shape emerges from the ensemble. This is the strongest demonstration of bottom-up dynamics in the sequence: simple rules, complex results, no central planner. In QFEP terms, the system's macroscopic behavior (the firework shape) is not in any individual particle's state — it is in the distribution.
Key artifacts: firework_launcher as the primary emitter demonstrating burst particle dynamics; force_fields providing the spatial force distribution; particle_systems as the general framework for many-body simulation.
Gap: A particle_inspector that lets the learner pause the system, click a single particle, and see its individual force/velocity/acceleration vectors — making visible how one particle's obedience to F=ma contributes to the collective pattern.
