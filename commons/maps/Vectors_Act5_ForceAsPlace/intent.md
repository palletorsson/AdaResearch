Concept: The final act of the Vector & Forces field — force as place. A 16×24 chasm map with no floor across the middle: a near platform, a void, and a far ledge. A vector_machine dials a force_field_zone that hovers over the gap; the learner tests the field by throwing force_cubes into it, then sets it up-and-across and steps in to be carried over. The whole field of forces resolves here into one move — a force is not a number on an arrow but a region of space with a rule you can turn.

Sequence role: The closer and the loop-back of the six-map sequence. Where Acts I–IV built vectors up from object → operation → field/motion → named forces, Act V collapses the lesson into a single embodied stake: the chosen vector *is* the bridge. Its far teleporter returns to Act I rather than ending, making the field a circle — the reflection beat ("The whole field of forces was this: vectors you can read, and turn") is the curriculum's closing sentence. It shares its mechanism with Void_Crossing, the sequence's other force-field-zone map, with which it forms the application pair.

Technical angle: A 16×24 map, max height 2, with the void carved as height-0 cells through the centre so there is genuinely no floor to cross on. The force_field_zone (class ForceFieldZone, group "force_field") is a cube that, while you are inside it, sets gravity_scale to 0 and adds its field vector as acceleration each frame — rigid bodies get linear_velocity += field·dt, the player_body gets velocity += (field − its own gravity)·dt — so the chosen vector becomes the only force in the cube. The vector_machine finds the zone via the group and calls set_field_vector(), giving the near platform a control surface for the bridge. Three force_cubes are visible test masses obeying the same field, so the invisible vector is made legible before the learner trusts their own body. Four subtitle truth-beats narrate — intro, truth_machine, truth_void, and the completion reflection.

Critical angle: The act's argument is its strongest and its riskiest: an abstraction that normally costs nothing (a wrong vector) is given a literal cost (a fall). That is the honest part — falling is reframed as a property of the field, not the void, so a wrong aim is not a wrong answer but a consequence. The loop-back to Act I makes the structural claim that understanding forces is not a line you finish but a circle you re-enter. The risk is punishment-over-instruction: a single mis-aim drops the learner. The force_cubes are the mitigation — read the field off thrown masses first — and the near-shore machine keeps the decision reversible until the learner steps in.

Key artifacts: force_field_zone is the bridge itself — a vector made into a place where it is the only force; vector_machine is the near-platform control that aims the field via set_field_vector(); the three force_cube test masses make the invisible field visible by obeying it first, so the crossing can be seen before it is taken.

Gap: As with Void_Crossing, the crossing is a puzzle with no on-floor instruction — an entry sign ("the field is the bridge; aim it across") would turn trial-and-error into a taught move. A failure readout (where the field pointed when a cube or the player fell) would convert each fall into feedback rather than a silent reset. And because this map closes the loop, a single far-ledge marker reading "back to Act I — the field is a circle" would make the return deliberate rather than incidental.
