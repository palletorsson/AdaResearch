Concept: Same L-system word as sb_ls01 (rule F→F[+F]F[-F]F, three iterations, pinned at base) instantiated as a spring-mass body — but with reduced spring stiffness, diagonal wind force, and longer integration. The plant bends, partially collapses, and the simulation records the failure trajectory. The map is a gallery study; the player walks around the single soft-body marker.

Sequence role: Second L-system→soft-body bridge (sb_ls02) in the imported soft-body gallery cluster within the Soft Bodies sequence (13th spine, integration phase). Contrast pair with sb_ls01 (same DNA, stiff, no wind, stands). Together they argue that an L-system word is half a description of an organism — the grammar gives form, the dynamics give the form's fate.

Technical angle: Same L-system→spring-mass instantiation as sb_ls01: each F segment becomes a spring chain, branches become child chains, base vertex pinned to the world. Differences: spring stiffness reduced by ~50%, a constant diagonal wind force added to each free vertex (proportional to face area projected into the wind direction), and integration extended to ~140 timesteps to let the collapse trajectory complete. Result: the trunk's restoring force can no longer hold the loaded canopy against the combined gravity-plus-wind load; deflection grows monotonically; the structure leans, then folds. Verlet or PBD integration with substepping to keep the failing structure numerically stable.

Critical angle: Plants in nature don't get to choose their stiffness or their weather. The DNA encodes a body; the environment decides whether that body survives. Sb_ls01/sb_ls02 stage this as a controlled experiment with the genotype held literally identical. The same observation drives developmental biology, robotics design, and the entire literature on phenotypic plasticity. Form is grammar plus context. The L-system gallery and the soft-body gallery are showing two halves of the same organism.

Key artifacts: gallery_marker_soft-body holds the sb_ls02_plant_wind_collapse configuration. The collapsed plant is the artifact — failure as study object, the trajectory toward failure as the lesson.

Gap: A wind-direction dial and a stiffness slider would let learners run the same DNA through a range of environments and see which combinations the plant survives and which it doesn't — turning sb_ls01/sb_ls02 from two snapshots into a survival surface.
