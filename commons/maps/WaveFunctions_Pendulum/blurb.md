Where does a movement put its past? A pendulum swings under a gallows frame and behind it a line of marks recedes down the hall: a target of 25 milliseconds meets a physics clock that can only record on its ticks; the bob’s position is kept with the time it was taken, and each mark is drawn at a depth proportional to its age. Seen side-on the record is a wave; the bob never drew one.

The `PendulumWave` holds its record with two limits, three hundred marks and ten seconds, and a plate beside the trail prints which one is binding. A panel on the post switches the sampler: FINE spends its memory on resolution, COARSE keeps more time in fewer marks, and STROBE samples once per measured period, so the record of a moving bob looks still. The `seismograph` on the west walkway keeps the same kind of past on a drum, where time is an angle.
