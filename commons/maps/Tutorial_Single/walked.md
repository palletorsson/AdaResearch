## The cast

single cube · grab · score node · teleporter

## The walk

One cube. One exit. A platform barely wide enough to stand on. You reach, close your hand, pick it up — the score registers, the teleporter opens, you move forward. That is the entire map, and its smallness is the teaching. Before an array can be traversed it must have a first element, and before any element can be handled you must know the one gesture the whole chapter is built from: the grab. This is **index zero**, the scalar before the sequence, the atom performed once in silence so that when a hundred of them arrive you already know them in your hand.

## The turn (critical)

Placed against Array_Patterns' self-building carpet, this map is a deliberate step *down* in scale, and the drop is the argument: **one is not yet a pattern.** A single cube teaches interaction but not structure — it has no predecessor, no successor, no position relative to anything, nothing an index could even name. The chapter is quietly honest here about what it is doing to you: isolating the input mechanism from the data structure so that neither hides in the other, refusing cognitive overload by refusing, for one map, to mean anything at all. It is the scalar held up on its own precisely so that the leap to *many* lands as a leap and not a blur — the empty set with a body, waiting to be counted from.
