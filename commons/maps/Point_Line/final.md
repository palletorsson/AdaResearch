## Lines are not given

```gdscript
var a := Vector3(0, 0, 0)
var b := Vector3(2, 1, 0)
```

Two positions. Nothing yet — no line exists in those four lines of code, only two places that could have one drawn between them. A line arrives when a rule names two points as ends and something agrees to draw the between.

<!-- @line -->

Here is that agreement, standing on its own.

The room's own summary puts it better than I can: *lines are not given; they are captured traces.* You know by now what that costs, because you watched the trace being taken and you saw what the taking left out. A line is what survives a movement once the movement has been thrown away — and it is not a poorer thing for that. It is a thing you can send to somebody.

<!-- @vectorline -->

And ordered, it points.

```gdscript
var direction: Vector3 = (b - a).normalized()
var length: float = a.distance_to(b)
```

Two facts pulled out of one relation, and each one throws the other away. `normalized()` keeps the way round and discards how far. `distance_to()` keeps how far and discards the way round. Neither can be turned back into the segment alone, and the segment is not recoverable from both of them together either — you would also need to know where it started.

Three rooms ago that was a sentence on a wall. Here it is two lines you could type from memory.

## The measure that stayed

<!-- @wall_abstract -->

Divided horizontally. A surface with a line across it and nothing else — no ends marked, no length given, nothing to measure against.

It is the same object as everything else in this chapter and it refuses every use you have been taught for one. You cannot take its distance, because there is nothing to take it *to*. You cannot follow its direction, because it is not going anywhere. It divides, which is the one thing a line does that needs no second point at all.

Above and below. That is a line's oldest job and the chapter never mentioned it once.

The bench is here, and its drawer is open. Everything in this chapter was made of the two things on it, and the room is not asking you to make anything.

## What a return is for

The sequence could have ended at the grid. Most curricula do: the last room is the most elaborate one, difficulty is treated as depth, and the way out is forward.

This one comes back to two points, and the coming back is the content. Nothing here is harder than the second room. What is different is that you now know what a line does not keep, what it costs to make one, that its direction and its length are separately extractable and jointly insufficient, and that a line drawn across a surface can mean *above and below* and nothing else.

None of that is in the line. All of it is in you.

The next chapter is transformation — what happens when the things you have learned to hold still are moved.
