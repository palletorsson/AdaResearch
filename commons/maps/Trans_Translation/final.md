Translation changes nothing about a thing except where it is. That is why a void can be crossed by it, and why a path can be kept as nothing but a list of positions.

This hall has holes in its floor. Walkways cross them, and transport cubes shuttle over gaps your feet cannot, rising and falling and sliding on their one axis. The whole room is displacement made into infrastructure. Nothing here turns and nothing grows. Here becomes there, and the body learns what the code already says: position is a number you can rewrite.

```gdscript
func move_to(node: Node3D, target: Vector3) -> void:
    node.global_position = target
```

One assignment, and the object is elsewhere. Everything it was, it still is.

Your own path, drawn. Every step you take writes another point, and the line behind you is translation and nothing else: a time-indexed list of where you were. Look back at it from the far side of a gap and you will see that the crossing left no trace of you in it except positions. That is what it means to say translation preserves the thing. The line is the thing's absence written as its route.

<!-- @pick_up_cube -->

Cubes to carry, five of them, scattered on both sides of the void. Carrying is the smallest translation there is: your hand rewrites the cube's position and rewrites it again with every step. Nothing about the cube changes on the way.

<!-- @pickup_gate -->

The gate at the end counts. It wants seven on the score you have been carrying since the first hall, and it is closed until the count gets there and open the moment it does. Nothing about the gate is clever, and that is the point: enough translated things changed a number, and the number changed a wall. Displacement, accumulated, becomes a condition.

## Here becomes there

The gaps in this floor are not obstacles. They are invitations, because translation is the operation that does not care what lies between two positions. The transport cube does not cross the void; it is at one end, and then at the other, and the only thing the machine keeps is the number that changed. What makes that a room and not a trick is your body, which has to be carried too, and which arrives on the other side unchanged. That is translation's promise, and it is the only transformation whose promise covers you.
