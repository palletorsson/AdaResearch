# Fractals_1 - Introduction to Recursion

## Overview
Fractals_1 introduces the fundamental paradox of fractal geometry: infinite complexity emerging from finite rules. The map presents recursion not as a programming technique but as a generative principle—simple instructions that, when iterated, produce unbounded structure.

## Spatial Layout
- **Dimensions**: 7×9 grid
- **Architecture**: Stepped pathway with recessed areas forming viewing alcoves
- **Height**: Up to level 2 with varied terrain

## Key Elements

### Interactables
- **Subdivision Cube** (1,4) - Interactive cube that subdivides on touch, demonstrating the recursive split
- **Fractal Recursion 1** (3,3) - Basic recursive pattern visualization
- **Fractal Space** (3,4) - Spatial representation of fractal dimension
- **Hilbert 3D** (3,7) height 5m - Space-filling curve in three dimensions
- **Pixel Cloud** (6,0), (6,1) - Point-cloud representations of fractal distributions
- **Noise Torus** (4,3), (6,7) - Torus surfaces with fractal noise displacement
- **Grab Cube** (1,2) - Interactive primitive for comparison
- **Dark Sphere** (3,2) - Ambient lighting element

### Utilities
- **Teleporter** (5,6) - Exit to Fractals_2

## Atmosphere
- **Background**: Sky blue [0.2, 0.3, 0.7]
- **Lighting**: Warm directional with cool ambient
- **Mood**: Exploratory, contemplative, mathematical wonder

## Learning Sequence
1. Player enters and encounters the subdivision cube
2. Touches cube to see it split into smaller copies of itself
3. Observes the fractal recursion pattern—self-similarity at multiple scales
4. Explores the Hilbert curve—a line that fills space
5. Examines pixel clouds—discrete samples of continuous fractal structures
6. Encounters noise torus—fractal principles applied to surfaces
7. Exits to continue deeper into fractal territory

## Design Intent
The map establishes the core insight: **simple rules, infinite complexity**. The subdivision cube is the central metaphor—each division creates smaller versions of the original, and this process could continue forever. The Hilbert curve demonstrates that a 1D line can fill 2D (or 3D) space given sufficient iteration.

## Connection to Sequence
- **Position in fractals sequence**: 1/14
- **Precedes**: Fractals_2 (Stochastic Trees)
- **Establishes**: Recursion as generative principle, self-similarity, dimension as spectrum
