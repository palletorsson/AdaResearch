A flat grid. Twenty by twenty. Two axes, two degrees of freedom — the minimum scaffold for spatial thought. This is where dimension becomes plural.

Capsules combine. Spheres array. Drag points expose the skeleton of a cube. A doublestick links two things at a angle, with a ratio. Each artifact sits at named coordinates — (1,1), (3,3), (1,6) — because in a 2D array, every position is an address. North, a ridge rises two units. The rest is open floor. A teleporter waits at (5,6).

One axis is a list. Two axes are a world. The jump from line to plane is not incremental — it is categorical. X alone is sequence. X and Y together are territory. The grid does not describe space. It produces it.
