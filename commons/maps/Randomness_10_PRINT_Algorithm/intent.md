Concept: The 10 PRINT algorithm — a single line of code that randomly selects between two characters (/ and \) to generate an infinite maze — demonstrates that minimal randomness plus minimal rules produce emergent complexity. One coin flip per cell, infinite visual structure.
Sequence role: Third map in Randomness sequence. Follows Random_Remove (randomness as destruction) and pivots to randomness as construction. The remove_random artifact carries forward from map 2, bridging subtraction and generation. Precedes Random_Cubes, where randomness shapes individual objects rather than grid patterns. The 13x13 grid (one cell larger than the standard 12x12) is a deliberate formal choice — the odd dimension may prevent symmetry, forcing the maze to resist visual completion.
Technical angle: The 10 PRINT algorithm in GDScript: for each cell, randf() > 0.5 determines slash vs backslash orientation. The 3D extension (ten_print_maze_3d) translates 2D character choice into spatial geometry — rotated cubes or angled planes. pickup_cube_placer and remove_random show that the maze is not static but can be modified by addition and subtraction. The nature_system_demo may layer organic behavior onto the algorithmic grid.
Critical angle: 10 PRINT is the paradigmatic example of computational aesthetics — beauty from a coin flip. The algorithm dates to the Commodore 64 era (1982) and became the subject of a collaborative academic book examining how one line of code encodes cultural, mathematical, and aesthetic complexity. The maze it produces is not solvable in the traditional sense — it is a texture, not a puzzle. This reframes randomness: not as noise to be filtered, but as generative medium.
Key artifacts: ten_print_maze_3d is the central artifact — the algorithm made spatial and navigable; clipboard#ten_print_axioms provides the formal statement; pickup_cube_placer enables additive modification of the maze; remove_random enables subtractive modification; nature_system_demo overlays organic randomness onto algorithmic randomness; dark_sphere anchors.
