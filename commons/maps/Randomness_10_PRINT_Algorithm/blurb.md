One line of code. Two characters. Infinite complexity. The 10 PRINT maze emerges from the simplest possible random choice — flip a coin, draw a slash — and fills the world with labyrinthine possibility.

The `ten_print_maze_3d` translates the 1982 Commodore BASIC line into spatial geometry: for each cell, a coin flip chooses slash or backslash. `clipboard#ten_print_axioms` holds the formal statement; `pickup_cube_placer` and `remove_random` let you modify the maze additively and subtractively. 10 PRINT is the paradigm of computational aesthetics — beauty from a coin flip. The result is not a puzzle to solve but a texture to read. This is randomness as generative medium rather than noise to filter. One line of code encodes more visual and cultural complexity than a sculpture. The Commodore knew.
