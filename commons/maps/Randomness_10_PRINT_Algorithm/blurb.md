One line of Commodore 64 BASIC — `10 PRINT CHR$(205.5+RND(1)); : GOTO 10` — prints one of two diagonals, over and over, and the diagonals meet at the corners of their cells until the screen reads as a maze.

This hall takes the line apart. A screen prints it, with a console that shows the program and lets you move the famous 205.5, which is the coin's fairness, and remove the semicolon, which is what lets one mark sit beside the next. The floor the printer stands on is the screen's newest seven rows, laid down as low ridges. Four grids after François and Vera Molnar fix how many cells turn and leave chance only where. And a block of solid walls, printed from the same coin at the height of a body, can be walked into — to find out whether twelve coin flips left a way through.
