Concept: Establishes that a vector is magnitude with direction — the fundamental encoding of spatial quantities (position, velocity, force) as decomposable arrows along basis vectors.
Sequence role: First map in the Forces sequence; transitions the learner from static geometry (Primitives, Transformation, Color) into the language of dynamics. Everything that follows — subtraction, cross products, forces, motion — builds on components, magnitude, and normalization introduced here; leads to VectorSubtraction.
Technical angle: Vector3 components as axis projections, magnitude via √(x²+y²+z²), normalization to unit vectors, basis vectors î ĵ k̂ as the coordinate frame. The dark_sphere and basis_vectors_rig artifacts demonstrate vectors in constant use — rotation, position, decomposition — without calling attention to the abstraction. The raised observation platform is pedagogically deliberate: viewing vectors from above collapses the y-axis, revealing how components depend on viewpoint while the arrow itself stays fixed.
Critical angle: Vectors are the S term in QFEP — the state variables from which all dynamics derive. The coordinate system is imposed, not discovered: basis vectors are declared, not found. At λ=0.3 this map is pure order, rigid scaffolding before oscillation enters. The philosophical core: every measurement inherits the frame that made it possible.
Key artifacts: basis_vectors_rig decomposes a target point into three colored axis projections, making linear combination visible; dark_sphere demonstrates vectors implicitly through rotation, position, and emission pulsing; VectorBasics artifact anchors the map's identity.
Gap: Three artifacts proposed in the technical — magnitude_visualizer (live √ calculation), unit_vector_normalizer (interactive normalization), vector_component_projector (viewpoint-dependent apparent length) — would make the observation platform interactive rather than passive.
