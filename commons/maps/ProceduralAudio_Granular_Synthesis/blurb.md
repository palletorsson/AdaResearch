# Granular Synthesis

A grain is a sound too short to hear. Typically 1–100 milliseconds — below the threshold of pitch, above the threshold of click. Granular synthesis takes thousands of these micro-fragments and scatters them across time. Each grain has an envelope, a pitch, a position in a source. Alone: nothing. Together: texture.

The parameters are spatial. Grain density controls how many overlap. Spray randomizes their placement. Stretch decouples duration from pitch — a voice slowed to geological time without dropping an octave. The 8×8 grid becomes a cloud of sonic particles, each cell a grain window into source material.

What emerges has no waveform in the traditional sense. No oscillator, no filter sweep. Just statistical accumulation. Enough fragments of a thing become the thing — or become something the thing never was. Identity through multiplicity. Structure from dust.
