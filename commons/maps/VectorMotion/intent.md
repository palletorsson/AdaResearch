Concept: Motion as the time-evolution of position through velocity and acceleration — Euler integration as the heartbeat of physics simulation, orbital mechanics as the emergent result.
Sequence role: Eighth map in Forces; completes the vector-to-dynamics pipeline (vector → field → force → motion). The circular arena is deliberate — orbits are the canonical example of force producing curved motion. After this, the learner understands the full loop: force changes velocity, velocity changes position, every frame; follows VectorForces; leads to Vectors_5.
Technical angle: position += velocity * delta, velocity += acceleration * delta as the Euler integration step. Orbital mechanics: centripetal acceleration from gravity produces circular/elliptical paths. The circular arena layout mirrors the orbital concept. Delta-time normalization ensures frame-rate independence.
Critical angle: Motion reveals that acceleration — not velocity, not position — is what force controls. You don't feel speed; you feel acceleration. This is the experiential core of dynamics. The integration loop (F→a→v→p) is the simplest possible physics engine, and every game, simulation, and planetary model runs some version of it.
Key artifacts: VectorBasics and basis_vectors_rig provide the coordinate frame; dark_sphere orbits or follows trajectories as the demonstrator of motion. The circular arena layout physically embodies the orbital path.
