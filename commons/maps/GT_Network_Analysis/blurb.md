Every network has a center. Find it.

Centrality measures ask: which node matters most? Degree counts connections. Betweenness counts how many shortest paths pass through. Eigenvector asks not just who you know, but who *they* know. Different questions, different answers — the most connected node is not always the most important one.

Flow turns structure into capacity. Ford-Fulkerson pushes flow through edges, finds bottlenecks, proves that maximum flow equals minimum cut. The weakest link defines the system's throughput. A network's power is bounded by its most constrained passage.

Stand on the hub. Arms radiate outward over void — four directions, four dependencies. The center holds because the edges permit it. Remove one bridge and the topology fractures. Graphs don't have centers by nature. Centrality is always a question you impose on relation.
