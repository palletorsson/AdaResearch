## The cast

## The walk

The grammar builds shelter. The CityGenerator derives streets and blocks the way the earlier rooms derived branches — roads as axioms, intersections as rewrite sites, a skyline as a paragraph. The lsystem_dungeon turns the same machinery inward: corridors and chambers unfolding from rules, the kind of space this very curriculum walks its players through, generated by the kind of text this very chapter teaches.

## What it fixes

The second walk lands the chapter in the built world. Cities and dungeons are the two poles of authored space — the one grown by many hands over centuries, the other designed for a single crossing — and the room shows one grammar reaching both. The quiet lesson is about urbanism as much as code: real cities *are* rewriting systems, plot by plot, each generation deriving from the last under zoning's production rules. The generator does not imitate a city's shape; it imitates a city's *process*, which is why its outputs feel plausible at angles no one tuned.
