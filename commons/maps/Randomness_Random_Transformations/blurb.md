A 12×12 grid. An 8×8 arena at its center. Algorithms enter, transform, compete — not against each other, but against determinism itself. Shuffle a deck. Sample without replacement. Walk a drunk walk across the grid. Each algorithm takes order and injects controlled chaos: permutations, selections, mutations applied to cells you can see and rearrange.

Randomness is not the absence of structure. It is structure's other mode. A Fisher-Yates shuffle touches every element exactly once — perfect fairness through arbitrary choice. Reservoir sampling holds finite memory against an infinite stream. These are not tricks. They are protocols for making decisions without reasons.

The arena doesn't ask what the right answer is. It asks what happens when you stop insisting there is one.
