# Posture

Four artifacts in this museum declare an axis called `posture`, and no two of them
share a single word. Fifteen words, four scripts, one overlap. Here ten of those
words are put on one body — a mount, six equal links, six hinges, bending in one
plane — so that a posture is six numbers and nothing else, and the words can finally
be held against each other.

The room does not file them by whose word they were. It files them by what you have
to do with your own body to meet one: stoop over the curled things lying on the
floor, stand level with the ones at your eye, tilt your head back at the one thrown
out over the last corner. That filing agrees with none of the four vocabularies, and
it was not meant to.

Two of the works in here are the same six numbers. One was reached by sweeping a
line of text to zero degrees; the other by asking a robot arm to point at something
exactly as far away as the arm is long. They are filed in different rooms, mounted a
quarter turn apart, and captioned with the words their own makers used. Nothing in
the room tells you.
