Concept: One artifact, `posture_bench`, hung ten times at eight of its ten postures
and two of its three readings. The bench exists because four artifacts declare an
axis called `posture` and mean four different constructions by it; the room exists
because a contact sheet can only ever say that from one standpoint, and a body that
walks can say it from all of them.

Shape of the room: a corridor into a rotunda, a chicane down into an east bay, a
single door west through a three-metre divider, a west bay, and a small measuring
room that is also the only way out. Nothing is evenly spaced, nothing shares a
facing with its neighbour, and no two works are presented in the same relation
except the one pair that is being measured.

Why it is filed this way: the room is organised by what the visitor's body has to
do, not by which artifact lent the word.

  - THE STOOP. `ball` sits on the floor of the entry corridor and `ring` on the
    floor at the centre of the rotunda, both at 0 to 0.68 m — you look down on
    them, and to read a curl you crouch the way the thing has crouched. `ring` is
    the one work in the round: at 60 degrees per hinge over six hinges the chain
    closes and its tip returns to the mount it left, which is a fact you check by
    walking around it, not by reading it. It is mounted so that the arrival sees it
    edge-on, a blade, and the loop only appears once you have crossed to its far
    side. That is the room's first lesson about standpoints and it costs nothing.

  - THE LEVEL. `walker`, `solid`, `sentry` (east bay) and `line`, `arc` (west bay)
    stand on 2-high plinths, so each figure spans 1.0 to 1.68 m above the walking
    floor — waist to eye. This is the register where a body meets another body
    face to face.

  - THE REACH. `cast` alone stands on a 3-high pier at the south end of the east
    bay, 2.0 to 2.68 m up, head-on to the walk. It is the longest reach in the
    family, 0.401 m against a chain 0.420 m long, and the room spends its only
    pier on putting that reach over the visitor's head on the way to the door.

This filing cuts every vocabulary in half, deliberately. `ball` and `walker` are one
member's two words and the room puts one at your feet and one at your eye. `sentry`
and `cast` are one member's words and the room puts one at your eye and one above
you. And it quietly agrees with the code against the captions: `walker` is filed as
a defence STATE and `solid` as a mechanical LOAD, and the room stands them at the
same height in the same bay in the same relation to your body — because the source
reads `_fold_amount` and `_compression` as one float wearing two nouns.

Why these eight, and not the others: `laden` is declined because it is the same
construction as `solid`, the same float sampled at 0.40 instead of 0.90; hanging
both would make the room argue "here is a slider", and the room already carries a
scalar's two ends in `ball` and `walker`. `coil` is declined because it is a second
tight curl that ends over its own mount, and two curls in one room invite the reader
to treat "curl" as a category — which is exactly the claim the measuring room
refutes. `cycle`, `drawn`, `stack` and `stoop` were declined by the bench itself,
with arithmetic, and the room does not reopen them.

The designed null: `line` (row 21, col 7) and `sentry` (row 21, col 13). Both are
six hinges at zero degrees — one from a text layout swept to nothing, one from an
animal-posture solver aiming at a point exactly as far off as its own chain is long,
so it takes the unreachable branch and straightens. They are on the same row, six
cells apart, with the divider wall between them, and the one walkable cell at its
midpoint (row 21, col 10) is the door and the only standpoint in the room that holds
both. They are captioned with their own members' words and their own members'
departments, LAYOUT and STANCE, and they are mounted a quarter turn apart — `line`
face-on to that standpoint, `sentry` edge-on. So the two plinths present visibly
different stages: the 0.80 x 0.36 m plate reads 0.80 wide on one and 0.36 wide on
the other. For any other posture in this family a quarter turn is the difference
between a pose and a blade. Here the cages differ by a factor of two and the figures
do not differ at all. That is the null made walkable: in a table it is 0.00 per
cent; in the room it is the only pair that survives being walked around.

The pre-registered closest pair, `solid` against `arc` at 2.60 degrees per hinge, is
staged as the room's other argument. Both stand on row 18, both on 2-high plinths,
both facing the divider — `solid` west from col 17, `arc` east from col 5 — so the
visitor meets each one head-on, on the same row, at the same height, in the same
relation, and can never hold both, because there is a wall and a walk between them.
The two most nearly identical works in the family are the two the room refuses to
put side by side.

What is levelled, and why: the measuring room. `ball` and `line` stand there in the
`envelope` reading, side by side on the same row at the same height facing the same
way — the only level, evenly spaced, same-facing pair in the building. The room
levels only where it is measuring, and what it is measuring is a claim worth
disagreeing with: a defence state is supposed to minimise the volume it occupies,
and the curl's hull is 0.0219 m2 against the straight stick's 0.0135 — 62 per cent
larger. What a curl minimises is reach, not volume, and reach is not hull. Putting
that last, as the only way out, means the room ends by contradicting the most
obvious thing anyone would have said about it.

Key artifacts: `posture_bench` x10 — `ball`, `ring` on the floor; `walker`, `solid`,
`sentry`, `line`, `arc` at eye level; `cast` on the pier; `ball` and `line` again in
the `envelope` reading in the measuring room. Captions carry each word and the
member that coined it, and nothing else; the room never prints its own opinion,
because the disagreement is the exhibit.

Gap: the `joints` reading is not hung. It draws a solid sector at each hinge
spanning that hinge's own angle, which is the reading that turns a pose into six
comparable quantities — and it is the one reading that would be improved, not
weakened, by the specimen row this room refuses to build. It belongs in a second
room with a different argument, not in this one.
