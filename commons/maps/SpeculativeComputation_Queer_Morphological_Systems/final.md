# When does a body become a history?

A morphogenetic system does not begin with a finished body. It begins with a local rule repeated until a pattern can hold itself together.

<!-- @morphogenesis_bench -->

Change one neighbourhood rule and watch the form alter over several generations. Keep the seed fixed, then change the seed while restoring the rule. The first comparison isolates a law; the second reveals how much the initial condition carries.

The body is not a metaphor added after the computation. Its folds, gaps, and edges are the record of what each local update allowed. A small rule can produce a large difference, but the difference is never free of its substrate.

Try a rule that rewards contact, then one that rewards separation. Ask which bodies each rule can host, and which bodies disappear before they can be named. The system is speculative because the criterion can be revised while the history remains visible.
