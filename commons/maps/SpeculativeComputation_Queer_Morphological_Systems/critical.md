# The body is an accumulated decision

Morphogenesis is often described as nature finding its form. The phrase hides the rule. A body becomes legible through repeated local decisions about contact, distance, growth, and removal.

The queer question is not whether the result looks unusual. It is whether the system can host more than one viable continuation, and whether its history allows us to see the criteria that made some forms persist. A morphology is an archive of permissions and exclusions.

Keep the seed and the rule separate. Otherwise the system’s contingency becomes an aesthetic effect, and the strange body is mistaken for an inevitable one.
