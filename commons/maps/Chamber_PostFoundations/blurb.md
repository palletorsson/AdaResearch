The chamber's verb is *to build anyway*.

The last chamber of the spine, and the quietest. You take the bracelet one final time and place a block — building with everything the crisis taught, laying stone in a frame you no longer pretend is complete.

The foundations crisis proved that every formal system has an outside. The chapters after it walked what it means to keep working past that proof: situated, paraconsistent, collective, rhizomatic, honest about the outside. Here that stance becomes a gesture. You found a structure knowing it cannot ground itself, place a first block knowing no block is bedrock, and build not despite incompleteness but with it.

Not a triumphant last theorem. A walker who has learned that foundation is a choice made in full view of its own limits, and who lays one anyway.
