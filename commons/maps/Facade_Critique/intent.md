Concept: Facade_Critique is the *refused actualization* of the facade assembly principle. Where the previous five expression maps each took a position in the virtual field — canonical, dramatized, hybridized, surfaced, modularized — this map takes the position of *refusal itself*. Two facades stand opposite each other in an asymmetric room: Superstudio's *Continuous Monument* on the north wall (a single infinite slab refusing bay rhythm; the building denies the rule that buildings have bays), and a Memphis-Group totem-stack on the south wall (vertical stack of unrelated glyphs refusing hierarchy; the building denies the rule that storeys are ranked). The two refusals refuse different axes of the same principle, which is the point: refusal is not random destruction — refusal is *specifically targeted at the rule the player has been installed with*.

Actualizes: Facade_Assembly_Principle, in the *refused* mode. The position in the virtual field is where one axis (rhythm for Superstudio, hierarchy for Memphis) is held at *negation* — not at any value of the axis but at its rejection-as-axis. The map is the demonstration that the principle's *legibility* makes refusal legible. Walk into this room having walked the previous five maps; without that walk, neither facade reads as a refusal of anything — they read as just strange. With it, both refusals carry their critique articulately.

Sequence role: Seventh map of the facade_assembly branch (Principle → Classical → Baroque → Venetian Gothic → Rustication → NYC Tenement → Critique → Chamber). The synthesis-of-refusal before the synthesis-of-composition (which is the Chamber). The two synthesis modes — refusal and composition — are complementary: Critique shows what the player has the *intellectual* power to refuse; Chamber gives them the *practical* power to compose. Together they close the sequence.

Technical angle: Asymmetric 12x7 room with no front-back orientation. Two facades face inward from opposite long walls, neither privileged by spawn position or lighting. The Superstudio wall (north): a single horizontal slab that runs the full 12-unit width with no bay markers, no openings, no cornice, no base — a featureless mat-black plane four storeys tall. The Memphis wall (south): a stack of six unrelated glyph-tiles arranged vertically — each glyph a different style (a stripe, a half-circle, a checkerboard, a single dot, a zigzag, a square with a triangle inside) — bright primary colours, no relation between the levels, no continuity of vocabulary. Pass-4 build: the room walks today as 12x7x4 with spawn at centre and teleporter in the far corner; the refusal-vocabulary artifacts (superstudio_slab, memphis_glyph_stack, totem_layer_stripe, totem_layer_dot, etc.) are queued for the architectural primitives session.

Critical angle: Critique requires the rule. Without bay rhythm being installed first, a flat infinite slab is not a refusal — it is just a flat infinite slab. Without hierarchy being installed first, a stack of unrelated glyphs is not a refusal — it is just a stack of glyphs. The previous five maps installed the rule rigorously enough that this room's two facades read as *targeted critiques*. Superstudio's *Continuous Monument* (1969) was an explicit Italian Radical-architecture refusal of corporate-modernist buildings as bay-systems; the architects insisted the building is *one indifferent surface*, and Western architectural history could not metabolise the proposal because it refused to participate in the grammar. Memphis Group (Sottsass, 1980s) refused architectural hierarchy as part of a broader refusal of "good taste" being aligned with Renaissance/Bauhaus rules; the totem stacks unrelated forms because hierarchy was the unmarked assumption. Both refusals are *operations of critique*, not just stylistic choices. The room's asymmetry — spawn in the middle, teleporter in a corner, two walls speaking different refusals — refuses the symmetric room as well. Critique that uses symmetric composition is critique that secretly still believes in the rule.
