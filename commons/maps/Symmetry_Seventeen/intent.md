Concept: The census that closes. There are exactly seventeen ways a flat pattern can repeat — not roughly seventeen, not seventeen so far, but seventeen provably, with no eighteenth possible. The map walks the learner through the wallpaper groups, then hands them the tools to build a pattern of their own and asks them to say which of the seventeen they made. Naming, for once, with nothing left over.

Sequence role: Closing map of the Color sequence, adopted into it on 2026-08-24 (f3a891a09) when the symmetry sequence dissolved and its wallpaper heroes came here. It is the last room of the composition arc: Array establishes addressing, Pattern_Foundry manufactures repetition, the color maps thread hue through it, and this room finally counts the repetitions and finds the count finite. It is also the one place in the book where a classification finishes, which is why it sits at the end rather than the middle — closure reads as an event only after the learner has spent a sequence in the open-ended.

Technical angle: Twenty by twenty-two, five heights, thirteen placements. `wall_pattern_gallery` paints the walls with wallpaper-group tilings and carries the census claim. Four named looms and mills work specific groups rather than the abstract idea of a group: `loom_alhambra_p6m` and `mill_alhambra_p6m` weave and turn p6m, the sixfold group the Alhambra's artisans used most; `mill_persian_p4` and `mill_escher_p4g` take the fourfold groups; `loom_escher_mirror` and `pattern_loom` run the reflection cases. `vr_tile_editor` lets the learner author a tile, `pattern_tile_puzzle` asks them to classify what they authored, and `kaleidoscope` on a plinth gives rotational symmetry as something to look into rather than at. `pattern_atlas_gallery` closes the room as a makers' wing — thirteen plates of world pattern traditions running as shaders, each tagged with the names of its makers. `tile_meander_floor` and `pattern_artifact` ground the walk.

Critical angle: This is the only classification in the entire curriculum that terminates, and the room is built so the learner feels that as the rare thing it is. Everywhere else Ada teaches the uncountable and the unclosable — fractal dimension refusing an integer, Cantor dust of measure zero, the Mandelbrot boundary that never resolves, Gödel's sentence that no system closes. Ada is a book about the open census. Here alone the list ends, and that exception is what makes the rest of the book's openness legible; you cannot feel what it means for dimension to refuse the integers until you have stood once in a room where the count actually closes. The turn's crown is historical: the tile-cutters and plasterers of al-Andalus — craftworkers forbidden figural images, pouring that constraint into geometry — exhausted all seventeen groups by hand centuries before group theory existed to prove there were only seventeen. Proof-without-theorem, done as devotion and as day-labour, by master *alarifes*, zellige-cutters and *sebka* carvers whose names almost none survive on the walls their mathematics finished. When mathematicians finally proved the list complete they were confirming what the workers of Granada had already built. The map's honest move is to say so, and to stop calling that nobody — which is why the learner leaves past the makers' wing rather than past a wall of mathematics.

Key artifacts: `wall_pattern_gallery` states the census on the walls; `loom_alhambra_p6m` and `mill_alhambra_p6m` weave and turn the richest group, p6m; `mill_persian_p4` and `mill_escher_p4g` hold the fourfold cases; `vr_tile_editor` hands the learner authorship; `pattern_tile_puzzle` turns that authorship into classification, which is the proof of the chapter; `kaleidoscope` makes rotation something you look into; `pattern_atlas_gallery` names the makers at the exit.

Gap: A dedicated wall showing all seventeen groups labelled — the deluxe version of the flagged "seventeen wall" — is still missing; the makers' wing added on 2026-07-08 half-answers it by naming traditions, but the census itself is never laid out as a complete visible list, which is the one image the map's whole claim asks for. Whether classifying your own pattern into the seventeen lands bodily is untested: not yet walked in VR. And with symmetry dissolved as a sequence, the four looms and mills now stand as the surviving remnant of a longer arc, so the room carries more groups than it has floor to explain them on.
