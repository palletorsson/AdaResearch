## The walk

The chapter opens with the friendliest of the three operators and quietly warns you not to trust it. The csg_union_demo takes two primitives and declares them one — A∪B — and where they overlapped, the seam simply vanishes, leaving a single skin that wraps both as if they had never been separate.

## What it opens

The threshold's whole argument is in the word *not*: composition is **not** addition. Union looks like setting two things side by side, but it does something stranger — it fuses them, dissolving the boundary between them into one continuous surface, so the inside of the whole is no longer two insides adjacent but a single renegotiated volume. This is the chapter's founding correction to intuition: solids do not stack like blocks, they *compose* like clauses, and the moment you join two the interface between them is rewritten. Everything that follows — the cut, the joint, the wall — is this same lawful composition, and the room spends its length making sure you feel that even the gentlest join is an act.
