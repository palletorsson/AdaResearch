# FM Synthesis

One operator modulates another. FM synthesis builds sound not from static waveforms but from frequency relationships — one oscillator bending the pitch of another at audio rate. John Chowning discovered it by accident in 1967. Yamaha turned it into the DX7. Six operators, thirty-two algorithms, a generation of sound.

The math is simple: carrier frequency plus modulator amplitude times modulator frequency. The result is not. Small changes in modulation index cascade into dense harmonic spectra — metallic bells, electric pianos, sounds that analog circuits cannot produce. The spectrum expands combinatorially. Two sine waves become infinite sidebands.

Modulation is not addition. It is interference. One wave reshaping another from inside, producing harmonics neither contains alone. Identity through mutual distortion.
