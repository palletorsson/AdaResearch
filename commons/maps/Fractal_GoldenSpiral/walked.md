## The walk

The sequence pivots from deletion to growth, and the exemplar is a vegetable. The `fibonacci_sequences` station runs the recurrence you already met in the pagoda — 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 — and shows the ratio of consecutive terms creeping toward φ = 1.618. The `golden_rectangle` demonstrates the additive twin of Cantor's deletion: cut a square off a golden rectangle and what remains is a *smaller golden rectangle*, forever — self-similarity built by leaving-behind rather than removing. Then the map earns its keep by putting a `romanesco` in front of you. Its florets spiral at the golden angle, 137.5°, each one a smaller copy of the head, the same logarithmic spiral you just watched drawn from φ — except no one drew it. The plant *is* the algorithm, running in cells. Count the spirals: they come in Fibonacci numbers, 8 one way and 13 the other, because that is what packing at 137.5° produces.

## The turn (critical)

Why 137.5°? Because φ is the **most irrational number there is** — its continued fraction is nothing but 1s, which makes it the number hardest to approximate by any ratio, the slowest to fall into a repeating pattern. And that is precisely why nature reaches for it. A plant laying down seeds around a stem wants no two to shadow each other; any *rational* angle eventually lines seeds up into spokes and wastes the light; the maximally irrational angle never repeats, so it packs the plane with no gaps and no collisions. The turn is the inversion this forces: irrationality, the property mathematics once treated as a defect — numbers that *refuse* to be a clean ratio, that broke the Pythagorean dream of a world made of whole-number proportions — turns out to be the exact resource life exploits to grow well. The unmeasurable is not the flaw in the system; it is the optimum. The romanesco is the chapter's tenderest object because it collapses the wall the whole curriculum was built beside: the fractal is not a metaphor imposed on biology, and biology is not "inspired by" the math. The cauliflower computes φ with no brain, no intention, no blueprint — form emerging from a single irrational constant faithfully applied — and it does so more beautifully than any drafted figure, because it never had the option of rounding off.
