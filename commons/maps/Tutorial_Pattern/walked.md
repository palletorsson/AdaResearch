## The cast

tile grid · mirror tile · wallpaper-group analyzer · pulsar visualizer

## The walk

Now the grid stops being a place you navigate and becomes a place you **generate.** You put colors on tiles and watch a rule run across the indices: a checkerboard falls out of `(x + y) % 2`, a wave falls out of a sine swept along a row, the mirror tile turns four cells into a kaleidoscope. Every pattern is an equation the eye solves before the mind derives it — you *see* the checkerboard instantly and only afterward could you write the formula that made it. The pulsar visualizer stacks the array into Joy Division's *Unknown Pleasures*, and the map's lesson clicks: arrays are neutral containers until a rule runs across them, and then structure simply appears.

## The turn (critical)

Arrays give order; rules give **meaning**, and this map stages the exact cognitive bridge between the two — the moment data becomes pattern. The critical point is subtle and the map gets it right: the checkerboard formula is trivial, but *recognizing* that so small a rule produces so much structure is the whole insight, and the eye does it in the wrong order — pattern first, formula after. That reversal is the chapter's deepest claim about computation: the machine derives the pattern from the rule; the human perceives the rule from the pattern; and the array is the shared surface where those two directions of understanding meet. The pulsar makes it uneasy on purpose — the same stacked-array move that draws a beautiful record sleeve also draws a neutron star's radio sweep, a reminder that "pattern" is never innocent of what it is a pattern *of*.
