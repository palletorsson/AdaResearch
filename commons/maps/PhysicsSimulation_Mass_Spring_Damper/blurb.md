# Mass-Spring-Damper

Mass, spring, damper. Three elements, one equation, every vibration ever analyzed. Hooke's law pulls back — F = -kx — proportional, restoring, faithful. The damper resists — F = -cv — converting motion into heat, order into entropy. Between them, the mass oscillates.

Tweak the stiffness k and the system rings. Tweak the damping c and the ringing dies. Underdamped: the platform bounces, overshoots, settles in spirals. Overdamped: it crawls back, sluggish, refusing to oscillate at all. Critical damping — the knife-edge ratio where the system returns to rest as fast as possible without a single overshoot. The shortest path to equilibrium. The optimal forgetting.

Every suspension bridge, every shock absorber, every eardrum. The second-order system is how matter negotiates between holding shape and dissipating force. Stillness is not the absence of motion — it is motion perfectly opposed.
