Concept: Attraction and repulsion at a distance — gravitational and electromagnetic force laws, inverse-square falloff, and the transition from contact forces to field-mediated interaction.
Sequence role: Seventeenth map in Forces; pivots from contact forces (friction, drag) to action-at-a-distance forces. The inverse-square law (F ∝ 1/r²) is the governing principle of gravity and electrostatics — double the distance, quarter the force. The mass_spring_damper and spring_system artifacts bridge from Forces_3's springs to the new distance-dependent forces; follows Forces_5; leads to Forces_7.
Technical angle: F = G*m1*m2/r² for gravity, Coulomb's law for charge. Inverse-square falloff computed from displacement vector magnitude. Attractive (like gravity, opposite charges) vs repulsive (like charges) as sign differences in the force equation. The angular motion artifacts (example_3_2, example_3_3) show how attraction produces orbits when combined with tangential velocity.
Critical angle: Action at a distance troubled Newton himself — how does one mass "know" where another is? Fields resolve this: the force exists everywhere in space as a field, and objects respond to the local field value at their position. This is the conceptual shift from Forces_1's contact mechanics to modern field theory. The inverse-square law's universality (gravity, electrostatics, light intensity) hints at deeper geometric origins.
Key artifacts: example_3_2 (arbitrary angular motion) and example_3_3 (pointing in direction of motion) demonstrate attraction-driven orbits; mass_spring_damper and spring_system bridge the restoring-force concept from springs to gravitational wells.
