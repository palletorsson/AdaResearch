Concept: Vector fields as invisible infrastructure — an open channel with a central ridge where particle currents become visible, revealing how a field of local directions produces global flow patterns. Flow fields are the mathematical abstraction underlying all swarm navigation: each agent reads a local vector and the collective motion emerges from the field's topology.
Sequence role: Second map in Swarm Intelligence (14th spine sequence, integration phase), intermediate difficulty. After Physarum demonstrated biological flow networks, this map abstracts the principle: a vector field assigns a direction to every point in space, and particles following local directions produce coherent global streams. Connects backward to the vector sequence (vectors as fields, not just arrows) and forward to SwarmIntelligence_Boids_Algorithm where agents generate their own flow through neighbor interaction; follows SwarmIntelligence_PhysarumColony.
Technical angle: 2D or 3D vector field defined on a grid, with particles advected by bilinear interpolation of nearby grid vectors. Perlin noise or curl noise as the field generator for organic-looking flow. The central ridge in the channel creates a topological feature — a saddle point or divergence zone — where flow patterns bifurcate. Visualization via streamlines, particle traces, or line integral convolution (LIC).
Critical angle: Flow fields make stigmergy mathematical: the environment carries directional information, and agents need no communication channel beyond reading the field at their location. This is the swarm intelligence thesis distilled — local rules produce global intelligence because the environment mediates between agents. The flow field is also an aesthetic object: the patterns of convergence, divergence, and curl are the visual grammar of fluid dynamics and meteorology.
Key artifacts: FlowFieldMain implements the flow field simulation with particle advection, likely including the open channel geometry with central ridge and controls for field parameters (noise scale, particle count, speed).
Gap: Needs a field editor — an artifact that lets the learner paint custom vector directions onto the grid and immediately see how particles respond, making the causal relationship between local field values and global flow patterns manually explorable.
