# Tutorial_3D — walked

## The cast

4×4×4 cube · stepped platforms · lifts · dark_sphere

## The walk

Two dimensions flatten; three dimensions **inhabit.** The grid grows a vertical axis, and what was a spreadsheet becomes a volume you climb. Stepped platforms rise along two edges, lifts carry your body upward through identical layers of the same array, and at the center a 4×4×4 cube hangs in space — sixty-four cells, each addressable, each exact, `grid[x][y][z]`. Three indices to name a point in a room. And crucially the order still holds: every voxel sits at a known address even as you climb the Z-index by physically ascending through the structure.

## The turn (critical)

This completes the chapter's dimensional ladder — scalar, line, field, volume — and the completion carries a claim the whole rest of the curriculum will cash: **the third dimension is where arrays become worlds.** A 2D grid is a floor plan; a 3D grid is a building. Every voxel game, every volumetric simulation, every physics field later in the book addresses space with three indices, and this map is where that literacy is installed in the body by making you *climb the data structure* rather than read it. There is a quiet ontological joke in the stepped platforms: you ascend the Y-index with your legs, proving that "abstract" array dimensions and "real" spatial ones were never two different things — the array was always a space, and the space was always an array. The chapter has taught you to walk inside `grid[x][y][z]`. From here on, everything the book simulates lives in a room this map built.
