# PG Branching Growth

Two strategies for filling space. One follows rules. The other follows noise.

The branching algorithm grows like a tree — a seed point extends, splits, extends again. Each fork is a decision encoded in geometry. The structure remembers every choice it made. Deterministic bones. In the void pit, the skeleton builds itself downward, each branch a frozen instruction.

On the raised platform, organic space does the opposite. No skeleton, no decisions — just noise shaped into continuous terrain. Fields instead of forks. The ground swells and recedes according to gradients that have no author.

Rules produce structure that looks grown. Noise produces growth that looks structured. The corridor between them is the question: when a form branches, is it following a plan or finding a path? The difference matters less than the threshold where both strategies produce worlds.
