## The walk

The chapter opens in a laboratory of sentences. The lsystem_editor puts the axiom and rules in your hands — F becomes F+F−F, apply, apply again — and the fractal_lsystem_string shows the text itself growing, a one-letter seed swelling into a paragraph of turtle commands. The grammar_provenance panel keeps the receipts: which rule produced which limb. And at the end of the bench the lsystem_tree stands as the payoff — the same string, read as instructions, suddenly vertical and green.

## What it opens

The threshold teaches the chapter's two-step: rewrite, then interpret. A grammar is just text replacing text — pure symbol-shuffling, no geometry anywhere — until a reader walks the string and turns letters into turns and strides. Meaning arrives at interpretation, not before. A sentence can become a forest, but only because something chose to *read* it as one; the lab hands you both the writing and the reading, and the chapter spends the rest of its rooms widening what the reading can be.
