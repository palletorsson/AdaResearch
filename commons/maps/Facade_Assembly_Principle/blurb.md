Mosaics are arrays in stone. Facades are assembly.

A column has an order — Doric, Ionic, Corinthian. A facade has bays, repeating across its width with a rhythm the eye reads before the mind names. It has hierarchy: the base supports, the middle storey speaks, the attic crowns. Windows are not decoration; they are punctuation. Rustication is the wall's voice, not its silence. Cornices mark where the composition begins and ends.

Six moves. Walk past each tile and meet one. Then walk the six expression maps — Classical clean, Baroque dramatized, Venetian Gothic hybridized, Naples diamond rusticated, NYC tenement modularized, the critique refusing it all — and feel what the grammar can do, including when it is refused.

A facade is legible because its rule is shared. This map installs the rule so the rest of the sequence can speak.
