Concept: Facade_Assembly_Principle is the rulebook for the facade_assembly branch. Where mosaics are arrays in stone — addressable cells with adjacency rules — facades are *assembly*: hierarchical composition of parts. This map names the six compositional moves the rest of the sequence will exercise: column orders, bay rhythm, hierarchy, fenestration, rustication, cornice/base. Each is a way the assembly grammar speaks; together they are the vocabulary every facade in the sequence draws from.

Virtual field: The differential field of *all possible facades composable from six compositional moves* — column orders × bay rhythms × hierarchy stacks × fenestration types × rustication surfaces × cornice/base framings. The field is continuous in each axis (rhythm spacing varies, column proportions vary, hierarchy weights vary), and a facade is one path through it. Naming the six axes is naming the dimensions of the virtual; the expression maps that follow are each a single position in that field, actualized with a body. The principle map does not enumerate the field; it opens it.

Sequence role: First map of the facade_assembly branch sequence (branch off boolean_surfaces). Establishes the principle before any expression map is walked. Following the spirit of Geometric_1 in computationalgeometry — the principle is the subject, the geometry is the body. Player arrives carrying CSG composition (union, intersection, difference) from boolean_surfaces, 1D-2D-3D understanding from array_tutorial, and basic transformation vocabulary. This map points all that substrate at architectural form. Leads forward to Facade_Classical (the principle clean), Facade_Baroque (dramatized), Facade_Venetian_Gothic (hybridized), Facade_Rustication (surface), Facade_NYC_Tenement (modularized), Facade_Critique (refused), and closes in Chamber_Facade.

Technical angle: A facade's grammar has six load-bearing moves. (1) Column orders — Doric, Ionic, Corinthian — give the vertical primitive its semantic. (2) Bay rhythm: the horizontal partitioning of the facade into modular bays, regular (Renaissance) or syncopated (Baroque). (3) Hierarchy: base / piano nobile / attic, the vertical stack with the middle storey privileged. (4) Fenestration: window types, their shape, their placement, their semantic load. (5) Rustication: surface texture as voice — smooth marble, diamond bossing, vermiculation. (6) Cornice and base: the framing operators that mark where the facade begins and ends. Each move can be applied, omitted, or refused; refusal is itself a move when the others are in place. The CSG vocabulary from boolean_surfaces is the substrate: every cornice is a union, every window opening is a difference, every column is an intersection of a base, shaft, and capital.

Critical angle: The principle map is short because the principle is short. Where the orphan facade stubs in array_tutorial declared 26 named exhibits without ever building one — gestures at variety without a vocabulary to ground it — this map does the inverse. Six tiles, six moves, six things a facade can say. The expression maps that follow are not exhibits of facades; they are demonstrations of what the principle does that a single rule cannot. A facade is legible because its grammar is shared; deviation reads as deviation only against the shared rule. Memphis Totem refuses hierarchy intelligibly because Renaissance hierarchy is the legible default. The principle map installs that default explicitly so the refusals can speak.
