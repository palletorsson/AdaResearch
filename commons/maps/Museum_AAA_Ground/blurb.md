# The Ground Room

A ground is not a background colour. It is the layer a painter chooses because of what will be
put on top of it — red clay under gold leaf so the gold warms from beneath, green earth under
flesh so the pink laid over it stops shouting.

You arrive standing on the ground, beside a white panel with nothing on it. That is how this
family of artifacts has always shipped: four colours and no mark. Then you climb one step and
walk. From here on the grounds are a metre below your feet, cut into the floor, and the marks
are a metre above them on shelves cut out of the wall. You are walking in the gap between the
two layers the whole way.

Three rooms, three grounds, and then a wide pale hall where the argument stops working.
