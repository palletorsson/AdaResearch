# Situated Computation — Summary

SpeculativeComputation_Situated_Computation is the fourth map in the Post-Foundations Crisis sequence. It takes Donna Haraway's argument on situated knowledge and treats it as a design constraint. Objectivity is not the absence of a perspective; it is the careful accounting of which perspective a system is occupying.

The room is a small observation chamber with three viewing platforms, each marked with a different standpoint: a ground-level view, an aerial view, and a slanted view from a local community's vantage. A shared dataset sits at the centre of the room, and each platform renders the dataset differently. Heights and distances are the same in every view; the meaning of those heights and distances changes with the platform.

A central bench lets the learner annotate what each view can see and what each view cannot. Trees appear in the aerial view but hide figures beneath them; the community view identifies the figures but loses the spatial extent. The annotations accumulate, and a running panel reads out the combined account as partial, located, and accountable rather than as universal.

A second station stages the same principle on a small classifier. The learner chooses a standpoint before training; the resulting model labels the dataset from that standpoint, and the panel makes the standpoint visible in the model's outputs. Within the sequence, Situated_Computation replaces the view from nowhere with an explicit account of where the view is coming from.
