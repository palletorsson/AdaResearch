Critical angle: Torque is not a force — it is the force's opinion about rotation, measured by how far off-center and how perpendicular it acts. This distinction matters because it reveals that the cross product is not just a mathematical curiosity from VectorOperations but a physical operator that determines whether things spin or translate. The attraction exercise enacts the sequence truth most directly: "An orbit is a perpetual fall that always misses the ground." The learner grabs the attractor, drags it, and watches the orbit deform — making explicit that orbital structure is not designed but emerges from the balance between tangential velocity and radial attraction. This is a microcosm of QFEP's core insight: complex behavior (orbits, stable patterns, oscillation) emerges from simple rules (inverse-square law plus initial conditions) without any global coordinator. The throwing artifact closes the loop between understanding and action — the learner must internalize the vector decomposition to aim accurately, which is the pedagogical goal: mathematics that lives in the hands, not just the head. The random acceleration exercise quietly demonstrates that deterministic rules plus noise produce unpredictable but bounded behavior, a preview of the chaos maps to come.
