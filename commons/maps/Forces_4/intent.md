Concept: Friction as resistance to motion — static friction holds until threshold, kinetic friction decelerates, and the distinction between the two reveals that starting motion costs more than sustaining it.
Sequence role: Fifteenth map in Forces; introduces dissipative forces after the conservative spring (Forces_3). Where springs store and return energy, friction destroys it. This is the first encounter with irreversibility in the Forces sub-sequence — energy converted to heat is lost; leads to Forces_5.
Technical angle: Static friction: F_s ≤ μ_s * N (holds until threshold exceeded). Kinetic friction: F_k = μ_k * N (constant deceleration during motion). Normal force N as the perpendicular surface reaction. Coefficient of friction μ as surface property. The transition from static to kinetic as a discontinuity.
Critical angle: Friction is where idealized physics meets material reality. Without friction, the world is frictionless ice — everything slides forever. Friction introduces grip, traction, control, and energy loss. The static-to-kinetic transition is a phase change in miniature: below threshold nothing happens, above it everything moves. Irreversibility enters the sequence.
Key artifacts: VectorBasics and basis_vectors_rig provide the coordinate frame; dark_sphere as the sliding body.
Gap: A friction_surface_demo with adjustable μ values showing how the same push produces different outcomes on ice vs rubber vs sandpaper would make the coefficient tangible.
