## The walk

The chapter opens on a city. The KonigsbergBridge lays out the old riddle at walking scale — seven bridges, four land masses, one Sunday question: can you cross each bridge exactly once? Euler answered by refusing to see the city. He squinted until Königsberg became four dots and seven lines, and in that act of disrespect invented a mathematics. Beside it the graphspace hangs the abstraction pure: vertices and edges, nothing else, the diagram the city collapsed into.

## What it opens

The threshold teaches the chapter's whole move in one gesture: squint. Throw away distance, shape, stone, river — keep only *what connects to what* — and questions that looked geographic become answerable by counting. (The answer is no: too many odd corners.) Everything after this room is the same squint aimed at new targets.
