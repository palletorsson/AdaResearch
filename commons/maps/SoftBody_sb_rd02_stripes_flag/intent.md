Concept: A flag-shaped cloth pinned along its top row, blown sideways by wind, with a Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion field on its surface tuned to the stripe regime. Activator concentration modulates per-vertex spring stiffness, so the stripe pattern emerges as mechanical ridges rolling across the waving banner. The map is a gallery study exposing the live simulation.

Sequence role: Second reaction-diffusion DNA-bridge study (sb_rd02) in the imported soft-body gallery cluster within the Soft Bodies sequence (13th spine, integration phase). Sister to sb_rd01 (coral cloth, spotted regime). Together the pair shows that the same Gray-Scott PDE, tuned to two different parameter neighborhoods, produces qualitatively distinct fabrics — spots versus stripes — and that the choice propagates from chemistry to mechanics to wind-driven motion.

Technical angle: Two-field Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion solved on the cloth grid each frame; feed rate F and kill rate k chosen in the stripe-producing neighborhood of parameter space rather than the spot-producing neighborhood. Activator concentration mapped to a per-vertex stiffness multiplier on structural and shear springs. Cloth integrates as standard spring-mass-Verlet with gravity and a constant sideways wind force per face. Coupling remains one-way (chemistry to mechanics), keeping the system stable while making the pattern visible in the dynamic response.

Critical angle: Sb_rd01 staged the ontological claim — pattern is also material — on a static draping cloth. Sb_rd02 stages the same claim under motion. When the wind moves the flag, the stripes do not slide across an invariant cloth; the stripes are the cloth's stiffness map, so what moves and what is patterned are the same thing. Brain ridges, fingerprints, and the dorsal stripes of a zebra all share this structure: the pattern is not on the surface, the pattern is the surface arranged differently in different places. The cluster makes that arrangement legible by allowing the surface to wave.

Key artifacts: gallery_marker_soft-body presents the sb_rd02_stripes_flag configuration — Gray-Scott chemistry in the stripe regime coupled to a wind-blown flag — as the room's centerpiece.

Gap: A regime-switch artifact (a small dial or button cycling between spot, stripe, and labyrinth Gray-Scott parameters) on this same flag geometry would let learners watch the cloth's mechanical signature change live as the chemistry's parameter neighborhood is shifted, making the chemistry-mechanics coupling perceptible as a single continuous transformation.
