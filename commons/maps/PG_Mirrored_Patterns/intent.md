Concept: Mirrored cellular automata create kaleidoscopic textures, rhizomatic mazes branch without hierarchy. Symmetry as a generative operator — reflection transforms local cellular rules into global ornament, while rhizomatic growth produces labyrinthine structure with no center and no edge. Pattern without author, maze without solution.
Sequence role: Seventh and final map in the Procedural Generation sequence (12th spine sequence, lambda_edge phase). The capstone that combines cellular automata (from the 9th spine sequence), symmetry operations, and non-hierarchical growth into a synthesis of procedural pattern-making. Intermediate difficulty — the learner sees that procedural generation's toolkit extends beyond the biological and architectural metaphors of earlier maps into pure pattern and topology. Connects backward through the entire Procedural Generation arc and laterally to the Cellular Automata sequence where the base CA rules were established. Closes the lambda_edge phase; follows PG_Sculpted_Forms.
Technical angle: The mirror_cellular_texture_for_3d artifact runs a 2D cellular automaton and applies reflection symmetry (bilateral, quadrilateral, or radial) to produce kaleidoscopic textures suitable for 3D surface mapping. The mirroring operation multiplies the automaton's complexity without additional rules — symmetry is a free amplifier. The rhizomatic_maze_space artifact generates mazes through a non-hierarchical branching algorithm — unlike tree-based mazes, rhizomatic mazes have multiple entry points, loops, and no privileged root, producing Deleuze-and-Guattari-style rhizomatic topology.
Critical angle: Mirror symmetry reveals that the perceived complexity of a pattern is partly a property of the observer's symmetry expectations — a simple CA mirrored four ways looks vastly more intricate than the raw automaton. The rhizomatic maze challenges the hierarchical branching that dominated the L-Systems sequence: not all structure needs a root, a trunk, a direction. The lambda_edge phase closes with its fullest expression — rules and randomness produce not just worlds but world-types, from the crystalline order of kaleidoscopes to the anarchic connectivity of rhizomes.
Key artifacts: mirror_cellular_texture_for_3d generates kaleidoscopic textures through symmetry-amplified cellular automata for 3D surface application; rhizomatic_maze_space produces non-hierarchical labyrinthine spaces with multiple entry points and loop topology.
Gap: Needs a symmetry-group selector that lets the learner apply different wallpaper group symmetries (p1, p2m, p4m, p6m) to the same CA output, making the relationship between symmetry group and perceived pattern complexity experimentally accessible.
