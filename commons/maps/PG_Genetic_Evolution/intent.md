Concept: Creatures evolve through selection pressure. Genetic programming shapes 3D forms not by design but by fitness — populations of candidate solutions breed, mutate, and compete across generations. The world doesn't need an author; evolution is the author.
Sequence role: First map in the Procedural Generation sequence (12th spine sequence, lambda_edge phase). Opens the post-grammar generative arc by introducing evolution as an alternative to rule-writing — where L-Systems required explicit production rules, genetic programming discovers structure through selection. Advanced difficulty — the learner must integrate concepts from multiple prior sequences (randomness, iteration, fitness landscapes from search/pathfinding). Connects forward to PG_Space_Colonization where growth algorithms replace evolutionary ones, and backward to LSystems_Living where grammar-driven ecosystems first hinted that worlds can self-organize.
Technical angle: Genetic programming represents candidate 3D forms as expression trees (programs) rather than fixed-length genomes. Selection pressure evaluates fitness against criteria (structural stability, aesthetic metrics, behavioral goals). Crossover recombines subtrees from parent programs, mutation randomly alters nodes, and tournament selection drives convergence. The population evolves over generations toward forms that no designer explicitly specified — structure emerges from the search process itself.
Critical angle: Genetic programming makes the lambda_edge truth operational: a world doesn't need an author, rules and randomness are enough. But "enough" conceals a deep philosophical tension — the fitness function is authored, and it encodes values. Evolution is directionless only if selection pressure is; in practice, the designer's hand moves from form to fitness. The map invites the learner to ask: who is the author when the algorithm designs?
Key artifacts: GeneticProgramming implements the full evolutionary pipeline — population initialization, fitness evaluation, selection, crossover, mutation, and generational replacement — producing evolved 3D forms.
Gap: Needs a fitness-landscape visualizer that maps the population's position in genotype space against fitness values, making the search dynamics (convergence, diversity loss, local optima traps) visible across generations.
