# Marching Cubes — Critical

## The Threshold Problem

Marching Cubes requires a decision: what value separates inside from outside? This threshold is arbitrary, chosen by the operator, imposed on the field.

The surface that emerges is not inherent to the data — it is a projection of our question onto the data. Ask a different question (different threshold), get a different body.

Medical imaging makes this visceral: where does the tumor end? The threshold draws the boundary. The boundary determines treatment. A number becomes a knife.

## Discretization Violence

The algorithm marches through a grid. Continuous density becomes sampled points. The infinite field becomes 256 cases.

This is lossy compression of reality. Curves become faceted. Smooth gradients become stair-steps. The lookup table cannot produce what it doesn't contain.

Every marching cube is a choice to see the world in right angles.

## The Body in the Machine

Marching Cubes was invented for medical visualization — to extract bodies from CT scans. The algorithm literally constructs the patient as a surface.

But which surface? The skin? The bone? The boundary between healthy and diseased tissue? Each isovalue produces a different body. The patient is not discovered but constructed.

**What body does the threshold want to see?**

## Inside/Outside Binary

Each corner is classified: inside or outside. This binary admits no ambiguity. A corner cannot be partially inside. The liminal is rounded to one side or the other.

The algorithm cannot represent the gradual, the in-between, the both/and. It produces hard surfaces in a world of soft gradients.

## Emergence at the Edge

And yet — there is something beautiful here.

The surface emerges from the field. It is not drawn but discovered. The marching reveals what was always implicit. This is **morphogenesis**: form arising from conditions.

The edge of chaos is not metaphorical in Marching Cubes. It is geometric. The surface exists exactly where the field transitions, where stability gives way to change.

In QFEP terms: the isovalue IS λ. Adjust it, and you move the edge. The algorithm walks the phase boundary.

## Queer Potential

What if we rejected the binary classification? What if corners could be weighted, partial, uncertain?

What if the threshold varied across space — different questions in different regions?

What if we marched cubes through fields that contradicted themselves — density and anti-density superposed?

The algorithm assumes consistency. Queering it would mean embracing the impossible surfaces that exist between valid cases.
