An L-shape faces the wrong way. The grid agent spins it — 90° at a time — until it locks into the target orientation. Same structure, same cells, same mass. Nothing added, nothing removed. Only the relationship to the axes has changed.

Copy preserved identity across space. Translate preserved identity across position. Rotate preserves identity across orientation. Three operations, one question: what stays the same when everything moves? The shape doesn't know which way it faces. Facing is imposed from outside — by a coordinate system, by an observer, by a goal pattern that says *this way, not that way*.

Orientation is not intrinsic. It is relational. The turner learns that structure is invariant under transformation — and that "correct" is just one rotation among many.
