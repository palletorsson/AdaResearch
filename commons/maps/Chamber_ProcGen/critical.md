# Destruction as material — bricolage, the unrebuildable creature, and the politics of ongoing composition

Chamber_ProcGen stages a creature that cannot be defeated in the ordinary combat sense. The bricoleur_golem's body is an assembly of nearby fragments. Every strike knocks pieces off; every knocked-off piece becomes material for the next version of the body. Destruction is the golem's input, not its endpoint.

Claude Lévi-Strauss introduced bricolage as a mode of thought that works with what is at hand rather than with purpose-made tools. The bricoleur uses whatever the environment provides; the engineer designs tools for specific tasks. Lévi-Strauss's opposition is not a hierarchy but a typology. Some tasks suit the bricoleur; others suit the engineer; both are legitimate modes of assembly. The chamber operationalises bricolage. The golem is the bricoleur. The learner's strikes produce the at-hand material. The assembly is ongoing.

The chamber is small and cluttered. Loose geometry lies scattered across the floor: blocks, rods, broken pieces of older artifacts. The golem moves through the clutter, picks up fragments, and attaches them to its body. When the learner strikes, pieces fall off, and moments later the golem retrieves them and re-attaches them in a slightly different configuration. The golem cannot be destroyed in the ordinary sense; each strike produces a new body, and the body is composed of the same material rearranged.

The science screen on the wall labels this behaviour as bricolage. It shows the golem's current body as a small graph of connected parts. The graph mutates with each strike. Edges break; nodes detach; the golem retrieves the detached nodes and reattaches them with new edges. The graph is never the same twice, but its general topology — connected, assembled, walking — is preserved.

Lévi-Strauss's framing applies with a small extension. The golem is not merely reusing what is at hand; it is converting what the learner's strikes produce into what is at hand. The strikes are the generative event, and the bricoleur's response is an assembly from the strike's output. The learner's violence is converted into the golem's material, and the conversion happens without the golem expressing resistance or dismay.

A second panel on the wall tracks the learner's strikes as training signal. Which parts the learner targets shapes which parts the golem prioritises in the next rebuild. A learner who keeps striking the golem's legs produces a golem that rebuilds more legs; a learner who strikes the head produces one that rebuilds more heads. The golem's morphology is not fixed; it is a function of the learner's attention, and the learner's attention is the input the golem's assembly rule responds to.

The politics of bricolage-as-combat are in the inversion of the combat economy. A combat chamber usually produces an equilibrium in which one side prevails. Chamber_ProcGen produces an equilibrium of ongoing re-composition. Neither side wins, and neither side loses; the golem continues to walk, and the learner continues to strike, and the clutter continues to be converted into golem. The equilibrium is stable because the loop has no termination condition built in.

The bricoleur_golem is the curriculum's argument about procedural generation condensed into a creature. The Procedural Generation sequence claims that structure can arise from rules rather than from designers, and the golem is this claim's social embodiment. No designer is assembling the golem. No finished form is aimed at. The assembly is the whole activity, and the activity continues as long as the learner is present to provide input.

Within the sequence, Chamber_ProcGen turns destruction into reconstruction. The chamber hands the learner back to the Lab with the procedural-generation posture intact: no finished form, only ongoing composition. The sequence's argument — that generative systems can be primary rather than supplementary — is carried forward as a body-level recognition that assembly is the ordinary case, and finish is an interruption of it rather than a culmination.
