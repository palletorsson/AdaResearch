Concept: Torque as rotational force — the cross product of lever arm and applied force, where distance from the pivot amplifies the twist. Every door handle, wrench, and spinning top demonstrates τ = r × F.
Sequence role: Twenty-first map in Forces; the rotational counterpart to linear force. After VectorThrowing grounded linear dynamics in bodily experience, this map does the same for rotation. The compact room focuses attention on the single concept. Connects back to VectorCrossProduct (map 3) where the cross product was introduced geometrically — now it has physical meaning; leads to Forces_Drone_Game.
Technical angle: τ = r × F (torque as cross product of position vector and force vector), moment arm as the perpendicular distance from force line to pivot, angular acceleration α = τ/I (rotational analog of F=ma), moment of inertia I as rotational mass. The compact room layout constrains the demonstration to a single rotation axis for clarity.
Critical angle: Torque reveals that where you push matters as much as how hard you push. The same force applied at different distances from a pivot produces different rotational effects. This is leverage — the oldest force multiplier, the principle behind every simple machine. In the cross product formulation, the sine of the angle between r and F determines effectiveness: pushing perpendicular to the lever arm is optimal, pushing along it produces zero torque.
Key artifacts: VectorTorque demonstrates the τ = r × F relationship with adjustable force application point and direction; dark_sphere anchors the rotational space.
Gap: A torque_lever_demo where the learner applies force at varying distances from a pivot, with the resulting angular acceleration displayed as a speedometer-style readout, would make the leverage principle kinesthetic.
