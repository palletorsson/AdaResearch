# What should this body keep?

Which kind of stability are you asking this body to preserve?

Use the specimen’s own LAMBDA and PHI sliders. Hold one steady while changing the other, and compare the body’s shape and continuing movement. Watch for long enough to separate a persistent response from the timed breathing already present in the specimen.

Choose PAIR B and watch the pressure reading while it says PULSE. Press PULSE / STEADY. The number settles at its baseline. Has the body finished moving? Stay with one part of it for a moment. You have stopped the imposed breathing; the specimen remains subject to its other forces. Steady pressure is something you have given it. Stillness would be something else.

LAMBDA requests changes to stiffness and pressure. PHI requests changes to damping and drag. But the route does not end at the script. In the engine used here, drag is unused; changing the stiffness property on a running body does not rebuild its existing constraints. Pressure and damping do update. The intended mapping, the number on the plate and the mechanical response are three things we have to check against one another.

Resisting a deformation is still different from reducing ongoing motion. A body can resist changing shape while retaining movement. This apparatus cannot demonstrate every distinction its original labels promised. That limit belongs in our account of it, alongside what it does make available.

Watch a change settle over time rather than comparing only two instantaneous shapes. A shape can look similar at two moments while the continuing movement differs. Conversely, a temporarily different shape may return towards a familiar arrangement. The separate controls invite you to name these distinctions: resistance to deformation, persistence of motion, and the effects of pressure. Following one region of the specimen through several moments can make the comparison easier than watching every moving surface at once.

The mapping is authored for this specimen. Requested stiffness decreases across the lambda range, pressure has a designed peak near the middle, and signed phi changes damping while its drag request goes unused. Breathing runs only while LAMBDA sits between 0.3 and 0.7, and within an amplitude the maker selected; outside that band the plate says STEADY whatever the button says. A property readback alone cannot establish that a running constraint changed. Nor does the numerical scale establish that nature uses the same one.

Compare two combinations outside the presets: lambda 0.2 with phi +0.8, then lambda 0.8 with phi -0.8. Both give the same steady pressure baseline, but damping differs. The running body's built stiffness stays in place. Which difference do you notice first? Avoid reducing both observations to one word such as rigid or fluid. Naming what actually changed gives you a more useful account than naming what the interface intended to change.

The name above the glass stays. Inside, the terms of holding together have changed. The jar supplies another set of terms: a bottom, sides, a lid. Its clear surface lets you watch while its boundaries limit where the specimen can go. The sliders do not move those boundaries.

The laboratory has now used its terms in several ways: arithmetic weights on a console, spatial analogies in a hall, and controls mapped to a body’s mechanics. Their shared vocabulary invites comparison, but the mappings need to remain explicit. Even PHI’s signed range here differs from its range on the introductory console.

A responsive specimen is not an assay of queerness or a test for life. The floor still promises an imago and a return transformed; the working copy asks us to distinguish requested settings from supported effects. Its KEEP button preserves settings for this run, not a recoverable body. The question the specimen's name opens is what forms of continuity and variation a model makes available. A future observation could preserve a trace of deformation over time, letting different responses be compared beyond their immediately attractive appearance.

Self-Q turns the investigation back towards the maker. After giving this body a name, a container and a range of responses, what have we trained ourselves to recognise as possible? Which part of the apparatus would we change next?

Post-crisis begins with representation itself. After learning to name a model’s choices, ask whose distinctions those choices make clear and whose they compress.
