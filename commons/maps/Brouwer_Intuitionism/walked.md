## The walk

The critical room belongs to the dissenter who refused the rescue. The excluded_middle_demo puts the classical law on trial: *either P or not-P* — Brouwer's answer is that you may not say it until you can *build* one side. The constructive_proof shows what proof means under that discipline: not a victory over contradiction but an object in the hand. The constructive_staircase only has the steps someone has actually made; the choice_sequence grows a number decision by decision, its future genuinely open because nobody has chosen it yet.

## The turn (critical)

Intuitionism is the chapter's sharpest politics: Brouwer accused mathematics of colonizing the infinite — speaking about completed totalities no mind had constructed. His mathematics is smaller, slower, and honest about its labor; existence claims cost a construction, truth has a *tense*. The critique lands past mathematics: any system that asserts more than it can build — a proof, a map, a curriculum — is spending credit. This project, which builds its claims as rooms you walk, has quietly been on his side all along.
