# Additive Synthesis

A sine wave is a single frequency. Pure, featureless, the auditory equivalent of a point. Additive synthesis starts there and asks: what if we stack more? Every complex sound — voice, violin, thunder — is a sum of simple oscillations. Fourier proved it. Helmholtz heard it. The organ builder always knew.

Each oscillator contributes one partial. Frequency, amplitude, phase. Three numbers per voice, and voices pile up. Twelve partials and you have a bell. Thirty and you have a choir. The timbral spectrum is a construction site — harmonic scaffolding erected sine by sine.

The trick is that order produces richness. Not noise, not randomness — precise ratios of frequency. Integer multiples sound harmonic. Non-integer multiples sound metallic. The difference between music and texture is arithmetic.
