# Subtractive Synthesis

Start with everything. A sawtooth wave contains every harmonic — a bright, buzzing totality. Subtractive synthesis begins there, then cuts. A low-pass filter sweeps down, removing frequencies above a threshold. The sound darkens. Resonance peaks at the cutoff point — the filter feeding back into itself, emphasizing the exact boundary where removal happens.

Envelope controls shape the filter over time. Attack opens it. Decay closes it. Every pluck, every bass hit, every pad is a story of controlled loss. The Minimoog, the SH-101, the TB-303 — instruments defined not by what they generate but by what they withhold.

Additive synthesis builds from nothing. Subtractive starts full and sculpts. The material is always already there. Identity emerges through what gets removed.
