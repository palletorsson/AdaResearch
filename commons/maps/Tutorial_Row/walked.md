## The cast

column_3_z rig · xyz_coordinates marker · score cube · teleporter

## The walk

A corridor: seven columns wide, nine rows deep, but only one lane runs forward — the rest is buffer, context, the neighbors you can see and cannot enter. You walk the row, read the `xyz_coordinates` marker at the far end, and the map makes its single quiet substitution land in your legs: **walking forward is incrementing an index.** Position is just a number that goes up. The `column_3_z` rig teaches traversal by *forbidding* every direction but one, so that the first data structure you inhabit is the simplest one that has order at all — a line where each element has a before and an after.

## The turn (critical)

Between the scalar (Tutorial_Single) and the grid (next), the row is where **sequence** is born, and sequence is a heavier idea than it looks. A point had no order; a row does — elements gain predecessors and successors, and position starts to *encode* something (this comes after that) rather than merely locate. The buffer columns you can see but not walk are the map's sharpest touch: they make legible that a one-dimensional structure is a choice to privilege one axis and hold everything else fixed as mere context. To index is already to decide which difference is the difference that counts — and the corridor lets you feel that decision as the walls of a lane you did not draw.
