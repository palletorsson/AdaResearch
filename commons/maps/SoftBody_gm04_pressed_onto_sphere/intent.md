Concept: A sheet-like soft body falls under gravity onto a solid external sphere placed in the world below. The sphere acts as an exclusion volume — the body cannot pass through it. The body drapes over the top of the sphere, flattening against the upper hemisphere where it lands, and hangs off the edges where unsupported vertices fall away. The final shape is a body wrapping an external form, the topological inverse of the cavity-bound configurations in gm01-gm03. The map is a gallery study; the player walks around the single soft-body marker.

Sequence role: Fourth and final glass-meets-mould study (gm04) in the imported soft-body gallery cluster within the Soft Bodies sequence (13th spine, integration phase). Closes the gm-family by inverting its topology. gm01-gm03 are body-in-cavity configurations: the body inflates against an enclosing wall. gm04 is body-over-obstacle: the body drapes around a solid form. Pair-contrast with gm03: gm03 documents the negotiation between body-curvature and cavity-edges from the inside; gm04 documents the same negotiation from the outside. The pair together completes the inside/outside topological inversion. Cross-family contrast with sb01-sb04 (the cloth-drape cluster): those drape cloth from pins onto nothing, falling freely; gm04 drapes a body onto something, replacing the pin with an obstruction.

Technical angle: Cloth-like or sheet-like soft body — N×N mass points connected by structural springs along grid edges, shear springs along diagonals, and bending springs across two-step neighbors to maintain local flatness. Sphere collision: each vertex tests its distance from the sphere center; if less than the sphere radius, the vertex is projected outward to the surface of the sphere along the surface normal, and the radial velocity component is zeroed (or reflected with damping). Gravity applied to every mass. Verlet or PBD integration for ~200 steps. Without pins (or with only a starting position above the sphere), the body falls freely until contact begins; from that point on, contact constraints propagate outward across the surface as more vertices reach the sphere. Vertices that overshoot the sphere's silhouette continue to fall — these form the drape that hangs below the equator.

Critical angle: The gm-family closes with the topological inversion because the inversion makes the family's deeper claim explicit. The claim is not "moulds shape glass." The claim is "geometric constraints other than the body's own physics shape the body." The cavity (gm01-gm03) and the obstruction (gm04) are both external geometries that the body must respect; the body's own physics (pressure for inflation, springs for cohesion, gravity for direction) is the same in all four cases. Whether the external geometry encloses or interrupts is a topological choice, not a physical one. Either way, the vessel that emerges is the body's record of the encounter. Industrial pottery, glass-slumping, vacuum-forming, draping in fashion — all share the gm-pattern: a flexible material conformed against a rigid form, whether the form is a cavity (inside) or a master (outside).

Key artifacts: gallery_marker_soft-body holds the gm04_pressed_onto_sphere configuration. The body draping over the external sphere is the artifact and the demonstration of obstacle-as-form.

Gap: A swap-cavity-and-master toggle on the gallery marker would let learners flip the same body between gm01's cylinder cavity and a cylinder master — same physics, inverted topology, recognizably related vessels. The gallery would gain a new axis: inside/outside as a parameter of the cluster.
