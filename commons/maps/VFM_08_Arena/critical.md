# A candidate is not an identity

The crab compares the nominated visitor and its own food by distance and sight. Those categories can produce the same turn without becoming the same thing. The selection contract makes a relation actionable; it does not exhaust either participant. VISITOR lets the reader enter that contract and feel the difference between being present in a room and being selected by its controller.

The scoped lesson makes some choices particularly stark: two candidates, no sight memory, no bite. These are declared experimental conditions, not a discovery that the underlying creature was harmless all along. Other configurations remain elsewhere in the hall.

Completed meals accumulate into a programmed threshold. Five changes the permitted actions without changing the body's form. Calling the resulting stillness friendship would add an interpretation the implementation cannot verify. Such interpretations can be material for artistic research precisely when the selection rule, retained counter and state transition remain inspectable beside them.

The collision carts impose a different contract. Their total momentum and energy follow from an isolated elastic one-dimensional model, while the crab receives commanded position changes. Neither image should borrow the other's warrant. The room lets controller, memory and mechanical constraint become separate things we can alter and compare.
