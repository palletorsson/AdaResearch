Concept: Projectile motion as embodied vector release — the hand's velocity at release becomes the initial velocity vector, and gravity writes the parabolic arc that follows.
Sequence role: Twentieth map in Forces; returns to beginner difficulty after the advanced Forces_8. The tall chamber emphasizes vertical displacement and hang time. Throwing is the most physically intuitive force interaction — everyone has thrown a ball. This map grounds the abstract dynamics of Forces_1–8 in bodily experience; leads to VectorTorque.
Technical angle: Release velocity as the initial condition vector, ballistic trajectory from constant gravitational acceleration, arc shape determined entirely by release speed and angle. The tall chamber makes vertical trajectories visible — throw straight up and watch the object decelerate, pause, and return. VectorThrowing artifact handles the release-velocity capture and trajectory computation.
Critical angle: Throwing is the moment where the learner's body becomes the force applicator. The hand imparts an impulse (force × time), the release creates initial conditions, and physics takes over. The learner does not compute the parabola — they throw, and the parabola happens. This is the experiential proof of everything the sequence has taught: forces produce trajectories, not the other way around.
Key artifacts: VectorThrowing captures release velocity and computes the ballistic trajectory; dark_sphere serves as the throwable test mass.
Gap: A release_angle_analyzer showing how the same throw speed at different angles produces different ranges and heights — the classic projectile optimization problem — would connect the embodied throw to the mathematical analysis.
