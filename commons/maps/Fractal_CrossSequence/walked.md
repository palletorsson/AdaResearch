## The walk

The chapter ends where it began — a recursive tree — but the tree has learned two new things. The `fractal_stochastic_tree` is the map from the second map returned with every knob exposed: angle, length ratio, branching probability, depth, all with variation built in, so it is at once perfectly rule-bound and never the same twice. Around it the room reaches into neighbouring sequences. The `cellular_automata_3d_stacked` runs a 1D automaton and stacks each generation as a layer, so time becomes a third axis — and the stack of Rule 90's history *is* the Sierpiński triangle, the same fractal you deleted your way to now emerging from a grid of on/off cells with no geometry at all. The `evolutionary_algorithms` station searches a fitness landscape, and those landscapes are themselves fractal — self-similar valleys and ridges an adaptive process climbs. `Hilbert3D` and the space-filling gallery close the loop back to dimension: a one-dimensional line that visits every point of a volume, D racing up to 3. Walk the room as a set of doors: geometry, computation, evolution, all opening onto the same principle.

## The turn (critical)

The close makes the chapter's largest move — from *fractals are a topic* to **fractals are what complex systems look like at the edge**, wherever you find them. The stochastic tree is the QFEP in one object: quantum (discrete branching events), fractal (self-similar at every scale), emergent (global form from purely local rules), and poised exactly on the seam between the deterministic grammar and the random perturbation. But the deeper turn is the cellular automaton, because it dissolves the last special-ness of fractal geometry: Rule 90 was never trying to be Sierpiński. A row of cells following a three-neighbour update rule, stacked in time, simply *is* the triangle — which means the fractal was not a fact about shapes at all but a fact about computation, appearing identically in geometry, in a grid of bits, and in a fitness landscape a genetic algorithm is blindly climbing. And Rule 110, one of these toys, is Turing-complete — a full computer hiding in a line of cells — so the chapter's uncomputable thread ties off here too: the edge of chaos is not only where the richest structure lives, it is where *computation itself* lives, the exact poised regime in which a system is complex enough to compute anything and not so wild that it computes nothing. The map's circular shape is the argument. It opened with one recursive rule and returns to one recursive rule, and everything between — deletion, coastline, sponge, spiral, complex iteration, the infinite dive — was that same rule met at different edges. The fractal was never the monster in the collection. It was the shape the world takes wherever order and chaos hold each other in tension long enough to make something, which is to say: nearly everywhere, which is to say: here, and you can walk back through any of these doors and begin again.
