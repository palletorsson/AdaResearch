## The cast

four agent stations · the mediation station · coverage display · wiki_fragment

## The walk

Four reasoners, one question. Each agent station runs a different logic — classical first-order, paraconsistent, probabilistic, constraint-solving — and each displays, as standard equipment, a specification of what it *cannot* prove. Walk the stations and read those limit-plaques first; they are the tutorial's real content. Then the central mediation station collects the four outputs and does the unusual thing: it records the disagreements instead of resolving them, and reports the set of claims the commons can support. The coverage display beside it shows the claims no single system could reach alone — the commons' surplus, made visible as a list. The `wiki_fragment` grounds the abstraction in the most successful working example: an entry that is not a fact but a *ledger of disagreements, continuously rewritten*, edit history as epistemology. No single station in this map carries the thesis. Walking between them carries it — which is the map teaching its lesson through your route: the knowledge here lives in the transit, not at any stop.

## The turn (critical)

Gödel's theorem was about *single* formal systems; he said nothing about what happens when you put several in a room and let them talk. This map is the sequence's quiet exploitation of that silence: each system is incomplete on its own, but each system's outside can be another system's inside — and so the commons covers more than any member, while remaining incomplete in a different, more *tractable* way. The turn hinges on one substitution: these infrastructures do not try to be **consistent**; they try to be **corrigible**. A wiki can hold a bad entry because another node will challenge it; peer review is not truth-detection but a distributed-cognition protocol with known failure modes. Consistency is a property you prove once and defend forever; corrigibility is a property you practice — and only one of them survives contact with the actual world. This is also the sequence's answer to its own romanticism, and the feminist counter to the pure line of flight: escape from broken systems is a solitary gesture, but an archive is a *shared* one — the commons does not flee incompleteness, it pools it, so the margin between any two entries becomes generative rather than fatal. Gödel's shadow does not disappear here. It gets redistributed — held in common, where it can finally be worked on rather than merely suffered.
