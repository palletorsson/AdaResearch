# What the next step cannot read

The red bead seems to hesitate. Let that reading stay long enough to become a question. Which part of this implementation would have to consult its past for hesitation to mean something in its rule?

The step generator never reads the stored trail. That is a specific absence of a connection. The program still keeps a position, a pseudorandom generator state, a mode and timing state. Calling it memoryless without naming the level would conceal the very machinery that makes the walk replayable. The counterfactual is precise: delete the trail in a controlled test, keep the generator and movement state, and the later positions agree.

This is one place where “what bodies are possible?” can land. The current bead cannot avoid its trail, respond to another bead or choose a goal. Those would require additional relations. Their absence need not make the existing tangle uninteresting. A pattern can invite attachment before the program possesses the capability we read into it. The experiment lets that desire suggest an implementation without treating the present simulation as evidence that it already has one.

## A record with several omissions

The trace retains only two hundred positions. It records each position before updating the bead, so its newest point is already one step behind. At step 300, the release and first ninety-nine subsequent positions have left the stored line. The current location cannot recover that order by itself. The named seed, the unchanged stepping procedure and a known count can reconstruct it here; that possibility depends on retained machinery beyond the visible bead.

Reflection introduces another omission. The line joins accepted endpoints without drawing the wall contact that would connect an attempted overshoot to its reflected continuation. The shorter chord is accurate about the samples it joins and incomplete as an account of the proposed movement. Increasing the trail length would not recover that missing point. It would retain more samples of the same representation.

Under 3D, projection can join stretches that are separate in depth. Moving around the case can reveal the separation. It cannot restore discarded temporal samples or wall contacts never stored. Different kinds of absence require different investigations; “look closer” is not a universal remedy.

## The boundary is active

The tank changes endpoints. A turn near its edge need not express a preference for return: the rule folds an overshoot back. This connects to the landscape question without pretending that all enclosures have the same effect. These walls reflect; another system might absorb, wrap, refuse or release a body. Each choice changes what paths are possible.

Glass is one staging decision. An open walk could instead be shown through a moving camera or another scale; visibility does not logically require confinement. Here confinement is part of the experiment and must remain in any account of the observed spreading. The cabinet's five-body MSD is bounded and can decrease. It is not a measurement of trail complexity, thermodynamic entropy or the quality of exploration.

## What an observation changes

ONE dims four walkers while they continue consuming draws and moving. ALL makes them bright again. The view selects a subject without removing the population. The observer has changed what is easy to follow. In this configuration that visual change does not change the trajectories.

The two clocks expose another selection. A stalled frame supplies time that the five-step cap will not convert into movement. The implementation keeps the clock's advance and discards some possible simulation updates. The discrepancy is a designed computational limit, not evidence that the visitor has stopped time. RESET begins another account while the surrounding museum runs on.

The research question is therefore not a universal equation between randomness and freedom. It is which relations a rule permits, which records make them inspectable, and which losses occur when they are drawn and paced for a visitor. A longer line, an extra coordinate, a responsive neighbour or a different boundary would each make another body possible. The terrarium gives us a procedure against which those changes could be tested.
