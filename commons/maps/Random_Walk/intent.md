Concept: The random walk — each step chosen independently with no memory of the path — is the fundamental model of stochastic processes. No destination, no plan, only the next step. From Brownian motion to stock prices to diffusion, the random walk is how randomness moves through space and time.
Sequence role: Sixth map in Randomness sequence. Follows Random_Rotate_Random_XYZ (randomness in orientation) and introduces randomness as temporal process — a sequence of decisions unfolding in time rather than a static configuration. Precedes Random_Gaussian, where accumulated random steps produce the bell curve. This is the pivot from random states to random processes; the map where entropy acquires a narrative.
Technical angle: Step-by-step position update: position += random_direction * step_size, where random_direction is chosen uniformly from available directions (4-connected, 8-connected, or continuous angle). random_walk_128 likely implements a 128-step walk for visualization. random_walk_leash constrains the walk within a boundary (reflecting or absorbing barriers). random_walk_terrarium may show multiple walkers in a bounded ecosystem. pixel_cloud visualizes the spatial distribution of walk endpoints. GDScript implementation using Vector3 accumulation per frame or per timer tick.
Critical angle: The random walk is the mathematical formalization of purposelessness — motion without intention. Einstein's 1905 paper on Brownian motion proved that the jittering of pollen grains was evidence of molecular reality. The walk's expected displacement grows as the square root of steps, not linearly — meaning randomness is slower than purpose but never stationary. The random_walk_collection may show that many walks together form a distribution, foreshadowing the Central Limit Theorem in map 7. Philosophically: the walk is free but not unconstrained — each step is independent, but the path is always continuous.
Key artifacts: random_walk_128 is the primary walk visualization; random_walk_collection shows population-level walk behavior; random_walk_leash introduces boundaries and constraint; random_walk_terrarium provides an ecological framing for bounded walks; pixel_cloud maps walk endpoints into spatial density; dark_sphere anchors.
Gap: No artifact overlays the theoretical sqrt(n) displacement curve onto observed walk distances — this would connect the visual experience to the mathematical prediction and prepare the ground for Gaussian statistics in map 7.
