# A display can end before its question does

A later ring can look round enough from where we stand. The judgment is real, but the phrase from here carries part of it. Approach the ring and another edge may become available. Move away and the edge can recede without leaving the mesh.

The ladder holds twenty finite objects. It can let us encounter a direction of refinement; it cannot make us walk through infinitely many subdivisions. The difference matters because the walk has a body, a duration and an exit. The mathematical sequence can remain available after we leave.

## Which direction counts as progress?

Select a ring, then reverse the progression. The same mesh resolutions occupy the same sequence of positions in the opposite order. The beginning now offers what the earlier arrangement reserved for later.

The geometry did not establish that increasing smoothness must be the goal. The ordering presented that possibility as a path. Reversing it makes the choice available to inspection: perhaps the faceted opening is the one whose silhouette we want to keep. Perhaps a smoother ring better serves another encounter. Each purpose gives refinement a different stopping rule.

This is an authored change in emphasis, not a proof that all orders are equivalent. A route still has consequences for what can be compared, what can be reached and what a visitor encounters first. The selected gold ring directs attention too. It helps us find a member while making the others less prominent.

## The number names its object

The side display reads a torus's subdivisions and mesh triangle count. It also calculates the perimeter of a separate unit-circle polygon with the selected ring count as its number of sides. Those calculations sit together because they can help us think together. They are labelled separately because they do not measure the same object.

The polygon's perimeter gap decreases with refinement. It does not follow that a whole portal has measured its distance from perfection. A tolerance is useful when we say which quantity it constrains and what the constraint is for.

The previous hall asked what a triangle count included. Here we ask what the displayed error belongs to. A number can be exact about a chosen model while leaving the choice of that model unresolved.

## The runners have targets and bodies

Advance the two markers one stage at a time. The intervals shrink. At stage ten, Achilles still has about 5.86 millimetres to the limit. His visible body is larger than that gap. The marks can overlap before their anchor positions agree.

Play the same stages automatically. Easing curves now give the figures a journey between targets. Pause partway through and the current gap differs from the gap calculated for the stage endpoint. There is another rule between a sequence and its appearance, and the instrument lets us hold that difference long enough to read it.

Neither visual overlap nor a completed display cycle establishes equality with the mathematical limit. Nor does an infinite subdivision establish that ordinary motion is impossible. The finite performance, the convergent sequence and a physical account of movement need their own terms.

The room continues while this little instrument pauses. We have made time for one question by leaving other processes unattended. The final stage now waits for us to restart it. That waiting is a design decision about attention, not a discovery that the sequence has no next term.

## A button is not an eighty-seven-metre wall

Adding the controls exposed a practical assumption in the museum. Its footprint checker treated any collider inside an artifact as evidence about the artifact's whole extent. Small button shapes could therefore cause the long portal ladder to be classified as an obstacle.

The correction distinguishes a marked local instrument from the subject's footprint. The buttons keep their local interactions; a real subject collider still counts. This does not certify the whole corridor as accessible. It prevents one small interaction mechanism from silently deciding the status of the entire passage.

That distinction belongs to the method of this research. We make something available to inspect, watch what its implementation changes elsewhere, then revise the account. An aid to access can introduce an obstruction if we fail to examine the rules which receive it.

## Choosing an ending

One ending is to find a refinement sufficient for a chosen view. Another is to return to a coarse opening and make its angles the reason to stay. Both can be deliberate; neither exhausts the family.

We leave a finite display with a question still capable of continuing. Melencolia gathers tools which can produce determinate results, then asks for a purpose those results cannot select on their own.
