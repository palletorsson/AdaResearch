Concept: A crossing puzzle where the bridge is a vector. The room is split by a void — a near floor band, an empty gap, a far floor band — and the only way across is the force_field_zone, a cube of space in which a single chosen vector becomes the one rule of physics. Aim the field across the gap and it carries you (and the loose force_cubes) over; aim it wrong and you fall. The map makes a vector something you stand inside rather than something you read off a console.

Sequence role: A field/application map in the Forces sequence, sitting after the vector foundations and operations and alongside the field maps (VFM_04_Fields, VectorApplied). The learner already knows that a vector is direction plus magnitude; here that abstraction is given stakes — get the direction right and you cross, get it wrong and the void takes you. It rehearses the sequence truth that "acceleration is the only thing you feel" by making felt acceleration the literal mechanism of travel.

Technical angle: The force_field_zone (class ForceFieldZone, group "force_field") is a 4 m cube that, while you are inside it, sets gravity_scale to 0 and adds its field vector as acceleration each frame — for rigid bodies linear_velocity += field·dt, and for the player_body velocity += (field − its own gravity)·dt — so the chosen vector is the only force in the cube. The vector_machine finds the zone via the group and calls set_field_vector(), giving the walker a control surface for the bridge. Three force_cubes act as visible test masses that obey the same field, making the invisible vector legible before you trust your own body to it. Dimensions are 14×18 with the void carved as height-0 cells through the centre rows.

Critical angle: This is the sequence's argument made spatial — a force is not a label on an arrow but a region of space with a rule, and changing the rule changes where you can go. The void is the honest part: an abstraction that usually has no cost is given a cost, so a wrong vector is not a wrong answer but a fall. The risk is that a single mis-aim becomes punishing rather than instructive; the force_cubes mitigate this by letting the learner read the field off thrown masses before committing their own crossing, and the near-shore vector_machine keeps the decision reversible until they step in.

Key artifacts: force_field_zone is the bridge itself — a vector made into a 4 m place where it is the only force; vector_machine is the near-shore control that aims the field via set_field_vector(); the three force_cube test masses make the invisible field visible by obeying it first, so the walker can see the crossing before they take it.

Gap: The crossing reads as a puzzle but offers no on-floor instruction — a single entry sign ("the field is the bridge; aim it across") would turn trial-and-error into a taught move. A failure readout (where the field is pointing when a cube or the player falls) would convert each fall into feedback rather than a silent reset, and a faint marker on the far ledge would name the target the vector is meant to reach.
