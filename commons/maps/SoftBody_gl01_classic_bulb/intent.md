Concept: A spherical soft body pinned at a small top ring is allowed to settle under gravity plus moderate internal pressure for 200 timesteps. The result is a bulb-shaped vessel whose final geometry is determined entirely by the pressure-gravity-stiffness triad over time. The map is a gallery study presenting one vessel from the glass-blowing series.

Sequence role: First glass-blowing study (gl01) opening the glass cluster within Soft Bodies (13th spine, integration phase). Follows the jelly grids (sb05–sb08) and L-system / CA / reaction-diffusion DNA bridges, and precedes deeper variations: long bottle (gl02), wide bowl (gl03), lopsided vase (gl04), collapsed sag (gl05). The cluster argues that vessel typology can be parameterized as a small set of physics knobs rather than a sculpting gesture.

Technical angle: Spring-mass sphere mesh with a fixed pin ring at the top vertices, internal pressure modeled as a force normal to each face proportional to enclosed volume (gas-law analogue), gravity as a uniform downward acceleration, and settlement counted in fixed timestep iterations. Stable integration via small-step Verlet. The vessel shape is the steady state where pressure outward balances gravity downward inside the elastic envelope.

Critical angle: A blown-glass bulb is usually framed as a craft object indexed to the maker's skill. The simulation reframes it as a fixed point of three parameters — pin topology, pressure magnitude, gravitational duration. Skill, in this reading, is the human ability to hold those parameters approximately stable for the seconds the glass remains molten. The artifact reveals that vessel form is process-determined rather than shape-determined: not a thing carved but a record of forces.
