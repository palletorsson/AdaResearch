A sphere pinned at one small ring on top. Gravity pulls everything that isn't pinned. Two hundred timesteps of moderate internal pressure pushing outward, gravity pulling down, springs trying to hold the sphere's memory. What settles is a bulb — a glass shape made entirely of these three forces in slow argument.

The vessel is a record. Of how much pressure the simulation set. Of how strong gravity was. Of how long it ran before it stopped. Change any one number and a different glass is born. Same algorithm, different ancestor.

This is glass-blowing by physics simulation. A real glassblower does the same thing — pressure from the lungs, gravity from below, the cooling glass holding shape one moment at a time. The bulb is what happens when you remember a sphere while falling.
