# Random Remove — Summary

Random_Remove is the second map in the Randomness sequence. After Random_Definition established what randomness is, this map shows what randomness does to existing structure. The grid begins fully populated — maximum order — and the learner watches random selection subtract from it, one cell at a time or in larger patterns.

The space is a 12×12 arena with an 8×8 interior region where cubes are stacked in a tidy population. A central artifact, the remove_random rig, exposes four deletion modes: by column, by row, by range from a centre point, or uniformly over the whole grid. Activating any mode runs the selection and deletes the chosen cells, so the learner can watch the grid thin itself out according to the mode they selected.

A second rig demonstrates the consequence of removal as gameplay. Random gaps in the floor become a navigable risk field; the learner must cross the grid while more cells are removed in real time, and a side panel logs each deletion with its mode, its selected index, and the time at which it occurred.

The sequence of deletions is one-directional. Nothing in the map can re-populate a cell once it has been emptied, so the map stages irreversibility directly. Within the sequence, Random_Remove is the chapter on entropy as erasure. Random_Noise_Types will next generalise to spectral identity, and later maps will show randomness as generative rather than destructive. This one argues that the grid after random removal is more informative about the removal process than the intact grid was about its construction.
