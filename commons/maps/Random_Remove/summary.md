# Random_Remove — Summary

Who was eligible to disappear? A board of sixty-four cubes stands on a pedestal in the middle of the hall, numbered along two edges, with a tilted panel of buttons and two lines of text above its far edge. On arrival sixteen cubes are amber — the four-by-four block from column 2 to 5 and row 2 to 5 — and forty-eight are grey. The line says which set, names the run's seed, and counts: eligible 16, remaining 16, removed 0.

REMOVE ONE turns one amber cube red for a moment and takes it; its slot plate stays, and the line names the column and row. Sixteen presses empty the square in an order you could not know toward a shape you could. RESET puts all sixty-four back and replays the same seed's order, so two histories can be compared; NEW SEED starts another. ROW and COLUMN offer eight candidates each along one axis; ALL offers every cube. The same seed over two sets shows the same draws landing on different cubes.

The bench keeps three decisions apart that the word "random" folds together: the rule that admits candidates (one match over the cube's own board coordinates), the even draw among what remains (`randi_range` from a private generator), and the deletion (a transform scaled to nothing). Only the middle one is chance, and it never sees a grey cube.

In the sequence, this room takes the repeatable arrangement of Random_Definition and the rearranged sample of Random_Entropy and hands Random_Walk a set that changes as chance acts inside it. The hall's other bodies — a hazards demonstration and a dark sphere — stand a metre off as company.
