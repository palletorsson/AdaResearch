Concept: Randomness as subtraction — removing elements from a grid by stochastic selection (range, column, row, or all-at-once) reveals that randomness is not only generative but destructive, and that deletion patterns expose the distribution that produced them.
Sequence role: Second map in Randomness sequence. Follows Random_Definition, which established what randomness is; this map shows what randomness does to existing structure. Precedes Randomness_10_PRINT_Algorithm, where randomness becomes generative. The remove_random artifact reappears in map 3, linking destruction here to construction there.
Technical angle: Array manipulation via random index selection — randi_range() for picking removal targets, Array.remove_at() and queue_free() for deletion. Filtering by column (x-coordinate match), row (z-coordinate match), range (distance threshold), or unfiltered (uniform over all). The hazards_demo likely demonstrates that random removal can create dangerous gaps — the grid becomes a navigable risk field.
Critical angle: Entropy as erasure. The grid begins fully populated — maximum order — and random removal increases entropy by destroying regularity. This is the thermodynamic arrow made spatial: you cannot un-remove. The learner witnesses irreversibility for the first time in the sequence. Philosophically, removal reveals structure: a grid with random holes is more informative about the removal process than the intact grid was about its construction.
Key artifacts: remove_random is the core mechanism — parameterized deletion with mode selection (range/column/row/all); hazards_demo extends removal into gameplay consequence, showing that random gaps create navigable risk; dark_sphere anchors the map.
Gap: No visualization of the removal distribution — a heatmap or histogram showing which positions were removed across multiple runs would connect deletion patterns back to the uniform distribution defined in map 1.
