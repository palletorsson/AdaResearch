# PG: Genetic Algorithms

Darwin's engine, compressed into code. A population of candidate solutions. Each one scored against a fitness function — how well does it solve the problem? The best survive. The rest die. Survivors recombine: crossover splices two parents into offspring, mutation flips random bits. Repeat. Generation after generation, the population climbs the fitness landscape toward peaks it cannot see.

Selection pressure is the knife. Too sharp, it converges too fast — trapped on local optima, blind to better solutions elsewhere. Too dull, it wanders forever. Mutation rate is the tremor that keeps the search honest. Crossover is the bet that recombination produces something neither parent could reach alone.

No designer. No gradient. No plan. Just differential survival and recombination — and from that, structure emerges. Evolution doesn't solve problems. It dissolves the distinction between solver and solution.
