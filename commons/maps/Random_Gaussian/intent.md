Concept: The Gaussian distribution — the bell curve — emerges not from a single random event but from the accumulation of many. The Central Limit Theorem proves that sums of independent random variables converge to the normal distribution regardless of their individual distributions. Order from chaos through aggregation.
Sequence role: Seventh map in Randomness sequence, the conceptual center. Follows Random_Walk, where accumulated random steps implied a distribution; this map names and formalizes that distribution. Precedes Random_Mushrooms, where Gaussian-distributed growth produces organic form. The galton_board is the canonical physical demonstration — balls bouncing through pegs arrive at a bell curve, making the theorem visible without equations.
Technical angle: Gaussian random number generation in GDScript — Box-Muller transform (two uniform randoms to one Gaussian), or summing multiple uniform randoms as CLT approximation. Parameters: mean (mu) and standard deviation (sigma). The distribution_sampler likely provides interactive parameter control. GaussianBlurShader applies the distribution to image processing — convolution with a Gaussian kernel. GaussianPaintSplatter uses Gaussian-distributed offsets for procedural art. random_bell_curve and gaussian_random visualize the PDF directly.
Critical angle: The Central Limit Theorem is one of mathematics' most profound results — it explains why the bell curve appears everywhere from measurement error to human height to thermal noise. The Gaussian is the attractor of aggregation: add enough independent contributions and the shape is inevitable. This is the sequence's philosophical hinge: randomness at the individual level produces predictability at the population level. Entropy and order are not opposites but partners across scale. The Galton board makes this visceral — each ball's path is unpredictable, but the collective shape is utterly reliable.
Key artifacts: galton_board is the definitive physical model — individual chaos producing collective order; distribution_sampler provides interactive parameter exploration; GaussianBlurShader applies the distribution to image processing, showing the bell curve as spatial filter; GaussianPaintSplatter uses Gaussian randomness for procedural mark-making; random_bell_curve and gaussian_random visualize the distribution directly; dark_sphere anchors.
Gap: No artifact lets the learner vary the number of aggregated samples and watch the distribution converge from uniform to Gaussian — an animated CLT demonstration with a sample-count slider would make convergence tangible.
