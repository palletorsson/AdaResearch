The reference images ask for a room that can resemble a bodily interior, a polished machine or an unfamiliar mineral without settling into one identity. The pleasure belongs partly to that unresolved appearance. The shader can make a hard, fixed boundary seem viscous; the floor returns its firmness to the feet. This difference is available to investigate rather than explain away.

An aperture has more than one use. It can admit someone, frame a view, expose a neighbouring interior or allow someone to remain partly concealed. Making the aperture larger is not automatically more generous: a shelter can lose its privacy. The question is what a particular arrangement permits and whom it permits to remain.

Keep form and material available to compare. A screenshot alone cannot establish access; a collision test cannot establish pleasure, comfort or the association a visitor brings. Let the actual encounter earn the prose.
