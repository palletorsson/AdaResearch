# Cut a colour space

From outside, the first room is still a block. The opening interrupts it. Inside, a rounded hollow meets another, then another. Their edges overlap above you. A line has passed through this volume and left more than a mark: it has left somewhere to go.

Spheres placed along a path cut the passage. Each reaches into its neighbours. The shared floor keeps the walk level while the hollows rise around it. Two higher cuts let the route acquire a ceiling that the feet do not have to follow.

The surface catches pink and blue. A turn of the head changes the colour near an edge. What looked soft from the entrance stays firm underfoot. The skin suggests one body; contact gives another account.

On the entrance plinth, change PEARL to AMBER, then CHROME. The room keeps its openings. A different finish catches the same curved boundary. CHALK quietens the sheen and makes another reading possible. None of these surfaces is the room before appearance. Each gives the eye something else to work with.

## Only where they meet

The second chamber begins with two overlapping spheres. This time their intersection is the cutter: only the region common to both is taken from the block. The overlap becomes a room. Cylindrical cuts let the path reach it from either end; a further cut opens the ceiling.

The wall turns towards a seam overhead. Two volumes have supplied one boundary, but each still helps determine where it bends. The operation does not negotiate their difference. We have chosen their positions, their sizes, and the part of their meeting we want to inhabit.

## A shell with neighbours

Two rounded outer bodies join in the third construction. Smaller volumes hollow them out. Further cuts perforate the shell. Some openings carry the walk; others let you see through the wall while remaining inside.

Dark metal and fine pink lines can make the enclosure look mechanical. Pearl brings out another resemblance. The construction has not become alive, yet its appearance can trouble the distance between a room and a body. We can stay with that uncertainty long enough to make something from it.

The DRIFT control advances the surface pattern slowly. Stop it and the phase holds. Nothing in the wall changes its position; the passage remains available. The first encounter is already a small separation of powers: the rule that makes a surface appear to move need not be the rule that moves the boundary around us.

These rooms offer a place to begin developing that relation. A hole may carry a body, a glance or light. What we cut away determines more than the outline of what remains.
