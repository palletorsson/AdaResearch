Concept: Newton's three laws made tangible — inertia as the default state, F=ma as the mechanism of change, and action-reaction as the symmetry of interaction. Bouncing balls with different masses show how the same force produces different accelerations.
Sequence role: Twelfth map in Forces and first of the Forces sub-sequence; the moment vectors become physical. After 11 maps of vector math, this map applies F=ma. The beginner difficulty is deliberate — the physics is new even though the math is familiar. Connects directly from VectorForces (map 7) which introduced force as concept; now force obeys laws; follows VectorWorkbench; leads to Forces_2.
Technical angle: F=ma as the governing equation, gravity as constant downward force vector (0, -9.8, 0), mass as scalar resistance to acceleration, momentum (p=mv) determining impact behavior. Bouncing with restitution coefficient for energy loss. The map's artifacts are still vector-framework (VectorBasics, basis_vectors_rig) — the physics layer sits on top of the vector foundation.
Critical angle: Newton's laws are the transition from kinematics (describing motion) to dynamics (explaining motion). Inertia is philosophically radical: objects don't need a reason to keep moving, they need a reason to stop. F=ma is the reason. The sequence enters the oscillation phase here — QFEP dynamics, where states evolve under forces.
Key artifacts: VectorBasics and basis_vectors_rig carry over from the vector sub-sequence, reinforcing that forces are vectors; dark_sphere serves as the test mass receiving forces.
