## The walk

All three operators laid out on one bench. The csg_compose_workbench holds union, intersection, and difference side by side and lets you chain them — combine two solids, subtract a third, keep the overlap of the result — watching complex form assemble from the same three verbs applied in sequence.

## What it fixes

The workbench is where the chapter reveals its grammar: solids have an *algebra*. Constructive solid geometry is the claim that any shape, however intricate, is a sentence in three words — join, share, remove — and that you can say a mechanical part, a building, a gear, by composing primitives lawfully enough. The power is combinatorial: three operators over a handful of primitives spell an unbounded vocabulary of form, and the order of operations matters the way it does in arithmetic, difference-then-union saying something union-then-difference cannot. The room hands you the whole language at once and lets you feel the leverage — that mastery here is not modeling every surface by hand but knowing which three-word sentence produces the shape you meant.
