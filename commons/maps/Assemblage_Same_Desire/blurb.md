The room where the whole thread ties. You enter past the loom — an array with rules, the first computer, still weaving — and a 10 PRINT bolt running one coin-flip inside its symmetry machine. The world's pattern makers stand in the middle distance: Kente, ikat, zellige, the meander, thirteen traditions as living shaders with their makers named. At the heart, the origin: Kristina Torsson's patterns, the landscape this whole work began in. And at the far end, facing the cloth across the room, Godel's sentence and the balance that cannot weigh itself.

They are not opposites. The Kente weaver composing a named proverb in strips and the logician composing a sentence that outruns its own system are running the same desire: to reach, by rule, past what the rule can hold. Midway through the spine is where that should be said — after you have learned the rules, before you learn their limits.
