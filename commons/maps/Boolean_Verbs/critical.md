# Critical register · The Three Verbs

Boolean language looks neutral because it is short. `OR`, `AND`, `NOT`: three words that appear to settle relation. In a boundary representation they settle it by refusing an interior. A solid is a skin plus a predicate about which side of that skin a point occupies. The verbs redraw the skin, and the body experiences the redraw as a room, a shared patch, or a missing world.

Union is the generous verb, but its generosity is abrupt: there is no interval between two and one. Intersection is the discipline of agreement, including the possibility that agreement is empty. Difference is asymmetric and therefore political in the smallest computational sense: `A − B` and `B − A` are different bodies, yet the compact grammar gives the order no visual emphasis.

The hall also exposes a measuring bias. A still camera can see the outside of a cavity more easily than the cavity can be inhabited. A clean boolean result may therefore score well as an image while failing as a place. The sills keep the three outcomes in sight so the claim is comparative and embodied rather than a single canonical view.

This is the queer opening of the chapter: the set is declared closed, but the lived possibilities are not. The enum has no word for partly, for becoming, or for a surface that belongs to two accounts at once. Those remainders return in the gallery and in the heavy architectural hall.
