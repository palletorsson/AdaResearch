# Coordinates — implementation reference

Point_Coordinates was separated from Point_One on 29 September 2026. Its layout develops the old wider room; artifacts.md records the current placed cells. Source cells are not museum world coordinates.

The first artifact is `point_held` at (7,2). `point_condition.gd` builds its label and instantiates the production `interactive_point_origin.tscn`, an XR Tools pickable. Its black sphere and spherical collider both have a 7 cm radius, half the previous radius; the earlier white points keep theirs. The black sphere keeps `coordinate_id = point_one_main`; this legacy identifier intentionally remains stable across the split.

`CoordinateSystem3M` is at (4,12), with `adopt_external_point:1`, `external_point_id:point_one_main` and `floating_point:0`. WORLD at (2,9) and LOCAL at (2,11) use the same `readout_channel`. The Fontana recess contains a secondary force comparison; the two cards explicitly select the original held point. The frame's origin sign is suppressed; the actual Point Zero origin remains in the preceding arrival hall.

The L-shaped basin is the union of [4,7,1,6] and [4,12,6,1]: eleven cells, depth one metre, glass collision at deck height, no fire. The coordinate artifact's +1 metre placement offset compensates for the basin floor. Do not raise its frame above the museum floor.

The hall opts into opaque, shaded pink/grey/black axes and matching projection markers. Axis length is 3.4 local units (5.1 m of shaft at the unchanged display scale 1.5), with radius 0.08 local units (24 cm diameter in the hall). Enlarging the drawing does not rescale LOCAL coordinates. The arms keep their existing placement along the basin; the Z arm points towards negative Z and its end label says −Z. The colours identify X/Y/Z independently of sign. Solidity here means opaque geometry, not a new collision barrier across the floor. Other placements retain the default RGB palette and transparency.

The target is at (6,10). Its existing radius is 0.45 metres and it rearms after departure. The target accepts eligible point-group members; the cards select the named primary point. The manuscript distinguishes these functions without teaching all their implementation here.

The canonical tutorial now uses this existing museum point, frame and both cards. It reads the supplied XRToolsPickable relation, changes only the held branch's starting position, compares `pt.global_position` with the frame's `to_local(pt.global_position)`, and changes the card's formatting from two to three decimal places. The point's parent-local `position` is not the coordinate frame's LOCAL reading. The frame's root scale of 1.5 participates in the conversion; its `axis_length` changes the drawn extent without changing that scale. Card formatting does not round the stored position. The old two-marker desktop coordinate study remains a separate companion, not the main lesson. Point_Lines is next and owns distance. The critical manuscript and hall placements are unchanged.
