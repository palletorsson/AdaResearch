# Noise as shared condition — unpredictability on both sides of the boundary and the politics of mutual illegibility

Chamber_Random is the catalyst chamber for the Randomness sequence. It levels the encounter by making both bodies unpredictable. The learner's chaos catalyst draws each projectile's trajectory from a fresh PRNG seed. The octapod_crawler creature moves with noise-perturbed direction and speed. Neither side can read the other's next move, and the reading becomes the encounter's impossibility rather than its content.

Michel de Certeau's work on strategy and tactics distinguishes between the spatial practice of those who own a space — who can survey and plan — and the temporal practice of those who move through a space they do not own. The tactician cannot see all of the map at once; they respond to what presents itself, and the response is opportunistic rather than strategic. The chamber collapses this distinction by denying both sides the survey. Neither can see; both tactic.

The chamber is small and lit unevenly. The octapod paces the floor. The learner holds the chaos catalyst. Every shot's path is unpredictable even to the learner, so the aim is not a skill but a distribution. The creature has no reliable counter-strategy, because its pursuit logic depends on a legible target trajectory to intercept, and the learner's fire has none. In the other direction, the learner cannot avoid the octapod's movement by reading a pattern, because the octapod's movement is also noise-perturbed.

A science screen on the wall scatters the encounter across a position axis. Hit and miss distributions form clouds rather than lines. Neither side dominates on any single frame, but both converge toward a shared statistical footprint over time. The screen's reading is not a scoreboard; it is a joint distribution, and the joint distribution is what the encounter actually produces.

De Certeau's tactics-from-within reading lands on the catalyst's seed refresh. A fixed seed would make the learner's aim predictable to anyone who could observe the seed, and the observation would give the octapod an advantage. The fresh-each-frame seed denies this advantage. The learner's fire is as opaque to the learner as to the creature, and the opacity is what levels the encounter. Both are tactic because both are operating inside their own noise.

The politics of mutual illegibility are in the refusal of strategic advantage. Most combat encounters reward skill at reading the opponent; this chamber refuses to reward reading, because neither side produces the legible patterns that reading could exploit. The refusal is the chamber's closing gesture to the Randomness sequence: a body that has internalised randomness should be able to meet another randomised body without lapsing into strategy.

The chaos catalyst is the learner's side of the mutuality. The octapod_crawler is the creature's side. Neither is independently random; both are structurally random because the chamber's rules enforce randomness on both. The enforcement is the chamber's political intervention. A chamber that let one side be deterministic and the other stochastic would be a chamber about mastering randomness. This chamber is about inhabiting randomness as a shared condition.

Within the sequence, Chamber_Random hands the learner back to the Lab with the chaos catalyst in their kit and with the feel of an encounter in which no strategy wins. The sequence's thesis — that randomness is the ordinary case — lands as a practice: negotiating with another randomised body is what catalyst chambers can be when both sides give up the survey. The chamber's quiet argument is that shared illegibility is a relational mode rather than a deficit, and that relational modes are the point of catalyst encounters more broadly.
