## What this map holds (seed)

Translation left every direction the same. This map ends that. Rotation produces space as anisotropic — front differs from back, left from right — and the map stages the difference as a long floor you read station by station. `rotate_grid_cubes` opens with collective rotation: a lattice turning as one, relative positions kept while every facing changes. A `science_screen` runs its wave alongside — the 2D abstraction of what the room is doing in 3D.

Then the pair that carries the lesson: two `spin` objects, same form, same rate, offset 0° and 180° — synchronized and permanently opposed. `dark_sphere` sits beside them as the invariant reference, the one silhouette rotation cannot betray. The floor text says it outright: *Rotation produces space as anisotropic.* At the far end, `pick_up_cube` and a counting `pickup_gate` make you prove you can navigate a space where facing determines reach.

## Why it was built (seed)

Concept: Rotation makes direction matter — where translation treats space as isotropic, rotation introduces anisotropy. Facing, orientation, and the non-commutativity of composed operations become the central lessons.
Sequence role: Fourth map. Pivots from translation to rotation. After two maps proving displacement is axis-decomposable and order-independent, this map demonstrates that rotation is neither — the sequence shifts from additive to multiplicative logic. Follows Trans_Translate_2; leads to Trans_Rotation_2.
Critical angle: Non-commutativity as the first algebraic structure that defies translation-built intuition. Order matters; process is not reducible to outcome. Space acquires directional grain, and position stops being where you are and becomes which way you face.

## The walk

You arrive, and the room is already turning. `rotate_grid_cubes` holds the entry: a grid of cubes rotating together, none of them moving relative to each other, all of them changing what they face. The map hangs its signature over this piece — it is the thesis stated first. Beside it a `science_screen` runs a wave, the flat diagram of the round fact.

> **Dwell — arrive in transformed space · ~50s**
>
> The register names this map's hero, and it is not an artifact — it is what
> just happened to you. You arrived into a space already transformed: the
> grid was rotating before you had a facing to lose. Watch it work. Every
> cube keeps its position relative to its neighbours while the whole lattice
> reorients — the family moves to a new city, the relationships inside
> persist, the relations to the city change entirely. That is the chapter's

> the grid's unity. You did not sign that agreement; you arrived inside it,
> the way you arrive inside every coordinate convention this curriculum has

The floor runs long and south. `dark_sphere` first, dark and still, and then the pair: `spin` at 180° and `spin` at 0°, one step apart, turning at the same rate and never once agreeing about where front is. The floor text stands beside them — *Rotation produces space as anisotropic* — the map captioning itself at its own center.

> **Dwell — `spin` · ~55s**
>
> Two of them, same shape, same speed, offset by half a turn. When one faces
> you, the other faces away — phase opposition, the minimal difference that
> produces maximal opposition, and opposition only possible because they
> share an axis. Stay longer, though, and the ruling's edge shows. These
> rotations never stop. There is no target orientation, no slerp toward a
> goal, no hand adjusting the angle — rotation with no one at the gimbal.
> The grid at the entry turned under control; these turn because turning is
> all they are. Which makes them clocks: watch long enough and you can
> estimate time passed by how far they have come around. Rotation continued
> through time stops being orientation and becomes duration — the object is
> never in the same facing twice, so it never gets to *mean* any facing.
> That is the anti, and it is not speed. It is ownerlessness. An orientation
> someone chose is a declaration — this way and not that way. An orientation
> that merely cycles is weather. The pair teaches the difference by refusing
> to stop and let either facing count.

Then the long empty run — most of the map is this walk, direction made palpable by how much of it there is — down to the working end: `pick_up_cube` in your hand, the `pickup_gate` counting to seven, reach now depending on facing. The teleporter waits at the far edge, beside a single punched cell of void. You leave facing it.

## The turn (critical)

The claim under this map is that **anisotropy is the birth of valuation**. The moment rotation gives space a grain — a front and a back, a toward and an away — directions can be ranked, and they immediately are: facing the light over facing the dark, dexter over sinister, the Latin roots of handedness already loaded with moral charge. To rotate is to declare *this way and not that way*, and the map is honest that the declaration is never neutral.

And this is where the chapter's trans reading runs, and runs with dignity rather than as ornament. Reorientation changes everything about how a form is met — which face is offered, which is withheld, what the world decides it is seeing — while the invariants hold: same structure, same relations, same thing. The 0° and 180° pair are not two objects; they are one form at two facings, opposed only along the axis they share. Identity preserved under transformation is not an abstraction here — it is the exact grammar of a body that turns and is treated as if it had become something else. What changed was the world's angle on it.

The non-commutativity seals it: rotate X then Y is not Y then X. Order matters; history matters; you cannot undo the sequence of events, only compose onward. And the anti presses the last question — the spin pair turn forever with no one at the gimbal. The map's quiet demand is that your orientation not be weather. Someone should be at the gimbal of a facing, and it should be you.
