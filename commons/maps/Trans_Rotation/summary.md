# Trans_Rotation

The 51 by 60 map has two primary encounters.

1. **Centred blades.** Three 8.4 by 2.4 by 0.2 m panels rotate around their middle. Y connects level banks at ninety degrees. X and Z turn as paddles and pause at approximately 23.2 degrees, making climbs to grid landings three metres higher. All static landings are ordinary grid structure, with still routes around the trials.
2. **Increasing rotation in a 2D array.** Four 4 by 10 bands compare x, y, z and combined rotation. Forty cubes per band, 160 total. Fixed centres are spaced two metres apart; the angle increases by ten degrees per row from zero to ninety along +z. The array is static during the walk. Outlined cubes and row-angle labels keep the rule readable.

The blades hold a crossing pose for twelve seconds, then turn in the reversed direction through another half-turn over twelve seconds. The array compares spatial variation, without adding a moving timer to that argument.

The actual-grid capsule probe reaches the three-metre landings on X and Z, crosses Y at ground level, and crosses all three unrotated array controls. With the stronger gradient, the test walker stalls first in XYZ, then X, then Z; Y remains traversable along the tested path. Human VR timing and alternative paths remain to be examined.

The same two primary tokens remain in the book and necklace: `rotation_wall_crossings`, then `rotation_array_compare`. The previous prototype is preserved under `doc/space/rotation-studies-2026-09-09/centre-pivot-revision/before`.
