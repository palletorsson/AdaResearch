# Symmetry_Mirror_Rotor — walked

## The cast

pattern_tile_mirror · mill_persian_p4 · kaleidoscope

## The walk

Two new moves join translation. The `pattern_tile_mirror` doubles whatever you paint across a line — draw on one side, and the reflection appears, exact and reversed, the way a face meets itself in a mirror. The `mill_persian_p4` is the other move made into a machine: a kaleidoscope mill that takes one wedge and *turns* it, four times around a centre, so a single painted sliver becomes a fourfold rosette. Reach into the `kaleidoscope` on its stand and look — the oldest optical toy there is, and it has known reflection-and-rotation for two centuries. Paint a wedge, spin the rotor, and count the copies: two-fold, three-fold, four-fold, six-fold. Try to make a fivefold rosette that also tiles the plane, and you can't — the mill won't let you, the tiles won't close. That refusal is not a limit of the machine. It is a law.

## The turn (critical)

The map's turn is a rare thing in this curriculum: a place where symmetry *forbids* absolutely. There are only ever 2-, 3-, 4-, and 6-fold rotations in a repeating plane pattern — never 5, never 7 — because those are the only folds whose tiles pack the plane with no gap and no overlap. This is the **crystallographic restriction**, and the map lets you feel it with your hands before it is ever proven: try the forbidden fold and the pattern simply refuses to close. Sit with how strange and beautiful that is — symmetry gives freely, infinite surface from one wedge, and in the same breath rules out most of the folds you might want, not by taste but by geometry. It is the sequence's first taste of *closure as constraint*, the idea the group map will make central: the complete set of moves is not "anything you like" but a fixed, countable, non-negotiable list. Fivefold symmetry does exist — Penrose found it, aperiodically, decades later, and the curriculum keeps that artifact at depth — but it cannot *repeat*. The kaleidoscope, that child's toy, has been quietly obeying a theorem the whole time: some beauties tile the world, and some are forbidden to, and no amount of wanting moves the line.
