Concept: A pressurized soft-body sphere pinned along a small top ring (same configuration as gl01) settles under a non-axial gravity vector — gravity has both a vertical (down) and a horizontal (sideways) component. The vessel that emerges droops off the world's vertical axis, with the mouth tilting and the base settling to one side of the pin's projection. The map is a gallery study; the player walks around the single soft-body marker.

Sequence role: Fourth glass-vessel study (gl04) in the imported soft-body gallery cluster within the Soft Bodies sequence (13th spine, integration phase). Continues the gl-family parameter sweep with a new axis: gravity direction. gl01 small-ring pin → bulb, gl02 narrow neck → bottle, gl03 wide ring → bowl, gl04 non-axial gravity → lopsided vase, gl05 over-cooked low-stiffness → collapse. Pair-contrast with gl05: gl04 perturbs one parameter slightly (gravity direction) to get a recognizable variant; gl05 pushes another parameter past its limit (stiffness) to get a vessel breakdown. Together they extend the family from "parameter variation" to "parameter limits."

Technical angle: Spherical mesh of mass points with structural and bending springs (same as gl01). A small top-ring set of vertices is pinned to fixed world positions. Internal pressure applied per-face proportional to area and inflation. Gravity now applied as a 3D vector with non-zero x or z component in addition to the standard -y component — typical: g = (0.3, -1.0, 0) or similar tilt. Verlet or PBD integration for ~200 steps. Because the pin holds the top ring fixed but the rest of the body falls along the gravity direction, the lower portion of the sphere shifts laterally and downward relative to the pin, producing an asymmetric vessel whose central axis is no longer aligned with world-up.

Critical angle: The gl-family makes the integration-phase claim — form is what a parameter tuple leaves behind. gl01-gl03 made the claim using one parameter (pin radius). gl04 makes it using a different parameter (gravity direction) on the same body. The point is that the parameter space is multi-dimensional, and the vessel-family inhabits a region of that space rather than a line. A craft glassblower navigates the same multi-dimensional parameter space using physical levers — breath, gravity (positioning the piece), thermal gradient — that the simulation does not have access to. What the simulation has is direct control of the parameters. The vessels look the same because the parameter spaces overlap, not because the simulation knows anything about glass.

Key artifacts: gallery_marker_soft-body holds the gl04_lopsided_vase configuration. The off-axis drooped vessel is the artifact — the simulation's record of one specific (pin-radius, pressure, gravity-vector, timesteps) tuple where the gravity vector is non-vertical.

Gap: A gravity-direction control on the gallery marker would let learners walk the gravity vector around the sphere and watch the vessel's tilt axis trace out a corresponding cone. The same body, the same pressure, the same pin — different "down" — different vessel.
