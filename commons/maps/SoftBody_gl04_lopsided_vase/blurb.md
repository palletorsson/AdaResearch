Same sphere, same pressure, same pin ring as gl01. The single change is that gravity no longer points straight down. A sideways component is added — small, but enough to bias every settling step. As the sphere relaxes against the pin, every unpinned vertex falls a little to one side as well as down. After two hundred steps the vessel has drooped — its axis no longer lines up with the world's vertical. The mouth tilts. The base settles off-center.

It reads like a glassblower's first vase of the day, taken out a moment too early, slumped while still soft. A craftworker would call it a defect; a parametric study calls it a third dimension of the family. Pin radius gives you bulb-bottle-bowl. Gravity magnitude scales the whole vessel uniformly. Gravity direction — that's what gl04 adds — controls axis tilt.

The body is the same equilibrium under a slightly different field. The vessel that emerges is what the body had to become given where down was pointing this run.
