# Random_Entropy — Summary

Which differences disappear when a sequence becomes one number? A Shannon entropy gauge hangs on a post at the corridor's west side, over a desk. The gauge draws two hundred symbols from ten, tallies them, and prints H = −Σ p log₂ p over the tallies against the ceiling log₂(10) = 3.32 bits. On the desk, all two hundred draws stand as coloured tiles in the order drawn, with a mark under the forty the panel's strip spells out.

Three buttons on the desk. SORT regroups the same tiles into ten blocks: the bars and the number do not move, and a plate prints the sorted copy's H beside the reading, equal, with the number of tiles that changed place. CONTRAST puts a second sample on the wall — the same ten symbols, the same two hundred draws, from a source that gives the first symbol half of them — and the number falls to about two bits with nothing but the shares changed. DISCLOSE steps the gauge's own disclosure ladder: the formula appears, then the source and its seed with a pale bar of the declared law behind each live bar, then the panel goes down to the number alone and climbs again, the reading the same at every rung.

The number keeps everything about the shares and nothing about the arrangement: not the order SORT destroyed, not a pattern between neighbours, not whether the row meant anything. Around the desk the room keeps its specimens — a jar in which order decays, a gradient from order to displacement, harvested and seeded sources side by side, butterflies and a dark sphere at the unpredictable end — as company, not as evidence.

In the sequence, this room takes a repeatable arrangement from Random_Definition and hands Random_Remove the thought that a rule decides what can be chosen before anything is chosen.
