## What this map holds (seed)

Scale me. The object speaks first — not "you may scale" but a request, almost a plea. Trans_Scale closes the Transformation sequence with the last degree of freedom: after position and orientation, magnitude. A long platform under a blue sky, one artifact that wants to be other than it is, and a line of forms that refuse to change so that change can be seen at all.

`scale_me` puts the transformation into your hands — spread to grow, pinch to shrink. The `prism_block` line is the reference set: geometry whose angles hold at every size, the proportion that stays while magnitude sheds off. The `chair_assembly_puzzle` makes scale a constraint to be solved — correct assembly requires matching magnitudes. And `dark_sphere` makes its final appearance: the sequence's persistent invariant, unchanged since the chapter's door.

## Why it was built (seed)

Concept: Scale as relational change — magnitude shifts while shape relations persist. Size is not intrinsic but assigned through comparison, making scale the transformation that most directly reveals invariance.
Sequence role: Sixth and final map of the Transformation sequence. After translation (position) and rotation (orientation), scale addresses magnitude — the last degree of freedom in the 4×4 transformation matrix. Closes the sequence by returning to the question posed at the door: what stays the same when everything changes? Scale's answer: proportion.
Critical angle: Scale is the transformation that most nakedly exposes the relational nature of measurement. There is no absolute size — only size relative to something else. The invitation "scale me" makes the learner the agent of transformation, collapsing the subject–object boundary.

## The walk

You come in at the platform's north lip and read the room lengthwise: a raised bench mid-platform, a lone object on the open floor beyond it, and — laid clean across the map like a ruler — a line of forms that will not move for you. The bench first. `chair_assembly_puzzle` waits in pieces beside the `clipboard` that holds the VR scale controls, and a `science_screen` runs its bars — the parametric face of what your hands are about to do. The map states its method before it lets you play: scale will be read *against* something, or it will not be read at all.

Then `scale_me`, alone on the floor, asking. You take it in both hands, spread them, and it grows; draw them together and it shrinks. Nothing else on the platform moves. That stillness is doing the work.

Past the line, `dark_sphere` hangs where it has hung all sequence — the invariant that followed you here, dark and exact. Behind you the chair still wants assembling, and it will only assemble at the right magnitudes: scale not as play now but as a constraint you must satisfy. And then the map does something no earlier map in the sequence did: it offers three `t` teleporters and does not say where they go. The playground has no gate and no required pickup. You can scale forever or scale once and leave. The sequence ends not with resolution but with divergence — take the gesture, take the reference frame, choose an exit you cannot see past.

## The turn (critical)

There is no absolute size. That is the flat, technical version of what this map stages, and it is already subversive: magnitude, which feels like the most objective of properties, turns out to exist only in comparison — assigned, not possessed. The invariant under scaling is proportion, and the map's antagonist is the form that holds it: the right triangle whose angles no gesture can touch. What stays the same when everything changes? The ratio. The relation. Never the size.

But the sharper turn is about the reference nobody names. The prisms are the declared measure; the undeclared one is you. Your body enters every comparison on this platform — the chair is chair-sized *for you*, the prisms knee-high *to you* — and the room silently calibrates all its magnitudes against a standing human it never mentions. The register keeps a seat open here for the Modulor thread — the body pressed into service as a standard ruler — and the room already implies it: the body is the unit, and when the world is scaled around a body that cannot scale itself, the unit is what the transformation violates. You may transform; you may not be transformed. The map hands you the transformer's seat, and the turn asks who built the seat, who is denied it, and what it does to a body to measure everything while remaining unmeasured.

The next map revokes the seat. In Trans_Pit the operations act on you without asking — and the chapter finds out what its truth is worth when the body is inside the transformation instead of outside it.
