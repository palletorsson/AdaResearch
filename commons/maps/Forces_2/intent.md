Concept: Gravity and falling — parabolic trajectories emerge from constant downward acceleration, mass scales momentum but not fall rate, and bouncing introduces energy loss through restitution coefficients.
Sequence role: Thirteenth map in Forces; deepens Forces_1's introduction of F=ma into the specific case of gravitational free fall. The parabolic arc is the first emergent shape produced by forces — not drawn but computed from initial velocity plus gravity each frame. Connects forward to friction (Forces_3) and drag (Forces_5).
Technical angle: Projectile motion: position += velocity * dt, velocity.y += gravity * dt. Parabolic trajectory as the natural shape of ballistic motion. Mass-scaled momentum (heavier objects hit harder despite falling at the same rate). Bounce mechanics: velocity.y *= -restitution on ground contact, energy loss per bounce.
Critical angle: Galileo's insight: all objects fall at the same rate regardless of mass (in vacuum). This is counterintuitive and profound — mass resists acceleration (inertia) but also attracts gravity, and these two effects cancel exactly. The parabola is not designed; it is the inevitable consequence of constant acceleration on an initial velocity vector. Emergence from simple rules.
Key artifacts: VectorBasics and basis_vectors_rig provide coordinate context; dark_sphere as falling test body.
Gap: A trajectory_tracer that draws the parabolic arc as an object falls, with velocity and acceleration vectors visible at each point along the path, would make the emergence of the parabola from frame-by-frame integration visible.
