# Where does again spend itself?

The opening hall makes three budgets available for comparison: the number of operations we request, the geometry they leave behind, and the distinctions a visitor can actually perceive. These are related without being interchangeable. Seven stored squares remain seven when COLLAPSE makes them coincide. Eighty-five rings and eighty-five cells are equal populations produced by different operations. A twelve-level drill can invest most of its attention in details the visitor can barely distinguish.

That difference matters to the book's question, *what bodies are possible?* A stopping condition is part of a body's production. The square's one-centimetre minimum and seven-level desk limit are authored constraints. They are not universal boundaries of recursion, perception, or a particular headset. Removing them would change the workload; it would not automatically produce a richer encounter.

Selection is equally consequential. DRILL repeatedly chooses a corner of the newest litter. SPREAD chooses from the shallowest cells. LOTS uses a seeded draw from the extant population. Each distributes the same twelve-step allowance differently. A budget does not decide its own allocation. This is a concrete place to ask what an apparently neutral implementation gives attention to, and what it leaves comparatively undifferentiated.

The chair exposes a more explicit prior. Sixteen of twenty-seven cells receive roles associated with legs, seat and back. Eleven fail that role test. GHOST makes the exclusions available again, but the completed chair also requires translation, deformation and new parts. The artifact's name does not establish a recursive implementation: its present code executes five authored stages. Nor does a cube contain a uniquely discoverable chair. It supports a construction whose choices could have been otherwise.

The queer possibility here is an invitation to investigate those choices. A leg could be kept where the recipe removes a cell; a seat could have a different orientation; an apparently wasted operation could become the principal encounter. Those alternatives are questions for further construction, not implemented modes of this desk. Keeping the actual controls distinct from possible extensions lets the text remain hospitable to discovery without pretending the experiment is already done.

There is also a limit to the room's language. Object count is not an entropy measurement. More overlap is not automatically more topological complexity. These finite displays show geometry, allocation, visibility and authored roles. Later halls can test connectivity and bodily passage directly. Here we learn to notice when an image, an implementation and an explanation have begun to say different things.
