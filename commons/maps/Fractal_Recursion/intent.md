Concept: The gateway to infinite complexity from finite rules — recursion as the engine that turns a single instruction into unbounded structure. A simple command (divide, repeat) applied to cubes, chairs, tables, and circles reveals that self-similarity is not ornament but mechanism.
Sequence role: First map in the Fractals sequence (10th spine sequence, lambda_edge phase). Opens the fractal arc at beginner level, establishing recursion as the generative principle before any formal fractal geometry appears. The cube_staircase and recursive_chair ground abstraction in familiar objects; fibonacci_pagoda hints at the golden ratio thread that will surface in Fractals_7. Connects forward to Fractals_2 where recursion takes organic form as trees.
Technical angle: Recursive function calls with base case and depth parameter, subdivision as the canonical recursive operation (divide cube into 8 sub-cubes, repeat). The fibonacci_pagoda introduces the Fibonacci recurrence F(n)=F(n-1)+F(n-2) as a recursive structure distinct from geometric subdivision. Boolean operations on recursive geometry (recursive_boolean_cube) demonstrate CSG as a recursive algebra.
Critical angle: Recursion is the lambda_edge thesis made concrete: finite rules produce infinite complexity because the output of a rule becomes its own input. The recursive chair and table are uncanny — familiar objects made alien by self-reference. This is the first encounter with the idea that complexity is not designed but generated, that a five-line function can produce structures no human could draw by hand.
Key artifacts: cube_staircase and cube_subdivision demonstrate depth-controlled recursion on cubes; example_8_3_recursion_circles_vr shows radial recursive packing; fibonacci_pagoda bridges recursion to Fibonacci; fractal_recursion_2 is the generic recursive template; recursive_boolean_cube, recursive_chair, recursive_table apply recursion to familiar objects; dark_sphere anchors the research space.
Gap: Needs a side-by-side depth comparator — an artifact that renders the same recursive rule at depth 1 through 6, making the exponential growth of complexity visible as a discrete progression rather than a sudden reveal.
