Infinity lives in finite rules. A simple instruction — divide, repeat — becomes unbounded complexity. Here, recursion reveals its paradox: order generates chaos, the whole contains copies of itself, and dimension refuses to be an integer.

The `cube_staircase` and `cube_subdivision` demonstrate depth-controlled recursion: one rule (subdivide into eight sub-cubes), varying depth, exponential visual complexity. `fibonacci_pagoda` bridges geometric subdivision to the Fibonacci recurrence. `recursive_chair`, `recursive_table`, `recursive_boolean_cube` apply the same principle to familiar objects, and the result is uncanny — chairs made alien by self-reference. This is the first encounter with the lambda-edge thesis: finite rules produce infinite complexity because the output of a rule becomes its own input. Complexity is not designed. It is generated by a five-line function that recurses.
