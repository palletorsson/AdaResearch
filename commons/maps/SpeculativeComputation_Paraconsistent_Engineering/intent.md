Concept: Paraconsistent logic staged as engineering practice — a database that holds both P and not-P without collapsing into triviality, and a live inference stage that continues to return useful answers on the non-conflicting parts of the knowledge base.
Sequence role: Third map in the Post-Foundations Crisis sequence. After Applied Ethics turned the diagnosis of bias into method, this map turns contradiction from a fatal system condition into an engineered affordance. Follows CriticalAlgorithms_Applied_Ethics; leads to SpeculativeComputation_Situated_Computation.
Technical angle: A spherical rig modelled on Florensky's pedagogical sphere holds a small dataset with deliberate contradictions. A classical inference engine crashes under the contradictions; a paraconsistent engine — four-valued logic with true, false, both, neither — continues to produce defensible answers. A small production pipeline next to it demonstrates the same mechanism on realistic sensor data that disagrees across channels.
Critical angle: Classical systems must be kept pristine because a single contradiction can be used to prove anything. Paraconsistent systems are built for the messy world. The map argues that contradiction is a first-class load to carry rather than a state to avoid, and that the engineering discipline of annotating and containing contradictions is as important as the logical discipline of deriving theorems.
Key artifacts: the Florensky sphere database carries the contradictory knowledge base; the paraconsistent inference engine carries the live reasoning; the production-pipeline station demonstrates the same principle on live sensor data.
