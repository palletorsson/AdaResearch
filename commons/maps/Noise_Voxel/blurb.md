Three-dimensional noise carved into blocks. Where the function exceeds threshold: solid. Where it falls below: void. Caves, overhangs, floating islands — topologies impossible with heightmaps alone. The voxel grid quantizes continuous noise into habitable space.

The `perlin_terrain_sculptor` lets you manipulate the threshold live: slide the cutoff and the cave system reshapes, the islands float higher, the overhangs thicken. `voxelnoise` shows the raw grid at visible block-scale, exposing the sampling lattice beneath the terrain. Thresholding is the act of decision — continuous ambiguity collapsed into binary certainty. Every voxel world is a political boundary: where does solid end and void begin? The threshold is arbitrary; the resulting topology feels inevitable. Minecraft's aesthetic is not incidental — it is the most culturally legible demonstration that noise generates worlds.
