## The cast

maximum matching · Edmonds' blossom algorithm · odd-cycle contraction

## The walk

Given a graph, find the largest set of edges where no vertex is used twice — every node paired at most once. In bipartite graphs it is straightforward. In general graphs, **odd cycles break everything**: a ring of three, five, seven nodes always leaves one out, and the matching cannot close. Edmonds' blossom algorithm solves it by refusing the problem's terms — when it meets an odd cycle it *contracts the whole structure into a single vertex*, collapses the impossibility, matches at the higher level, then expands the blossom back. You watch pairing happen across a field of nodes, and watch the algorithm handle the exact places where naive pairing fails.

## The turn (critical)

The sequence closes by lifting from physics into abstraction, and the title tells you why: **topology, entropy, morphogenesis** — structure emerging from disorder through self-organization. Maximum matching is morphogenesis in its barest form: from a field of all possible pairings, select a maximal consistent subset — *form emerging from constraint, not design*, the same claim the jelly cube and the leopard made, now stripped to pure combinatorics. And blossom contraction is the map's quiet philosophical jewel: odd cycles are local impossibilities with no clean answer at their own level, so the algorithm *resolves them by context* — contract, solve above, expand back down. That is exactly how biological development handles a local contradiction, and exactly how the softbody obstacles negotiated their collisions: not by forcing a resolution where none exists, but by changing the level at which the question is asked. The sequence that began by squeezing a cube ends by naming its own method — structure is what a system finds when it refuses to solve an impossibility on the impossibility's terms.
