# Museum: The Spine Court

A night at the museum where the collection is the game's own heroes.

The typology is borrowed from the courts where placement became an art: you enter high, on a terrace, with the whole collection below you — then descend into it. A two-story arcade wraps the room; an iron-and-glass grid roofs it; orange trees keep the rhythm and benches keep the pauses. The light comes from above, so the collection casts the shadows.

The placement follows the museum grammar. One monumental anchor holds the far axis — the Incompleteness Scale, the room's Temple of Dendur, the thing every sightline ends on. A flanking pair guards the stair head where you first look down. A greeter waits on the mid terrace. The perimeter keeps a steady rhythm of smaller pieces under the arcade — fractals north, oscillators south — and the center stays open, because in a museum the center is for you. Only the floor itself is art there: the Pompeii mosaic, two thousand years of placement grammar underfoot.

Walk it the way you walk the Cour Marly: down, in, around, and finally up close to the anchor. The room is also an argument — the same grid that holds every other map reads as architecture the moment rhythm and top light arrive.
