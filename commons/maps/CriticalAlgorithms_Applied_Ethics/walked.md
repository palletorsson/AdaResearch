## The cast

three case stations · calibration slider · re-weighting panel · godel_statement_plaque · the off-center path

## The walk

Three live cases, each one a classifier committing to a verdict: a loan approved or refused, a hiring shortlist made, a post moderated away. Each station shows the small feature set the model saw, the prediction it rendered — and, on the adjacent panel, what it did not see. That second panel is the map's tutorial invention: the *outside* of each classification displayed next to the classification, as routine instrumentation rather than as scandal. Then the intervention station hands you the working parts. Move the calibration slider and watch the threshold slide across real populations — every notch trades one group's false rejections against another's false approvals; there is no setting where the trade disappears. Re-weight a feature and read the downstream-consequence panel. The lesson is operational: fairness is not a property you verify at the end, it is a *dial with no neutral position*, and someone's hand is always on it. Note the room itself as you work — the walls carve an off-center path, a room designed after Gödel: you walk the outside of what the grid would have been.

## The turn (critical)

Ethics, here, is not a postprocessing step — not a filter bolted in front of a finished model — and the map's whole design enforces the point: it is **the shape of the room**, who gets to enter, where the walls run, whose presence the system was built to hold. That relocation is the post-crisis move in miniature. The previous map diagnosed; this one asks what practice looks like once the diagnosis is accepted, and its answer is deliberately unheroic: audit, calibration, refusal — annotated excerpts placed *against* live cases rather than recited as doctrine, because an ethics that floats free of its cases is just decoration. The slider is the honest object in the room. It refuses the fantasy that some clever setting makes classification innocent; it converts "the system has an outside" from theorem into felt trade-off, executed by your own hand, with the affected populations reading out in real time. And that is the difference between this chapter and a critique: the crisis said *every classifier is incomplete*; applied ethics says *you will classify anyway* — loans will be decided, queues will be ranked — so the responsible act is not to abolish the cut but to stay at the station, keep the outside visible on its panel, and never let the hand on the dial pretend it isn't there.
