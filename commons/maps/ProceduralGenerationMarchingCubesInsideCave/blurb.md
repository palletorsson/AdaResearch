# Marching Cubes: Inside Cave

Marching cubes carve volume from nothing. The algorithm reads a scalar field — density values at grid corners — and decides where surface exists. Above threshold: solid. Below: void. Between: interpolation. Triangles emerge from lookup tables, 256 possible configurations per cube. The cave assembles itself from rules about insideness.

A 12×12 grid. An 8×8 arena at its center. Dark spheres pulse with purple emission, slow-rotating sentinels marking where algorithms perform. The cave is the output — marching cubes turned inward, walls generated by the same process that could have made terrain. Landscape inverted into enclosure.

Every surface here is a decision boundary. Solid or empty, inside or outside — the algorithm draws the line, but the threshold is arbitrary. Move it and the cave breathes. The boundary between structure and void is never where you think it is.
