A square of cloth pinned at all four corners. Gravity does the rest. The middle sags into a soft dome — the belly of a tablecloth thrown over a table that isn't there. Between each pair of corners the edge scallops gently, a shallow catenary running side to side.

Each vertex is a mass; each neighbor is a spring. With four pins instead of two, the cloth has nowhere to rotate, no preferred axis. The downward pull is shared evenly. The shape that settles is the symmetric one — the same mathematical surface a soap film would find stretched across the same boundary, minus the surface tension. A minimum solution under a rectangular constraint.

Two corners gave a catenary curve. A top row gave a curtain. A side edge with wind gave a wave. Four corners give a dome. Same cloth, same physics — the boundary chooses which surface gets to exist.
