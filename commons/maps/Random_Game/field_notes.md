# Random_Game — field notes

## 2026-09-12 — R5: how can you plan when the next state is known but its timing is not (Fable, the next hall after Random_Mushrooms; Astra's card: "restore a small, clearly bounded crossing arena with the cycling cube as the actual support over a shallow recoverable gap; check old grid data before cutting the museum floor; give the player a reliable observation position, return path and bypass; keep enemies and projectiles out of the first timing experiment; compare the existing during-motion beacon with an optional genuine advance cue, sampling the wait once and counting down to that deadline"; Palle: "be bold here, this needs to be more like a Lara Croft adventure")

**The ruling.** The card and Palle's brief point the same way, and the room already held the pieces. `random_cycle_cube` (the map's `r_c`) was a one-metre tile that sinks out from under you on drawn timing, with a fixed four-state order, a collider it switches itself, and a mote lit while it moves. The map had a five-by-three pit of `0` cells in its west corner — a hole the museum lays no floor on — with two folding enemies and a spire standing in that void and the tile itself parked in another void row in the far south-east corner, sixteen cells from the pit it was written for. So the crossing was not built: it was scattered across the map. The bold reading is not a new mechanism but the arrangement the mechanism was always for — a tomb chasm: the hall's own hole, lined and given a bed; three of these tiles in a row across it; the order cut in stone on the near lip; the drawn waits live on a tablet beside it; a lit idol on the far lip with the crossing's five-digit seed cut into its face. What you carry out of a trap room is not the prism but the ability to run it again.

**The hole stays a hole.** Astra asked twice for the support to be verified rather than assumed, and the honest way was to keep the museum's void and let the artifact supply the three things a void needs before a body may be asked to cross it: a bed one metre down (the artifact's own slab, so the drop is 1.02 m whatever is or is not underneath), a ramp out of it, and cut sides. A ray down the pit in the probe finds `BedCollider` at 1.02 m and no museum tile — that is the check the old final could only say was missing. The pit's cells are asserted still `"0"` in the same run, so nobody can quietly floor them later and leave the claim standing.

**Both ends of the pit are closed, and that cost two runs.** The first build left the near and far walls out so the end stones could lap their lips; a standing eye then looked straight through both openings, over the bed, and out under the hall's own floor at the museum's starfield. The second build broke each end wall for the row's width — and the two gaps line up by construction, so the eye went through again. The walls now run the full width with their tops four centimetres under the floor: the stones lap them with clearance, and the probe carries a ray from the standing eye down the row that must meet the crossing and not the sky.

**The staging takes its own height, and the row stands proud.** The map-authored lane places a body's origin ON the deck, and the token's own `y` offset is not read there (measured: the tile's top stood 0.50 m over the floor and the whole crossing with it; `r_c:0:-0.5#...` changed nothing and was withdrawn). So `_prepare_crossing` lowers the tile itself and re-reads `_base_position` before the sink ever runs. It does not take the whole half metre: a row flush with the floor is invisible from a standing eye — the pit's own near wall occludes the stones' bodies and a top face at a grazing angle has no thickness — so the stones stand 0.22 m proud, as stepping stones, with a worn threshold ramp at each lip so the step onto them is never a wall for a body the engine will not lift. A sunken stone still comes to rest two centimetres proud of the bed: the floor that left you is the floor you land on.

**The deadline is stored, which is what makes a countdown possible.** The loop drew its wait and handed it straight to a timer, so the number existed only inside that timer: nothing could say how much was left, and a bar redrawn each frame would have been a fresh throw of the dice wearing a clock's face — Astra's exact warning. `_note_step` records the kind, the draw and the millisecond it expires; the tablet's bar, the advance ring and the probe all read that. The probe samples every 0.25 s for 22 s and fails if the drawn figure moves while a step is still running: zero moves over five stands and four gones.

**The cue is an interface, not a mechanism.** `advance_seconds` is 0 in all seven shipped placements, which leaves the tile its original beacon (lit while moving). Above 0 the tile grows a crown ring, lit only while it still stands and the stored deadline is inside the window. The probe asserts both halves: every crown moment is a standing moment with support still on and within the window, and the shipped beacon never lights while the tile stands. CUE on the lip turns the ring off and on for all three stones at once, so the comparison is a press and not a rebuild.

**A stone stands longer than it is gone.** The shipped band (1–7 s, one band for both ends) makes an absence as long as a presence, which is a coin toss with a one-metre penalty rather than a rhythm to read. The crossing sets stands 2.4–4.6 s and gone 1.1–2.2 s through a second band that is off by default (`hidden_span_seconds` 0 = the shipped call, unchanged). The gaps between standing stones are 0.12 m — a step, not a jump — and the end stones lap their lips by the same amount, which is the step onto the row.

**The bypass is the map's own corridor, once a doorway was cut.** The pathfinder showed the spawn corner sealed off by the pit to its south and a height-2 column at x 6: every artifact in the room was unreachable from the spawn, and the crossing would have been the only way out — the opposite of a bypass. Cell (6,2) drops from `"2"` to `"1"`, one doorway through that wall stub, and the room opens: 173 of 233 cells reachable, all nine artifacts, and the probe flood-fills the map's own structure from the north door to prove the far lip is reachable with no stone at all. The museum's own walk report is empty of severings for this hall in every run.

**The enemies moved out of the pit, not out of the room.** `scissor_stalker` (1,3) and `kaleidocycle_enemy` (5,3) stood inside the void; `kresling_spire` (3,4) stood exactly where the crossing's middle stone belongs. They are now at (2,8), (4,10) and (12,13) — in the arena, which the visitor meets after the crossing, with the projectile field further south again. That is Astra's "activate them as a distinct later variation" read as a route: the chasm first, the bestiary after.

**Exactness decisions.** Three stones, not five: five at this pitch make an 8.2 m set piece that fills the hall and crowds the corridor, and three independent draws already make the question ("will all three stand long enough?") the real one. The idol is a prism on a plinth rather than a treasure: what it carries is the seed. The tablet sits west of the row after the first live capture showed it blocking the whole crossing from a standing eye — panels outboard, the row clear — and the stele moved to the lip's east after it photographed against the hall's west wall with a museum wall work clipping it and a brazier drawn over it; east is also where a visitor arrives through the new doorway. The braziers' coals came down from 2.6 to 1.1 emissive after they photographed as white lamps.

**Rejected.** A raised stone apron with a watch ledge and a rim bypass (the first design): it needs ramps up from the floor and runs 9 m, it severs the hall's north–south corridor at the entrance, and it makes the gap a construction above the floor rather than the hall's own hole — the opposite of the thing Astra asked to have verified. Also rejected: flooring the pit and staging the chasm on top of it, for the same reason.

**Evidence.** Bare lane 47 checks / 0 failures; live lane 57 / 0 with the desktop rig pressing REPLAY, NEW SEED and CUE through the pointer, walking the row (across runs: one walk reached the far side with all three standing, one dropped at a stone that left under it), and a deliberate fall — the body stands on the middle stone at y 0.22, the stone leaves, the bed catches it at −1.02, it crosses to the ramp's foot at (1.51, −0.98, 5.56) and climbs out to y 0.00 on the hall floor.
