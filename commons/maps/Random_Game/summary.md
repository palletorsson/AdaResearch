# Random Game — Summary

Random_Game is the thirteenth and final map in the Randomness sequence. It converts every concept the sequence has introduced — distributions, independent samples, stochastic selection, entropy as erasure — into a playable arena where survival depends on inhabiting the distributions rather than predicting the instances.

The space is an 8×8 field inside a larger 12×12 map. Cubes fall from above on independent random timers, each with a sink-and-rise cycle that ignores the other cubes. The floor is therefore probabilistic rather than solid: any tile can be open or occupied at any moment, and the learner must cross the grid as the pattern churns. A cube_projectile_spawner controls the distribution and exposes mode toggles — uniform, clustered, wave-based — through a control at the edge of the arena.

Five origami enemies inhabit the field, each weaponising a different stochastic behaviour. Kaleidocycles tumble forward through four attack faces — fire, ice, spike, shield — cycling at random intervals. Kresling spires twist from flat discs into sniper towers and collapse again to relocate. Miura crawlers lie nearly flat on the floor, then unfold from the noise. Scissor stalkers extend telescoping legs to three times their compact length. Armadillo egglings roll in at randomised speeds.

A small controller at one corner manages game state and scoring. Within the sequence, Random_Game is the playable capstone. It does not test knowledge of randomness; it tests whether the learner can act inside it. The arena is the sequence's final exam, and passing it means having internalised the distributions well enough to move inside them as material rather than as obstacle.
