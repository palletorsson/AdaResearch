# Random Algorithms Arena

An 8x8 field of falling cubes. Each one sinks and rises on its own random timer — no pattern, no warning, no rhythm to learn. Cross the grid or get hit. The floor itself is probabilistic.

Origami enemies fold through the chaos. Kaleidocycles tumble forward, rotating through four attack faces — fire, ice, spike, shield — each color a different threat. Kresling spires twist from flat discs into sniper towers, then collapse to relocate. Miura crawlers lie flat, nearly invisible, then pop up from the noise. Scissor stalkers extend telescoping legs three times their compact length. Every enemy is a geometric mechanism that weaponizes uncertainty.

This is the arena where randomness stops being theory. Twelve maps of entropy culminate here — not in understanding randomness but in surviving it. The algorithm that cannot be predicted must be inhabited.
