# NonEuclidean_Spaces — Summary

The second map in the foundationscrisis sequence. A 15x13 grid, max height 4, built as a split dual landscape: a hyperbolic bowl on the left (heights descending to center, negative curvature) and an elliptic dome on the right (heights rising to center, positive curvature), connected by a narrow bridge at the midline.

Five artifacts span the two halves. `hyperbolic_surface` renders a room-scale saddle where geodesics diverge and triangle angles sum to less than 180 degrees. `elliptic_surface` renders a dome where all lines converge and triangles exceed 180 degrees. At the bridge, `curvature_slider` parametrizes the entire space of constant-curvature geometries along a continuous axis from K = -1 to K = +1, letting the learner morph one geometry into the other with a single drag. `poincare_disk` maps the infinite hyperbolic plane into a finite circle. `riemann_sphere` wraps the complex plane plus infinity onto a closed surface.

The map's argument is constructive: rather than asserting that the parallel postulate is independent, it builds the alternatives and lets them stand. Euclidean geometry is one point on a continuous spectrum of geometries, all equally consistent. The curvature slider makes this palpable — flatness is a parameter setting, not a law of nature.

Position: map 2 of 8 in foundationscrisis. Difficulty: advanced. Estimated time: 5-6 minutes. Follows Euclid_Parallel. Leads to Russell_Paradox, where the crisis shifts from geometry to logic.
