Concept: A hanging cloth pinned at its two top corners under gravity finds a catenary curve — the mathematical signature of any mass distribution suspended between two points. The map is a gallery study: a single soft-body marker holds the simulation; the player walks around it.

Sequence role: Cloth study (sb01) opening the imported soft-body gallery cluster within the Soft Bodies sequence (13th spine, integration phase). First of four cloth-drape variants (sb01–sb04) that systematically vary pinning topology — two corners (sb01), top row (sb02), one corner (sb03), flat tablecloth (sb04). The cluster establishes that pin-pattern alone determines drape signature.

Technical angle: Spring-mass cloth with structural, shear, and (optionally) bend constraints between mass points. Two corner masses pinned to fixed world positions; remaining masses fall under gravity until elastic equilibrium. Verlet or position-based-dynamics integration. The catenary emerges because the gradient of stored elastic energy must vanish at rest — and for a uniform line under gravity that minimum is hyperbolic-cosine-shaped.

Critical angle: The catenary is a thesis statement for the integration phase: form is not given by design but found by relaxation. The same equation governs suspension cables, hanging chains, soap-film arches, and this piece of cloth. Material specificity collapses into mathematical universality once you describe the constraints. The cloth is doing physics without knowing it is doing physics — which is what every material does.
