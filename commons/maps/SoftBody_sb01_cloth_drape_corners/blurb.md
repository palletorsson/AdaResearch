A rectangle of cloth held by its two top corners. Gravity does the rest. The middle sags into a catenary — the curve a hanging chain makes when the only forces are tension and weight. No designer drew this shape. It emerges from the argument between pinning and pull.

Each vertex is a mass point. Each neighbor relation is a spring. The form you see is the cloth solving thousands of small equations at once: how much can I stretch before my neighbor complains, how far can I sag before something has to break. The catenary is not imposed. It is found.

Hang anything by two points and it remembers the same shape. The cloth and the bridge cable speak the same language. Materially different, mathematically identical.
