# GT_Connectivity — Critical Reflection

Three rooms. Three strongly connected components. The passages between them run one way. Within each room, you can reach any point from any other point — full reciprocity, mutual access, everyone can get to everyone. Between rooms, the connections are directional. You can pass from A to B but not from B to A. Connection without reciprocity is just direction. The map makes directed graphs architectural by building rooms you can enter but cannot leave the way you came.

Foucault's analysis of enclosure — the prison, the hospital, the school, each a space of internal circulation and controlled entry — maps precisely onto the strongly connected component. Inside the component, movement is free. Every node reaches every other. The component is a zone of mutual reachability, a space where communication is symmetric and access is guaranteed. But the boundary of the component is a one-way membrane. You cross into the next room through a narrow passage, and the passage does not work in reverse. The SCC is not a community of equals; it is a zone of internal freedom bounded by irreversible transitions. Foucault would recognize the topology: freedom within the enclosure, discipline at the threshold.

The `tarjan_algorithm` artifact discovers these components in a single DFS pass. Its identity is the lowlink counter: `low_link[v] = min(low_link[v], discovery_time[w])` for back edges, and when `low_link[v] == discovery_time[v]`, pop the stack to extract an SCC. The algorithm descends into the graph, painting vertices yellow, then suddenly flashes an entire component in color when a root vertex is found. The discovery is retroactive — you do not know you are inside a component until you reach its root, the node whose lowlink equals its discovery time. The root is defined by what the algorithm finds after it, not before. Structure is revealed in retreat, not in advance. The SCC exists before the algorithm runs, but it becomes visible only during the backward phase, when the DFS retreats and the stack empties.

The `kosaraju_algorithm` takes the opposite approach: two passes. First, traverse the original graph and record finish times. Second, reverse every edge and traverse again in reverse finish order. What survives the reversal was strongly connected all along. The artifact's desire is to see the graph flip — the transpose switches edge directions, and the SCCs survive because mutual reachability is symmetric by definition. If A reaches B and B reaches A, then reversing all edges preserves both connections. The inversion is the proof. The reversal is the test.

Ranciere would find the most political structure in the condensation — the DAG that remains after all SCCs are contracted into single nodes. The strongly connected components become points, and the one-way connections between them become edges in a directed acyclic graph. The condensation strips internal complexity — all the mutual reachability, all the reciprocal communication within each component — and replaces it with a hierarchy of one-way dependencies. The DAG is the graph's power structure: an ordering where some components can reach others but not vice versa. Topological sort lays this flat, producing a linear sequence from the most upstream component to the most downstream. The condensation takes a graph with rich internal circulation and reduces it to a pipeline.

This is the operation Ranciere calls the police: the reduction of a complex social field to an ordered distribution of positions. Inside the SCC, politics is possible — everyone can reach everyone, communication is reciprocal, dissent can circulate. Between SCCs, politics is foreclosed — the connection is one-way, the downstream component cannot influence the upstream one, the hierarchy is structural. The condensation literally compresses the spaces of political possibility into points and preserves only the power relations between them.

The map's three rooms make this tangible. Room A (left, h1) holds Tarjan. Room B (center, h2) holds Kosaraju. Room C (right, h1) is connected to the exit. The height differences between rooms encode the hierarchy: B is elevated above A and C, the central component literally higher than the peripheral ones. The passages between rooms are narrow, single-cell, one-way. You experience the directed edge as a physical transition — a passage you can walk through but cannot walk back through. The map does not enforce one-way movement mechanically (the grid system does not implement directed edges), but the layout and wall placement create a spatial flow that discourages return. The direction is atmospheric rather than absolute.

What the map normalizes is the clean decomposition of a network into components and hierarchy. Tarjan finds the components. Topological sort orders them. The condensation produces a legible structure from a tangled graph. But the tangling was the point. The original graph had cycles — feedback loops, reciprocal dependencies, mutual influence. The condensation eliminates the cycles. What remains is a DAG, an ordering, a hierarchy. The algorithm that discovers structure also destroys it, replacing the graph's messy reciprocity with a clean one-way flow.

Connection without reciprocity is just direction. Structure is what survives when you flip every arrow. The question Tarjan and Kosaraju both answer — where are the strongly connected components? — is also the question they both suppress: what is lost when the components are contracted, the cycles eliminated, and the graph flattened into a sequence that flows one way and cannot talk back?
