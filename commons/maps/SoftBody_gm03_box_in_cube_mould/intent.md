Concept: A pressurized soft-body sphere is dropped inside a rectangular (box) mould with dimensions ~0.9×1.4×0.9. Internal pressure pushes vertices outward; contact constraints against the six flat faces of the cavity stop them when they reach the wall. The body settles into a rounded-corner box — flat-against-the-faces where the walls allowed it, rounded-along-the-edges where the bending springs resisted the sharp transitions. The map is a gallery study; the player walks around the single soft-body marker.

Sequence role: Third glass-meets-mould study (gm03) in the imported soft-body gallery cluster within the Soft Bodies sequence (13th spine, integration phase). Continues the gm-family with a non-axisymmetric cavity. gm01 cylindrical (constant circular cross-section) → uniform bottle; gm02 tapered (varying circular cross-section) → amphora; gm03 rectangular (flat faces, square edges) → rounded box. Pair-contrast with gm04: gm03 is a closed cavity that wraps the body; gm04 is an external solid that the body wraps around. The two complete the inside/outside topological inversion that the gm-family stages.

Technical angle: Spherical mesh of mass points with structural and bending springs. Pre-inflation pushes the body outward to fill most of the cavity. Mould collision: for each vertex, test against all six face planes of the box; if outside any face, project back to the nearest in-cavity position and zero outward velocity (or reflect with damping). Internal pressure applied per-face proportional to area. Gravity applied to every mass. Verlet or PBD integration for ~200 steps. Two competing constraints shape the corners: pressure pushes vertices into the corner region, but the bending springs resist the sharp curvature change between adjacent faces. The equilibrium settles at a finite corner radius determined by the ratio of pressure to bending stiffness — high pressure with low bending stiffness gives sharper corners; low pressure with high bending stiffness gives more rounded corners.

Critical angle: The gm-family's claim — cavity geometry encodes vessel silhouette — is sharpened by a non-axisymmetric example. With a cylinder or a taper, the cavity has a single radial dimension at each height; the vessel inherits this radius. With a box, the cavity has corners; the vessel inherits the corner-roundedness as a function of the body's own bending stiffness. So gm03 makes legible something that gm01 and gm02 hid: the cavity does not fully determine the vessel. The body's own material parameters (here, the bending stiffness) decide how cleanly the vessel can fit the cavity. Cavity + body together give the vessel; neither alone is enough. This is the integration-phase point made more precise — form emerges from the meeting of multiple rule systems, and the corners are where that meeting becomes visible.

Key artifacts: gallery_marker_soft-body holds the gm03_box_in_cube_mould configuration. The rounded-corner box pressed into the rectangular cavity is the artifact and the demonstration of body-cavity negotiation at edges.

Gap: A bending-stiffness slider on the gallery marker would let learners watch the corners sharpen and round as the parameter changes — same cavity, same pressure, different body, different vessel. The corners are where the parameter shows itself.
