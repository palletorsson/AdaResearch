Concept: Cloth hangs from a spinning carousel while soft mushrooms dangle and swing outward — centripetal force meets soft body compliance, turning rotation into a visual argument that gravity and inertia co-author shape. The carousel is the simplest machine that reveals how soft materials negotiate between attachment and momentum.
Sequence role: Second map in Soft Bodies (13th spine sequence, integration phase), beginner difficulty. After the jelly cube established spring-mass fundamentals, this map adds rotational kinematics — soft bodies constrained to a spinning frame. The beginner level is deliberate: the physics is visually intuitive (things swing outward) even though the math involves angular velocity and centripetal acceleration. Connects forward to SoftBodies_Obsticals where soft bodies negotiate static obstacles; follows SoftBodies_Soft_Body_Deformation.
Technical angle: Cloth simulation via spring-mass grid with structural, shear, and bend springs; fixed attachment points on the carousel hub create the constraint. Angular velocity drives centripetal acceleration (a = omega^2 * r), and the soft body's response is the visible swing angle. grab_long_stick and pick_up_cube introduce user interaction with soft body systems — grabbing and releasing to test real-time deformation response.
Critical angle: The carousel makes centripetal force legible through softness — a rigid pendulum swings too, but cloth and mushrooms reveal the force gradient across their surface, each point at a different radius experiencing different acceleration. This is distributed physics made visible: the material is the instrument that measures the force field it inhabits.
Key artifacts: cloth_straps simulate fabric strips hanging from the rotating hub; soft_mushroom demonstrates volumetric soft body behavior under rotation; revolving_joy_ride is the carousel mechanism itself; grab_long_stick and pick_up_cube provide interactive soft body manipulation.
Gap: Needs a variable-speed control — an artifact that lets the learner sweep angular velocity from zero to high, watching the transition from gravity-dominated drape to centripetally-dominated horizontal extension in real time.
