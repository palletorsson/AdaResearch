# Distances, membranes and strands

Noise_Inside_Noise changed the address used to sample an enclosing surface. This map takes the nearest-feature construction from [The Book of Shaders, chapter 12](https://thebookofshaders.com/12/) into three dimensions.

cellular_volume.gd uses seeded 3D sites. A cell border is a bisector plane. The distance from p to the plane between its nearest site a and another site b is (|p-b|²-|p-a|²)/(2|b-a|). Finite wall thickness turns the borders into solid partitions. Arched passages are subtracted along routes to the sixteen ground-level sites, and central wall portions are deleted inside a 4.6 m radius. Four upper sites make overhead faces. Marching tetrahedra extracts the resulting boundary and the same mesh creates its collision. The womb variant instead deforms an ellipsoidal shell with 3D Worley noise.

voronoi_strands.gd tetrahedralizes 150 seeded 3D feature points with Geometry3D.tetrahedralize_delaunay. Adjacent tetrahedra share a triangular face; joining their circumcentres traces a Voronoi edge. Hull faces supply outward rays. The network is clipped to an ellipsoid, unanchored boundary tips are pruned, and retained boundary points receive separate tethers. Every retained cell-edge midpoint is checked for equal distance to at least three nearest sites.

The sculpture uses batched six-sided tubes and small junction spheres, with no per-frame regeneration or line colliders. The attachment cords connect the Voronoi boundary to actual surrounding floor, wall and ceiling surfaces. The preview's R key changes the seed; Space highlights shared junctions. Box Noise next keeps the field while changing extraction to exposed box faces.

strand_network.gd splits actual 3D centreline contacts into shared junctions, while strands separated in depth remain separate. Unanchored tips are removed to convergence and only one connected component is retained. Tethers attach directly to existing floors, walls and ceilings. Outward 3D raycasts stop at the first surface; when they miss, lower wall probes locate alternatives and a second raycast checks the complete cord. Mesh endpoints, shared IDs, reachability and surface attachments are checked independently. The completed graph is then released into the tension solver; each junction remains one shared particle as the geometry deforms.

voronoi_weaver.gd animates an enlarged spider along a depth-first traversal of the retained edge graph, starting at an anchor. A new strand grows to its spinneret; completed lines enter the existing MultiMesh batches. Return trips follow finished strands. The cell geometry remains unchanged. Eight articulated legs move with distance travelled. The standalone preview provides B to replay, P to pause, F to reveal the complete structure and T to change speed. Map exhibits start automatically; #weave:false skips construction; #tension:false disables forces and #weave_speed:2 sets metres per second.

Real spiders also build [three-dimensional tangle webs](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/spider-webs.html). Here the graph comes from Voronoi distances and the spider traces it. The construction animation is artistic, not a biological growth model. The completed net uses an illustrative cable model for gravity, tension and damping.

Surface attachment generation waits for the map build signal and physics synchronization before sampling. It searches visible StaticBody3D surfaces on collision layer 1. Upward normals identify floors, downward normals identify ceilings, and lateral normals identify walls. The standalone preview supplies matching floor, wall and ceiling meshes and collision boxes. The open-roof map uses its existing floor and walls; it never invents a ceiling anchor. #surface_reach:80 sets the maximum search distance in metres; #wall_reach remains a legacy grid alias.

strand_dynamics.gd gives every shared knot one particle and every strand a tension-only elastic constraint, using [XPBD](https://mmacklin.com/xpbd.pdf). It integrates gravity at 120 Hz with six constraint passes and velocity damping. A stretched cable pulls; a slack cable cannot push. Room anchors have zero inverse mass. The mesh endpoints and junction spheres all read the same particle positions, so existing knots cannot separate. Sweeps against the room prevent moving knots from passing through its surfaces.

Construction remains an illustration until all strands are laid. The completed net is then released; unbuilt strands never provide invisible support. In the standalone preview, F finishes and releases it, G applies an impulse to a free knot near the centre of the view, P pauses and B restores the undeformed construction. The HUD shows peak cable force and extension. #tension:false disables relaxation; #compliance sets cable compliance in m/N and #knot_mass sets kilograms per free junction. The default values are 0.00002 m/N and 0.02 kg. These are illustrative cord parameters, not measured silk properties.

Relaxation preserves the graph, not the exact nearest-site geometry. The approximation does not model bending, breakage, strand self-collision, distributed strand weight or the spider's weight; new crossings created by motion do not become knots. The checks include an isolated hanging mass with extension mg/k and tension mg, slack cables with zero compression, frame-rate invariance, fixed room anchors and transmission of a tug to neighbouring knots.
