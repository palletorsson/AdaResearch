Concept: N-body dynamics and chaos — gravitational interactions between multiple bodies produce deterministic but unpredictable trajectories, where the three-body problem demonstrates that simple laws can generate fundamentally irreducible complexity.
Sequence role: Nineteenth map and the climax of the Forces sub-sequence; the most advanced map in the sequence. After particles (Forces_7) showed emergence from many identical bodies, this map shows chaos from few interacting bodies. The three-body problem is the canonical example of deterministic unpredictability — the equations are known, the solution is not; leads to VectorThrowing.
Technical angle: N-body gravitational simulation: each body computes force from every other body (O(n²) per frame). Two-body produces stable orbits; three-body produces chaos. Sensitivity to initial conditions — infinitesimal changes produce divergent trajectories. The chaos_attractor shows strange attractors; forcedirected3d applies the same mathematics to graph layout.
Critical angle: Chaos is where the sequence's dynamical arc reaches its limit. Forces_1 introduced F=ma as the key to prediction; Forces_8 shows where prediction fails despite knowing the law exactly. The three-body problem is not unsolved because we lack information — it is unsolved because the dynamics are inherently non-integrable. Determinism without predictability. This is the QFEP entropy term asserting itself: even in a purely mechanical system, complexity wins.
Key artifacts: three_body_problem as the canonical chaotic system; nbody_simulation for the general N-body case; example_2_8 (two-body attraction) as the solvable baseline; example_2_9 (N-body attraction) for the full ensemble; chaos_attractor for the geometric signature of chaos; forcedirected3d for N-body math applied to non-physical domains (graph layout).
