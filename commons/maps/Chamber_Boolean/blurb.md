The chamber's verb is *to compose*.

A workshop after the wall of cavities. You place a block, and then another, and the room asks the only question the chapter cares about — join, share, or remove? Union fuses and dissolves the seam. Intersection keeps only the shared interior. Difference carves a hollow shaped by absence.

You are not stacking blocks but composing them, and the order of your verbs matters the way it does in arithmetic. It is the fitting seed for a chapter that reduced building to a grammar: three words, and with them you can say any shape at all. A wall is just a difference. A joint is just an intersection. All the way down.
