# LSystems_Living — Summary

The grammar becomes the world. The terrain itself — platforms rising at organic heights, clearings opening where no rule reached — follows branching logic. The structure layer encodes an L-system's output as ground, with branch depth mapped to platform height and voids marking where the rewriting process terminated.

At the center, the `genetic_tree_sculptor` provides eight sliders controlling a tree's DNA: depth, forks, branch angle, length taper, leaf density, tropism, twist, and phyllotactic arrangement. The Randomize button generates novel genomes. The Plant button arms the branching catalyst, letting the learner place authored trees into the living ecosystem. Each planted tree grows according to its grammar, competing with existing organisms for shared light and soil.

The `AnimatedTree` demonstrates step-by-step growth within the ecosystem context. The `branching_coral` extends grammar-driven morphogenesis to marine forms. The warm amber lighting and green-tinted sky signal the transition from lab to living environment.

This is the L-Systems capstone, the synthesis of six maps. Grammar Lab established the pipeline. Growth added time and context. Grammars_And_Curves formalised the mechanism. Architecture changed the interpreter. Competition introduced ecology.

Living dissolves the boundary between tool and world. The forest does not know it is a sentence. The grammar has individuated so completely into its milieu that the rules are invisible in the terrain they produced. The teleporter leads to the Procedural Generation sequence, where the question becomes: if grammar can build worlds, what else can?
