Concept: The loss landscape as terrain — optimization as walking downhill through a surface defined by error. Gradient descent replaces evolution's blind search with a directed step: compute the slope, move opposite to it, repeat.
Sequence role: Second map in the Machine Learning sequence (15th spine sequence, integration phase). Bridges from evolution's stochastic search to calculus-driven optimization. The landscape metaphor introduced here persists through every subsequent map — neural networks, classification boundaries, and generative models all live on loss surfaces. Connects back to ML_Evolution (random search) and forward to ML_Classification (applying descent to decision boundaries).
Technical angle: Gradient computation via partial derivatives, learning rate as step size, local minima vs. global minima, saddle points in high-dimensional spaces. Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) as a noisy approximation that paradoxically escapes local minima better than exact gradients. Momentum and adaptive learning rates (Adam, RMSProp) as refinements.
Critical angle: The loss landscape is the central metaphor of modern ML — you optimize what you can measure, and the landscape's shape determines what solutions are reachable. Saddle points outnumber local minima in high dimensions, which is why deep learning works at all. The landscape you walk is defined by your loss function, which encodes your values — optimization is never value-neutral.
Key artifacts: gradient_descent_visualizer provides an interactive 3D loss surface with configurable learning rate, momentum, and starting position to demonstrate convergence behavior.
