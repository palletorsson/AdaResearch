# Procedural Generation 5

A grid. Eight by eight. The space where algorithms perform — not as abstractions but as visible, spatial events. Processes that rely on chance made concrete in cells you can watch populate, shift, dissolve.

An L-system tree grows from a single axiom: `F→F[+F]F[-F]F`. One rule. Five symbols. The result is branching complexity that looks grown, not designed. Layered membranes and implicit surfaces emerge from the same logic — simple instructions iterated until structure appears from nowhere in particular.

This is the arena where randomness proves it has shape. Where procedural generation reveals its central paradox: the more you constrain the dice, the more organic the output. Order doesn't oppose chaos here. It metabolizes it.
