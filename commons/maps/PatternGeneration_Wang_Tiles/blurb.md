# Wang Tiles

Square tiles with colored edges. Place two tiles adjacent — their touching edges must match. Hao Wang conjectured in 1961 that any tile set capable of covering the plane could do so periodically. He was wrong. Certain sets tile forever without repeating. Constraint produces aperiodicity. Rules that demand local agreement generate global inexhaustibility.

An 8×8 grid. Each 2×2 sub-tile carries a slightly different height configuration — subtle variations in a field of sameness. Walk the room and feel the pattern that never quite settles. Edge matching is local. The consequence is infinite. The simplest possible constraint — neighbors must agree — is sufficient to guarantee that no repetition will ever occur. Order so thorough it becomes indistinguishable from freedom.
