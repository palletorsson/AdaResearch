The Morphology Engine

What is the universal vocabulary of form? Not form as in shape — form as in process. A tree is not a shape. A tree is the record of a growth algorithm encountering light and gravity over time. A crystal is not a shape either. A crystal is the record of a lattice energy minimization. The shape is evidence. The process is the thing.

Every shape is primitives + modifiers + time. The question is not what it looks like but how it was made.

The Problem

Ada Research has four kingdom-specific morphology builders: one for trees (L-system branching), one for creatures (spine segmentation), one for flowers (Bézier petal rings), one for fungi (revolution caps). Each hardcodes its construction strategy. A tree can only be grown. A creature can only be extruded. There is no vocabulary for saying: what if a tree were carved? What if a creature were crystallized?

The existing DNA system already hints at the answer. Genes like part_twist and part_curve describe process, not fixed form. The same gene means helical body twist for creatures, petal twist for flowers, and stem spiral for fungi. This polymorphic interpretation is already a universal morphology engine — it just does not know it yet.

Three Classes

The engine is three GDScript files. No external dependencies. Each is a static library of pure functions.

MorphoPrimitive

Atomic form generators. Every shape starts here. The tube function — extracted from identical code duplicated in both CreatureMorphology and TreeMorphology — is the workhorse. It sweeps a circular cross-section between two points with start and end radii.

Surface generators: tube, multi_tube, bezier_sweep, revolution
Godot wrappers: sphere, cylinder, cone, box, torus, capsule, prism, quad, plane
Field generators: sdf_mesh + 6 SDF combinators (union, subtract, intersect, smooth_union)
Instance generators: multimesh_scatter

The SDF combinators are the most interesting. They let you compose distance functions: a smooth union of two spheres makes a metaball. A subtraction of a box from a sphere makes a carved shell. The sdf_mesh function evaluates any distance function on a voxel grid and extracts the surface.

MorphoModifier

Mesh transformations via MeshDataTool. Each takes a mesh and returns a modified mesh. They compose by chaining:

noise_displace(twist(taper(mesh)))

Order matters. Taper-then-twist produces a tornado. Twist-then-taper produces a drill. Same two operations, different sequence, different form. This is the morphological equivalent of non-commutativity.

MorphoPipeline

The composable form recipe. A pipeline chains:

The children field makes it recursive. A tree trunk pipeline has branch children, which have leaf children. This captures the vast majority of biological forms: spine with limbs, stem with petals, trunk with branches with leaves.

Taxonomy of Process

The deepest idea is not the code. The deepest idea is the classification. Every form in the universe was made by a process. The process has thermodynamic character: how stable is it (F)? How much entropy does it inject (E)? How fast is it changing (φ)?

Grown

F: High | E: Low | φ: Low
Trees, coral, blood vessels. L-systems, fractals.

Extruded

F: Medium | E: Low | φ: Low
Worms, spines, tubes. Sweeps, profiles.

Carved

F: High | E: Medium | φ: Medium
Shells, caves. Boolean ops, SDFs.

Folded

F: Variable | E: Variable | φ: High
Insects, proteins, leaves. Origami, springs.

Crystallized

F: Very High | E: Very Low | φ: Very Low
Minerals, diatoms, snowflakes. Polyhedra, lattices.

Dissolved

F: Low | E: High | φ: Medium
Slime mold, lichen, clouds. Reaction-diffusion.

Assembled

F: Medium | E: Medium | φ: Medium
Colonial organisms, machines. Graph composition.

Instanced

F: High | E: Medium | φ: Low
Forests, swarms, colonies. Arrays, scatter.

Each existing kingdom maps naturally. Trees are Grown + Instanced. Creatures are Extruded + Assembled. Flowers are Extruded + Instanced. Fungi are Grown + Instanced + Assembled. But the vocabulary is open. A creature can be Carved. A tree can be Crystallized. The form_process gene is a continuous float from 0 to 1, so 0.35 is something between Grown and Extruded — a transitional process with no name yet.

All continuous. All heritable. All subject to crossover and mutation. A creature with form_process=0.55 and surface_method=0.5 will be built via SDF carving instead of spine extrusion — a carved creature, something the old system could not express.

The Biome Preview

To see these organisms in context, the creature editor now has a biome mode. Select a curriculum stage — from barren primitives to lush self-generating ecosystems — and toggle Biome Mode. The viewport fills with ground, foliage, fog, and a ring of organisms matching that stage’s kingdoms.

Ground: PlaneMesh with biome shader (grey → brown → green by density)
Foliage: MultiMesh grass quads, flower spheres, tree cylinders, fungus caps
Atmosphere: Fog density + ambient color lerps from clinical lab to deep forest
Organisms: 4–8 DNA creatures from the stage’s active kingdoms
20 stages: From empty (density 0) to self-generating (density 1.0)

The dashboard at /morphology mirrors this with data visualization: a donut chart of kingdom distribution, histograms of gene frequencies, radar charts of the five process genes per creature, and the 20-stage biome progression as a clickable timeline.

The left end is maximum free energy — rigid, stable, crystalline form that resists change. The right end is maximum entropy — dissolved, diffusing, reaction-diffusion form that embraces instability. The fold system sits in the middle as the rate-of-change term: spring velocity, tension, misfold. The mesh itself is the Markov blanket — the boundary between what the organism is (DNA, energy, fold state) and what the world is (environment, other organisms, the player).

Form is not a thing. Form is a process with a thermodynamic address.

What This Makes Possible

Before today, a creature was always an extruded spine with limbs. Now it can be a carved SDF surface. A folded origami shell. A crystallized polyhedron. The same DNA system that breeds trees and flowers can breed math objects, architectural elements, and abstract sculptures.

The five new genes are heritable. When a tree breeds with a creature, the offspring’s form_process is a blend — something between grown and extruded. Over evolutionary generations, the population explores the full process space, finding forms that the four kingdom builders could never produce.

The pipeline is recursive. A creature with high recursion_depth grows branches from its limbs, which grow sub-branches, which grow leaves. The same pipeline that makes a tree can make a fractal creature. The same modifiers that twist a petal can twist a building.
