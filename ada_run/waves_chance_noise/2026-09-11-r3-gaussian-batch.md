## One reusable improvement, and its conditions

**The expectation on the bars' own scale, with the fold counted.** A histogram normalised to its tallest bar always looks full; a density curve at its own scale always looks like the law. Neither can be compared with the other. Drawing the expected count of every bin at the present N behind the bars — the mass beyond the display folded into the edge bins, and the number of folded draws printed — gives the sample a comparison of its own kind. Conditions: the expectation computed from the law the sampler actually runs (its own transform, its own clamp), not from a textbook density; the same normalisation as the bars; the edge bins carrying the folded mass; the count of folds printed beside them; the bins printed too, and a re-binning that touches no draw. Random_Entropy's ghosts (expected counts under the active law) were the first use; this is the second, in a room where the clamp matters. A third would justify extracting it.
